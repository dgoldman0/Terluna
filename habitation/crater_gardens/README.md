# Crater garden settlements

The settlement is a small town built in terraces around a central lake. Pale arcades, planted roofs and balconies, vine-covered walks, bridges and a pavilion on an island give the town its garden character. Buildings step down the crater wall; outer gardens extend beyond the rim. The reference illustration establishes the architectural character, while the dimensions below are adopted planning estimates.

## Size and form

The built ring and crater measure **500–1,000 m rim to rim**, with **750 m** as the representative example. In that example the central lake measures **300–400 m across**, the rim stands **40–70 m above the lake**, and buildings generally have **2–4 storeys** arranged along **4–6 terraced bands**. The settlement and its outer gardens together measure approximately **1.5 km across**, occupying **1.8 km²**. The lake's water depth remains a design choice. Shallow, worn or partly filled craters are the preferred starting terrain.

The adopted population calculation takes the lake's diameter as half the settlement's diameter, so water occupies one-quarter of the enclosed plan area. It sums the buildings' footprints across the terraces and multiplies each footprint by its storeys. Open terraces and roof gardens provide outdoor space. Homes, workplaces, shops and community facilities share the resulting built floor area.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Building footprint as a share of ground outside the lake | 30% | 35% | 45% |
| Mean storeys per building | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Total built floor per resident, including work and services | 100 m² | 70 m² | 70 m² |

These rounded estimates follow from enclosed area × 0.75 × building coverage × mean storeys ÷ floor per resident. The 750 m representative settlement provides approximately **350,000 m² of built floor for 5,000 residents**, while about **74% of its enclosed plan area remains water or open ground**. The 70 m² allowance follows the [summit metropolis brief](../summit_metropolis/README.md); 100 m² is the spacious variant. [design.json](design.json) records the calculation inputs in a machine-readable form.

## Number and setting

The current [buildout plan](buildout.json) selects the best **2.5% of all catalogued craters 200–1,000 m across** for eventual open communities, with **0.1% of the same catalogue population** planned for the initial vacuum period. The initial communities form a subset of the eventual set, approximately one in twenty-five of its sites. These fractions replace the earlier 20,000–100,000-site abundance scenario as the programme target; that scenario remains in [design.json](design.json) and the decisions register as the original comparison. The fractions specify how many sites to seek, while geometry, location and engineering determine which sites can support the plan.

Every initial community must function efficiently through a potentially long vacuum period and occupy a location suited to the completed Open Moon. Pressurised travel and freight connect communities throughout that period. As terraforming changes external pressure, weather and water, the enclosure and its services must continue to work under the changing loads. Once a sustainable breathable atmosphere is established, the enclosures are removed and the communities become domeless. The study evaluates this transition without assigning an opening date or changing the adopted architectural population cases.

The initial settlements each house approximately **500–1,000 people beneath one contiguous dome**, with the eventual community's gardens, terraces, shared spaces and everyday life substantially in place. Using the existing representative floor-space case to interpret that population gives crater diameters of approximately **238–336 m**. The study starts with these smaller complete communities and assesses the single dome's requirements. Subdivision remains an option for larger settlements.

Watered uplands and the transition between wetter and drier regions are the first settings to examine. Irrigated gardens remain possible farther into the dry regions. The project's current climate products supply regional warmth and rain; each crater's water balance, local air, soil and planting still require assessment within the long lunar light cycle.

## First catalogue count

The first count belongs to `domain/provisioning`, in `research/studies/crater_gardens/`. It selects the adopted 500–1,000 m diameter band and a further 200–500 m comparison band, uses published depth estimates where available, and applies broad standing-water and climate exclusions. The report retains every exclusion's effect and the catalogue's coverage and quality limits. Counts describe terrain candidates at the published data's resolution; excavation volumes, foundations, water tightness, access, overlapping estates and detailed heritage evaluation follow with terrain inspection.

The smaller bowls also serve a proposed vacuum-era settlement phase. A supplied engineering discussion considers pressure enclosures, their anchoring, shielding and subdivision, with a 300 m dome as its suggested reference. Its pressure, material, roof and shielding choices remain proposals. The crater census supplies the available terrain by size and depth; pressure-vessel structure and a feasible roof span require their own engineering study.

The design and population assumptions were adopted on 12 November 2026 in the [decisions register](../../research/decisions.md#crater-garden-settlements-12-november-2026). The catalogue work tests the initial abundance scenario against identified craters while preserving these adopted settlement dimensions.
