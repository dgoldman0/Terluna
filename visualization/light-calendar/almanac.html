READING THIS WORLD

Twilight has room to travel.

Terluna’s deep atmosphere carries scattered sunlight far beyond the horizon. Sunrise and sunset describe the Sun’s position; lux describes the light available at the surface. Earth adds its own changing light across the near side and the lunar limb.

The atmosphere and light sources

The calculator uses the solved, horizontally uniform 1.2-atm molecular atmosphere and the selected solar shield. It integrates the Sun and a phase-dependent Earth disk through a spectral scattering model. Earth’s spectrum and total phase brightness come from published models and observations.

The horizon is open and level. Local terrain, clouds, aerosols, refraction, starlight and airglow are additional inputs. An eclipse flag identifies times when the unobscured reference is affected by Earth crossing the Sun.

Calendar accuracy and time

TT is the uniform time scale used by the orbital calculation. UTC uses historical leap seconds through 2017 and assumes a 69.184-second TT−UTC offset thereafter. Future changes to civil time can be updated separately.

Lux, colour and visual appearance

Lux measures light falling on a horizontal surface, weighted for daytime human vision. Surface brightness also depends on reflectance and viewing direction. The chart spans many orders of magnitude, so each major step is ten times brighter. The sky compass and Earth icon show geometry.

Earthlight is a mean model. Earth’s rotating continents, seasons and changing clouds cause variation that this calendar does not forecast. Displayed values are rounded to useful precision.
