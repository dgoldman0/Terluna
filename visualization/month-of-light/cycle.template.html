Weather over the bay

A broken cloud layer, with moving shadows over the water.

Clouds and weather are selected scenarios. Their cover, optical depth, visibility and motion are independent inputs. They are not a forecast of lunar weather or a coupled climate simulation.

The sky beneath the weather comes from the spectral calculation. Cloud shading, cloud shadows, fog, wet surfaces and rain use reduced visual approximations. The lux badges retain the clear-sky baseline, so they do not mislabel the weather effects as validated photometry.

Cloud layer: prescribed 1.5–3.6 km; lighting sampled at 2.5 km. Cloud / wave animation uses a separate clock. Rainfall rates, cloud formation and ecological responses are not inferred.

What midday means here

Noon places the Sun overhead. Use Overhead or Follow Sun to look toward it; West / bay keeps the landscape in view. The preceding twilight viewer ended at +12° and could not show this part of the day.

Light through the complete cycle

Logarithmic lux axis. Earth and Moon use matching Sun angles. The playback clock changes elapsed time, leaving the underlying optical calculation fixed.

Calculated atmosphere

Straight spherical rays, visible spectral molecular scattering through repeated scattering orders, ozone absorption, a finite solar disk, and Lambertian ground reflection. Molecular profiles remain reduced optical proxies. Refraction, climate closure, full absorption bands, polarization and a solved ozone distribution are outside this model.

Scenery and weather

The wooded cove is an illustrative local landscape, with detailed geometry, diffuse and direct lighting, approximate shadows, aerial perspective and sky-reflecting water. Its terrain, tree forms, water and weather have been chosen to make changing light legible. They are not measurements or predictions of a particular lunar site.

Cloud optics, fog and surface wetness use a reduced display model driven by the calculated illumination. Cloud formation, winds, rainfall and changing temperature have not been solved. Dawn uses the same stationary atmosphere as dusk. The scene uses local tangent geometry; the sky solver retains planetary curvature.

Night and exposure

The light source is the Sun. Earthlight, stars, airglow and artificial lights are omitted. Below −35°, the diffuse source grid remains coarser and its values are exploratory. Fixed daylight exposure preserves the large brightness change. Camera auto is a display aid, with a shared exposure in Compare; it is not a physiological eye-adaptation model.
