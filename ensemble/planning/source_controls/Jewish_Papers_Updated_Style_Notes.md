# Style notes governing both papers

Verification and revision date: **2026-07-26**

These notes preserve the user’s standing prose requirements and incorporate the diagnosis developed after review of both manuscripts.

## Governing movement

The papers advance their claims through affirmative historical, legal, and theological development. Counterpositions are answered within that development at the point where their premises arise. Freestanding objection sections, defensive inventories, and conclusion-as-recap structures are excluded.

A paragraph must do more than place true propositions beside one another. It should unfold through historical sequence, cause and consequence, institution and human effect, principle and application, or tension and resolution. Its final sentence should complete that movement or create pressure inherited by the next paragraph.

## Sentence-to-sentence inheritance

Except at a deliberate turn, every sentence should receive something from the sentence before it: an actor, object, image, legal term, causal result, temporal development, or unresolved implication. The inherited element should then be transformed or carried forward.

The reorderability test is mandatory. When two or more sentences can exchange places without changing the paragraph’s logic, the relationship among them remains unwritten and the passage must be reconstructed.

Repeated paratactic declaratives create the “beads placed beside one another” effect. A chain of proposition–stop–proposition sentences should be recast through syntactic hierarchy, using subordination, apposition, participial construction, relative clauses, or periodic structure to express how the claims depend upon one another.

Short declaratives remain available for documentary shocks, hinges, and landings. Their force should arise from surrounding variation.

## Cadence and syntax

Sentence openings, subjects, lengths, and stress patterns should vary with argumentative force. Repeated subject–verb resets and mechanically parallel sentence frames require revision even when each sentence is individually sound.

Coordination belongs to ideas of equal force. Causal, temporal, constitutive, concessive, and evidentiary relations should appear within the syntax. Passive voice may be used when agency is already known, when an institutional process matters more than an individual actor, or when it improves continuity and rhythm.

Every major transition and conclusion receives a read-aloud pass. Breath, stress, recurring grammatical frames, sentence-length distribution, and paragraph landings should be adjusted until cadence follows the argument.

## Vividness through action

Vividness comes from exact nouns and verbs grounded in the record. Communities build, recopy, petition, bury, preserve, transmit, resist, and return. Courts admit, ministries classify, states withhold or restore status, households carry inheritance, and archives disperse.

Generic analytical verbs—*shows, provides, supports, demonstrates, allows, gives, establishes,* and *reflects*—must earn their place. Where the evidence names an action, the prose should name that action.

Historical actors remain visible through work, loss, choice, institutional life, correspondence, protest, and memory. Legal mechanisms should reveal what they do to persons, families, and communities.

Sustained lexical threads may bind distant sections when they grow from the documentary record. Relevant threads include:

- house, household, and House of Israel;
- manuscripts that traveled while descendants remained;
- appeals and answers;
- inheritance carried through lineages;
- institutional doors opened, closed, or withheld.

These threads should recur sparingly. Decorative adjectives and invented sentiment are excluded.

## Scholarly stance

Basic concepts expected of the intended readership should be situated through their use and argumentative relevance. Elementary definitions, tutorial phrasing, reader management, and repeated announcements of obvious implications are excluded.

Self-reference to “this paper,” “this article,” “the present analysis,” or similar framing should appear only where genuine methodological disclosure requires it. The argument should normally stand in declarative scholarly prose without describing itself.

Negation is reserved for propositions that logically require it. Affirmative formulations should carry the paper’s central structure.

## Comparative precedents in the Kaifeng paper

Beta Israel and the Samaritans receive coequal argumentative weight, source rigor, human presence, structural placement, and consequence while performing distinct work.

Beta Israel carries collective Jewish recognition across centuries of autonomous legal and ritual development beyond rabbinic centers. The Samaritan arrangement carries civil return for a distinct, patrilineal people of Israel under its own communal institutions. Kaifeng joins those grounds through a historically Jewish source community whose inherited membership traveled through paternal lineages.

Coequal treatment does not require identical word counts or mirrored subsections. It requires both precedents to shape the opening history, central legal inference, remedy, and conclusion. A detachable precedent catalogue fails the requirement.

Beta Israel patrilineal kinship and genealogical memory may be discussed where supported. Israeli recognition may not be represented as adoption of a general patrilineal Jewish-status rule. Established Beta Israel remains analytically distinct from Falash Mura, Feres Mura, and related cases involving ancestral Christianity and separate governmental programs.

## Conclusions

A conclusion should gather the paper’s historical, juridical, and human lines through syntactic accumulation. It should neither replay the section order nor place one summary sentence beside another.

The final paragraph of the Kaifeng paper remains centered on Kaifeng descendants and the answer owed to the historical appeal. Beta Israel and Samaritan recognition should funnel the argument back toward Kaifeng, after which the final sentence should land on inherited membership and return.

## Required editorial passes

1. **Bead-string pass:** identify chains of equal-weight declaratives and write their relations into syntax.
2. **Inheritance pass:** confirm that each sentence receives and transforms material from the sentence before it.
3. **Reorderability pass:** reconstruct paragraphs whose sentence order remains arbitrary.
4. **Verb pass:** replace generic evidentiary verbs with historically or juridically exact action.
5. **Human-presence pass:** restore documented persons, families, institutions, choices, losses, and resistance where abstraction has displaced them.
6. **Transition pass:** carry a live actor, object, image, term, or unresolved act across paragraph and section boundaries.
7. **Defensive-structure pass:** remove objection catalogues, anticipatory disclaimers, and sections organized around hostile questions.
8. **Redundancy pass:** remove explanations that merely tell the reader what the evidence has already made apparent.
9. **Cadence pass:** read aloud all major transitions, conclusions, and paragraphs with dense legal exposition.
10. **Final-page pass:** ensure the prose accelerates toward the concluding obligation instead of flattening into recap.
