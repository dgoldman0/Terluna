# Governing style notes

These notes govern both papers and every later revision in this project.

## 1. Argument moves through prose

A paragraph must develop through cause, consequence, sequence, inheritance, institutional action, or human experience. It may contain several propositions, but those propositions must act upon one another. A paragraph should cease to work if its sentences are freely reordered.

Each sentence should inherit an actor, object, image, term, or unresolved pressure from the sentence before it, except where a deliberate turn opens a new movement. Transitions should arise from the subject matter itself. Formulaic signposts and announcements of what the reader should conclude weaken the argument.

Repeated paratactic declaratives require revision. A sequence in which one sentence states a proposition, stops, and yields to another proposition produces a row of separate beads. Relations should instead be carried through subordination, apposition, participial phrases, relative clauses, periodic structure, anaphora used sparingly, and changes in sentence length. Short declaratives belong at hinges and landings, where their finality has earned force.

## 2. Cadence and syntax

Paragraphs should vary sentence openings, grammatical subjects, lengths, internal pauses, and locations of emphasis. Consecutive sentences beginning with the same abstract noun usually signal a list disguised as prose. Abstract nouns may lead when they govern a real movement; otherwise, people, institutions, texts, courts, households, and laws should act.

A strong paragraph commonly opens with orientation, develops through two or more linked sentences, and lands on a sentence whose language gathers the paragraph's work. The pattern is flexible. Mechanical alternation and ornamental rhythm are equally undesirable.

## 3. Vividness through exact action

Vividness should come from precise nouns and verbs, not from decorative adjectives. Communities build, copy, bury, petition, teach, preserve, disperse, remember, and return. Households register children, guard genealogies, tend graves, and carry names. Courts admit, classify, recognize, and withhold. Ministries register, narrow, restore, and administer. Manuscripts travel; institutions collapse; legal categories open or close doors.

Human beings remain visible whenever the sources permit. Chao Nien-tsu, Ester Guo, Moshe Lee, Beta Israel immigrants, Samaritan families, rabbis, officials, and community elders should appear as historical actors rather than illustrations attached after an abstract argument.

Recurring images may bind distant sections when they arise from the evidence: the house or House of Israel; manuscripts that travelled while descendants remained; appeals and institutional answers; lineages carrying what buildings and schools once held; doors opened by recognition and closed by misclassification. Recurrence must deepen meaning rather than repeat a slogan.

## 4. Scholarly stance

The papers assume a scholarly reader. Familiar legal and historical vocabulary should be used without tutorial phrasing, while contested or newly proposed concepts receive exact argument. Definitions belong where ambiguity would otherwise alter the reasoning. Elementary explanations, rhetorical questions, and phrases that escort the reader toward an already visible conclusion should be removed.

Self-reference should be exceptional. Phrases such as "this paper," "the present article," "the argument below," and references to accompanying records should disappear from the papers themselves. The prose should state the history, law, and theory directly.

Affirmative exposition governs structure as well as diction. Counterpositions should be answered within the positive development of the argument before they harden into separate defensive sections. "Common objections," rebuttal catalogues, and other adversarial appendages are excluded. Negative words remain available where logical accuracy requires them; defensive contrast formulas such as "not X but Y" and "rather than" should be rare.

## 5. Comparative precedents in the Kaifeng paper

Beta Israel and the Samaritans carry coequal argumentative weight while performing distinct work.

Beta Israel establishes collective Jewish recognition across centuries of isolation, autonomous Torah life, divergent institutions, incomplete state documentation, and contested historical theories. Its history also records the injury produced when people already received as a Jewish community are processed through conversionary suspicion. The comparison must distinguish the established Beta Israel community from Falash Mura questions.

The Samaritan arrangement establishes the civil capacity of Israel to receive a distinct, patrilineally constituted people of Israel through the Law of Return while preserving Samaritan identity, courts, and personal law. It should never be described as a general judicial holding for every Israelite-descended population.

Kaifeng stands where the two histories converge: a Jewish source community whose surviving Israelite membership travelled through patrilineal households. The comparisons should emerge from the same historical and legal architecture as the Kaifeng analysis, shape the evidentiary and remedial sections, and return in the conclusion. They must never read as detachable examples added after the main argument.

## 6. Conclusions

A conclusion should accumulate the paper's history and concepts without restating section headings in miniature. Its paragraphs should move from concrete inheritance, through institutional or legal meaning, toward a final formulation that belongs specifically to the people and history at issue.

The final paragraph of the Kaifeng paper beginning "The question sent from Kaifeng in 1850" is protected. Future revisions may alter it only in response to a factual error or an explicit instruction from the author.

## 7. Required editorial passes

Before release, each paper receives the following separate reviews:

1. **Bead-string pass:** locate consecutive proposition-stop-proposition sentences and create explicit relations among them.
2. **Inheritance pass:** verify that each sentence carries forward an actor, term, image, or pressure from the previous sentence.
3. **Reorderability pass:** revise paragraphs whose sentence order could change without consequence.
4. **Verb pass:** replace inert abstractions with exact institutional and human action where the evidence permits.
5. **Human-presence pass:** restore people, households, communities, and decision-makers to passages dominated by impersonal categories.
6. **Transition pass:** remove generic signposts and build transitions from the argument's materials.
7. **Defensive-structure pass:** remove objection catalogues, anticipatory apologies, and structures organized around hostile readers.
8. **Redundancy pass:** cut repeated definitions, elementary explanations, and conclusions already made visible by the evidence.
9. **Cadence pass:** vary syntax, sentence length, openings, and terminal stress while preserving scholarly restraint.
10. **Final-page pass:** read the abstract, opening two pages, and conclusion as one arc; verify that the paper promises, develops, and lands on the same governing claim.
