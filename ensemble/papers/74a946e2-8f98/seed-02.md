# Establishing and Sustaining a Lunar Biosphere

**Paper ID:** `74a946e2-8f98`

**Status:** revised conceptual seed for author review; uncommitted.

A lunar biosphere would begin with organisms brought from Earth and develop through descendants growing under new conditions. As they reproduced, those organisms would also alter the places available to subsequent generations. Microbial communities could change nutrient availability in prepared ground; vegetation could retain water and organic material; the resulting soils could support communities with different requirements. Establishment would therefore involve a sequence in which living populations progressively participate in making their own habitats. The central biological achievement would be sustained reproduction and ecological function across that changing environment.

Choosing the founding populations would require evidence at the scale of complete lives. Growth under an unfamiliar gravity or light regime would have to be followed through development, reproduction, and the health of descendants. In orbital experiments with mice at approximately one-sixth gravity, soleus-muscle mass was preserved while changes in muscle-fiber composition persisted.[^biosphere-gravity] Each proposed community would bring further dependencies: a plant population might require a disperser, a grazer a continuing food supply, and both a functioning microbial environment. For human settlement, the corresponding biological questions extend through childhood, reproduction, aging, and the interventions needed to sustain health.

The lunar light cycle would connect those individual requirements through a shared energy budget. In regions receiving prolonged darkness, reserves accumulated during illumination would have to sustain respiration and feeding until adequate light returned. Some populations might reduce activity, while others continued using retained food or resources supplied from neighboring habitats. In aquatic communities, oxygen supply and mixing would enter the same assessment as food availability. Testing repeated light–dark cycles across linked producers, consumers, and decomposers would reveal whether a proposed assemblage could recover its reserves while continuing to reproduce. Communities with different storage and timing strategies could develop markedly different patterns of activity.

Their productivity would also depend on the ground and water into which they were introduced. Earth-based experiments with Apollo regolith achieved plant germination and growth alongside substantial stress responses, making substrate preparation an important part of biological establishment.[^biosphere-regolith] A staged program could combine mineral conditioning with organic inputs and selected microbial communities, then follow how growth and decomposition changed nutrient availability. Agricultural production could contribute biomass for extending those processes into new terrain. The transition from cultivated substrate to functioning soil would be measured through continued plant performance and material cycling as external inputs changed.

Once such processes were established, ecological structure could exploit opportunities created by the environment. Reduced gravitational loading makes a wider range of plant architectures worth testing, including taller growth and branches supporting additional living substrates. Hydraulic resistance, wind loading, and reproduction would determine which forms could persist. The same mechanical setting could change the costs of flight and dispersal, allowing some animals to connect habitats across a greater vertical range. These possibilities concern relationships as much as size: vegetation could supply elevated feeding and breeding sites, while flying organisms transferred pollen, propagules, and nutrients between otherwise separated communities.[^biosphere-mechanics]

Productive communities could also support habitats beyond the reach of sufficient light. Organic material entering deep water or connected subsurface spaces might sustain consumers there, provided the source populations could maintain that transfer. Across drier terrain, a different assemblage could retain resources within long-lived tissues and repeatedly pass through low-activity periods. Those contrasting strategies suggest a biosphere whose diversity comes partly from different ways of occupying the same planetary cycle. Tall forests, open vegetation, aquatic communities, and sheltered interiors would each require a functional account of production, exchange, and renewal before their connections were expanded.

Expansion would change the conditions under which those accounts had first succeeded. Connecting habitats permits migration and material exchange, while also opening routes for competitors, disease, and disturbance. Early ecological construction could retain monitored regions and recovery reserves as larger networks developed around them. Comparisons between connected and more isolated communities would help determine where exchange improved persistence and where separation remained valuable. Over time, organisms themselves could extend suitable habitat through soil formation and reproduction, reducing some establishment work while creating new demands for observation and management.

Long-term persistence would require attention to both biological change and the movement of essential elements into less accessible reservoirs. Nutrients buried in sediment might require recovery; altered communities might need different replenishment or disturbance regimes. Within those maintained conditions, succession and evolution could produce arrangements beyond the original planting designs. The enduring contribution of the project would be the establishment of another setting in which life can continue generating habitats for life. Its mature forms would belong partly to the descendants of the organisms introduced there, through the ways they grow, reproduce, and change the world around them.

[^biosphere-gravity]: Hayashi et al. (2023), *Lunar gravity prevents skeletal muscle atrophy but not myofiber type shift in mice*, Communications Biology 6, 424. DOI: 10.1038/s42003-023-04769-3. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37085700/. Primary abstract rechecked; the claim concerns the studied adult mice and muscle outcomes. [^biosphere-regolith]: Paul, Elardo and Ferl (2022), *Plants grown in Apollo lunar regolith present stress-associated transcriptomes that inform prospects for lunar exploration*, Communications Biology 5, 382. https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-022-03334-8. Abstract, Results and discussion, and relevant growth-method passages were inspected in this revision. Plants were grown on Earth with supplied nutrients and irrigation. The text proposes substrate-development work without inferring a self-sustaining soil or field ecosystem.

[^biosphere-mechanics]: Conditional mechanical reasoning: gravitational loading is proportional to g at fixed mass, the gravitational hydraulic contribution is proportional to rho*g*h, and steady aerodynamic lift depends on density, speed, wing area and lift coefficient. These relations motivate tests; they establish no tree height, aerial food web, reproductive outcome, or automatic biological advantage. The adopted outline supplies the biome and dispersal concepts.
