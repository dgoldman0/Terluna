# The petal gown: a design brief for the first full costume

Status: a proposal of 2 November 2026, with its open choices at the end. Nothing in it is built or accepted. The numbers are closed-form estimates from the garment study's own screen, applied to the petals and sleeves by /media/projectspace/terluna-research/humanities/dance-runs/costume_design/petal_screen.py (output beside it); they are screens, which the solver trials listed at the end will test.

## What the phrase asks of a costume

*Under the Canopy, with the hanging salto* gives the costume a role in every unit (units.json, "garment"). The sleeves carry the first half: they follow the port de bras with a lag of a second or more, open into the canopy overhead and settle over the dancer for 6-8 s, stay above her as she tips into the long fall, and stream behind her in the orbit run. The skirt carries the second: it opens with the jeté's split and blooms on the descent, swings wide round the carve, makes the whirl's figure with its flare, and floats open round the inverted body in the salto.

The first dressed renders showed what a costume for this phrase must avoid. A circle skirt falls over an inverted torso and hides it while the salto hangs; a full skirt is trapped between the legs in a deep landing lunge; chiffon on a 2 cm mesh renders as crumpled tissue; the sleeves render too thick; and on the dress form a skirt dropped from a flat start dresses bunched at the hips (README, "The cloth against the rendered body"). The costume is to be elegant, and the finer meshes that elegance needs are accepted as its cost.

## The design

The gown is one long line standing still and separates into petals when it moves.

- **Bodice.** Fitted, in heavy silk satin, with a softly wrapped neckline. It moves with the body and is rendered on it, as the leotard is now, so it costs no simulation.
- **Petals.** Eight gores cut on the bias from heavy silk satin (charmeuse weight, 90 g/m2), hanging from the high hip, 0.93 m above the floor in standing, to 0.13 m above it: 0.80 m long, 16.5 cm wide at the hip (half again its 11 cm share of the 0.88 m hip, so neighbours overlap by about 2.8 cm) and 40 cm wide at the hem. Below the hip they hang free of one another. The bias lets the satin follow the hip and fall in fluid folds, and it lengthens under load, which tethers to the hip line keep within 5%. A light weighted hem tape (10-50 g/m, chosen by trial) sets how far the petals rise in the whirl. An optional second ring of eight petals in silk georgette, set between the satin ones, would move on a slower clock than the satin and leave a sheer layer lingering as the satin settles.
- **Sleeves.** Sheer sleeves fitted to the wrist, rendered on the arm, ending in the existing georgette water sleeves (1.5 m long, 0.50 m round, tethered to their cuffs), which make the canopy and stream in the run.
- **Feet.** Soft slippers; the hem leaves the feet and ankles in view, so the foot work in the motion plan shows.
- **Colour (to choose).** Deep indigo shading to pale at the petals' hems, with silver-grey georgette; or ivory and champagne. Deep indigo would stand out against the warm cyclorama; ivory would catch the stage light.

The language's cultures (questions.md, question 8) enter only as vague influences, each taken on its merits: the wrapped bodice recalls the wrapped cloth of Austronesian dress, and the indigo the indigo-dyed cloth of Hakka dress. Beyond these notes the gown is its own design.

Two other elegant directions were weighed. A column gown with a wing cape has the most classical line, but its canopy repeats the sleeves' figure and the gown falls over the torso in the salto. A layered formal gown is the richest look, but its long full skirt brings back both of the first renders' failures.

## What the screen predicts

For a petal 0.80 m long hanging from a hip radius of 0.14 m, against the phrase's run (1.6 m/s) and whirl (0.45 turns a second). The whirl's range runs from fold drag (low) to skin friction alone (high).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| silk satin, heavy (90 g/m2) | 2.7 | 1.37 / 0.53 | 15° / 38° | 2.2 / 0.85 | 42-74° / 77° | 5-13° |

| silk georgette (34 g/m2) | 2.2 | 2.03 / 0.78 | 9° / 24° | 3.4 / 1.3 | 43-73° / 77° | 0-13° |

Each figure the phrase gives the skirt comes out larger and slower on the Moon. In the run the satin petals stream nearly level behind the dancer, 15° below the horizontal where on Earth they would hang at 38°. In the jeté the dancer descends from a 1.2 m rise in 1.2 s and lands at about 2 m/s, while a lifted satin petal falls at no more than 0.42 m/s, so the petals are left above her and bloom on the way down, as the unit asks. Let go, a satin petal takes 2.2 s to swing back to vertical against 0.85 s on Earth.

In the whirl, the screen holds the study's satin half circle at the 30° cone its own hoop allows, and a full circle, free to rise to 75°, gathers its spare width into folds whose drag outweighs the outward pull 6.6 times (the screen's wind-up index), enough to wind it round the body. Petals have no spare width to fold: their 3.2 m of hem is less than the 5.7 m round a hem flared to 0.9 m. They should therefore sit near the skin-friction figure, 74° from vertical without a hem weight and 77°, nearly level, with 50 g/m; the fold-drag figure (42°) bounds them from below. On Earth the same turn lifts them 3-13°. The whirl's rate is the choreographer's control of that flare, since the outward pull grows as its square.

In the salto, the tucks turn the body at about 250° a second, and at a petal's hem 0.5-1.0 m from the axis the turn's outward pull is 6-12 times lunar gravity, so the petals should fling outward and open round the body in the tucks; in the hang at 89° a second the pull is 0.7-1.5 times gravity, and the petals should float. Separated petals leave gaps through which the inverted torso shows. These last expectations rest on the ratio of pulls alone; the solver decides them.

The bending lengths set the mesh. On the Moon the satin's is 2.7 cm and the georgette's 2.2 cm, so folds are broad and a 1.5 cm mesh resolves the satin's, with 1.2 cm for the georgette. Organza (5.5 cm) would hold crisp shapes and is kept out of the petals for that reason.

## What it needs from the solver and renderer

- An open gore (patterns.gore, added 2 November): each petal is a ring sector of 0.294 rad between radii 0.561 and 1.361 m, with its warp at 45° to its centre line for the bias.
- Shear that jams. In the first design round (2 November) bias-cut satin petals overstretched in the whirl, 230% at the 99th percentile, because along a bias-cut petal only the weave's shear holds its length and the solver's shear keeps yielding where a woven fabric jams at large shear angles; on the straight grain the warp carries the length. Bias petals wait for shear that locks, and the round's petals are cut on the straight grain.
- The petals pinned along the dress form's hip outline (pelvis and thighs), alternate petals 4 mm apart so the overlaps start clear of one another, simulated together as one merged piece so that self-contact keeps them apart, and tethered to the hip line at 1.05 times their pattern distance.
- Dressing from a hanging start: each petal begins hanging straight from its pin line rather than dropped from a flat disc. The dress form's bunching must still be understood first, since its cause is open.
- Rendering: one level of subdivision smoothing on the cloth (cheap at 480p), satin and georgette looks in render.FABRIC_LOOK, sheer sleeves that read as silk, and the bodice and fitted sleeves as materials on the body.

## Trials and acceptance

Short clips first, each a few minutes on the GPU: the gown hanging in standing (hem height, overlaps, bias lengthening, bias against straight grain); the whirl (flare against the screen, at 0, 20 and 50 g/m of hem weight); the jeté (petals parting round the split, no petal trapped); the salto (petals opening in the tucks, the torso in view in the hang); the run and carve (trailing). Acceptance: 99th-percentile strain under 10% (12% if the bias's own lengthening needs it), under 0.5% of cloth vertices inside the rendered skin in any frame, the hem within 3 cm of its design height at the motion's start, no petal caught between the legs, and 1080p stills of each unit's moment judged by eye.

## Compute

About 28,000 cloth vertices: 9,300 in the satin petals at 1.5 cm, 14,500 in georgette petals at 1.2 cm if they are used, and 4,400 in the sleeves (about 14,000 without the georgette petals). The skirts of the first renders had 5,100-8,500 and simulated about 0.2 s of motion per second of compute, so the gown should take 10-15 minutes of GPU time for the 36 s phrase. The trials take about 30-45 minutes, a few full runs 30-40 minutes, and a 480p render 35-45 minutes: about 1.5-2 hours of GPU time in all, under 2 GB of system memory, alongside CPU work.

## Open choices

1. The petals' fabric: heavy silk satin (lustrous, swings), crepe de chine (matte, lighter), or viscose jersey (a heavy fluid knit, modern).
2. Whether to interleave sheer georgette petals.
3. How high the petals should rise in the whirl: nearly level (weighted hems), lower (lighter hems or a slower whirl), or by trial.
4. Colour: indigo shading to pale with silver-grey georgette, ivory and champagne, or another palette.

## Design rounds (2-3 November)

The first round dressed three gowns, each with layered water sleeves (a georgette tube inside a longer chiffon one), in one cloth each on a stand-in body (body.json's trunk and round waistband with the dress form's fitted limbs, since the full form still dresses skirts short), and rendered each standing and mid-whirl: a sheer chiffon full circle over a weighted satin half circle (layered), three sheer layers graded in length (which reads as tiers), and the petal gown on the straight grain. The graded layers were preferred, with two criticisms: nothing made the pieces one outfit, and the sleeves were plain tubes held at the wrists.

The second round makes the tiered gown one outfit. The bodice matches the tiers in matte silk with a sheer yoke over the shoulders, a satin sash with two tails marks the waist and hides the skirts' bands, and the sleeves dress the arm and echo the skirt: full sheer sleeves from the armhole to the wrist, gathered at both ends, then two flounces at the wrist graded in length like the skirt's tiers, then the long chiffon extension the canopy needs. The palette is provisional (champagne, ivory and gold).

A variant with stiff organza caps after the terno's butterfly sleeves was built and held back. Pinned along the top of the shoulder, the caps were swept by the arms in the port de bras and stretched over 200%; attached to the upper arm, as a sleeve cap is, a cap that stands above the shoulder swings into the head when the arm is raised to the side, which the dressing pose, the port de bras to fifth and the canopy's cast all do. A standing cap of that kind restricts a raised arm, so for this phrase a terno-like element needs another form: a lower crest at the neck clear of the arms, soft gathered caps that fold as the arms rise, or butterfly sleeves kept for a dance with the arms low.

The third round fixed the waist and gave the sleeves their ends. Round 2's skirts and sash hung from a round band about the old capsule axis, 5-6 cm behind the rendered waist, with the layers' tops held 1.8 cm apart by the solver's contact distance: the sash stood 1.2 cm off the belly, 5 cm off the sides and 13 cm off the small of the back, and from the side the waist read as a shelf with the skirts hung from its edge. Round 3 dresses on the measured dress form (with slim shoulders), whose bunching came from the skirts' starting layout and is cured (dance README, "The cloth against the rendered body"): the skirts' bands lie on the waist outline 4, 7 and 10 mm off the skin, their tops lie together in one contact group, and the sash is fitted 16 mm off the skin.

The round-2 wrist flounces, cut from three-quarter circles whose hems were two to three times as long as their rings, read as ruffles, and the plain extension ended in a blunt open tube. In their place three sheer tiers hang at each wrist, graded in length as the skirt is (1.40, 0.75 and 0.35 m; the longest, the canopy's, in champagne georgette inside, the others in ivory chiffon), each widening by a fifth toward its hem and each hem bound in 1 cm of satin, gold in the stills to echo the sash. Of the two variants, hems cut on a slant (12% longer at the back of the arm) fall to soft points that trail in the canopy and the whirl; level hems end square and read as the ends of tubes. Tiers lying one inside another must differ in circumference by more than about 7 cm at a 1.5 cm mesh, or the solver stretches the outer ones round the inner. The solver also resists compression as stiffly as stretch, so cloth gathered into a seam cannot fold as gathers do: the first centimetre below each tier's seam stays stretched (to 43-46% at the 99th percentile in the outer tier), as the bishop sleeves' gathered ends are (9-24%). Neither shows at the stills' framing; a fabric model soft in compression would cure both and needs the garment study's checks rerun. The longest tier pools on the floor while the arms are down, and the bodice is unchanged. The stills are in dance-runs/costume_design/stills3 (sheet_round3.png), rendered on the NVIDIA GPU at 256 samples, which leaves the sheer layers smooth.

The fourth round (3 November) keeps round 3's outfit with level sleeve hems and binds the skirt's three tiers in 1 cm of the gold satin that binds the sleeve tiers, so every tier of the gown ends in the same edge. The solver now meets compression along warp and weft with 1% of its tensile stiffness (README.md, "Fabric"), and the cloth below the gathered seams hangs at its cut lengths: the three sleeve tiers' 99th-percentile stretch at the median frame falls from 8-46% in round 3 to 5-6% through the opening of the canopy phrase, and from 12-46% to 6-7% in the whirl (7-9% in the worst frames), with the bindings within 2%. The pieces the change leaves alone stretch as before: the georgette half circle by 11-13% at the median frame of the whirl in both rounds (17% in its worst frame, with its binding's weight added), the bishop sleeves by 22-25% in one frame of the canopy phrase, and the sash's tails for a single frame at the whirl's start; their causes are still to be found. In the stills the skirt's tiers hang level with their gold edges, and in the whirl they open as three separate layers where round 3's flared as one mass of chiffon. The longest sleeve tier still pools on the floor while the arms are down. The stills are in dance-runs/costume_design/stills4 (sheet_round4.png, round 3 above round 4).

The bodice became a garment in the same round. Coloured on the leotard zone, it followed every contour of the skin and read as moulded on the body; the renderer now builds it as a shell over the torso that spans the body's hollows, skinned as the body is, with a neckline piped in gold (visualization/dance/README.md). Four options were rendered close, from the hips to the head (dance-runs/costume_design/bodice1, sheet_bodice1.png): structured with princess seams and a sweetheart neckline, ruched with a sweetheart neckline, pleated with a V neckline, and structured with a straight neckline. Any style takes any of the three necklines. The choice is open.

The fifth round (3 November) designs the waist and asks whether the dancer can still jump in the gown. The flat sash read as a ring round the waist: a band 5 cm wide, level and even all round, pinned 16 mm off the body, its tails at the back where no front view sees them. In its place a draped sash of soft satin (69 g/m2) is knotted at the left hip (README.md, the outfit's pieces): a band gathered across its width into four folds of unequal depth, covering 6 cm at the back and widening toward the knot, held along both edges 2 cm off the body on a slant that falls to the hip; a knot of wrapped satin, held, over the band's two ends; and two tails of 0.55 and 0.75 m hanging from the knot over the skirt, cut on a slant at their ends. A sash knotted below the waist must go on after the skirts hang: they start as cones and the full circle as a flat disc at the waist, which falls over anything hanging below it. So the round dresses the skirts and sleeves alone for 4 s of the raised hold, presses their cloth under the band's reach in under it, and ties the sash on with its tails tilted out over the dressed skirts before dressing on with everything. In the stills the sash reads as intended, a gold band dipping to a knot at the hip with its tails falling over the tiers nearly to the knee. Its tails stay within 3-9% at the 99th percentile in every motion, but the band, held along both edges, carries the cloth pushed against it: in the canopy phrase 2-3% of its triangles hold 8-12% from the shear its taper builds into the held edges, and in the whirl the skirts flare up into its lower folds at the back and the torso twists against the hips (21 degrees at 1.5 s, which moves the band's two edges apart since the lower one follows the pelvis more), so its 99th percentile reaches 16% at the median frame and 36% at worst. Holding both edges in one blend and tacking the lower edge at intervals instead of all round would let it ride over the skirts. Close to, its folds look lumpy rather than running into the knot. The rest of the gown stretches as in round 4.

The jump checks dressed the gown on three jumps: the hanging salto, the sleeve flight with its lunge landing, and the leap from the canopy phrase (round5/jump_check.py). The motions were solved without the gown, which therefore cannot stop a jump; what the checks measure is what it would cost her and whether its cloth lets her move. It weighs 0.39 kg of simulated cloth, 0.7% of her 55 kg. From the cloth's momentum, its weight and the solver's air law, its pull on her peaks at take-off at 15-21% of her lunar weight and over each rise would take 3-4% off it if she jumped the same: 7 cm of the salto's 1.66 m, 3 cm of the sleeve flight's 1.05 m, 4 cm of the leap's 1.25 m. The cloth does not let her jump cleanly. The longest skirt tier, the georgette half circle 0.90 m long and the narrowest cut, is caught between her legs in the leap's run-up, where her strides open to 0.8 m with the hips low, and stays stretched through the leap (70% at the 99th percentile at the median frame); the middle tier is caught there too and frees itself in the air. In the salto's crouch the same tier's hem reaches the floor and her feet stand on its edge as she takes off. Her arms close round her head in that crouch and the full sleeves pass through it (114 of their vertices inside the head at 0.5 s) and stay tangled through the jump and after it (113% at the end), as they do in the sleeve flight; cloth pinched between parts of the body does not slide free in the solver, so part of this is the solver's, but full sleeves carry much cloth to the head in a tuck. In the salto's hang the skirt falls over her in half the inverted frames, as circle skirts did on 2 November, and the sleeve flight's lunge landing traps the skirts and the sash as it trapped the full skirt then. The canopy phrase and the whirl pass: no cloth under a foot, at most 0.35% of the cloth near her inside the body.

The bishop sleeves' brief overstretch in the canopy phrase, in every round since the second, lies within 5 cm of the armhole ring on both arms, as the motion starts (0.1-0.3 s) and as the arms rise overhead (2.4-2.8 s, 27% at the 99th percentile at 2.75 s): where the sleeve's top meets the raised arm, not in its gathers. It is not yet fixed.

The round's stills are rendered under a softer lighting rig (visualization/dance/README.md, `--light soft`), set beside the stage rig on round 4's views in stills5/lighting_test.png: it lights her face where the stage rig left it dark at 2.6 s and takes the amber and blue casts off her skin, while her legs in the whirl still run pale under the shin-level booms. The stills are in dance-runs/costume_design/stills5: sheet_round5.png (the four views, then each jump at take-off, top and landing), close_round5.png (the sash with the three bodice styles) and lighting_test.png.

The sixth round (3 November) tries three cuts for the longest skirt tier and a second hold for the sash (round6/run_gowns6.py). The tier is the champagne georgette, innermost under the 0.70 and 0.50 m ivory tiers, which the leap catches on her shins. It was cut 0.83 m long as a half circle, as the same half circle slit up each side to the knee, and as a full circle. Each slit runs from the hem to 0.54 m down the pattern, her knee's height below the waist, with its lips bound in gold like the hem. It follows the line of the pattern that hangs at her side once dressed, which leaves the waistband 70 degrees off centre front: on the fifth round's dressed skirt the line from the side of the waist drifts 20-25 degrees back over the hip. Laid on with the other tiers, a full circle would start as a flat disc at the waist outside the three-quarter circle's cone, so it dresses alone for 3 s first; dressed, 3% of it bulges more than 2 cm past the middle tier, against 1% for the half circle. None of the three frees the leap. Through the run-up 15% of the fifth round's tier is stretched more than 30% past its cut, and 11% through the rise; shortened, 22% and 19%; slit, 15% and 9%; as a full circle, 13% and 12%. In every one the lower half of the tier, 0.45-0.8 m down, is caught round her shins as her legs pass in the stride. The slits open 23-27 cm at the median frame and up to 42 cm, and the slit tier frees itself sooner in the air (3% of it stretched at the top of the leap, against 8%), but between the slits the front and back panels each wrap a shin. These differences lie within the runs' own scatter: the middle tier, the same in all four runs, carries 12-48% at the 99th percentile at the median frame from one run to the next. Cloth caught between the passing shins does not slide free in the solver, any more than the full sleeves pinched in the salto's tuck do, while real georgette would slide off a shin long before it stretched by 30%; the leap measures the solver's limit as much as the cut. The cut's own limit is the hem's reach: across a split leap the hem must span about 1.8 m from the front foot to the back, which a half circle's 3.4 m hem barely wraps and a full circle's 5.9 m wraps easily.

In the hanging salto every cut keeps the tier within 1% of its triangles stretched past 30%. The shortened and slit tiers lie under a standing foot at take-off in 5-6 frames (11 in the fifth round) and within 12 cm of her head in 44 and 49 of the 119 inverted frames (57); the full circle, 0.11 kg heavier, in 69, and its pull at take-off peaks at 28% of her lunar weight (21%). Over the rises every variant costs her 4-5 cm of the leap's 1.25 m and 6-7 cm of the salto's 1.66 m. In the hang the skirts fall over her torso and head in every variant, so that she hangs as a column of cloth with her legs above it (stills6/sheet_salto6.png). The full sleeves tear in the salto's tuck in every run (113-135% at the 99th percentile at the median frame): at 0.5-0.6 s her arms close round her head, and the sleeves' cloth, pinched between the upper arms and pressed against the head, passes into the dress form's head (190-200 vertices more than 3 mm inside it at 0.5 s) and comes out stretched, the stretch staying from 0.8 s at the armpits and wrists. The water sleeves of 2 November went through the same salto at 6%, so it is the full sleeve's cloth round the upper arm that the tuck traps. In the stills the slit is hard to find: under the two sheer tiers it reads as one more parting among the folds, and only in the strides does a leg pass through it (stills6/side6.png, sheet_leap6.png).

The tacked sash, its band held along the top edge and tacked every 8 cm along the bottom, every held part moving with one share of the pelvis and abdomen, stretches as the fifth round's did on the canopy phrase (10.7% at the 99th percentile at the median frame, against 10.6%). In both the stretch lies along the band's edges, mostly the bottom one, where its cells are sheared by its taper against a pattern cut to its longest rows and its lower edge rides over the skirts' gathered tops; the hold does not cause it. In 1080p closes with her arms raised (stills6/sash2_r5 and sash2_r6r5tack), the two look alike: the band crumples into small lumps with patchy highlights where satin would fall in a few long folds, the knot reads as a lump of clay, the tails hang flat and stiff, the band's top edge stands off the waist, and the gold reads as tan. Seen whole at 720p it reads as a knotted sash. The bodice is now the ruched shell with the sweetheart neckline, and the renders' default lighting the soft rig with its shin-level booms at half: on the fifth round's whirl the shins, which the soft rig had left pale, now take the tan of the thighs (stills6/lighting_check.png). The round's sheets are in dance-runs/costume_design/stills6: sheet_leap6.png and sheet_salto6.png (each cut at the jumps' moments), side6.png, sash6.png (the closes from the knot's side, where her arm and sleeve hide the knot), lighting_check.png, and the logs with each run's stretch reports, jump checks and tier comparisons in round6/logs.

Cloth squeezed between parts of the body tore the full sleeves in the salto's tuck and held the longest tier on her shins in the leap. Measured in the runs (round6/pinch_diag.py), the leap's catch begins at 1.0 s as her shins pass each other: 72% of the caught cloth lies within 1.2 cm of both and 45% more than 3 mm inside a leg, and afterwards it hangs round one shin from the far side. The sleeves are caught between her forearms, then between the forearms and the head, at 0.45-0.6 s. The body contact was changed in two ways (README.md, Contact): friction comes from a contact's press alone, where before it also came from the solver undoing its own overlap, and cloth within reach of two body parts whose surfaces face each other is held where it reaches equally into both, riding on their mean motion, with its layers let lie together. Rerun with round 5's gown, the leap's longest tier is caught half as much in the run-up (7% of it stretched more than 30%, against 15%) and hardly at all in the rise (1%, against 11%), its 99th percentile at the median frame falling from 70% to 30%. The rest lies in the motions, which keep no part of the body out of another (../README.md, the body through itself): the catches fall where the body passes through itself, the shins 82 mm into each other at 1.0 s in the leap, and in the salto the arms through each other (78 mm) and an upper arm into the head (99 mm) at 0.45-0.75 s. Cloth between such parts is inside the body whatever its contact does.

With the body kept out of itself in the solve (../README.md, the body kept out of itself) the catches go. On the leap re-solved with the barrier, round 5's longest tier stretches more than 30% over 0.1% of its triangles at the run-up's median frame and none in the rise (from 15% and 11%), its 99th percentile at the median frame 7.9% (from 70%). On the re-solved salto (4 November) the full sleeves no longer tear: their 99th percentile at the median frame is 13% (from 108%), and a patch of at most 3.5% of them stretches more than 30% from 0.7 to 3.2 s, worst at 1.1 s as she leaves the floor with an upper arm touching her head, then settles after the landing. The salto's jump check is otherwise as before: the gown costs 6.9 cm of her 1.68 m rise (4.1%), pulls on her at most 16.7 N (18.7% of her lunar weight; 18.7 N before), 5.2 N of it at the wrists (8.6 N before), presses no more than 0.7% of the cloth near her into her (1.6% before), lies under a standing foot in 17 frames (at most 12 vertices), and reaches her face in 71 of the 98 frames she is upside down. The sash's worst frame on that motion stretches its 99th percentile to 258% (32% on the old motion). A 480p video of the salto (videos/hanging_salto_apart_r6r5.mp4) shows the rise well, the arms arched overhead with the sleeve tiers sweeping, and the hang poorly: from 2.0 to 3.5 s the skirts fall over her body and head and the gown reads as a sack with her legs out of its top, the full sleeves bunched at her wrists.

## Design round 7 (6 November)

The longest tier's four cuts, rerun on the re-solved leap and salto, now differ little. In the leap all four stay free in the run-up and the rise (at most 0.3% of the tier stretched more than 30% at the median frame), and at take-off the 0.83 m half circle and the slit catch briefly (10.7% and 22.1% in the worst frame, at 3.0 s) where the 0.90 m half circle and the full circle do not (0.9% and 1.3%); the full circle alone puts cloth under her landing foot (18 frames, up to 76 vertices). In the salto the shorter cuts save her 0.6-0.7 cm of rise (6.2-6.3 cm lost against 6.9 cm) and reach her face in 63-65 of the 98 upside-down frames against 71, while the full circle loses 7.2 cm, pulls hardest (21.3 N) and reaches her face in 77.
