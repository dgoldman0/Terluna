# The dance programme

Work in order; each stage ends in something the author can watch or read, with its checks.

1. **Foundation (1 November).** Intake of the two bundles; the body model; the physics envelope; the vocabulary and
   grammar with three example phrases; the motion engine (whole-body optimal control in Crocoddyl, replayed in
   MuJoCo), the garment solver (cloth with per-triangle aerodynamics, an Earth control and analytic validation) and the
   renderer (the CC0 female base mesh skinned to the body, a dance stage, MP4). The run, leap and carve at the centre
   of *Under the Canopy* were realised, dressed and rendered on 1 November. The stage closes with the whole phrase:
   its grammar junctions and its engine (pivots with the floor's turning friction, kneeling, footholds planned for the
   whole phrase, solving in windows) were written on 1 November, and on 2 November the whole phrase was solved and checked
   (friction, contact force, joint ranges, torques within strength, every body part above the floor; on 3 November a scan found the legs passing through each other for 7.2 s of it, up to 14 cm deep, which no check had looked for; a barrier in the solve now keeps the body out of itself, and re-solved with it on 6 November the legs touch for 0.03 s at most, 3 cm deep); its whirl was
   rendered in two skirts, and a longer phrase built on it, ending on the hanging salto, was rendered dressed, and on 6 November it was danced in the tiered gown at 480p (what its video showed is under *Where the work resumes*, below). Its replay waits for the
   tracking controller (stage 2).
2. **The body's fidelity.** First, one body for physics and picture. The rendered mesh's hips and knees sit 3 cm
   lower, its shank 3 cm shorter, hands 2.4 cm longer and spine joints 2-5 cm further forward than the physics body's
   (the renderer's landmark check). Either the physics body's joint centres and segment inertias are fitted to the
   mesh, with de Leva's table as the check (recommended), or the mesh is reshaped to the physics body; the choice is
   open in [questions.md](questions.md). Then the open literature values (hip and spine ranges, floor friction, silk
   shear) and De Leva's table read on the printed page. Then strength that falls with joint speed, a compliant sole and heel pad, the garments'
   loads fed back into the body (two-way coupling) and swing-cone limits at the hips and shoulders. The MuJoCo replay
   needs a tracking controller or model-predictive control: the optimiser's own feedback gains hold a plan for about
   0.4 s against MuJoCo's soft contacts. The feet move as realistically as the work allows wherever the dress leaves
   them in view (their movement, not the toes' detail): pointed in flight, rolling through the foot in take-offs and
   landings, turning on demi-pointe, with the stored motions' 4 cm floor sink and ankle overruns corrected.
3. **Garments and the picture.** Light and weighted fabrics both, since different dances suit different combinations: light silk for sleeves and canopies, heavier or hem-weighted skirts for turning, studied as a matrix of fabric against unit (whirl, leap, run, canopy). The first study of fabrics and cuts (1 November; [README](README.md), "Fabrics and cuts on the Moon") built a library of twelve fabrics, a closed-form screen of each on both worlds, half-circle cuts and weighted hems in the solver, and trial runs of Cusick's drape test, skirts in steady turns and panels trailing in travel. Next for the garments: the air's added mass and its push at shallow incidence in the solver, then air that turns with a turning skirt (each changes how light silk moves, which the study could only estimate); shear that locks as a woven fabric jams, so that a heavy hem needs no tethers; measured bending for the silks whose stiffness is a modelling choice (question 5); more cuts (a double circle, capes, trains, tiers, panels at the wrists) and layered garments (a light overlay on a weighted underskirt); then attire designed for particular dances, fabric against unit. Since 2 November dressed runs collide with a dress form measured from the rendered dancer, which keeps the cloth outside the skin it is rendered on (the skin check finds 0.14% of the whirl's chiffon inside it, against 3.1% on the capsules) until stage 2's one body replaces it; the bunching a skirt first showed on the form came from its starting layout and is cured (README, "The cloth against the rendered body"). The first costume ([garment/costume.md](garment/costume.md)) is a tiered gown with tiered sleeves, refined from comparison stills in design rounds. Since 3 November the fabric model is soft in compression, so gathered cloth folds; the skirt's tiers have gold-bound hems like the sleeves', the waist a draped sash knotted at the hip, and the bodice is a ruched shell with a sweetheart neckline. On 6 November the gown was danced in the whole phrase with its aerial work (*Under the Canopy* with the hanging salto, 36.7 s), and every part of it held (design round 7 in the costume record). The longest tier stays the 0.90 m half circle; with the body kept out of itself in the solve, the tier no longer catches on her shins in the leap's run-up and the full sleeves no longer tear in the salto's tuck. The salto's hang still closes the skirts over her torso and head. The gown's next version takes a billowy chiffon sash with gold-bound panels and a front open through the two longer tiers, both to be judged against the phrase's video. The first dressed runs (2 November) add three needs: a cut for inversions, since a circle skirt falls over an inverted torso; cloth caught between parts of the body in a kneel or a deep lunge, which still overstretches (holding it with the body tore a hem, so it was removed); and sleeves that render as sheer silk rather than as heavy cloth. The renderer's work goes to the meshes' physics (cloth folds at chiffon's scale, its added mass and the air it carries, the body mesh bending where the physics body bends) and to seeing the dance clearly, before fine detail such as face, hair and skin that does not change how the dance reads.
4. **The vocabulary's depth.** The aerial figures the long flights open were added on 1 November (corkscrew, star wheel, flat spin, hanging salto, sleeve flight, with the *Air Study* phrase), and on 2 November the engine gained planned attitudes and rates of turn in flight: the hanging salto and the sleeve flight solve and were rendered for the author to judge. Next for the others: the star wheel's take-off gives two thirds of the momentum its cartwheel needs, and a longer take-off does not change that, so the shape should gather early in the flight and open into the star at the top, which lowers the momentum the turn needs; the corkscrew needs an asymmetric arm shape (one arm lowered) for its twist, then a staged solve; the flat spin needs a staged solve (the turn first, then the flat lay); the *Air Study* needs a step before its flat spin, which now rebounds out of the sleeve flight's landing. The figures that need height (a jump to a ledge, a drop from it, a leap over a standing partner) wait on a future study of levels. The figures are judged for beauty from their renders. Then more units per family, floor work, and a notation export (Laban-style symbols beside the count score); the generator's transition weights learned from composed phrases rather than set by hand. Partnering with a two-body model (lifts, tosses, catches, counterbalances) is a future study, once the solo work is much further along.
5. **A dance.** A first complete piece of two to three minutes, built from motifs and their development, with music
   timed to the lunar pulse, in the garments the author chooses, rendered at final quality.
6. **Beyond the solo.** Partnering, groups and canon in a hall's real dimensions (the habitation note's halls for
   moving), the other movement-art families (contact and momentum, suspended, aerodynamic, martial), and human-factors
   tests with dancers in the analogues that exist.

## Where the work resumes (6 November)

The whole phrase's 480p video (videos/phrase_r7r5.mp4 in dance-runs/costume_design on the drive) sets the next work. Two checks written for it read the motion file: round7/slip_check.py follows each foot point's height and speed against the solver's contact flags, and round7/zmp_check.py finds where the motion needs the floor to push, from the body's centroidal momentum averaged over two nodes, against the points held on the floor (their output is in round7/logs, the review frames in stills7/review_phrase).

**The finish.** The hanging salto lands at 35.4 s with her weight drifting back over her heels. The window that solved the landing reached only 0.6 s past it, and the last window, the finish's one second from 35.7 s, stops the drift with a lurch the floor cannot give: as her chest pitches to 37°, the floor carries 3 N of her 89 N at 36.0 s, and a tenth of a second later the motion needs its push from 0.7 m outside her feet. She ends still, her centre of mass 5 cm behind her heels and beginning to topple backward, slowly at lunar gravity. A still landing from this salto is doubtful physics, so the finish changes (decisions register, 6 November): the landing carries on downward into a deep bend and she rises out of it. A longer stroke spreads the landing's braking, so the floor's push can stay smaller and inside her feet, and the rise can bring her weight forward over them; the solve will show whether it is easier on the body. The window that holds the landing should reach through the whole finish, about 2-2.5 s, and end with her centre of mass over her feet.

**Landings (7-8 November).** The salto's finish lands on the balls of the feet and carries on into a deep bend and a rise (README, "Landings with a stroke"). On 8 November the engine gained a cost on landing forces, set against people's landings on Earth, and its landing impulses now carry force derivatives (README, "Landing loads"). The finish as written cannot sink: its heels come down into a bend that flat feet cannot reach within the ankle's range, so the body stops on the balls before they land. Landing on the balls and sinking on them (solved at run time on 8 November), she pushes at most 7.7 lunar body weights against 17, sinks 21 cm into the full bend, rises and ends still over her feet. Next: the author's choice of how she lands ([questions.md](questions.md), question 10); then the whole phrase solved again with the Runge-Kutta flights that are now the engine's default (the author's choice for drafting, questions.md 9) and the landing cost, in windows sized to their memory, the grand jeté's long flight given the ballistic warm start that aerial figures have; then the phrase dressed and rendered again. The stored phrase of 6 November and its video keep the still landing until then.

**Feet on the floor.** No foot that bears weight slides. Every floor force stays inside the friction pyramid, whose μ of 0.5 gives her about 0.81 m/s² of braking or turning while the floor carries just her weight (4.9 m/s² on Earth), and the long fall, the run, the jete, the carve landing and the whirl use all of it. Feet that bear no weight skim the floor, which reads as slipping: in about 18 brief moments, most in the run, a swinging toe runs along the floor within 5 mm of it or a few millimetres into it; at the jete's take-off the right toe drags 49 cm and dips 21 mm in, and in the long fall the right heel slides about 70 cm in two strokes. The floor barrier keeps free feet from going far below the floor and asks nothing more, so the solve should hold a swinging foot clear, perhaps by 15 mm, except near lift-off and touchdown. Landings set the feet into the floor: after the salto both stand about 4 cm down until the end (stage 2's floor sink), most likely because the touchdown falls between two nodes 25 ms apart and the contact holds each foot where the node finds it; a cost on the foot's height at the touchdown node would put it on the floor. The legs' one remaining crossing, 65 mm for two frames at 15.27 s, catches nothing, and the salto's solve works at the floor's friction and the torque limits.

**The face and hands.** The softened face reads well with her head level and lifeless when it tilts back. The eyes are fixed in the head, so in the whirl they point at the sky, the lowered lids cover the irises, and at 480p each eye shows as a pale crescent. The next build gives her a gaze: a level eyeline held toward the audience or her leading hand within the eye's range, the upper lids following the eyes, a little more contrast in the iris and an occasional blink. The hands still splay; a dancer's soft hand needs the base mesh's fingers separated first.

**The gown.** The salto's hang still closes the skirts over her torso and head (cloth within 12 cm of her head in 71 of the 98 upside-down frames when the salto was dressed alone); the open front frees her body's line there and leaves her face covered. The full sleeves keep a patch of 2% stretched past 30% at the salto's take-off, and a section dressed into a first pose with the arms overhead tears them before its motion starts, which the whole phrase avoids. The next version takes the billowy sash with gold-bound panels and the open front, judged against this video. Still open from earlier rounds: the fabric study's rerun (garment_study_2026-11-03b) and the bishop sleeves' armhole.
