# Open questions for the author

Choices the dance work has made provisionally and would like the author's word on, in the order they matter. Answered
questions move to the decisions register (section "Dance and the movement arts, 1 November 2026").

1. **One body for physics and picture.** The physics body takes its joint centres and segment masses from de Leva's
   average proportions scaled to 1.64 m; the rendered dancer is Blender Studio's sculpted mesh, whose own anatomy puts
   the knee 3 cm lower and the spine joints 2-5 cm further forward. The renderer bends the mesh at the physics body's
   joints, so the knee bends a few centimetres above the mesh's knee and the back folds in front of its spine. They
   become one body either way:
   - the physics body is fitted to the mesh: its joint centres move to the mesh's anatomy and its segment masses and
     inertias come from the mesh's own volume at tissue density, checked against de Leva (recommended: the sculpted
     body is one consistent person where the table is an average of many, and the motions then read exactly as solved);
   - or the mesh is reshaped to the physics body's skeleton, keeping de Leva's proportions and moving the flesh.
   Either way the solved motions are solved again on the fitted body.
2. **The first dancer.** The body is a 1.64 m, 55 kg trained woman, sized to the CC0 female base mesh the renderer
   skins. A second body (the male base mesh, or other statures and builds) comes with partnering. Is a female soloist
   the right first dancer?
3. **The floor.** Indoor, still air at the sea-level datum (1.40 kg/m^3), on a flat vinyl dance floor with a friction
   coefficient of 0.5. The whole-phrase work shows how much hangs on that number: a long fall to 35 degrees asks 0.49
   of it, and a carve landing and the whirl's preparation reach it. Lunar halls could carry floors of higher grip or
   banked rings for travel at speed (the habitation note's halls for moving are 6 m high). Should the dance assume
   Earth-like floors, or design its own? Levels are planned as a future study.
4. **Music.** Lunar flights set slow pulses (a 2.7 s flight is three beats at 66 a minute) under quick articulation.
   Should phrases be timed to music, and is there music to work with?
5. **Silk measurements.** The garment library's bending stiffness is measured for chiffon, georgette, a plain wool and
   a plain cotton; for organza, habotai, taffeta, velvet, tulle and jersey it is a modelling choice, and every drape
   result for them rests on it. The tables that would close it were out of reach in two literature passes: the KES-F
   measurements of woven silks in Matsudaira and Kawabata (1988, Journal of the Textile Institute 79(3), three parts)
   and the drape of habutae, crepe, georgette and crepe de chine in Matsudaira and Yang (2003, Textile Research
   Journal 73(3)), both paywalled, and an open-access study of silks for hanbok (Fashion and Textiles, 2024,
   doi:10.1186/s40691-024-00388-6) behind a login redirect. Can you obtain any of them?
6. **Wings and apparatus.** The sky-ship world has personal wings, and the six families include suspended and
   aerodynamic dance. They stay outside the flowing solo dance; should one of them follow it?
7. **Names.** The lunar units carry working names (orbit run, skim, flight phrase, slow salto, unfolding turn, carve,
   canopy, long fall, long penché). Rename any?

Answered on 1 November: the garments (light and weighted fabrics both, combined to suit each dance), the next step

renderer's priorities (the meshes' physics and the dance's clarity before fine detail), the body's proportions (to be
improved; the method is question 1), the aerial figures (judged from their renders) and the work after the checks (a
study of fabrics and garment shapes, toward designing attire).
