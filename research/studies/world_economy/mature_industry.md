# Mature industry under the 1.2-atmosphere lock

The mature Open Moon has credible industrial advantages in large local construction, aerial infrastructure, selected shape-sensitive fabrication and sustained partial-gravity qualification. Artificial-vacuum production can continue where those advantages pay for the enclosure, pumping and delivery. The strongest near-term evidence supports particular deformation mechanisms; it does not yet establish an export process with lower qualified cost than both equally advanced Earth and array factories.

This study retains the 1.2 atm lock and the 301 K environmental design point. Its purpose is to select durable industrial opportunities for the three-world economy, including markets beyond initial construction. [Inputs](mature_industry_inputs.json) and [results](results/mature_industry.json) separate physical ratios, illustrative cost assumptions and recurring demand. None supplies a new gross-world-product increment. Source retrieval and reading limits are recorded in [the source ledger](mature_industry_sources.json), alongside the earlier [manufacturing evidence](manufacturing_sources.md) and [industrial mechanics sources](industrial_sources.json).

## Priorities at atmospheric or controlled-gas pressure

Lunar gravity provides a broad, continuously available body force over a fixed working surface. It reduces self-weight while retaining settling, drainage and ground-based access. Those properties can help a complete production system even where microgravity improves one isolated step. Conversely, slower drainage, weaker natural convection and reduced traction can hinder it. Cleanrooms, dry cells and inert-gas furnaces remain necessary when chemistry requires them; open air is not a clean processing environment.

The evidence labels below distinguish **P**, a physical scaling; **L**, a short lunar-gravity experiment; **M**, evidence from microgravity; and **C**, a proposed product or business. They do not express commercial readiness.

| Priority | Candidate and durable customers | Mechanism and decisive comparison | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Local buildings, garden and recreation canopies, utility frameworks, aerial platforms and maintenance yards | Lower self-weight, assembly sag and lifting work can reduce supports or permit larger assemblies. Earth uses cranes, jigs and modular construction; orbital construction removes weight further. Wind, pressure, inertia, thermal strain and connections remain | P/C; strongest domestic engineering case |
| 2 | Aerial vehicles, lifting systems and floating infrastructure: manufacture, refit, inspection and repair | Low weight reduces required lift and ideal hover power, supporting a distinctive local market. Exported vehicles must satisfy their destination's atmosphere and gravity; local operating advantages are not an exported material-quality premium | P/C; substantial recurring demand is possible |
| 3 | Complex printed preforms, ceramic cores, production moulds and functional monoliths | Reduced sag may preserve unsupported channels or allow softer formulations. Compare Earth support baths, rheology and fast cure with orbital free fall or rotation, then include debinding, sintering and final qualification | L for brief bridge printing; C for finished products |
| 4 | Large, long-duration partial-gravity qualification facilities | Whole machines, fluids and structures experience the intended body force together. Earth suspension only reproduces selected loads; array centrifuges compete on size, duration and cost | P/C; physical testing service, with domestic customers first |
| 5 | Controlled-gas foams, porous solids and soft biological constructs | Reduced drainage and sag may help particular recipes. Earth stabilizers/support media and orbital microgravity are strong controls; diffusion, cure, cell health and destination strength remain | M/P/C; research options without a demonstrated lunar optimum |

The most direct new lunar evidence is [Kauzya et al.'s 2024 printing experiment](https://matterassembly.org/assets/pdf/preprints/2024_direct_ink_writing.pdf). During approximately 25-second parabolas, an 80 mm clay bridge retained its shape substantially better at three seconds under nominal lunar gravity than on Earth. The 133 mm comparison belongs to Earth versus microgravity. Clay used a 0.27 liquid/solid ratio and a 1.5 mm nozzle. Seven-layer simulant walls showed overlapping Earth, lunar and Martian height losses. Bridge remnants were removed during intervening 1.8 g segments; walls were wiped away. No complete cure, final strength or commercial yield was established. The paper also gives inconsistent residual-microgravity units, approximately 0.05 g and 0.05 m/s². These limits prevent turning the bridge observation into a general printing multiplier.

Finished performance can reverse an attractive intermediate result. In [Cordonier et al.'s 2023 foam study](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.3c09658), the direction of the printed-height effect depended on speed, while Earth generally produced higher volumetric flow. Separate photocatalysis measurements did not isolate a functional benefit from microgravity printing. Earth [support-bath collagen printing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31371612/) is an established competitor for weak biological material. A [2026 bioprinting study](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202520715) further reports broad tolerance of changing gravity in its tested system, with some feature-dependent differences. Biomedical production remains valuable locally, but these results supply no lunar commercial premium.

## Vacuum processes worth retaining

The best mature vacuum candidates combine reduced gravitational deformation with valuable local or export output. Their chemical recipes must specify whether they need vacuum, inert gas or a sealed ampoule. A low-gravity forming step does not make every subsequent coating or heat-treatment step gravity-sensitive.

| Priority and candidate | Lunar mechanism | Competitive limit and future demand |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering study: large hot assemblies | Reduced self-weight sag, hot creep and fixture reactions during selected vacuum brazing, joining or heat treatment | Earth supports and alternate processes compete; orbital fixtures operate near final users. Thin-wall heat exchangers, thermal shields, radiator subassemblies and precision frames serve power, transport, industry and repair. Bond pressure and thermal strain remain |
| Engineering study: large optical/reflector preforms | Lower blank/tool weight; a rotating liquid paraboloid reaches the same curvature at 0.407 of Earth's angular speed | Forming may use gas, with vacuum reserved for coating. Earth already [spin-casts large blanks](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.319295); proposed [orbital fluid shaping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06474) avoids surface launch. Consider transportable segments and preforms for imaging, beam transfer and heat concentration |
| Materials experiment: liquid-phase-sintered refractory and composite parts | Less segregation and slumping, potentially with useful finite-gravity contact or pore motion | Earth skeletons, supports and pressure-assisted recipes compete; array rotation can choose acceleration. Wear components and high-temperature equipment are plausible customers, but an intermediate optimum remains unproved |
| Materials experiment: capillary-supported melts and contactless growth | Lower hydrostatic distortion, levitation force and buoyant flow | Earth already levitates materials; microgravity often improves these effects further. Detector and laser crystals, high-purity stock and reactive alloys require measured quality/yield gains before a lunar export case |
| Local capability: vacuum repair, final assembly and qualification | Proximity to lunar equipment, installed skills and large partial-gravity fixtures | Can remain useful without dominating exports. Generic deposition, membranes and recipes benefiting mainly from ambient vacuum face a strong orbital competitor |

The sintering evidence makes the finite-gravity question substantive. A [NASA-hosted experimental report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19990019810/downloads/19990019810.pdf?attachment=true) examined millimetre-scale tungsten-alloy specimens in evacuated, sealed containers. Low-solid-fraction samples distorted in both environments: gravitational slumping on Earth and spheroidization in microgravity. Pore and composition distributions also differed. Testing intermediate acceleration is justified, but no lunar optimum follows from two endpoints.

The adverse comparison is equally important for contactless processing. [NASA's levitation laboratory](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/g-657270-16-em-materials-levitation.pdf?emrc=e1bc83) already processes millimetre samples on Earth in vacuum or neutral gases. The [TEMPUS facility paper](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0182719) describes how microgravity reduces field-induced heating, stirring and shape distortion. A [Spacelab-J floating-zone report](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjacg/21/4/21_KJ00000857358/_article/-char/en) also attributes improved crystal behaviour partly to surface chemistry suppressing Marangoni flow. Gravity is therefore one control among several, and the array has a strong claim where still less disturbance helps.

Long-term Solar System demand could include replacement thermal systems, power-transfer hardware, propellant-handling equipment, scientific instruments, industrial catalysts and transport infrastructure. Each requires a product specification, installed stock and renewal schedule. The Moon could make robust preforms and complex assemblies, buy ultrapure constituents from the array and perform local repair. Generic thin-film production and purification should not receive a gravity premium merely because their customers use advanced technology. Products whose only lunar advantage was ambient vacuum may relocate or wind down; their machinery and knowledge can still support other lines.

## The physical advantage and the pressure bill

With shared constants, lunar/Earth gravity is 0.165624. At fixed geometry, self-weight and liquid hydrostatic gradients fall by that ratio. A fixed-section beam can span 2.457 times farther at the same self-weight bending stress, or 1.568 times farther at the same absolute self-weight deflection. These ideal limits exclude wind, payloads, pressure, instability and joints. Pressure-vessel membrane stress, machining force and acceleration loads do not fall sixfold.

Capillary length grows by 2.457 at matched fluid properties. Ordinary liquid Rayleigh scaling permits a 1.821-times characteristic length at fixed Rayleigh number and transport properties. These are geometry screens, not production-volume multipliers; wetting, thermal gradients and Marangoni flow remain. In a matched continuum gas, approximate Rayleigh scaling as gravity times pressure squared gives a lunar/Earth ratio of 0.2385 at 1.2 versus 1 atm. Weak natural convection can increase cooling costs. In sufficiently high vacuum, buoyancy may already be too small for lower gravity to matter.

For aerial operations, the [lift equation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/lift-equation-2/) and [ideal momentum theory](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/propeller-thrust/) supply the scaling. Matched temperature/composition and unchanged lift coefficient give a flight-speed ratio of 0.3715 at equal vehicle mass and wing area. Ideal induced hover power is 0.06153 at equal mass and rotor area; profile drag, losses, control and reserves are excluded. Buoyant supported mass per volume rises only with the assumed 1.2 density ratio because gravity cancels. Wind pressure at a given speed also rises by 20%. These are local operating comparisons, not certified vehicle designs or terrestrial-global-average comparisons.

For artificial vacuum, compare 121,590 Pa outside the lunar chamber with 101,325 Pa on Earth, holding volume, temperature and the 0.1 Pa target constant:

| Quantity | Lunar/Earth ratio | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| External differential pressure | 1.2000002 | Approximately 20% more pressure load |
| Gas admitted per complete vent | 1.2 | Equal chamber volume and temperature |
| Reversible evacuation work | 1.2000027 | Thermodynamic minimum, not electrical consumption |
| Constant-speed pumpdown time | 1.0131843 | Logarithmic dependence on starting pressure |
| Ideal spherical buckling thickness | 1.095445 | Selected elastic model; practical design must satisfy other loads |

With no continuing gas load, the 150 m³ example at 1 m³/s effective pumping speed adds 27.35 seconds of ideal pumpdown. Minimum evacuation work rises from 4.222 to 5.066 kWh. Neither figure makes the whole factory 20% dearer. Load locks, vessel design, actual pump curves, contamination and desorption determine which costs change.

Continuous internal gas load requires separate treatment. At pressure-volume throughput Q (Pa m³/s) and target pressure p, the minimum inlet speed is Q/p. The separate 1 Pa m³/s process-load example needs at least 10 m³/s to approach 0.1 Pa, so the 1 m³/s pumpdown example cannot meet that loaded target. Reversible compression to ambient adds Q ln(1.2) when discharge pressure rises from 1 to 1.2 atm; for the 0.1 Pa target this is a 1.32% increase in that ideal power. External leakage can change separately. A 1 atm workshop inside the locked atmosphere merely reallocates pressure boundaries, and vacuum production at 1 atm still requires pumping.

The array can reproduce partial gravity. Acceleration is Ω²R and radial variation is approximately work-depth/R. A 100 m radial work zone with 1% variation needs about 10 km radius at 0.1217 rpm; lunar gravity varies only about 0.0115% over 100 m height. For smaller or differently oriented apparatus, much smaller rotating facilities suffice. Coriolis effects depend on motion; existing rotating infrastructure can share costs. The Moon's broad uniform field is an engineering advantage to price, not a prohibition on orbital competition.

## Delivered cost and continuing demand

The cost comparison must use an accepted product delivered to the same buyer. Include capital service, inputs, energy, fixtures, contamination control, rejected units, qualification and transport. The [normalized calculation](mature_economics.py) deliberately gives Earth an optimized process and the array the same assumed partial-gravity saving as the Moon, with a separate site-service allowance. Its coordinates are illustrative, not estimated factory prices.

For the ambient recipe, 30 of a reference 100 cost units belong to the potentially gravity-sensitive step. Earth saves 15% of that step; Moon and array save an assumed 20%. At a 5-unit orbital site allowance and the stated delivery assumptions, the results are:

| Buyer and recipe | Earth delivered cost | Moon delivered cost | Array delivered cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Moon, ambient/controlled gas | 100.50 | 94.50 | 107.00 |
| Array, ambient/controlled gas | 103.50 | 99.00 | 99.50 |
| Earth, ambient/controlled gas | 96.00 | 102.00 | 104.00 |
| Moon, vacuum | 101.25 | 96.03 | 101.00 |
| Array, vacuum | 104.25 | 100.53 | 93.50 |

The vacuum example assigns 25% to the selected step and 1%, 3% and 3% to vented-gas work, pressure-shell service and pump-cycle occupancy. Applying the physical pressure factors only to those shares adds about 0.526 units. The array avoids those ambient-vacuum charges while retaining internal process and containment costs. A free-fall orbital recipe could be cheaper still.

These chosen coordinates demonstrate why useful domestic production can coexist with weak exports. The ambient delivered-cost advantage at the array is only 0.50 units; reducing lunar accepted yield to 95% reverses it. At 90% yield, even its domestic advantage disappears. Changing actual freight, support cost or array accommodation can change the result. Savings must be measured within a complete process rather than inferred from the gravity ratio.

Recurring local demand can be large without being new output in the world accounts. The inherited aerial-carrier stock is 140 Gt with a 40-year life, implying 3.5 Gt/year of replacement. An illustrative 10% eligible fraction and 10% contract share select 35 Mt/year, just 1% of renewal. At the inherited 3 U/kg replacement cost, customer spending is 105 billion U/year. This is conditional procurement already belonging to replacement and consumption of fixed capital; it is neither a profit estimate nor an additional GWP term.

The array's separate 75 Gt structural stock gives 0.75–1.5 Gt/year under the new 100–50-year life sensitivities. The same 1% combined selection yields 7.5–15 Mt/year and 37.5–75 billion U/year at the inherited 5 U/kg allowance. Awards, capacity and budgets remain explicit assumptions; passive shielding is a different account. Local construction, renewal and amenities can support extensive industry even where export shares stay modest. External debt-service receipts require external customers after imported inputs and financial obligations; avoided imports and lower replacement costs improve the economy through different accounts.

[Early capital campaigns](early_campaigns.md) consider the temporary opportunity to manufacture durable assets before atmospheric closure. Those assets can continue earning service income after a production line converts or retires. Mature industry should be sized around subsequent operation, replacement and repair, with fragile giant assemblies completed near their users and compact qualified components transported between worlds.

The decisive next studies are complete-process comparisons: a large hot assembly with quoted supports and enclosure; a printed functional core carried through cure, inspection and delivery; and a sintered composite tested at zero, lunar and Earth gravity with each process optimized. Measure accepted throughput, useful performance, energy, capital occupancy and delivered cost. These tests would establish which proposed specialties deserve investment while allowing the mature Moon's substantial domestic industrial base to develop on its own merits.
