# Annual accounting choices for three peer worlds

Earth, Moon and array each have territorial production, depreciation, resident income and debt cash flows. The array's shield, dedicated power and magnetic protection remain array assets even when the Moon benefits. Lunar service purchases do not transfer the supplier's depreciation to the Moon. Gross value added already includes the effect of imported intermediate inputs; commercial operating bills cannot be deducted again from gross product.

The common ordinary depreciation assumption is 15% of gross product, with 10% and 25% sensitivities. This covers ordinary housing, productive equipment, food systems, ordinary energy hardware, computing, orbital habitat structure and intangibles. It excludes the incremental assets priced separately in construction. The model adds those assets' scenario cost divided by an assumed life: aerial carriers and controls 40 years; ground receivers 40; critical reserves 20; environmental establishment 100; optical shield 15; dedicated orbital power 40; magnetic protection 100; passive bulk habitat shielding 500. These are sensitivity assumptions and current-price replacement proxies, not measured service lives. They presume a maintained mature stock and constant real replacement prices.

Air and water import expenditure remains in construction finance but receives no automatic cost/lifetime depreciation. Treating the endowment as maintained is a study convention; actual losses and potential instability require physical replacement and risk accounts. Depreciating the entire import bill by an arbitrary percentage would conflate those mechanisms with the construction history. Environmental establishment's separate produced-asset treatment remains provisional.

The natural-depletion default is zero because local resource depletion is unpriced. It is not evidence of no extraction or environmental drawdown. Sensitivities allocate 0.5% or 2% of product on all worlds, or 1% on Earth alone. Source extraction beyond these three territories belongs to the source economy; it must not be charged again as lunar depletion merely because the Moon imports the product.

The proposed 2% common operating allocation and additional 1% on Moon and array are valid only as a chosen budget for collective final environmental and safety services. There is no engineering evidence that these percentages measure necessary costs. The implementation therefore shows them separately and reports an optional resource residual after allocation. Gross product, net product, resident income and cash resources before that allocation are unchanged. This budget excludes commercial power and habitat operating expenditure, producer intermediate inputs, depreciation, capital replacement and debt service. Half/double allocation sensitivities expose the arbitrary scale.

Construction financing has six cases: retired liabilities; patient completion debt; rolling tranches; contemporaneously paid construction interest; fully deferred stress debt; and staged debt observed after repayment has finished. The Earth creditor share is explicit, with the remaining lunar or array liability held domestically. Earth also has an independent inherited-debt sensitivity of zero, one or five annual gross products owed to the array. These are ownership and timing assumptions, not forecasts or financing recommendations.

The construction bill prices the initial or end-state asset inventory. It omits renewal and operating expenditure during the 500-year build. Staged repayments require early orbital/source-industry revenue or external sponsorship; they cannot assume a mature eight-billion-person lunar economy throughout construction. The optional linear-buildout memo, one-half mature depreciation times build years, estimates gross replacement spending under that assumption. It is not automatically added to debt: contemporaneous revenue, existing construction allowances and replacement ownership must first be reconciled.

With no new external borrowing, asset sales, current transfers or additional financial flows, required net exports equal minus net primary income minus net principal receipts. The three worlds' financing counterparts therefore close; an arbitrary independent trade matrix would generally break this closure. Gross domestic absorption equals gross product minus required net exports. Subtracting fixed-capital replacement gives consumption capacity, and subtracting the separately displayed depletion reserve gives the NWP-based resource pool. The depletion reserve is an unallocated planning allowance; actual restoration investment requires a revised expenditure and financing schedule. The 5%, 15% and 30% export-share flags are assumed capacity screens. Demand, prices, bilateral trade and verified capacity remain unmodeled.
