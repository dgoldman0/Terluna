# Earth, Open Moon and array: world products and construction finance

31 October 2026. This study compares a completed Open Moon at the accepted C′ population, 8 billion residents with 4 billion of them aloft, with an equally advanced future Earth of 9 billion and the inhabited array of 5 billion, as three interconnected economies sharing one mature technology of automation, energy and biology. Its accounts are proposals and calculations: a physical inventory taken from the project's committed products, the build priced element by element, sector scenarios for production, and financing, amenity, travel, industrial and early-income accounts. The 3 billion people of the colonies, and the source economies that would mine, process and send the air and water, stand outside the three accounts as a fourth economy still to be built. The reader is assumed to know the project's physical design; the economic definitions are introduced below.

**Production follows population and an assumed output per resident.** The sector scenarios set the Moon's value added per resident at 0.83, 1.05 or 1.26 times Earth's and the array's at 0.75, 1 or 1.5 times, each ratio a judgement informed by the physical studies ([productivity.md](productivity.md)). With 8 billion residents against 9, equal output per resident would give the Moon 0.889 of Earth's product, so the close-parity case's 1.049 gives Earth:Moon:array **100:93:56** in gross product, and **100:91:53** in net product after the stated depreciation and before unpriced local depletion ([comparison.md](comparison.md)). Across the three cases the Moon's gross product runs 74–112 and the array's 42–83.

**The build's physical demand is the firm result, and its price an assumption.** The middle inventory delivers 3.2×10¹⁹ kg of air and water over 500 years from 2.5×10²⁰ kg of source feed at a 1% nitrogen grade, and needs 46, 99 or 366 PW of purchased process power across the three cost cases; in the advanced case 82% of it is departure propulsion, launching the volatiles from their source bodies ([construction.md](construction.md)). The resources domain adds the braking at the hubs, 20–340 PW across its delivery range ([industry](../../../resources/industry.md)), so delivery and braking together run 120–440 PW in the advanced case (140–240 PW for this inventory's own 2.0×10⁹ kg/s), against the 19 September civilization-scale envelope of 501 PW. Sunlight at 40 AU is 1/1,600 of its strength at the Earth, so the source sites' energy has to come from somewhere other than inner-system collectors. Priced at an assumed 0.01, 0.1 or 1 U/MWh for that energy, with matching equipment prices, the build costs 11, 97 or 2,964 years of future Earth's output, 2.2%, 19% or 593% of it a year over the 500 years. The advanced case's imports alone, 78.8 quadrillion U, would be about 158 trillion U a year of production in the source economies, a sixth of future Earth's annual product.

**Financing moves resources between worlds and leaves production as it is.** In a patient example, half the build borrowed at 0.1% real over 500 years and half the debt held on Earth at full population, the Moon and the array keep **100:75:48** of Earth's resources after replacement and external principal; staged and current-interest cases leave the Moon 72 and 66, and full deferral at 1% fails. Who lends, who owns, who bears losses and who decides are arrangements the Moon's communities choose, through the collective decisions recorded in the [register](../../decisions.md#automation-the-network-and-food-12-october-2026); the accounts trace each arrangement's consequences, and the [monetary-system study](../monetary_system/README.md) treats money, settlement and credit.

**Leisure, gardens and travel take about an eighth of the Moon's product**, the leisure row's 104 of 839 trillion U. The hybrid regions' 4.68 million km² of megaparks and great gardens, 585 m² for each resident, hold resident and visitor services, and travel runs in all six directions among the three worlds ([amenities.md](amenities.md)). The connected case's 3.58 billion arrivals a year on the Moon, 9.8 million a day, are a share of each world's spending: transport, arrival ports and the register's transport rules are still to size them, and the trade balances they feed, which cover 30–57% of the patient case's export need in the strong-preference cases, carry that condition. Early income from the array, from enclosed communities, from sport in one lava-tube community and from reserve-constrained mining reduces the debt modestly ([development.md](development.md), [early_exports.md](early_exports.md), [sports_finance.md](sports_finance.md)); the planetary bill stays far larger than any of them.

**Lunar industry's strengths are particular.** Before the air arrives, a deliberately deferred release keeps an interval of ambient vacuum for large workpieces; under uniform loading the surface passes 0.1 Pa within hours, and afterwards pumped vacuum stays easy at low ambient pressure ([industrial_engineering.md](industrial_engineering.md), [industrial_transition.md](industrial_transition.md)). Lunar gravity suits filled castings with a constrained cure, spin-cast blanks at 40.7% of Earth's angular speed and slow-settling suspensions ([manufacturing.md](manufacturing.md), [slow_settling.md](slow_settling.md)); the 1.2 atm atmosphere gives 20% more buoyant mass per volume, 30.6% less fan work for a fixed heat duty and more clamping force ([pressure_advantages.md](pressure_advantages.md)), and its extra nitrogen a material-specific resistance to flame spread ([industrial_combustion.md](industrial_combustion.md)). Mature industry under the 1.2 atm surface pressure and finite early campaigns for the array's structures follow from these ([mature_industry.md](mature_industry.md), [early_campaigns.md](early_campaigns.md)), and the [distinctive activities](distinctive_activities.md) and [sport concepts](sports_concepts.md) set out what large low-gravity spaces with air, water and living landscapes offer. Equally advanced Earth processes and rotating array factories are the comparators throughout, and these accounts attribute output within the totals above.

## What the accounts measure

For each territory, gross world product (GWP) is the sum of gross value added plus net taxes on products. Gross value added subtracts intermediate goods and services from gross output, so producer purchases of energy, routine repairs and materials are already out of GWP and stay out of any further operating deduction. Household energy purchases remain final consumption. The production boundary includes nonmarket services under a consistent valuation convention. [BEA's GDP definitions](https://www.bea.gov/index.php/news/gdp-release-additional-information) establish these distinctions.

**Net world product (NWP)** follows the 2025 SNA: GWP less economic depreciation and natural-resource depletion. Depreciation covers wear, aging, obsolescence and ordinary accidental losses. Depletion covers nonrenewable resources extracted and renewable resources taken beyond regeneration, and is charged in the territory where it happens, so the Moon's imports carry their source's depletion in the source's account. The annual account treats the constructed atmosphere as a maintained endowment, its formal asset classification still open. [OECD's account of the 2025 SNA](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/measuring-natural-resources-in-the-national-accounts_420c7c2a-en/full-report/measuring-natural-resources_a0accc5d.html), [BEA's depreciation definitions](https://www.bea.gov/resources/learning-center/definitions-and-introduction-fixed-assets).

**Net resident income** adds net income earned from other territories to NWP, including interest and profits. A lender's interest receipt matches the borrower's payment. Principal repayment changes financial claims and cash resources while GWP and NWP stay as they are. Equity ownership also creates income claims. Domestic debt cancels in a consolidated world balance, while its contracts, distribution and fiscal treatment stay consequential. [OECD's production, income and financial-account distinctions](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-benchmark-definition-of-foreign-direct-investment-fifth-edition_7f05c0a3-en/full-report/links-between-fdi-and-other-statistics_e6f184d4.html).

The **cash-resource account** shows external principal payments and essential services separately. Reading net income as spendable resources requires replacement investment to equal depreciation, no unrecorded net investment, and an explicit treatment of depletion reserves, transfers and borrowing. The optional allocation residual subtracts a defined final-service allocation once. A collective budget's cash includes intermediate purchases, replacement and debt service already counted elsewhere, so only its final services enter that allocation.

The Moon's territorial account covers its surface and atmosphere. The array is a peer economy, with its own output and costs, including when it supplies lunar users. Ownership and service contracts determine which residents receive income and which territory purchases support services. Costs of a shared shield or power system carry explicit territorial and funding assumptions, and a funding obligation leaves the producer's GDP where the production is. Earth receives the same technological possibilities and a comparable allowance for maintaining its own infrastructure and environment. Income earned on Earth-owned orbital assets enters Earth's resident-income account, and the investment expenditure itself is a capital transaction.

## Construction finance through time

[finance.py](finance.py) calculates equal annual loan payments, debt remaining after payments, construction interest and completed tranches. Rates are effective annual **real** rates in a common transferable unit. Draws occur at year end. A tranche begins repayment one year after its completion; the maturity snapshot follows that year's payment, and mature debt service is the next year's payment.

For principal D, rate r and n annual payments, the payment is `D r / (1 − (1+r)^−n)`, or `D/n` at zero interest. At 0.1%, 0.5%, 1% and 2% real interest, a 500-year loan requires approximately 0.254%, 0.545%, 1.007% and 2.000% of principal each year. Extending repayment eventually approaches the interest-only floor.

Construction interest must also be financed. Equal annual draws over a 500-year build multiply the financed construction cost by **1.297 at 0.1%, 4.443 at 0.5%, 28.755 at 1%, and 1,995.557 at 2%** if every interest payment is deferred; these multipliers follow from the arithmetic of the draws. The module records contemporaneous interest payments when only part of interest is capitalized.

Repayment can begin well before full population. Ten successive 50-year construction tranches, wholly debt financed with interest capitalized during each tranche, then each repaid over 100 years at 1%, leave about 0.209 construction costs outstanding at year 500. They have already required about 1.739 construction costs in earlier repayments, including interest, which early settlement revenues or transfers must fund. A later full-population date can retire all these loans. The comparison includes both a mature state after legacy repayment and an explicitly dated first-full-population state.
