# Early exports from enclosed lunar communities

31 October 2026. This scenario study asks how much tourism-led businesses could contribute while the Moon's population still lives in enclosed communities. Under the reference assumptions, facilities serving **100,000 permanent residents assign about 115 million U a year to development** after their specified costs; at **100 million residents**, the corresponding amount is **185 billion U a year**. Tourism alone supplies most of that contribution. These are annual outputs at alternative operating milestones, with no adopted opening dates or population schedule.

The calculation uses one common real unit U, with future Earth annual GWP of 100,000 U per resident and nine billion Earth residents. It prices a hypothetical visitor and manufacturing portfolio, its new capital and its own loans. The result is **facility cash after a stated shared-community allowance**, not a complete city budget, net exports or an increase added to the previous whole-world GWP.

The [early sport and media account](sports_finance.md) examines a smaller starting point than these population milestones: one venue, a resident performance cohort, limited visitors and remote commercial receipts. Its venue cash and project-finance examples form an alternative early portfolio; they must be reconciled with this account before aggregation.

## What visitors buy before the open world

The early destination consists of inhabited tube-city spaces, enclosed gardens, public gathering places, low-gravity sport and flight within supplied air, heritage communities, and supported surface excursions. [Tube cities](../land_cities/README.md) and the early protective surface dome at [Tranquility Base (D6)](../conservation/README.md) already belong to the project's habitation and conservation direction. The [low-gravity design study](../../../habitation/low_gravity/README.md) supplies the movement and interior-design context. People can visit a functioning community, its architecture and cultural life, and return to see it develop. The mature open-Moon seas, aerial biomes and extensive outdoor gardens are later possibilities and supply no early revenue in this calculation.

Automated accommodation, food service and maintenance allow visitor capacity to grow without a fixed human staffing ratio. Physical capacity still requires beds, pressure and shielding systems, suitable air for flight, food, water, power, waste processing, access and emergency support. Earth offers advanced destinations of its own, and the array can reproduce reduced gravity. Lunar demand therefore depends on the combined experience of place, history, community and activities; the model assigns no exclusive low-gravity attraction or health benefit.

The no-manufacturing cases remove both factory revenue and factory capital. They leave about **84 million, 1.74 billion, 20.34 billion and 154.35 billion U annually** assigned to development. The reference financing contribution therefore does not depend primarily on the proposed manufacturing activity.

Each reference stage installs **0.2 visitor beds per permanent resident** and assumes a **14-night stay**. Occupancy ceilings rise through **50%, 65%, 70% and 75%**, while destination spending falls through **1,000, 750, 600 and 450 U per night**. Reference demand fills those allowances. The associated average visitor populations are 10%, 13%, 14% and 15% of the resident populations. Peak support is assumed to accommodate all visitor beds in addition to residents. The largest stage represents a network of enclosed settlements and visitor facilities, not a single demonstrated tube-city capacity.

Visitor demand is constrained separately by each origin's population, participation, spending budget and route capacity; Earth and array visitors then share the same lunar beds. The associated array populations are **10 million, 100 million, one billion and three billion**. Earth access fares decline from 20,000 to 2,000 U across the stages, and array fares from 10,000 to 1,500 U. These purchases count against the customer's travel budget but provide no assumed lunar carrier surplus. The screen covers inbound destination exports, so reciprocal lunar travel and the full interworld trade balance remain outside it.

## Capital, costs and the available cash

The four reference portfolios require **20.5, 155, 1,050 and 8,500 billion U of initial facility capital**. Half must come from sponsors or equity and half from new facility borrowing. Visitor capital includes an assumed allocation for accommodation, pressure and shielding, utilities, attractions and access facilities; its price per bed falls from one million to 400,000 U. The factory allowance adds 5,000 U per permanent resident. These are hypothetical capital budgets, with no engineering bill establishing their sufficiency.

The operating account deducts 40% of sales for variable intermediate purchases, 20% for compensation and distributed living resources, fixed operating costs equal to 1% of installed capital annually, and 10% of sales retained for business growth. Annual replacement equals capital divided by **40 years for visitor facilities** and **20 years for manufacturing**. Facility loans carry **0.5% real interest over 100 years**, with their annual interest and principal payments charged to cash. Gross and net value added are recorded separately; salaries, investment and principal payments are cash uses rather than deductions from net production.

After those facility expenses, the calculation reserves another **1,000 U per permanent resident per year** for otherwise unfunded shared-community needs. It assigns half of any remaining positive cash to development and retains half. A negative residual remains an explicit funding gap. The 1,000-U allowance is not a demonstrated public-service budget: permanent resident housing and its funding, other household activities and additional shared infrastructure require their own accounts. Assigning half the residual to development assumes the sponsors or equity owners permit that use. No required equity dividend is modelled; a contractual payout would reduce the available contribution. Their initial contribution does not make capital free.

The new facilities cannot be silently added to the mature model's ordinary-capital allowance if that allowance already includes them. Their capital, debt, depreciation and cash contributions need reconciliation before the two models are combined.

## Demand and cost can change the result substantially

| Case | Main changed assumptions | Assigned annual cash at 100 million residents, B |
|---|---|---:|
| Reference | 0.2 beds/resident; 75% occupancy; 450 U/night | 185.0 |
| Visitor-focused expansion | 0.5 beds/resident; 80% occupancy ceiling; prices ×1.5; participation ×2; route capacity ×3; stronger factory sales | 805.8 |
| Weak demand and high costs | 40% occupancy ceiling; visitor prices ×0.75; participation and route capacity halved; capital prices ×1.5; higher variable operating costs; weaker factory sales | 0, with a 484.7-B funding gap |

The expansion case retains the reference origin spending budgets. At 100 million residents those budgets limit realised occupancy to **64.6%**, despite its 80% ceiling. It receives about **843 million visitor trips annually** and requires **20,500 billion U of facility capital**, half supplied by sponsors or equity. Its 805.8-billion contribution is conditional on that larger financed destination and the specified customer demand; it is not the return from the reference stock of facilities.

The weak case produces **no positive development cash at any milestone**. Its annual funding gaps are approximately **1.14, 8.31, 55.95 and 484.74 billion U**. Automation does not remove the cost of unused capacity, capital renewal or debt service.

The [manufacturing investigation](manufacturing.md) develops physical reasons to test selected composites and spin-cast components at partial gravity. Its calculated operating windows do not determine factory sales. Manufacturing remains a secondary candidate. The reference assumes 100 kg of nameplate finished output per resident annually, 90% saleable yield, 70% utilization and a price of 50 U/kg, subject to external customer budgets. It does not identify a proven product mix or assign a gravitational quality premium. Product-specific processing, feed, energy, transport, rejection rates and customer demand must justify the future industry; raw mining revenue is not added separately. The stored factory net value added is before unpriced depletion.

## What this means for early financing

Tourism can contribute meaningful sums to an enclosed community's further development well before a planetary open environment exists. At the same time, the reference planetary construction bill is much larger. As a timing illustration, holding the 100-million-resident reference contribution constant for 100 operating years supplies **18.50 trillion U**. If those payments occurred in years 1–100 and avoided borrowing at 0.1% real, their year-500 debt equivalent would be **29.01 trillion U**, about **0.0535%** of the previous patient-case lunar debt. That illustration does not place a 100-million-resident community at the start of construction or integrate its new facility borrowing into the planetary schedule.

The useful next comparison is therefore a dated sequence of commissioned destinations and facilities, each with independently funded customers, continuing resident support and its own capital. This screen supplies candidate annual contributions for such a sequence. It neither adds them to the earlier 2%, 5% and 10% aggregate saving allocations nor changes the original GWP/NWP comparison.

## Reproduction and evidence

[early_exports_inputs.json](early_exports_inputs.json) records the assumptions. [early_exports.py](early_exports.py) computes origin demand, shared capacity, separate facility production and cash, borrowing, replacement and negative funding gaps; [results/early_exports.json](results/early_exports.json) binds the inputs and producer files. Run `python -m research.studies.world_economy.early_exports` for an ignored-run result. The calculations are conditional milestone screens, with no validated market, facility architecture, commissioning dates or gravity-dependent manufacturing advantage.
