# Activities that particularly suit the Open Moon

31 October 2026. The strongest prospective advantage is **extensive, continuously usable space in which low gravity, breathable air, liquid water, living landscapes and fixed ground coexist**. This combination could support ordinary personal flight, new sports and performing arts, unusual aquatic environments, aerial gardens and settlements, and large physical research facilities. Selected production processes remain candidates, but the wider opportunity includes what residents can do for themselves without buying a service.

This assessment is for planning across provisioning, habitation and ecology. It compares equally advanced Earth, Open Moon and array economies, retaining the 1.2 atm lock, approximately 17.5% oxygen and the 301 K design target. Physical reference calculations, conditional biological designs and possible demand are distinguished throughout. A target global temperature does not establish every region's weather or comfort. Earlier regional and ecological results have not all been refreshed for that target.

## What would be distinctive?

Actual lunar places, their history and the communities developing there are location-specific. Most individual physical conditions can also be reproduced elsewhere. Earth can supply pressure chambers, indoor climates, weight offloading and brief reduced-gravity flights; an array centrifuge can supply sustained partial gravity. NASA's [ARGOS description](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/jsc-eva-systems/) and [parabolic-flight account](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/parabolic-flight/) illustrate two existing Earth alternatives. Neither amounts to a continuous partial-gravity field throughout a landscape, its people, loose objects, water and vegetation.

The useful comparison therefore asks how much space, duration and freedom of movement an activity needs. A small closed production vessel is a much easier orbital competitor than a lake, forest or flying district. Large array habitats remain credible competitors at the project's assumed technology; they also offer control over gravity and weather that the Moon cannot match outdoors. This study estimates physical reproduction requirements, without assigning an uncosted lunar monopoly or profitability premium.

Other Solar System environments also combine low gravity and dense air. NASA explicitly identifies these as advantages for [flight on Titan](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/dragonfly/spacecraft-and-instruments/). The Open Moon adds the proposed warm, breathable, inhabited environment and proximity to the other two economies. Its institutions, skills and transport connections must develop before those physical possibilities become exports.

| Environmental condition | Activities it especially helps | Boundary that matters |
|---|---|---|
| Natural gravity of 0.166 Earth gravity across a whole surface | Flight, extended aerial movement, low-weight handling, large partial-gravity experiments | Inertia persists; traction, anchoring and passive settling weaken |
| 1.2 atm with near-Earth oxygen partial pressure | Aerodynamic lift, modestly greater buoyant capacity, liquid-pressure margin and selected industrial uses | It supplies essentially the same oxygen per volume at equal temperature; pressure alone does not give an exercise or combustion bonus |
| Open air over ground, water and inhabited landscapes | Activities that cross venues and media: flying between gardens, shore-to-air sports, field research and outdoor communities | The atmosphere and its protection are costly shared infrastructure; their operation belongs in the existing accounts |
| A deep usable atmosphere and designed aerial ecology | Elevated cultivation, living hosts, observation and aerial neighbourhoods | Altitude-specific lift, oxygen, water, nutrients, shading and weather need local budgets |
| Strong diffuse illumination and selected UV removal | More even illumination in gardens; a different outdoor light environment; potentially longer service for some exposed materials | Scattered light does not create photons. Retained UVA, heat, moisture and oxidation remain relevant |
| Managed fresh seas and extensive hybrid shores | Gardens beside water, recreation, aquatic facilities and coupled water/food landscapes | Freshness does not establish clarity, nutrient adequacy or cheap maintenance; slower settling can increase turbidity |
| Long solar day and night, lunar geology and regional Earth views | Distinctive festivals, journeys, observation and landscape arts | Long nights require storage or lighting; Earth visibility and darkness vary geographically |

These conditions are documented in the [sky-fleet](../sky_fleet/README.md), [low-gravity habitation](../../../habitation/low_gravity/README.md), [UV-biology](../uv_biology/README.md), [shore](../../../geography/README.md#shores-by-the-fresh-seas) and [daily-life](../daily_life/README.md) work. Their combined commercial implications are proposals developed here.

## Activities worth developing

The ranking concerns strength of environmental fit, not forecast revenue. “Strong” means that the physical mechanism substantially changes the activity. It does not establish a qualified design or willing customers.

|---|---|---|---|
| **Personal flight and aerial travel** | Low weight and relatively dense air reduce the lift-related speed and power requirements | Recreational flight, instruction, guided journeys, park access, inspection and light delivery; residents travelling for pleasure | Strong physical fit, especially across extensive landscapes. Array flying spaces compete; wind, traffic and dependable landing remain decisive |
| **Sports, dance and physical performance** | Bodies, costumes, balls, scenery and loose props all share slower gravity-driven motion | New games, aerial choreography, participatory theatre, training, live events and recorded works; informal play | Strong fit. Harnesses and synthetic media can reproduce parts of the experience; rotating venues can reproduce more |
| **Aquatic parks and underwater spaces** | Lower hydrostatic gradients and slower gravity waves alter useful depth and timing | Tall aquaria, underwater galleries, observation facilities, unusual fountains and experimental water sports | Strong geometric opportunity. Water quality, access, structural details and human exposure need separate designs |
| **Hybrid park–garden districts** | Ground, canopy, water and air become connected activity spaces | Public leisure, cultivation, food, education, gatherings and visitor accommodation in the same district | Strong combination with the accepted amenity plan; much of the benefit is resident welfare and local consumption |
| **Large physical testing campuses** | Sustained lunar gravity acts on whole interacting systems without attaching supports to each moving part | Flight/robotics trials, large assemblies, multiphase equipment, long-duration ecological observations and hardware qualification | Plausible export service when physical scale or duration matters. ### Flight, performance and everyday movement

At equal 301 K, the dry binary gas reference gives lunar air **1.194 times Earth's density**. For the same aircraft mass, wing area and lift coefficient, the required airspeed is **0.372 times Earth's**. With the same drag coefficient, aerodynamic power at that operating point is **0.0617 times Earth's**; the ideal induced-hover term at fixed disk area has the same ratio. These are matched-condition scaling results, not a sixteenfold saving in total vehicle operating costs. Reynolds number, propulsive efficiency, equipment, control and weather can change the comparison. At the same airspeed and drag coefficient, denser air instead increases parasitic drag.

This makes accessible recreational flying and movement between garden levels credible engineering directions. It also favours the equipment, maintenance, instruction and venues surrounding flight. Existing [sky-fleet concepts](../sky_fleet/README.md) supply candidate vehicles, rather than certified performance at the new design temperature. Buoyant payload mass per cubic metre does not gain a factor of six from low gravity: gravity cancels between displaced air and supported weight.

For a prescribed vertical takeoff speed of 2.5 m/s, the no-drag calculation gives **1.92 m rise and 3.08 s airborne**, compared with **0.319 m and 0.510 s** on Earth. That difference concerns an ordinary jump-sized impulse. It can change the vocabulary of dance, acrobatics and games, especially when loose objects and several performers interact. Actual takeoff speed, landing control and effort require biomechanics. Holding fall distance fixed gives a different timing ratio, and low gravity does not eliminate collision energy or make tall falls harmless.

Everyday benefits could be as significant as performances sold to visitors: children inventing games, neighbours moving among terraces, people joining amateur aerial ensembles. Accessibility must be designed around reduced traction and unchanged inertia as well as lower weight. The [partial-gravity health literature](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210019591/downloads/ICES-2021-142.pdf) supplies reasons to investigate long-term effects; its indirect evidence does not establish a therapeutic service or a measured safe gravity threshold.

### Water as an activity space

At a chosen **300 kPa absolute apparatus pressure ceiling**, constant-density water reaches **109.8 m below the lunar surface**, compared with **20.3 m on Earth**. The higher lunar surface pressure consumes some of that allowance, so the depth ratio is 5.42 rather than 6.04. This is a pressure-budget comparison for equipment. It is not a diving limit: gas exposure, decompression, breathing mixtures and rescue require separate medical and engineering assessment.

A gallery kept at the local surface pressure would also experience only 0.166 times the external water-pressure difference at the same depth. That can favour deep viewing rooms, large windows or tall aquarium structures where hydrostatic load dominates. Their spans, seals, foundations, circulation and water inventories still require design. More water volume is not automatically more biological productivity, and slow sediment removal can make visibility expensive to maintain.

For small-amplitude gravity waves of equal wavelength and water depth, phase speed scales as the square root of gravity. The [linear dispersion relation](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.376/ProblemSets/TheAnswers/ProblemSet202302ans.pdf) gives a 10 m wavelength in 20 m deep water a speed of **1.61 m/s on the Moon versus 3.95 m/s on Earth**, with periods of 6.22 and 2.53 s. This suggests deliberately slower water choreography and different wave-riding designs. Breaking waves, strong currents and natural storm waves lie outside that calculation; the Moon's denser air does not establish gentler weather.

A fountain jet with the same initial vertical speed has a greater ideal rise, while the same liquid's capillary length increases by **2.46 times**. The latter follows the [gravity–surface-tension balance](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-03sc-physics-iii-vibrations-and-waves-fall-2016/ef731c1b91d77a6db003f6c27e300d25_MIT8_03SCF16_Textbook.pdf). These effects provide material for installations and experiments, with drag, breakup and wetting still limiting the forms. Boats retain their displacement-volume requirement, and sailing craft lose gravitational righting moment as well as weight.

### Why the scale of the orbital alternative matters

For a rotating habitat with lunar acceleration at radius R, angular speed is `sqrt(g/R)`. Across radial height h, the fractional acceleration change is `h/R`; across circumferential arc width W, the direction of down changes by `W/R` radians. Motion perpendicular to the spin axis adds a Coriolis acceleration of magnitude `2 omega v`. Motion parallel to the axis has no Coriolis term. These are classical relations discussed in [Physics of Artificial Gravity](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20070001008/downloads/20070001008.pdf).

As an **illustrative fidelity choice**, consider a venue with 50 m of vertical extent and 1 km of circumferential extent, allowing 10 m/s motion in arbitrary directions. Require less than or equal to 1% gravity variation, 2 degrees of down-direction change and a Coriolis term no greater than 10% of lunar gravity. The three constraints require radii of **5.00 km, 28.65 km and 24.63 km**, respectively. Meeting all three requires **28.65 km radius**, rotating at about 0.072 rpm. This describes a geometric scale, not the cost or feasibility of a complete ring: a smaller occupied structure on a long tether is also a possible implementation.

The natural spherical lunar field varies by about **0.0058%** over the same 50 m height; down changes by **0.033 degrees** across 1 km, and the maximum rotation-related Coriolis term at 10 m/s is **0.0033% of gravity**. Local terrain and gravity anomalies are omitted. A rotating array venue can accept different tolerances, exploit axial length, restrict movement or use active controls. None of the chosen tolerances is a human comfort limit.

The resulting lunar advantage is most persuasive for long, freely multidirectional activities and large interacting environments. It is much weaker for small reactors or standardized lanes, where the array can reproduce partial gravity with less infrastructure and choose a different acceleration when useful. An export research campus therefore needs customers who require its physical conditions: the array can still provide most computation, simulation and generic professional services.

### Gardens, living structures and light

The accepted hybrid plan already contains **4.68 million km²** of mixed park, garden, recreation and amenity space. Low gravity can add routes through its vertical extent: canopy walks, flying clearings, water terraces and gatherings extending into the air. Where climate permits, these become components of neighbourhood life. Visitor spending is one way to support them; public provision and residents' own cultivation are others. The [amenity account](amenities.md) already includes services in these regions.

Aerophytes add a more conditional possibility: hosts that provide living area, nursery habitat and support for gardens or dwellings while remaining alive. The [provisioning assessment](../../../provisioning/aerophytes.md) identifies modular sky reefs and lesser aerophytes as cultivation candidates, with larger hosts potentially valuable for space. A nursery and stewardship economy could serve these communities through propagation, inspection, repair, nutrient recovery and replacement. Host growth, usable surface, supported wet biomass and surplus harvest are separate budgets. This study adds no host capacity or crop yield.

Scattered illumination can help distribute light through a planting or soften shadows in a performance space, but it cannot remove the shared photon budget. Reduced short-wave UV also makes some exposed material applications worth testing. Its industrial value depends on the material's spectral response: [NIST's PET study](https://www.nist.gov/publications/evaluating-environmental-factors-microplastic-generation-accelerated-weathering-study) found that temperature and humidity materially affected weathering under UV exposure. The project's low erythemal UV index therefore supplies no general polymer-life multiplier. A warm, humid region could offset part of a spectral benefit.

Long nights and twilight could support a distinctive calendar of night gardens, performances and observatories for local phenomena. [Living-light work](../living_light/README.md) remains conditional; contemporary dim bioluminescent plants do not establish useful path lighting. Long nights also require ecological reserves and powered services. These attractions belong with regional climate and community design, rather than a claim that a long photoperiod is universally productive.

## Timing and economic contribution

Early sealed communities could offer small flight halls, performance spaces and partial-gravity research facilities before the open atmosphere. Their enclosure, transport and resident-support costs remain significant. Early low-pressure fabrication campaigns are a separate, finite opportunity in the [existing industrial account](early_campaigns.md); the present assessment supplies no new assumption about their duration or sales.

As atmosphere, water, protected ground and reliable transport become available, activities can extend across larger regions. Physical trials and local construction services can expand before mature aerophyte habitats or a full visitor economy. In the mature world, the most durable advantage would be a connected environment used daily by residents, with visitors buying access to experiences and facilities that also have local purposes.

| Contribution | How it enters the three-world economy |
|---|---|
| Residents use gardens, flight, sport and culture | Purchased and publicly provided services contribute recorded production, including maintained parks with free entry. Informal play, beauty and social connection can improve welfare beyond that measured service |
| Earth/array visitors spend on the Moon | Lunar services can earn export income; imported travel and supplies reduce the lunar share. Moon residents' trips to Earth and the array generate reciprocal spending |
| Customers buy physical experiments, qualification or training | Charge for a specified facility service and accepted output; include equipment, capital renewal and imported array computation |
| Equipment, propagules, designs or recordings sell elsewhere | Count only what customers can use and will pay for. Conditions favourable to producing something on the Moon do not guarantee its usefulness after export |
| Repair and ecological maintenance sustain the environment | These services support lasting activity but consume resources; a larger maintenance burden is not an independent net economic advantage |
| Early operators retain a cash surplus | Only the surplus after operating inputs, necessary replacement/investment and existing claims can support development. Its use also depends on ownership and funding arrangements |

No new GWP, NWP or debt-repayment uplift is assigned. Much of this activity would occupy the existing amenity, tourism, construction, biological and service sectors. Counting a venue's entire sales, its suppliers' sales and its property premium as separate additions would exaggerate its contribution. A commercially attractive activity must still outperform its competitor after logistics and capital costs; a socially valuable public space need not earn external revenue to justify its local use.

## Where the advantage is weaker

Generic computing and much energy-intensive industry retain the array's advantages. Precision vacuum processes and some astronomical observations benefit from avoiding the Moon's atmosphere. Low lunar gravity alone does not establish cheap exports once atmospheric launch, delivery and handling are included. Earth retains its existing ecosystems, markets and terrestrial production environments, with equal access to future technology.

Bulk food exports, general medical rehabilitation and general reductions in large-building costs remain weak candidates for an assumed lunar advantage. Nutrients and light constrain production; long-term health remains unresolved; wind, pressure, inertia and buckling can determine a structure even when its self-weight falls. The [mature-industry](mature_industry.md), [slow-settling](slow_settling.md) and [combustion](industrial_combustion.md) studies retain process-specific opportunities without a general factory or fire-loss premium.

Authentic lunar geology is a stronger source of distinctiveness than an assumed abundance of ready-made caverns. The [2024 Mini-RF account](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/new-evidence-adds-to-findings-hinting-at-network-of-caves-on-moon/) describes evidence for a conduit extending more than 200 feet from a pit and leaves its full extent unknown. It does not qualify kilometre-scale halls for habitation or tourism. Atmospheric construction can also alter exposed geological records, making surveyed and preserved sites a finite part of the experience.

The strongest next design studies are a connected flying and garden district, an aquatic observation/recreation complex, and a large physical testing campus, each compared with an Earth or array facility delivering the same service. They would establish capacities, local environmental conditions, actual operating requirements and what users value. Living aerial communities deserve a parallel biological pathway, since their environmental fit is compelling while their dependable capacity remains a separate research problem.
