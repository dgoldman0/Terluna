# Early production and construction finance

The array can begin earning well before the Open Moon is complete. Early lunar industry, enclosed settlements and later opened regions can also contribute. The useful financing quantity is **independently earned cash assigned to development after operating inputs, consumption, replacement and necessary reinvestment**. A larger GDP, a sales contract or rising land values supplies no automatic cash to the construction programme.

In the illustrative phased case, allocating 5% of realised output to development at a 0.1% real financing rate retires the array's modelled construction debt by year 206. Lunar contributions reduce its debt at full population by **8.5%**. Transferring the array's subsequent surplus to lunar construction raises that reduction to **17.2%**. These are controlled financing scenarios with assumed revenue growth and saving allocations, ahead of named customers, institutions and a commissioning timetable. The [amenity and tourism account](amenities.md) develops resident services and reciprocal travel separately.

## Economic activity can precede the finished environment

| Operating milestone | Candidate paying activity | Dependency and financing boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Surveyed lunar sites, power and export transport | Selected rock and metal products for orbital customers; research and engineering services | Preserve future lunar reserves and heritage before extraction. Deliveries bought by the same construction programme are internal purchases; independent customers need a separate demand account. |
| Enclosed bases and commissioned orbital workshops | Scientific work, fabrication, repair, logistics and limited visits | Life support, equipment and safe transport must work. Research contracts already counted as sponsor contributions cannot be counted again. |
| Modular orbital generation, radiators and communications | Batch computing, training, simulation and archives; selective power services | Workload demand and export links must exist. Earth has the same technology and competing infrastructure. |
| Proven film factories and orbital fleets | Films, collectors, maintenance and transport for other orbital systems | External orders compete with this project's own requirements for manufacturing capacity. Renewing the lunar shield remains an expense. |
| Source extraction and delivery networks | Volatiles, propellant and processed material for other users | Source-site power, chemistry and capture must operate. Lunar construction purchases are already included in the construction bill. |
| Suitable regional atmosphere, water and ecosystems | Managed gardens, nurseries, specialist biological products and ecological visits | Safe open-air conditions and established organisms must precede these services. Gardens and giant aerophytes require different establishment times. |
| Stable occupied districts | Housing services, local amenities, education, recreation and broader tourism | Utilities, transport, food and continuing replacement must remain funded as population grows. |

The [population study](../../../provisioning/population.md) already considers array services to Earth during the first centuries. Its Earth–Moon communication delay favours work tolerant of latency. Its power comparison also limits an easy export assumption: the reference gives about 51% bus-to-bus delivery, roughly 20,400 km² of Earth receivers per TW and access constrained by lunar visibility; Earth's own geostationary systems can use much smaller transmitters. Orbital power remains useful, but the model does not assume Earth buys every available watt. [Industry locations](../../../resources/industry.md) distinguish the fleet vicinity, more distant hubs and the changing advantages of the lunar surface as atmosphere accumulates.

Service contracts can finance completed modules before the planetary project is finished. Collective ownership, dividends, royalties or levies can assign part of a business's surplus to development, in whatever arrangement the communities choose ([register](../../decisions.md#automation-the-network-and-food-12-october-2026)). Equity can reduce contractual borrowing while creating future ownership claims, and so has a cost of its own. Prepaid services and concessions likewise exchange current money for future obligations. A financing plan specifies those obligations and keeps enough operating cash to meet them.

## Controlled annual cash flows

The calculation retains the advanced construction case: **83.67 quadrillion U attributed to the Moon and 3.33 quadrillion U to the array**, excluding interest. Sponsors pay a fixed half of each annual construction draw. The other half and unpaid interest are borrowed; independently earned contributions first pay interest and current funding requirements, then repay debt. Once a world has funded that year's requirements and retired its debt, further cash is released. An optional transfer moves that released array cash to the Moon once, reducing array-retained funds by the same amount.

For opening debt `D`, annual real rate `r`, funding need `K`, fixed sponsor payment `G` and available independent cash `S`, the year-end balance with prepayment is `max(0, D(1+r) + K − G − S)`. Excess cash is released separately. The implemented ledger exposes interest paid, new borrowing, principal prepayment and cash conservation. Its comparison holds construction, sponsor funding and the rate fixed, withholding only the contribution applied to the loan. Earlier independent cash avoids both a borrowing draw and the interest that draw would accrue.

The revenue curves express realised independent economic activity as fractions of the mature reference GWP. They exclude the construction programme's own debt-funded output when identifying new financing sources. In the phased example, Moon fractions at years 100, 200, 300, 400 and 500 are **0.001, 0.01, 0.10, 0.40 and 1.00**; array fractions are **0.10, 0.35, 0.60, 0.80 and 1.00**, with 0.03 at year 50. The paths interpolate annually. These assumptions require progressively available markets, operating capacity and earlier lunar habitation than the older enclosed-worker roadmap. They are alternatives for testing financing, ahead of any opening dates.

The available-cash shares of **2%, 5% and 10% of realised GWP** are allocations of saving, dividends or taxes after other needs. They are shares of realised output, distinct from profit margins on gross sales. Active assets' replacement is included before that allocation; source-industry renewal is already included in the construction model's unit service prices. The calculation presumes that the ordinary capital allowance can accommodate the revenue-producing businesses. Additional business capital, unfunded upkeep or consumption needs reduce the contribution or require additional funding.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| No early contribution | 54.24 | — | Outstanding at year 500 |
| 2% of realised output, own funding only | 52.39 | 3.4% | 402 |
| 5%, own funding only | 49.62 | 8.5% | 206 |
| 5%, subsequent array surplus transferred | 44.91 | 17.2% | 206 |
| 10%, own funding only | 45.00 | 17.0% | 137 |
| 10%, subsequent array surplus transferred | 33.42 | 38.4% | 137 |

The 5% case contributes **4.26 quadrillion U** from lunar activity over 500 years and **5.86 quadrillion U** from array activity. After the array funds its own construction and debt, it releases **4.17 quadrillion U**. That release can remain with array residents or fund lunar development under an explicit ownership or fiscal arrangement. The model does not presume a permanent lunar claim on array income.

These payments change debt and retained resources, while the mature production assumptions remain fixed. With half the remaining debt held on Earth, the reference mature resource ratios after replacement and external debt service improve from **100:75.2:48.4** to **100:76.7:49.2** for Earth:Moon:array under the 5% own-funding case, and to **100:77.9:49.6** when released array funds support the Moon. Earth receives less debt service as the debt falls. Internal payments remain consequential to institutions and distribution even when they cancel within a world's consolidated account.

Higher interest can overwhelm late lunar income. At 0.5% real, the same 5% contribution leaves **179.13 quadrillion U** of lunar debt at year 500; at 1%, **1,190.45 quadrillion U** remains. The array retires its own debt in these particular profiles by years 241 and 409 respectively. These higher-rate balances show that obligations would need funding, repayment or restructuring earlier.

## Timing, replacement and a later opening

The alternative late-opening profile earns less early lunar cash and leaves **52.03 quadrillion U** at year 500 under the 5% and 0.1% assumptions. An earlier orbital profile moves array debt retirement to year 110. These comparisons identify the value of early operation while leaving its physical commissioning dates unestablished.

A separate enclosed-then-open case keeps lunar activity at **0.005 of mature GWP at year 500**, then reaches full activity at year 750. The year-500 figure corresponds to about 40 million mature-output-equivalent residents, an ambitious sensitivity beside the older ten-million enclosed-worker illustration. Atmospheric suitability, migration, habitat construction and ecological development must support the subsequent increase. The array reaches mature activity at year 500.

Both the contribution and zero-contribution cases retain the same 500-year construction draw schedule and are compared at year 750, so the baseline includes the additional 250 years of interest. At 0.1% real and a 5% saving allocation, lunar debt is **66.66 quadrillion U instead of 69.64 quadrillion U**, a 4.3% reduction. Applying released array cash raises the reduction to about 23.1%, leaving **53.52 quadrillion U**. Much of the array's contribution then arrives while the lunar population is still growing. Drawing all the end-state capital by year 500 is itself a financing assumption; a resolved later-build schedule could change both interest and replacement.

Installing productive assets long before they are used creates another cost. The principal contribution scenarios assume assets enter service with the activity they support. The premature-installation sensitivity instead places stocks uniformly over 500 years and charges extra care and renewal on the idle fraction:

`extra annual carrying requirement = max(installed fraction − active fraction, 0) × mature annual depreciation`.

Active replacement is already funded before project surplus, so it is not deducted again. The full extra requirement enters both financing comparisons, with the original sponsor payments unchanged. Under the phased profile this adds **21.04 quadrillion U** of lunar and **1.48 quadrillion U** of array expenditure over the build. At 0.1%, lunar debt becomes **76.15 quadrillion U with contributions, versus 80.77 quadrillion U without them**; array retirement moves to year 370. These are alternative installation assumptions, each a case of its own beside the phased one. They make commissioning and replacement funding central to the result.

The committed product records all 72 combinations of activity profile, saving share, interest rate and array-surplus allocation, plus the carrying-cost sensitivity. It includes matched baseline ages, annual cash controls and selected checkpoints. No construction-funded wage, supplier receipt or visitor purchase is automatically accepted as independent finance; the provenance of customer funds matters alongside the service actually produced.

## Early lunar mining with future resources reserved

Lunar mining can supply early external income and material for the array. The opportunity is strongest before atmospheric growth makes surface export harder, and before seas cover accessible mare deposits. That timing also makes **surveying and retaining future resources a prerequisite to export**. A low fraction of the Moon's total mass can still remove a large fraction of an accessible nutrient-rich deposit or destroy a scientifically important site.

The resource account needs separate reservations for initial settlement and habitat stocks; long-term industrial make-up and emergency stocks; phosphorus, potassium and other ecological nutrients; scarce water and other volatiles; accessible ore bodies and useful by-products; and protected landscapes or heritage. Future requirements should include a stated time horizon, realistic recovery losses and uncertainty. Recycled material reduces demand for virgin feed but does not remove losses or guarantee that the needed element remains accessible. Until deposit surveys and those requirements establish a surplus, this study's numerical export envelope grants no permission to extract it.

Candidate exports include selected construction feed, metals, glass and ceramic products, and services that turn them into useful orbital structures. The [industry study](../../../resources/industry.md) already places early orbital material supply on the airless Moon and later supply increasingly on asteroids. This suggests recovering and retaining useful components before selling low-priority bulk material, locating extraction where it is compatible with future geography, and preserving pre-flood access to deposits needed by the Moon itself. Direct export of imported future atmospheric water, nitrogen or ecological nutrients would undermine the development objective unless a genuine surplus were demonstrated.

There is a substantial but finite identified customer stock: the reference array habitat shielding allowance is **4,950 Gt**, with a distinct **75 Gt of habitat structure**. The model prices installed bulk shielding at **0.1 U/kg**, giving 495 trillion U of total buyer expenditure. That price is for a delivered, installed shielding service, of which the mineral and a lunar mining margin are only parts. The stock is already charged to array construction. Supplying it from the Moon would change procurement, local production and income allocation, while leaving the buyer's matching expenditure visible.

Purchases paid from this same joint construction programme create real supplier production but cannot also count as new external finance. Independently funded Earth-orbital customers, other settlements, or operating businesses with their own earnings can supply additional purchasing power. Demand, ownership, imported equipment, transport and installation costs determine how much reaches the lunar project. A lunar royalty redistributes mining income within the Moon; it cannot be added to the mining surplus a second time.

The numerical screen therefore uses a **synthetic raw-feed envelope**, rather than an estimate of lunar reserves: 10,000 Gt in the accounting pool, of which 4,000 Gt is reserved for future use, 3,000 Gt protected and 2,000 Gt withheld for uncertainty. The resulting 1,000 Gt export limit is also subject to an assumed 1,000 Gt of customer demand, 10 Gt/year processing over 100 years, and an illustrative **1 Mt total phosphorus-export allowance**. None of these reservation sizes or the nutrient allowance is established as sufficient or safe. Actual permission requires element-by-element and site-by-site inventories.

The phosphate calculation reads the existing [sea-phosphorus product](../../../resources/results/sea_phosphorus.json): its representative mare-soil grade is **0.000567 kg P/kg**, derived from 0.13 wt% P₂O₅. Highland and KREEP-rich soil grades are separate sensitivities. These are soil-composition references, ahead of measured export-feed grades and recoverable ore reserves, and the landing-site compositions behind them illustrate heterogeneity across the Moon. [Lunar Sourcebook, chapter 7, table 7.15](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/publications/books/lunar_sourcebook/pdf/Chapter07.pdf).

An ideal separation retains a chosen fraction of the feed's phosphorus on the Moon in usable or recoverable stock. Exported product mass is reduced by the retained phosphorus mass, and the phosphorus loss budget constrains throughput. Other retained components, process losses and their costs need a fuller beneficiation model. The screen gives no revenue credit to unmodelled co-products. In particular, potassium, trace nutrients and other scarce components impose further restrictions beyond the phosphorus example.

| 99% | 176.16 | 1.00 | 17.616 | 2.634 |
| 99.9% | 999.43 | 0.567 | 99.943 | 14.943 |

These are conditional arithmetic, especially the very high retention cases. The cash column assumes every customer is independently funded and applies the reference 0.1 U/kg to the delivered finished product. Per raw-feed-equivalent kilogram, it subtracts **0.04 U of intermediate operating and delivery/installation purchases, 0.01 U of capital replacement, 0.02 U of other senior cash uses and consumption, 0.01 U of retained business growth investment, and 0.005 U placed in a depletion reserve**. Those hypothetical costs must cover the proposed separation process and service chain; no industrial design establishes that they can. The initial export businesses must fit the capital allowance or receive separately identified funding.

Economic depletion is separately assumed to be 0.005 U per feed-equivalent kilogram and deducted from net product. A depletion charge is not itself money in a reserve: the equal cash set-aside here is an explicit policy assumption. Financial saving also does not restore exported ore or substitute automatically for ecological nutrients. The code subtracts depletion once in NWP and the actual reserve once in cash. [OECD, depletion and net aggregates under the 2025 SNA](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/measuring-natural-resources-in-the-national-accounts_420c7c2a-en/full-report/measuring-natural-resources_a0accc5d.html).

At 99% P retention, the illustrative operation produces **10.57 trillion U of gross value added and 7.92 trillion U of net product over the century**, before any aggregate-model reconciliation, and supplies 2.63 trillion U of project cash if all buyers qualify. A 50% independently funded customer share halves that available cash; a wholly programme-funded sale receives zero additional independent-finance credit. Actual production still occurs in every case, and its location and matching purchases must remain in the accounts.

If that independent cash arrives uniformly in years 21–120 and avoids lunar borrowing at 0.1% real, it reduces year-500 debt by about **4.05 trillion U**. Even the 99.9% retention and full-envelope case avoids about **22.96 trillion U**, or **0.042%** of the reference 54.24-quadrillion-U lunar debt. Under these bulk-material prices, mining can supply useful early capital, while the planetary air-and-water bill remains vastly larger. More valuable manufactured exports require separate production, demand and cost cases; a large rock stock does not establish such revenues.

Mining is a named potential contributor to the 2%, 5% and 10% aggregate cash allocations above. The standalone mining finance comparison shows its possible scale and timing; **it is not added on top of those allocations**. Protecting future stocks, retaining nutrients and shifting commodity feed toward other small bodies as appropriate can preserve the Moon's long-term usefulness while allowing a limited early export industry.

## Reproduction and evidence

`python -m research.studies.world_economy.amenities` writes the extension to ignored runs. Pass `--output research/studies/world_economy/results/amenities.json` to reproduce the committed product. [amenities_inputs.json](amenities_inputs.json) records every activity, visitor, cost, conservation and customer assumption. The output binds the previous comparison, construction costs, population/land product and phosphorus product by schema and hash; the original gross/net-product comparison remains unchanged.

The controls check source identity, six-route budget/capacity limits, territorial trade, asset and debt age, cross-world transfers, matched funding costs, nutrient/feed mass, finite reservations, depletion-versus-cash treatment and numerical reproduction. [checks.json](checks.json) records the completed checks. Physical commissioning, source-industry architecture, market demand, reserve adequacy, extraction and separation performance, transport/accommodation capacity and legal ownership remain unresolved design work.
