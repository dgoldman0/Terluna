# Early infrastructure campaigns and lasting value

A finite industrial campaign could supply a substantial share of the array's structural equipment before the Moon acquires its dense atmosphere. Its strongest products are durable, transportable modules: frames, tank panels, thermal assemblies and shipyard equipment that become much larger systems at their destinations. Such a campaign can earn lunar income and lower construction costs. A large financial contribution requires funded customers, a delivered-cost advantage and enough productive years to recover the plant.

The [calculation](early_campaigns.py), [inputs](early_campaigns_inputs.json) and [results](results/early_campaigns.json) test 108 combinations of capacity, duration, customer budget, cost advantage and advance payment. They extend the [industrial-transition account](industrial_transition.md) without adding a second set of array construction orders. The [mature-industry assessment](mature_industry.md) addresses subsequent production under the 1.2 atm lock. All monetary quantities use the study's common real unit U; none is a current currency quotation.

## What to build during the favourable interval

Low gravity reduces the self-weight of hot, soft or large parts while a fixed working surface provides foundations, access and reaction forces. Low ambient pressure initially reduces the enclosure and gas-removal requirements of vacuum-compatible processes. Lunar feedstock and a short route to nearby orbital customers can help the complete system. These are different mechanisms: an attractive lunar production site need not produce a material that only lunar gravity makes possible. Equally advanced Earth supports and orbital factories, including rotating facilities, remain the comparators.

| Product family | Early campaign and customers | Value retained after atmospheric loading |
|---|---|---|
| Habitat and industrial structural kits | Repeated beams, joints, floor or equipment panels and assembly fixtures for array habitats, depots and yards | Installed buildings, repairable frameworks, standard replacement interfaces and rental capacity |
| Tanks and fluid-handling systems | Nested shell panels, bosses, valves, pipe sections and qualified joints for propellant, industrial gas and water systems | Storage, transfer and distribution services; inventories can be sold separately from the facility |
| Collector and concentrator hardware | Frames, gimbals, support members and selected reflector segments for power and process heat | Operating energy assets and replaceable components; optical surfaces require contamination and figure qualification |
| Thermal and processing modules | Heat exchangers, manifolds, pumps, reactor supports and radiator subassemblies | Continuing heat-management and manufacturing services across the three worlds and transport network |
| Yard and transport equipment | Tool frames, manipulators, capture interfaces and durable machine structures | Construction, inspection, refit and freight services; machinery requires maintenance and eventual renewal |
| Lunar utilities and protected foundations | Dry-site foundations, buried conduits, utility corridors and reusable industrial premises | Avoided later construction and a base for atmospheric factories, subject to eventual flooding, weather and ecosystem plans |

The opportunity is to mass-produce repeatable modules and assemble the enormous final infrastructure near its users. Launching a complete delicate mirror, radiator sheet or habitat imposes acceleration and handling loads that low surface gravity does not remove. Dense or nested shipments preserve much more of the benefit. High-performance cable and optical materials need their own qualification; their mass cannot inherit structural-metal prices or yields merely because they share a factory site.

These product families are proposals, not separately measured markets. The quantitative case below prices only a generic qualified structural package. Thermal, optical and transport systems are possible customers or later extensions, rather than additional sales hidden inside its total. The [source ledger](mature_industry_sources.json) records the experimental limits behind the process candidates.

## How much could be produced?

The finite order allowance is **15 Gt**, 20% of the existing 75 Gt array structural allowance. The separate 4,950 Gt shielding allowance is excluded. Fifteen gigatonnes would be a major structural contribution while accounting for only about 0.3% of total array habitat mass. It is an assumed eligible procurement share, not a signed order or a complete array design.

Raw nameplate capacity is 100, 300 or 1,000 Mt/year. At 70% utilization and 90% qualified yield, normal deliveries are 63, 189 or 630 Mt/year. Output ramps through 25%, 50% and 75% of that normal rate in the first three years, then reaches it in year four. A 25-year campaign therefore has 23.5 full-output-equivalent years. Buyer budgets, remaining orders, power, routes and feed reserves can all reduce actual shipments.

In the largest unconstrained case, the plant delivers **14.805 Gt over 25 years**. Extending the scheduled horizon allows it to finish the 15 Gt order in year 26; the model stops production when the order is served. The same order cannot sustain a further 24 years of nominal production. New customers or mature replacement work would need a separately funded continuation case.

The largest plant processes 700 Mt/year at steady operation. At the assumed 100 MJ/kg of raw processing, that requires **2.22 TW**, within its assumed 5 TW supply allowance. Its 630 Mt/year delivery rate fits an assumed 700 Mt/year route. The inherited ideal ring-delivery energy adds about 54 GW at that shipment rate, before real propulsion, capture, losses and infrastructure. These power and route allowances are scale scenarios, not engineered systems. Refining chemistry, radiators, utilities, commissioning time and component-level equipment remain to be designed.

With 90% local feed and the chosen yield, the reference campaign uses 14.805 Gt of local feed. Its 100 Gt exportable reserve is a synthetic allowance after future-use and conservation reservations, not a geological survey. Bulk abundance does not establish suitable ore grades or permission to consume nutrients and scarce alloying elements. The earlier [resource-retention account](development.md) still applies.

## Sales, capital recovery and financing

The two cost cases assume a 10% or 25% reduction from a 5 U/kg reference production cost, including capital service. Neither reduction follows from the gravity ratio or the pressure calculations. Against a matched competitor delivered at 5.05 U/kg, lunar freight of 0.25 U/kg and equal sharing of the delivered saving give producer prices of 4.65 and 4.275 U/kg respectively. Stronger cost savings permit a lower price while improving the producer's margin.

Initial plant capital is calibrated from a 25% capital-service share and a 40-year life at a 1% real required return. Intermediate inputs, compensation and fixed operations are accounted for separately. The cash calculation charges actual initial capital and any replacement once; it does not also deduct annual capital service as a second cash cost. A campaign extending beyond year 40 buys replacement equipment if still operating. The specified wind-down recovers 30% of remaining straight-line book value and costs 2% of initial capital to decommission. These are chosen terminal scenarios, not compulsory consequences of atmospheric loading.

| Raw capacity | Campaign | Assumed production saving | Qualified output | Initial capital | Final cash after all capital and terminal settlement | Investment NPV at 1% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 Mt/year | 10 years | 25% | 0.536 Gt | 1.939 trillion U | −0.776 trillion U | −0.860 trillion U |
| 100 Mt/year | 25 years | 10% | 1.481 Gt | 2.327 trillion U | −0.246 trillion U | −0.530 trillion U |
| 100 Mt/year | 25 years | 25% | 1.481 Gt | 1.939 trillion U | +0.388 trillion U | +0.076 trillion U |
| 1,000 Mt/year | 25 years | 25% | 14.805 Gt | 19.393 trillion U | +3.876 trillion U | +0.764 trillion U |

The 100 Mt/year rows have a 0.5-trillion-U annual customer budget; the largest has 3.5 trillion U. Its steady delivered purchases reach 2.851 trillion U/year. These budgets are substantial commitments by the buyer, separate from the producer's financing requirement. Scaling both plant and budget multiplies the result where no other constraint binds; it does not demonstrate economies of scale.

For the largest positive case, **63.291 trillion U of producer sales** generates **21.475 trillion U of operating cash before capital**. Deducting the 19.393-trillion-U initial plant leaves 2.082 trillion U. Realized residual-asset recovery of 2.182 trillion U, less 0.388 trillion U decommissioning, gives the reported **3.876 trillion U final cash**. Capital is recovered from cumulative operating cash in year 23, before terminal proceeds. Cash interest, taxes, required equity distributions and unpriced depletion would reduce what can be transferred to lunar development. The 1% discount rate is a required-return assumption, not an offered loan. Holding the same physical programme, prices, initial capital and annual cash flows fixed, discounting at 2% changes NPV to **−1.820 trillion U**; the cash-flow internal rate of return is about **1.277% real**. Thus the positive reference depends on patient capital or better operating and residual values, even before financing contracts are chosen.

No 10-year campaign or 10%-saving case in the tested grid has positive investment NPV. Twenty-four of 108 combinations are positive; the grid is a set of sensitivities, not a probability distribution. Shorter campaigns may become worthwhile with a smaller dedicated plant, leased or reusable equipment, higher-value products, cheaper feed or larger verified savings. Those possibilities require their own costs. Assigning the initial factory for free would conceal the main problem.

Binding customer advances can change the timing substantially. A 20% advance on the largest campaign's producer contract supplies 12.658 trillion U at commissioning, reducing peak funding from **19.393 to 6.735 trillion U**. Subsequent invoice receipts are lower as the advance is earned. Final cash and unlevered investment NPV remain unchanged: the advance is a delivery liability, not extra production or profit. Its practical terms would need pricing, performance assurance and actual buyer liquidity.

## Where the financial benefit persists

Purchases from the joint Moon–array construction programme are useful lunar receipts and can reduce lunar borrowing. They require funding elsewhere in that programme. The default model therefore assigns zero independent-funding share. Earth commercial customers, independently financed array settlements or other Solar System buyers could supply outside income, but the same order must be tied to their budgets before it can support an external-debt claim. Lower delivered construction costs are a separate programme benefit, even when no independent outside income exists.

Ownership determines the continuing return. Outright sales produce cash during manufacture. Retaining shares in completed power systems, depots, habitats or yards can instead earn future service income, after their full operation and renewal costs. A retained asset cannot simultaneously supply the terminal sale proceeds in the table. The [earlier asset and reserve comparison](industrial_transition.md) treats these alternatives explicitly. Installed assets also avoid some storage and specification-obsolescence problems of a large unused stockpile.

The campaign's duration is a competitive-production interval, not a prediction that pristine ambient vacuum lasts 25 years after gas loading starts. [Pressure evolution and pumping](industrial_engineering.md) show why high-vacuum work needs local containment early. Preserving a longer low-pressure interval may require atmospheric sequencing, partial enclosures and export-route adaptation. Any costs of delaying settlement, biology or other construction belong in the programme decision. At maturity, selected lines can continue in pumped chambers, convert to controlled-gas work, or move equipment to orbit; others can retire after completing their orders.

The most useful next investment case is a modular yard supplying one qualified family of structural or thermal assemblies under milestone contracts. It should price lunar foundations and tooling, optimized Earth fabrication, orbital assembly, delivery loads, retrofit or relocation, and an identified post-campaign use. That comparison can establish whether early manufacture creates both a lasting infrastructure stock and cash available for further lunar development.
