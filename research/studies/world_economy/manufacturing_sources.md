# Manufacturing evidence and source access

31 October 2026. Targeted primary-source audit supporting the [partial-gravity investigation](manufacturing.md). The experiments below establish process behaviour under their tested conditions. No examined experiment establishes a commercial lunar-gravity premium over both Earth and microgravity. The companion investigation develops additional physical hypotheses and reproducible calculations.

## Direct evidence and its limits

| Process and primary source | What was actually done | What it supports here |
|---|---|---|
| Regolith laser melting: Reitz et al. (2021), *Additive Manufacturing Under Lunar Gravity and Microgravity*, [doi:10.1007/s12217-021-09878-4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12217-021-09878-4) | Small simulant samples produced at 1 g, 0.16 g and microgravity; geometry, mass, sections and porosity compared. Reported porosities were 49.70%, 46.77% and 53.93%, respectively. The authors conclude there are no major differences among the samples. | Direct three-gravity feasibility evidence, **not** proof that the numerical minimum at 0.16 g is a robust optimum. Strength, manufacturing yield, cost and scalable production were not established. Do not turn a few percentage points of porosity into a sales premium. |
| Regolith milling: MacRobbie et al. (2026), [doi:10.1038/s44453-026-00057-6](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44453-026-00057-6) | Blade, ball and mortar mills tested with JSC-1A in lunar-gravity parabolas and ground controls. Ball and mortar mills reduced particle size, but less effectively at lunar gravity; blade milling showed no significant reduction. Two lunar-gravity samples per device; no microgravity comparison. | A concrete counterexample to easier low-gravity processing. Reduced particle contact and the changed balance of gravity, cohesion and imposed forces require equipment adaptation. It provides no plant-throughput forecast. |
| Regolith hopper flow: Madden et al. (2025), [doi:10.1038/s41526-025-00501-z](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12489093/) | A small JSC-1A hopper emptied smoothly at 1 g, but jammed after initially flowing at approximately 0.19 ± 0.06 g. The experiment combines macro-scale flow observations with particle experiments and DEM work. | Some gravity does not guarantee easy powder handling. Cohesion can become more troublesome as weight falls. This geometry and simulant do not establish that every lunar hopper fails. |
| Gravity-independent powder printing: D'Angelo et al. (2021), [doi:10.1016/j.addma.2021.102349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09815) | A shear/shaking/compression approach was simulated and demonstrated with polymer powders on Earth and in parabolic-flight weightlessness. | Orbital plants need not accept poorly flowing unconfined powder. Engineered handling weakens an argument that a lunar floor uniquely enables additive manufacturing. This is not an industrial metal or regolith production demonstration. |
| Aluminium casting in printed regolith moulds: Sadeghi (2025), [institutional thesis record](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/handle/10589/240498) | Ground experiments produced aluminium castings in printed regolith shell moulds. Lunar gravity was investigated in simulation, yielding slightly longer filling times and broadly comparable predicted quality. | A promising connection between local feedstock and manufacturing. The favourable casting measurements are terrestrial experiments, while the lunar result is modeled feasibility. This master's thesis supplies neither a lunar-gravity quality advantage nor a 0 g comparison. |
| Aluminium solidification: Chiaramonte (1993), [NASA-TM-106190](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19940017131) | Radiography during approximately 16 seconds at ±0.02 g and ground tests showed that void placement depends strongly on wetting, capillary motion and container geometry. | Reduced gravity changes defects; it does not automatically remove them. This near-zero-g experiment identifies mechanisms rather than a lunar optimum. |
| Metal foams: García-Moreno et al. (2009), [doi:10.1016/j.colsurfa.2009.03.010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927775709001253) | In-situ X-ray observations during parabolic flight showed drainage at higher gravity and re-entry of liquid into foam in microgravity, restoring more uniform density and pore size. | Suppressing drainage can improve uniformity; this points towards microgravity as a serious competitor. No lunar-gravity optimum or saleable performance premium was established. Chemistry, coalescence and solidification still matter. |
| Dispensed polymer lenses: Fütterer et al. (2024), [doi:10.1115/1.4066697](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4066697) | Lenses dispensed and UV-cured at 1 g and microgravity showed modest shape differences. Lunar and Martian tests were future work; optical characteristics required further measurement. | Gravity is a process parameter. The study does not show a better lunar lens, demonstrated optical-performance premium or best gravity at 0.16 g. |
| Printed silver electronics: 2025 study, [doi:10.1038/s44334-025-00054-9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12488487/) | Silver nanoparticle lines were laser-sintered during twelve lunar-gravity and eight microgravity parabolas and on the ground. Reduced gravity changed coalescence and heat transport. Conductivity remained broadly in the same range; combined flight averages were 27–53% below ground values at the tested settings. | Manufacturing feasibility and a need to retune thermal processing. More melting does not itself demonstrate higher conductivity, commercial quality or a lunar advantage over microgravity. |

Access: publisher full-text sections were read for Reitz and MacRobbie; full article passages through PMC/search retrieval for Madden and the silver study; author abstract for D'Angelo; institutional thesis abstract for Sadeghi; NASA report abstract for Chiaramonte; publisher abstract and exposed experimental/conclusion passages for foams; publisher indexed full-text passages for lenses. Direct publisher opens for the lens and two Nature pages initially failed; the stated passages were obtained through indexed retrieval or PMC. No figure/image inspection or full thesis/report reading is claimed.
