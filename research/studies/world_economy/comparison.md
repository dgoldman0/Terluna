# Three connected world economies

The comparison sets a completed Open Moon and the inhabited array beside an equally advanced Earth, all three at the accepted population and with one mature technology. **Earth:Moon:array gross product is 100:93:56 in the reference production case**: the population split of 9, 8 and 5 billion times the assumed output per resident, 1.049 of Earth's on the Moon and 1 in the array. After ordinary depreciation and the separately priced engineered assets, **net product before unpriced local depletion is 100:91:53**. Construction financing moves resources between the worlds: the patient-debt example leaves the Moon and the array **100:75:48 of Earth's resources after replacement and external debt service**, counting the corresponding receipts on Earth.

The physical inventories constrain the construction model, and judgement supplies future prices, realised sector value added, asset lives and financing arrangements; the reference case is one illustrative combination of them. The calculation describes the settled state at the selected populations and technology, and the path to it is a separate question.

## Production and net product

Earth has 9 billion residents, the Moon 8 billion and the array 5 billion, all with the same mature technology; the colonies' 3 billion, and the source economies that send the air and water, form a fourth account outside these totals. Equal production per resident would give Moon/Earth 0.889 and array/Earth 0.556, and the Moon needs 12.5% more output per resident to match Earth's total. Lower gravity, distinctive habitation, ecology and proximity to orbital services support particular comparative advantages, each entered sector by sector.

The three production cases use eight sector assumptions for the Moon and an aggregate realised-output assumption for the array, 0.75, 1 or 1.5 times Earth's output per resident; [productivity.md](productivity.md) gives every sector, mechanism, weight and limitation. The array's output is its realised value added, exports included, at the common unit.

Each column below sets **the corresponding Earth measure to 100**. Net products use the advanced construction-price case, ordinary depreciation of 15% of gross product on each world, and the additional asset lives below. Local natural-resource depletion is unpriced and set to zero in this reference, with separate sensitivities for its size.

The wider construction-cost × production grid gives **0.635–1.114 Earth net products for the Moon and 0.269–0.831 for the array**, the extremes of the selected cases. The cheaper construction case and the stronger production case vary independently. Varying ordinary depreciation together on all worlds largely cancels in relative comparisons; the portfolio cases below test different capital mixes.

For scale, the reference takes future Earth's gross product as **100,000 U per resident per year**, or **900 trillion U annually**. U is one common transferable real unit for goods and financial claims, an accounting numeraire for future prices. Net product is gross product less depreciation and depletion. The ordinary percentage covers a disjoint portfolio of housing, productive equipment, food systems, ordinary utilities, computing and intangibles, and the separately priced assets sit outside it.

## What construction requires

[construction.md](construction.md) prices initial environmental delivery and the initial or end-state installed assets, separating source extraction, gas chemistry, departure, capture, placement, ordinary productive capital and the additional structures. The middle inventory takes an illustrative 1.5 × 10¹⁹ kg of crust water from within the existing bracket, ahead of a groundwater choice.

The central bill is about **83.67 quadrillion U attributed to the Moon and 3.33 quadrillion U to the array**, at an assumed 0.1 U/MWh for the source sites' energy: together **96.66 years of reference Earth gross product**, or 19.33% of that annual product spread over 500 years. Earth's corresponding ordinary capital stock, 4.5 quadrillion U, is the comparator.

| Source-industry and construction case | Middle-inventory construction, Earth-output years | Mean annual build / Earth output over 500 years |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patient Automation | 11.19 | 2.24% |
| Advanced Industrial | 96.66 | 19.33% |
| Expensive Energy Dilute Ore | 2963.53 | 592.71% |

The source-industry assumptions dominate this range. The reference imports alone cost 78.79 quadrillion U, 41.90 quadrillion of it departure propulsion with its transport equipment and service, and recoverable nitrogen at 1% of the source feed needs about 2.53 × 10²⁰ kg of processed material. Purchased process power averages about 99 PW through the build, 82% of it departure propulsion and nearly a thousand times the mature Moon's transmitted power. The resources domain's braking at the hubs, 20–340 PW across its delivery range ([industry](../../../resources/industry.md)), brings delivery and braking together to 120–440 PW (140–240 PW for this inventory's own 2.0 × 10⁹ kg/s), against the 19 September civilization-scale envelope of 501 PW. At 0.1 U/MWh the source sites need extremely cheap generation, and sunlight at 40 AU is 1/1,600 of its strength at the Earth. The model charges a catcher and control system for braking, with bulk capture still to design, and gives the incoming kinetic energy neither a purchase price nor a sales credit. The production behind these imports, about 158 trillion U a year over the build, happens in the source economies, a fourth account beside the three.

The larger water inventory raises the advanced-case total to 196.13 Earth-output years, and surface water alone lowers it to 62.98. The expensive, dilute-source case takes several Earth products a year even over 500 years, beyond a modest build budget; building it would need a different scale, timescale, technology or a wider sponsoring economy.

**Renewal during construction is additional real activity.** An illustrative linear buildout would need about 35.33 quadrillion U of lunar and 23.14 quadrillion U of array replacement spending over the 500 years at the reference mature depreciation. Early revenues, ordinary operating budgets and sponsors can pay it as it falls due, and a plan that leaves it unfunded opens a financing gap or lets assets deteriorate. Early source and orbital activity can earn revenue while the lunar economy grows through the build, so a resolved construction and settlement timeline is still needed.

## Financing and resident resources

Interest and foreign profits move income between worlds, and principal repayments change financial claims and cash resources; territorial gross and net product stay as they are in this accounting. Whoever holds a claim on Earth receives the counterpart of the Moon's or the array's payment, so a borrower-to-Earth resource ratio changes at both ends. [annual_accounts.md](annual_accounts.md) gives the equations, classifications and balance-of-payments conditions. Who lends, who owns and who bears losses are arrangements the communities choose through their collective decisions ([register](../../decisions.md#automation-the-network-and-food-12-october-2026)), and these cases trace the consequences of one family of them, borrowing and repayment.

The patient, staged and current-interest cases borrow 50% of construction expenditure, and the fully deferred stress case all of it; the retired baseline leaves the historical funding open. Half the debt is held on Earth at full population and half on the Moon, and the rest of construction spending is met as it occurs by sponsors or direct contributions. Domestic consolidation keeps contracts and unequal household obligations in view, and creditor residence at maturity leaves open where the early funding came from.

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|

| Patient | 0.1% real, 500-year build interest capitalized; 500-year repayment | 75.2 | 48.4 | 8.22% |
| Staged | 0.5% real; interest capitalized within 50-year tranches, then each repaid over 250 years | 72.2 | 47.5 | 9.91% |
| Current Interest | 0.5% real; construction interest paid currently, 500-year repayment | 66.1 | 45.6 | 13.58% |

| Staged Retired | Staged case observed at year 750, after every loan retires | 91.2 | 53.3 | 0.00% |

Here Earth's resources after principal are 100 within each row. The resource pool is the study's own: replacement investment equals depreciation, with no new borrowing or asset sales, no unlisted transfers or investment, and a reserved depletion allowance where one is specified. It includes the resources used for household and collective consumption.

Interest alone changes the patient-case net resident-income ratio to about **100:84.6:51.2**, and principal repayments then give the lower cash-resource ratios in the table. Principal receipts turn existing financial assets into spendable resources, with no new Earth production behind them.

In the patient case the Moon pays about **68.95 trillion U/year externally** and the array **2.74 trillion**. Earth's resources rise from 765 to 836.70 trillion U, while the Moon keeps 628.94 and the array 404.69 trillion. The Moon must earn net exports of about 8.22% of its gross product and the array about 0.55% of its own, and Earth's matching net-import capacity is about 7.97% of its gross product; whether customers buy those exports at these prices is the market question this balance poses.

The staged case has much less outstanding debt at full population, after about **100.03 quadrillion U of lunar funding payments** and **3.98 quadrillion of array payments**, direct construction contributions and loan service together. By year 750 the modelled loans have retired, after still more payments. A debt-free mature world is thus silent on whether its historical investment was inexpensive or preferable to alternatives.

Full deferral fails sharply. At 1% real interest, equal annual borrowing over 500 years leaves 28.755 times the financed construction expenditure outstanding: the reference Moon's external payment requirement exceeds fourteen times its gross product, and the array also exhausts its net resources. The corresponding Earth receipts are **claims beyond what the modelled resources can pay**, and a default, restructuring or transfer would need its own scenario.

## Continuing operations and trade

Routine producer materials, power and purchased services are already removed in gross value added, so they stay out of any blanket deduction from gross or net product. The calculation sets out several funding quantities that overlap the accounts in stated ways:

| Physical annual requirement | Reference price | Annual bill, trillion U | Accounting relationship |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Lunar delivered electricity, 22.12–105.3 TW | 5 U/MWh | 0.970–4.615 | Mixed intermediate and final uses: producer purchases enter GVA; household purchases use final consumption resources. Neither warrants another GDP deduction |
| Remaking lifting hydrogen | Same delivered price | 0.075–0.777 | Subset of that electricity, not an additional power bill |
| Shield film turnover, 2.31–4.64 Gt/year | 5 U/kg fabricated film | 11.54–23.19 | Overlaps the optical replacement/depreciation proxy; never charge both |
| Food phosphorus losses, 0.352–1.440 Mt/year | 10 U/kg replenished P | 0.0035–0.0144 | Material bill only; collection, sanitation and processing infrastructure remain separate |

These assumed prices set the scale of the bills, with market contracts still to set the prices. Available sunlight leaves the source sites' electricity price open, and an elemental phosphorus price covers the material, with the complete nutrient-return system priced separately. The optional collective-service budget allocates 2% of Earth's gross product and 3% each on the Moon and in the array to final environmental and safety operations, excluding capital and commercial intermediate inputs, with half and double variants; these allocations are choices of collective spending, kept apart from conventional net product.

The array's power, computing, materials and protective services enter its own production account, and lunar and Earth purchases connect the accounts through trade. A separate ownership example transfers an assumed 10% of array net product to lunar residents as profits, changing resident income while array production stays in the array; it allocates income, with no profit rate or asset value inferred, and the array's output carries no further export bonus.

## Amenities, travel and early earnings

The [hybrid-amenity extension](amenities.md) treats the 4.68-million-km² park and garden programme as part of ordinary resident life and models all six interworld travel directions. In its connected case lunar interworld tourism accounts for 24.23 trillion U of direct value added and local amenities for 35.25 trillion U, within the existing sector totals, and reciprocal travel and imported inputs limit net export earnings, so even the stronger lunar-destination cases leave part of the original financing requirement to other exports. These balances rest on travel flows set by each world's spending, 3.58 billion arrivals a year on the Moon in the connected case and 6–8.5 billion in the strong ones; the transport system, the arrival ports and the register's transport rules are still to size them. Free-entry public garden services enter measured production, and the enjoyment beyond them lies outside the measure.

The [development extension](development.md) tests independent earnings before full population, including early orbital services and reserve-constrained lunar mining. At 0.1% real, a phased 5% contribution from realised output retires the array's construction debt by year 206 and reduces the lunar debt by 8.5%, or 17.2% if the array's later surplus funds lunar construction; later opening and premature asset installation worsen the result. These cash-flow scenarios assume their customers, commissioning and saving, and leave the production ratios above as they are. Finite mining reserves, nutrient retention and the buyers' matching expenditure keep gross mineral sales from counting as new finance.

The [enclosed-community business screen](early_exports.md) estimates visitor and factory accounts at 100,000 to 100 million lunar residents. Reference annual development contributions rise from 0.115 to 185.0 billion U after specified facility costs, replacement, own loans and an additional shared-community allowance, 0.084 to 154.4 billion U of it from tourism. These businesses need 20.5 billion to 8.5 trillion U of initial facility capital, half from sponsors or equity, and the weak-demand cases need continuing support. The screen counts facility cash, short of a complete town budget, and its capital has still to be reconciled with the ordinary-capital allowance.

The [manufacturing investigation](manufacturing.md) identifies physically plausible intermediate-gravity processes, including a calculated bubble-removal and particle-settling window and lower-speed spin casting, as product-development hypotheses. A rotating array factory can reproduce partial gravity, so delivered cost, process controls and local feedstock decide whether lunar production wins orders.

The [industrial-transition account](industrial_transition.md) extends the manufacturing comparison to sealed and ambient-vacuum production. In a 50-year liquidation stress case, 100 million tonnes/year of gross nameplate capacity produces 63.7 million saleable tonnes/year. With a 10% assumed production-cost advantage, annual factory receipts are 485.7 billion U and assigned operating cash is 7.99 billion U, but investment NPV at 1% real remains **−0.589 trillion U** under the stated shutdown and salvage assumptions. Programme-funded array purchases dominate; independent receipts support only 0.122 billion U of that annual allocation under the default sales priority. Reuse, monetized terminal recovery and independently funded buyers change the reading.

Continued operation to year 100 gives +0.634 trillion U of investment NPV before transition costs, and an illustrative 0.5-trillion-U retrofit at year 50 plus 10 billion U a year of extra costs leaves +0.091 trillion U, with prices and orders retained. Loss of ambient process vacuum calls for an upgrade assessment, and the factory can continue. These cases stand in for the earlier generic manufacturing cases, with output counted once.

The same account compares durable reserves with future supply. Fifty years of unrecovered shield feed requires roughly 0.117–2.349 Gt with the stated attrition and recycling assumptions; complete replacement-film output is much larger. Orbital stocks and operating assets can preserve early production after surface vacuum is lost, while acquisition, storage, obsolescence, ownership and replacement determine its economic value. Stored manufacture is counted once; later distributions from lunar-owned orbital assets remain income from array production.

The [mature-industry extension](mature_industry.md) prioritizes domestic construction, aerial infrastructure, selected shape-sensitive fabrication and physical qualification under the 1.2 atm surface pressure. Artificial-vacuum factories keep a case where those benefits pay for pressure, containment and delivery, with orbital partial gravity as the competitor. A conditional 1% share of existing aerial-carrier replacement selects 35 Mt a year and 105 billion U a year of customer spending, an allocation within replacement already in the accounts.

The [early infrastructure campaigns](early_campaigns.md) test a finite 15 Gt subset of array structural orders. With an assumed 25% whole-production cost saving, the largest 25-year campaign delivers 14.805 Gt against 19.393 trillion U of initial capital, leaving 3.876 trillion U after all capital and realized terminal settlement; investment NPV is +0.764 trillion U at 1%, or −1.820 trillion U when the same cash flows are discounted at 2%. Cumulative operating cash recovers initial capital in year 23. A 20% customer advance reduces peak funding to 6.735 trillion U without changing output, final cash or unlevered NPV. All buyers are programme-funded by default, so independent outside income needs other funded customers, and every tested 10-year or 10%-saving case has an NPV at or below zero. Continuing operation or conversion after a campaign is assessed separately.

The [slow-settling study](slow_settling.md) broadens the industrial opportunity to constrained filled materials, fragile suspensions and suitable particle reactors. At a hypothetical 5% mixing-electricity share, the central extrapolation saves 4.56% of production cost before added duties. Pricing the capacity needed for a slower passive harvest leaves 3.12% savings in a long-batch example and a 17.03% cost increase in a short one. Quality gains are assumptions shared with equivalent rotating-array processes, and the study assigns them no lunar orders, revenue or GWP.

The [pressure-benefits study](pressure_advantages.md) identifies recurring infrastructure gains from the 1.2 atm atmosphere alongside its vacuum penalties. A matched-gas comparison gives 20% more gross buoyant capacity per volume and 30.6% less fan power for a fixed-heat, resistance-dominated air circuit; if that fan electricity is 5% of total cost, the latter saves 1.53% overall before other differences. Liquid systems gain 20.3 kPa of pressure margin. Vacuum-assisted tools can obtain more clamping force or meet the same force with a less demanding vacuum. These are physical and conditional cost screens beside the existing atmosphere costs and world-product totals.

The [industrial-combustion assessment](industrial_combustion.md) develops the atmosphere's material-specific nitrogen-dilution opportunity. Qualifying industrial assemblies at 1.2 atm and lunar gravity needs tests beyond the existing fire calculations, and the oxygen-feed, methane-exhaust and nitrogen-purge balances identify process costs and possible savings within the economic totals, with no general fire-loss reduction assigned.

The [distinctive-activities assessment](distinctive_activities.md) identifies a broader advantage in large, continuous spaces combining low gravity, breathable air, water and living landscapes. Flight, physical performance, aquatic facilities, aerial gardens and large testing campuses can serve residents and outside customers. Rotating array facilities can reproduce partial gravity, with scale and motion affecting the comparison. These activities develop the existing sector possibilities, counted within the amenity and sector output above.

The [early sport, media and project-finance scenario](sports_finance.md) examines one community before the larger visitor milestones. Remote rights and sponsorship can scale apart from arrivals, on demand and cost assumptions still to be tested, and its venue debt is project finance inside the community's own account. The separate [monetary-system study](../monetary_system/README.md) compares currency arrangements, banking, collective finance and external payments, keeping the construction and product estimates here.

## What changes the conclusion

The main sensitivities are different in kind. Source-site technology, nitrogen grade and water inventory determine construction expenditure. Realised sector activity and demand determine gross product. Ordinary capital composition and the prices/lives of engineered assets determine net product. Ownership, debt age, rates and the share financed determine what residents retain.

Holding physical prices fixed, the patient case gives Moon/Earth after-principal ratios of **0.60, 0.75 and 0.86** when future Earth annual gross product per resident is 50,000, 100,000 or 250,000 U. A richer economy carries the same fixed construction bill more easily, so the income level matters beyond its unit. Halving the special assets' lives lowers the reference net-product ratios to roughly **0.89 Moon and 0.51 array**; doubling them raises those ratios to about **0.92 and 0.54**.

Ordinary portfolios can matter more. The model also compares Earth/Moon/array depreciation shares of 10%/20%/25% and 20%/12%/15%, keeping technology common while allowing different capital mixes. These are deliberately broad scenarios for an unknown future mix. Additional cases vary local depletion, the externally held debt share, the fraction of construction borrowed, Earth liabilities to the array, and sector composition. Their values and exact inputs are in the result product.

Aerophytes can provide useful crops, materials and inhabited area, and their value enters through dependable services, substitution, demand and prices, with their biomass counted only through those. Free ecological abundance and a better daily life have substantial value outside measured product. A completed three-world production system could be economically large while its construction stays a demanding long-term choice, which a favourable mature product ratio alone cannot settle.

## Reproduction and evidence

[results/comparison.json](results/comparison.json) binds the model, scenario files, shared constants and eleven physical input products. [comparison_inputs.json](comparison_inputs.json), [construction_inputs.json](construction_inputs.json), [productivity_inputs.json](productivity_inputs.json) and [annual_inputs.json](annual_inputs.json) separate their assumptions. `python -m research.studies.world_economy.compare` writes to ignored runs; pass `--output research/studies/world_economy/results/comparison.json` to regenerate the committed result.

The study's tests check accounting and debt conservation, source identity, reproduction, territory, inclusion rules and sensitivity effects, and independent reviews checked the economic definitions, physical-cost boundaries and stored-result arithmetic; [checks.json](checks.json) records them. The industrial architectures, future markets and institutional arrangements are proposals these checks leave for testing.
