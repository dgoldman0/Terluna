# Three connected world economies

The present screen puts a completed Open Moon in the same economic order as an equally advanced Earth, with the array making a substantial separate contribution. In the reference production case, **Earth:Moon:array gross product is 100:93:56**. After ordinary depreciation and the separately priced engineered assets, **net product before unpriced local depletion is 100:91:53**. Construction financing can reduce the income and resources retained by lunar residents much further: the patient-debt example gives **100:75:48 in resources after replacement and external debt service**, including the corresponding receipts on Earth.

These are conditional estimates built from explicit assumptions. Physical inventories constrain the construction model; judgement supplies future prices, realised sector value added, asset lives and financing institutions. The reference is an illustration, not a statistically most likely outcome. The calculation assumes successful habitation at the selected populations and technology. It does not estimate the probability of reaching that state.

## Production and net product

Earth has 9 billion residents, the Moon 8 billion and the array 5 billion. The remaining 3 billion residents assigned to other colonies are outside these totals. All three receive the same mature technology. Equal production per resident would yield Moon/Earth 0.889 and array/Earth 0.556; the Moon would need a 12.5% per-resident advantage to match Earth's total. Lower gravity, distinctive habitation, ecology and proximity to orbital services support candidate comparative advantages. They do not give a universal productivity multiplier.

The three production cases use eight sector assumptions for the Moon and an aggregate realised-output assumption for the array. [productivity.md](productivity.md) gives every sector, mechanism, weight and limitation. Array output is 0.75, 1 or 1.5 Earth output per resident in these cases. It is not sunlight or theoretical computation multiplied by today's prices; exports are already part of the assumed realised output.

Each column below sets **the corresponding Earth measure to 100**. Net products use the advanced construction-price case, ordinary depreciation of 15% of gross product on each world, and the additional asset lives below. Local natural-resource depletion is unpriced and set to zero in this reference; that is an explicit limitation, with separate sensitivities rather than a claim of zero extraction.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Constrained Moon | 73.6 | 41.7 | 71.5 | 39.4 | 115.2 | 110.9 |
| Close parity | 93.2 | 55.6 | 91.2 | 53.3 | 148.8 | 144.5 |
| Realised specialisation | 111.7 | 83.3 | 109.7 | 81.0 | 195.1 | 190.8 |

The wider independent construction-cost × production grid gives **0.635–1.114 Earth net products for the Moon and 0.269–0.831 for the array**. These are the extrema of selected cases, not confidence limits or physical bounds. The cheaper construction case does not automatically coincide with the stronger production case. Varying ordinary depreciation together on all worlds largely cancels in relative comparisons by construction; the separate portfolio cases below test a different question.

For scale, the reference assumes future Earth gross product of **100,000 U per resident per year**, or **900 trillion U annually**. U is one common transferable real unit used for both goods and financial claims. It is not a prediction of future dollars, exchange rates or a measured future PPP. Net product is gross product less depreciation and depletion; it is not corporate profit or a government surplus. The ordinary percentage covers a disjoint portfolio of housing, productive equipment, food systems, ordinary utilities, computing and intangibles. The separately priced assets are excluded from that percentage.

The current 301 K redesign has not been propagated through every inherited power, food, hydrological and structural input.

## What construction requires

[construction.md](construction.md) prices initial environmental delivery and the initial or end-state installed assets. It separates source extraction, gas chemistry, departure, capture, placement, ordinary productive capital and the additional structures. The middle inventory includes an illustrative 1.5 × 10¹⁹ kg of crust water, within the existing bracket; it is not a selected groundwater inventory.

The central bill is about **83.67 quadrillion U attributed to the Moon and 3.33 quadrillion U to the array**. Together that is **96.66 years of reference Earth gross product**, or 19.33% of that annual product spread over 500 years. Earth's corresponding ordinary capital stock is 4.5 quadrillion U; it is a comparator, not an additional part of the lunar/array construction bill.

| Source-industry and construction case | Middle-inventory construction, Earth-output years | Mean annual build / Earth output over 500 years |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patient Automation | 11.19 | 2.24% |
| Advanced Industrial | 96.66 | 19.33% |
| Expensive Energy Dilute Ore | 2963.53 | 592.71% |

The source-industry assumptions dominate this range. The reference imports alone cost 78.79 quadrillion U, including 41.90 quadrillion for departure propulsion and transport equipment/service. Recoverable nitrogen is assumed to be 1% of source feed, requiring about 2.53 × 10²⁰ kg of processed material. Purchased process power averages about 99 PW during the build, far above mature lunar living requirements. Its assumed price, 0.1 U/MWh, demands extremely inexpensive source-site generation. The model charges a hypothetical catcher and control system for braking; bulk capture remains unresolved. Incoming kinetic energy is not purchased electricity and receives no assumed sales credit.

The larger water inventory raises the advanced-case total to 196.13 Earth-output years; surface water alone lowers it to 62.98. The expensive/dilute-source case requires several Earth products annually even over 500 years. It therefore fails a modest-build-budget interpretation. Building that case would require a different scale, timescale, technology or wider sponsoring economy.

**Renewal during construction is additional real activity.** A purely illustrative linear buildout would require about 35.33 quadrillion U of lunar and 23.14 quadrillion U of array replacement spending during those 500 years, using the reference mature depreciation. These amounts are not added automatically to debt: early revenues, ordinary operating budgets and sponsors may pay them as incurred. Conversely, a plan that omits their funding leaves a financing gap or permits assets to deteriorate. Early source and orbital activity can produce revenue; a mature eight-billion-person lunar economy cannot be assumed to exist throughout the build. A resolved construction and settlement timeline remains required.

## Financing and resident resources

Interest and foreign profits move income between worlds. Principal repayments change financial claims and cash resources. Neither changes territorial gross or net product in this accounting screen. An Earth creditor receives the counterpart of the Moon's or array's payment. Consequently, a borrower/Earth resource ratio changes at both ends. [annual_accounts.md](annual_accounts.md) gives the equations, classifications and balance-of-payments conditions.

The patient, staged and current-interest cases borrow 50% of construction expenditure; the fully deferred stress case borrows all of it. The retired baseline leaves historical funding unspecified. Half the debt is held by Earth residents at full population and half domestically. The rest of construction spending is funded contemporaneously by sponsors or direct contributions. Domestic consolidation does not cancel contracts or unequal household obligations. Creditor residence at maturity does not establish where early funding originally came from.

| Financing case | Terms | Moon / Earth resources after principal | Array / Earth resources after principal | Moon required net exports / its gross product |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Legacy Retired | Legacy liabilities already settled; funding history unspecified | 91.2 | 53.3 | 0.00% |
| Patient | 0.1% real, 500-year build interest capitalized; 500-year repayment | 75.2 | 48.4 | 8.22% |
| Staged | 0.5% real; interest capitalized within 50-year tranches, then each repaid over 250 years | 72.2 | 47.5 | 9.91% |
| Current Interest | 0.5% real; construction interest paid currently, 500-year repayment | 66.1 | 45.6 | 13.58% |
| Deferred Stress | 1% real; all cost and interest deferred over the 500-year build | Fails | Fails | 1443.34% |
| Staged Retired | Staged case observed at year 750, after every loan retires | 91.2 | 53.3 | 0.00% |

Here Earth resources after principal are set to 100 within each row. This is a study-defined resource pool assuming replacement investment equals depreciation, no new borrowing or asset sales, no unlisted transfers or investment, and a reserved depletion allowance where one is specified. It is not a liquid public treasury and still includes resources used for household and collective consumption.

Interest alone changes the patient-case net resident-income ratio to approximately **100:84.6:51.2**; principal repayments then produce the lower cash-resource ratios in the table. Principal receipts convert existing financial assets into spendable resources rather than creating new Earth production.

In the patient case, the Moon pays about **68.95 trillion U/year externally** and the array **2.74 trillion**. Earth resources rise from 765 to 836.70 trillion U; the Moon retains 628.94 and the array 404.69 trillion. The Moon must generate net exports of about 8.22% of its gross product, with the array supplying about 0.55% of its own. The corresponding Earth net-import capacity is about 7.97% of its gross product. The calculation establishes this required balance; it does not establish that customers will buy the necessary exports at those prices.

The staged case has much less outstanding debt at full population, but has already required about **100.03 quadrillion U of lunar funding payments** and **3.98 quadrillion of array payments**, including direct construction contributions and loan service. At year 750 the modelled loans have retired, after still more payments. A debt-free mature world therefore says nothing by itself about whether its historical investment was inexpensive or preferable to alternatives.

Full deferral fails sharply. At 1% real interest, equal annual borrowing over 500 years leaves 28.755 times the financed construction expenditure outstanding. The reference Moon's external payment requirement exceeds fourteen times its gross product, and the array also exhausts its net resources. The huge corresponding Earth receipts are **claims that cannot all be realized under the modelled resource constraints**. A default, restructuring or transfer policy would need its own scenario. These figures are not achievable Earth income.

## Continuing operations and trade

Routine producer materials, power and purchased services are already removed in gross value added. They cannot be charged again as a blanket deduction from gross or net product. The calculation nevertheless exposes several nonadditive funding quantities:

| Physical annual requirement | Reference price | Annual bill, trillion U | Accounting relationship |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Lunar delivered electricity, 22.12–105.3 TW | 5 U/MWh | 0.970–4.615 | Mixed intermediate and final uses: producer purchases enter GVA; household purchases use final consumption resources. Neither warrants another GDP deduction |
| Remaking lifting hydrogen | Same delivered price | 0.075–0.777 | Subset of that electricity, not an additional power bill |
| Shield film turnover, 2.31–4.64 Gt/year | 5 U/kg fabricated film | 11.54–23.19 | Overlaps the optical replacement/depreciation proxy; never charge both |
| Food phosphorus losses, 0.352–1.440 Mt/year | 10 U/kg replenished P | 0.0035–0.0144 | Material bill only; collection, sanitation and processing infrastructure remain separate |

These assumed prices do not establish market contracts. In particular, available sunlight does not guarantee low source-site electricity prices, and an elemental phosphorus price does not price a complete nutrient-return system. The optional collective-service budget allocates 2% of Earth gross product and 3% each on Moon and array to final environmental and safety operations, excluding capital and commercial intermediate inputs. Half/double variants are included. These are policy allocations, not engineering estimates of necessary cost, and are kept separate from conventional net product.

The array's power, computing, materials and protective services enter its own production account. Lunar and Earth purchases connect those accounts through trade. A separate ownership example transfers an assumed 10% of array net product to lunar residents as profits, changing resident income without relocating array production. It is an income-allocation example, not an inferred profit rate or asset valuation. No additional export bonus is added on top of array output.

## Amenities, travel and early earnings

The [hybrid-amenity extension](amenities.md) treats the 4.68-million-km² park and garden programme as part of ordinary resident life and explicitly models all six interworld travel directions. In its connected case, lunar interworld tourism accounts for 24.23 trillion U of direct value added and local amenities for 35.25 trillion U, within the existing sector totals. Reciprocal travel and imported inputs limit net export earnings: even the stronger lunar-destination cases leave part of the original financing requirement to other exports. Free-entry public garden services can enter measured production; broader enjoyment remains outside that measure.

The [development extension](development.md) tests independent earnings before full population, including early orbital services and reserve-constrained lunar mining. At 0.1% real, a phased 5% contribution from realised output retires array construction debt by year 206 and reduces lunar debt by 8.5%, or 17.2% if subsequent array surplus funds lunar construction. Later opening and premature asset installation materially worsen the result. These are controlled cash-flow scenarios with assumed customers, commissioning and saving; the original production ratios above are unchanged. Finite mining reserves, nutrient retention and matching buyer expenditure prevent gross mineral sales from becoming an unearned financing bonus.

## What changes the conclusion

The main sensitivities are different in kind. Source-site technology, nitrogen grade and water inventory determine construction expenditure. Realised sector activity and demand determine gross product. Ordinary capital composition and the prices/lives of engineered assets determine net product. Ownership, debt age, rates and the share financed determine what residents retain.

Holding physical prices fixed, the patient case gives Moon/Earth after-principal ratios of **0.60, 0.75 and 0.86** when future Earth annual gross product per resident is 50,000, 100,000 or 250,000 U. This changes the economy's ability to carry a fixed construction bill; it is not merely relabelling currency. Halving the special assets' lives lowers the reference net-product ratios to roughly **0.89 Moon and 0.51 array**; doubling them raises those ratios to about **0.92 and 0.54**.

Ordinary portfolios can matter more. The model also compares Earth/Moon/array depreciation shares of 10%/20%/25% and 20%/12%/15%, keeping technology common while allowing different capital mixes. These are deliberately broad scenarios, with no empirical claim about the future mix. Additional cases vary local depletion, the externally held debt share, the fraction of construction borrowed, Earth liabilities to the array, and sector composition. Their values and exact inputs are in the result product.

Aerophytes may provide useful crops, materials and inhabited area, but their biomass production is not converted into a GDP multiplier. Their contribution depends on dependable services, substitution, demand and prices. Free ecological abundance and better daily life can also have substantial value outside measured product. A completed three-world production system could therefore be economically large while its construction remains a demanding long-term choice; a favourable mature product ratio alone does not settle the construction decision.

## Reproduction and evidence

[results/comparison.json](results/comparison.json) binds the model, scenario files, shared constants and eleven physical input products. [comparison_inputs.json](comparison_inputs.json), [construction_inputs.json](construction_inputs.json), [productivity_inputs.json](productivity_inputs.json) and [annual_inputs.json](annual_inputs.json) separate their assumptions. `python -m research.studies.world_economy.compare` writes to ignored runs; pass `--output research/studies/world_economy/results/comparison.json` to regenerate the committed result.

The study's tests check accounting and debt conservation, source identity, reproduction, territory, inclusion rules and sensitivity effects. Independent reviews checked economic definitions, physical-cost boundaries and stored-result arithmetic. [checks.json](checks.json) records the completed checks and limits. Neither numerical reproduction nor those reviews validate the industrial architectures, future markets or institutional scenarios.
