# Early lunar sport, media and finance

31 October 2026. **Sport and entertainment can lead the visitor economy of one enclosed community, well before mass tourism or an open atmosphere.** A resident group of athletes and performers could produce competitions, demonstrations and continuing programmes for a much larger remote audience, while small visiting cohorts buy preparation and participation. This gives an early settlement an export that can grow without a proportional increase in passenger arrivals or imported attractions.

The development priority is sport first within the early community's recreation and visitor programme. Aquaria and larger destination complexes follow when capital, operating experience and demand justify them. Specific sports, prices, contracts and monetary institutions remain proposals. This study develops that sequence and its financing for a reader familiar with the three-world economy; its small cash account is a conditional illustration, not a business forecast. Earlier mining and infrastructure campaigns remain separate opportunities.

## A community can become a venue before it becomes a mass destination

A functioning lava-tube community can provide substantial usable space. The relevant first venue is a supplied, enclosed section or a habitat within the rock, with access and support shared with the community. The [land-cities study](../land_cities/README.md) already permits liners and independent habitats. Available geological volume does not establish gas tightness, an operating arena or the cost of supplying it. Site selection also matters to the venue's lifetime as later seas and groundwater develop.

The commercial product could combine three experiences: travel to the Moon, competence in an unusual habitat, and a physical activity available there. Travel preparation, habitat emergency competence and sport instruction have different purposes and providers. Present US commercial-space rules already require participant emergency-response training; [14 CFR 460.51](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-III/subchapter-C/part-460/subpart-B/section-460.51) supplies an analogy rather than future lunar law. Training physically supplied on Earth or in the array belongs in those territories' production. A distinct service delivered from the Moon or a licence can create separate lunar receipts; ownership changes income claims without moving the training itself between territories.

Early programmes could use jumping and aerial obstacle courses, controlled flight, acrobatics, races and team games adapted to longer airborne motion. Demonstrations would establish what participants can do; repeatable rules and credible competition would give audiences a reason to return. Athletes, designers and instructors would develop the disciplines together. Calling an activity an extreme sport identifies part of its appeal, while training, operating limits and emergency capability remain parts of the service being sold.

A useful sequence follows operating milestones:

| Stage | Main activity | Development purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Demonstration and first production | Resident performers, equipment trials, recorded programmes and selected invited participants | Establish compelling activities, operating procedures and a production record |
| Repeat events and small visitor cohorts | Competitions, participatory training packages, sponsorship and continuing coverage | Test repeat demand and dependable costs within the available beds, routes and arena time |
| Contracted programme and facility expansion | Renewed rights agreements, additional teams, equipment suppliers and selective refinancing | Fund capacity against a demonstrated service and identifiable repayment source |
| Broader destination economy | Additional venues, gardens, aquatic attractions and larger visitor numbers | Add material-intensive attractions as the community can support them |

The host community and its essential transport must already be funded. Grants, equity, institutional commitments and genuine customer advances could help establish the first programme; operating revenue begins when the programme actually supplies services. No opening date or population trajectory is selected here.

## The larger audience can remain off the Moon

The previous [early-export model](early_exports.md) starts at **100,000 residents**, with 20,000 visitor beds and roughly 261,000 annual arrivals. It includes sport in destination spending, but lacks a separate media business and arena/training capacity. A single smaller community therefore needs its own account.

Sport offers several products that travel electronically: live coverage, documentaries, recorded performances, instructional material, game adaptations and licensed use of competition brands or equipment designs. Remote viewers need neither lunar beds nor passenger seats. They still require a programme worth following, and its production consumes skilled work, equipment, communications and array services. Competing terrestrial sports and synthetic entertainment retain the same advanced technology.

The business structure has a present analogue: **media rights supplied 31.3% and sponsorship 21.7% of Formula 1's 2025 revenue**. This illustrates the importance of remote commercial products; the percentages and margins are not transferred to the Moon. [Liberty Media's results](https://www.libertymedia.com/news/detail/579/liberty-media-corporation-reports-fourth-quarter-and-year)

Ownership determines how much income stays with the settlement. A lunar venue could earn only a location fee while an Earth broadcaster owns most commercial rights, or lunar teams and institutions could retain rights and license distribution. The calculation below assumes the stated rights and sponsorship receipts reach the lunar operator. Distributor subscriptions, merchandise turnover and the same sponsors' advertising budgets are not added again. A sports ecosystem can also support local equipment repair, coaching, accommodation and production work; suppliers' gross sales cannot all be summed as additional value added.

A resident performance cohort provides a useful form of resilience. Passenger cancellations need not stop its programme. A common arena, pressure-system or communications failure could still affect both visitor and media businesses, so electronic exports do not remove shared operational dependencies.

## A small conditional venue account

The example has **100 guest beds**, twenty resident core performers/production staff, and a maximum planned guest-bed occupancy of 50%. Visiting teams and production consume 2,000 bed-nights from that allowance; the resident cohort leases accommodation and support through a separate host-service charge. Fourteen-night public visits then have a bed ceiling of **1,162 arrivals a year**, rounded. Travel capacity, training completions and available arena participant-hours, after professional requirements, impose independent ceilings of 1,500, 1,600 and 3,000 arrivals. None is a qualified operating capacity or a promise of collision-free scheduling.

The reference sells **1,000 visits** at **14,000 U each**, including local accommodation, training and sport. Access fares and off-Moon preparation are outside those receipts. The common real unit **U remains an accounting numeraire**: the earlier benchmark uses 100,000 U of annual future-Earth product per resident; it does not define a currency or today's purchasing power.

| Annual receipt or cash use | Million U | Assumption |
|---|---:|---|
| Visitor packages | 14.00 | 1,000 visits; inherited 1,000 U per destination night |
| Lunar media-rights receipts | 20.00 | Illustratively equivalent to one million paying audience equivalents yielding 20 U each to the rights owner; no audience forecast |
| Separate sponsorship receipts | 5.00 | Contracted sponsor service, separate from the media deal |
| Variable visitor costs | −8.40 | 60% of receipts, including compensation and purchased services |
| Variable media/sponsorship costs | −10.00 | 40% of receipts for incremental production, event work and commercial obligations; excludes the fixed core programme |
| Fixed facility operations | −1.20 | 1% of installed capital |
| Core programme provision | −4.00 | Recurring performance, production, emergency and programme allowance |
| Host-community services | −2.00 | Resident cohort accommodation/support lease and shared services; adequacy unqualified |
| Capital replacement | −4.50 | Visitor assets over forty years and production assets over ten |
| Tax allowance | −1.78 | 20% of positive operating surplus after depreciation; no interest deductions or loss relief |
| **Cash available before debt payments** | **7.12** | Before equity distributions or development transfers |

Installed capital is **120 million U**: an inherited synthetic visitor-facility allowance of one million U per bed, including attractions, plus twenty million U of production capital. The visitor allowance already contains the sports venue; a second arena bill is not added. Extrapolating it from a much larger settlement does not establish a first-community construction price. Five million U of working cash is funded separately. The host settlement, construction-period interest, ramp losses and any underestimated support provision need additional funding.

The tax allowance is a fiscal sensitivity, not an adopted tax system. Annual replacement is set equal to straight-line economic depreciation; actual replacement dates and peak cash requirements remain open. Variable visitor/media costs exclude the fixed core cohort and programme expenses; all cost lines are assumed non-overlapping. These are operator cash accounts: wages are costs here, so the residual is neither GWP nor net value added.

With reference visitor receipts and five million U of sponsorship, operating break-even before debt needs **5.17 million U of rights income**, or about **258,000 audience equivalents** at the assumed 20 U. Paying the reference bond described below needs about **15.06 million U of rights income**, or **753,000 equivalents**. The latter pays scheduled debt at coverage 1.0. Maintaining the 1.5 coverage target requires the reference twenty million U of rights receipts, or one million audience equivalents, with other inputs unchanged. These thresholds help define a commercial test; public attention, cumulative views and unique paying customers are different measures.

## Bonds connect a paying programme to new capital

A venue operator could borrow against its business before a lunar government has a broad tax base. Project-revenue debt, corporate debt and public obligations have different issuers and repayment sources. The distinction between revenue and general-obligation bonds has a useful [SEC explanation](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/bonds-or-fixed-income-products-0); present US law and tax treatment are not assumed for lunar institutions.

Fan interest could attract investors, sponsors and patient capital whose holders value participation in the project. It could also help secure advance commercial commitments. Those channels require separate accounts:

| Receipt | What the issuer owes or supplies |
|---|---|
| Earned ticket, training, rights or sponsorship income | The contracted service; its production and delivery costs belong in operations |
| Advance booking or rights payment | Future access, programming or refunds under the contract; capacity and costs remain committed |
| Grant or patron contribution | Any stated conditions; it is a transfer rather than the venue's sale of output |
| Equity capital | An ownership claim and residual risk/return |
| Bond proceeds | Future interest and principal under the contract |
| Redeemable payment balances | A redemption liability to the holder |

Advance rights and sponsorship payments have a concrete analogue in [Liberty Media's annual filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1560385/000110465926020653/lmca-20251231x10k.htm), which records customer prepayments as deferred revenue until services are supplied. A founding membership that reserves future visits also uses future capacity. It cannot be sold alongside a second full-price claim on the same place.

The example assumes ten-year level repayments at **5% real**, a cash-coverage target of **1.5**, a six-month debt-payment reserve and issuance costs of **2% of principal**. These are sensitivity assumptions, not available terms or a recommended loan. Real payments mean constant-purchasing-power or indexed-equivalent payments; an actual nominal contract would need its own inflation and currency account. The [World Bank's project-finance discussion](https://ppp.worldbank.org/financing/issues-in-project-financed-transactions) explains the relationship between operating cash, coverage, downside testing and reserves.

The reference cash permits **4.75 million U of annual debt service**, supporting **36.65 million U of gross debt**. Of the proceeds, 2.37 million U stays in the debt reserve and 0.73 million U pays issuance costs. **33.55 million U is available for capital and working cash**, leaving **91.45 million U for equity, grants or other explicitly funded sources**. The annual cash after debt is 2.37 million U before equity distributions, growth, reserve replenishment or any contribution to wider lunar development. Positive cash and coverage do not establish an adequate return on the initial capital: construction timing, terminal value, losses and required equity returns remain unpriced.

Gross borrowing is also capped by investor demand and the amount needed for the funded uses. Increasing assumed demand from sixty to two hundred million U leaves the reference principal unchanged because operating coverage binds first. Demand of only ten million U limits the issue to that amount. A lower coupon or longer term can change capacity, but short rights contracts do not establish revenues throughout a long loan. Secondary-market bond price increases supply no new cash to the issuer unless it undertakes a new transaction.

| Independent annual case | Receipts, million U | Cash before debt, million U | Coverage of the original bond |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Reference | 39.00 | 7.12 | 1.50 |
| Visitor arrivals stop; resident media programme continues | 25.00 | 2.64 | 0.56 |
| Rights and sponsorship receipts halve | 26.50 | 1.12 | 0.24 |
| Rights and sponsorship disappear; installed programme retained | 14.00 | −6.10 | −1.29 |
| Common venue shutdown | 0 | −11.70 | −2.46 |
| Larger audience: 100 million U rights, 10 million U sponsorship | 126.26 | 48.64 | 10.25 |

The larger-audience case fills the same bed allowance and demonstrates the different scaling of remote receipts. It prescribes unchanged capacity and variable cost shares; it is not evidence that such an audience exists or that production costs would scale this way. The media-loss cases retain the installed portfolio, whereas a separately designed visitor-only business could avoid some media capital and costs.

The full debt reserve bridges the visitor-interruption case for one year, with roughly 0.27 million U left. In the half-media case, about **1.25 million U remains unfunded after that reserve**, before any use of working cash or additional support. Each stress starts with the original full reserve; they are alternatives, not a sequence that repeatedly refills it for free. Working cash is funded but not drawn in this calculation. Negative coverage signals an operating deficit before debt. A separate doubled-capital case also records the **additional 120 million U of upfront funding** outside the original bond.

These results support a staged financing strategy: establish a programme with risk-bearing capital and genuine customer commitments, then test refinancing or expansion debt against demonstrated costs and enforceable receipts. Fans can contribute to demand for that finance; contract quality, operating history and the allocation of losses determine what the debt can promise.

## Currency can develop alongside the business

The settlement can have a recognizably lunar economy while using an established Earth/array settlement currency. Its early exports create outside receipts with which it can pay for imports and financial obligations. A lunar unit of account, a payment interface, bank deposits and an independently floating currency are different institutional choices.

| Monetary option | Possible early role | Main dependency |
|---|---|---|
| Established settlement currency | Price packages, receive rights fees, pay suppliers and service debt | Access to payment institutions and agreement on contract denomination |
| Redeemable lunar payment balances | Convenient local payments and a common community identity | Clear redemption claims, liquid settlement assets and reliable transfers |
| Bank deposits and lending | Finance payroll, equipment and working capital | Bank capital, credit quality, settlement funding and credible institutions |
| A separate floating lunar currency | Greater monetary autonomy as the domestic economy develops | Public acceptance, fiscal/monetary institutions, exchange markets and allocation of import/debt currency risk |

The [Bank of England's monetary account](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/quarterly-bulletin/2014/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy.pdf) explains that lending can create deposits while payments still create funding and settlement requirements. Creating a local claim does not by itself make outside suppliers accept it or increase available materials. The [BIS discussion of redemption and settlement](https://www.bis.org/publications/bulletin-73-stablecoins-versus-tokenised-deposits-implications-singleness-money.pdf) also distinguishes a payment claim from the assets supporting exchange at par; no token architecture is proposed here.

A simple payment example issues ten million U of redeemable balances against ten million U of liquid settlement assets. Redeeming seven million leaves three million of assets and three million of claims. If six million had instead been invested in an illiquid arena asset, only four million would be immediately available: the same redemption request would leave three million unpaid, even if the arena retained its book value. Reserve principal is a liability-backed funding source, not operating income. Payment fees and any net earnings on reserve assets are separate possible income streams.

An independent currency also changes who bears fluctuations. In a separate toy account, foreign receipts minus foreign costs equal five million U, and net local receipts equal ten million local units. At one U per local unit, cash is fifteen million U; at 0.7 U, it becomes twelve million. A business with foreign receipts and net local costs could move in the other direction. The [BIS firm study](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-758-foreign-currency-borrowing-balance-sheet-shocks-and-real-outcomes) supplies evidence that mismatches matter; these lunar examples are arithmetic, not exchange-rate forecasts. Creditor residence and contract currency remain independent: a lunar resident can own foreign-currency debt, and an Earth investor can accept lunar-currency debt.

The practical early monetary design question is therefore how to make interworld receipts, local payments and import obligations work reliably. Whether to establish a separate lunar currency can remain open while the community develops institutions and a wider range of exports.

## What this adds to the economic picture

An early lunar economy could grow around a recognizable sequence: distinctive physical activity creates a programme; the programme builds an audience and visiting community; contracts support recurring production and selective investment; local firms and financial institutions serve the resulting activity. Sport can help establish a shared identity as well as a revenue source. Its long-term strength would depend on people continuing to care about the competition, performers and place after the first novelty.

This smaller account replaces neither the old visitor milestones nor their financing assumptions. It is an alternative early portfolio that must be reconciled with them before aggregation. The old calculation already includes facility debt, so a new sports bond cannot be added as a second financing benefit. No new GWP, NWP, planetary debt reduction or monetary regime is adopted.
