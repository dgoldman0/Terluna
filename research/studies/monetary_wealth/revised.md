# Money and ownership after improved recovery and refurbishment

The revised industrial calculation changes how much equipment, replacement freight and operating expenditure the enclosed economy requires. This companion carries those changes into annual monetary and ownership accounts while preserving the original saving, purchasing and transfer policies. It covers all nine portfolios under breakthrough demand and both 0.5% and 1% annual real market growth, giving 54 ownership paths from year 0 through year 220. The original [wealth study](README.md) and its frozen result remain the comparison baseline.

All monetary quantities below are **trillions of constant USD2041 purchasing-power equivalents**, unless stated otherwise. U remains the project's general economic unit, potentially one currency or a basket of U-related currencies. The amounts measure reference values of claims and balances; they do not specify nominal currency faces or a U exchange rate.

## Local accumulation under the three ownership arrangements

In the propellant-and-logistics case with **1% annual real market growth**, year-200 local net wealth changes from **0.509 to 0.525** under continued outside ownership, from **1.056 to 0.908** under paid purchases, and from **285.414 to 329.780** under the negotiated transfer. Relative to the prior physical baseline, the transfer case gains 44.367 trillion of local net wealth under the retained accumulation policy. All three allocate the same revised programme payouts.

The paid-purchase result moves in the opposite direction because it uses two different valuations for distinct purposes: the conditional price of future cash rights rises, while the included physical establishment requires fewer cost-valued assets. The purchased stake falls from 1.27% to 1.18%. At a consistent transaction price, buying equity swaps cash for a claim; the lower own-funds mark separately records the purchase premium. This decline in reported book wealth is not an operating cash loss.

## Productive assets, terminal claims and outside returns

In the worked case, year-200 net productive assets plus work in progress change from **38.914 to 33.023** trillion, while cumulative programme payouts increase from **347.655 to 415.568** trillion. The revised establishment contains 30.368 trillion of net service-valued assets and 2.654 trillion under construction. Fewer mining and freight assets support the unchanged commercial orders, so a lower replacement-cost stock can accompany better cash performance. It does not demonstrate lower delivered service or welfare.

The separately recorded year-200 retained-core restoration flow is 0.545 trillion. That flow identifies restored service potential inside the commissioned stock; it is not added to the stock again.

The asset producer values surviving equipment by its expected remaining service and records construction in progress separately. A repaired unit enters the restored productive stock once. The value assigned to the retained core's restored service is identified separately from the new repair inputs; it is not another cash receipt, procurement saving or asset to add above that unit. Repair work in progress includes the priced new inputs rather than the completed unit's entire restored service value. Full original-service restoration, including repeated repair cycles, remains an engineering assumption alongside the proposed recovery coefficients.

Included enterprise own funds equal net productive assets plus construction in progress, programme cash and restricted reserves, less debt. Equity is a claim on that total. Adding the same programme cash or reserve assets again to terminal equity would count them twice. Outstanding owner custody is a separate claim because those funds have already left the enterprise's distributable-cash account.

The conditional whole-enterprise cash-claim quote is 126.672 trillion, versus 54.920 trillion of own funds. With continued outside ownership, marking equity at that quote while retaining only the included assets gives a -71.751 trillion enterprise residual and -71.226 trillion of consolidated residual net wealth. These are alternate valuation residuals, not operating cash deficits.

Distributed-cash net present value includes dated contributions and actual payouts or share-sale receipts. The separately labelled terminal-claim comparison adds the outside owner's remaining equity and outstanding custody at the checkpoint once, discounted at 3%. It assumes a valuation, not a completed asset sale. The finite-horizon share quote, used for paid purchases, discounts the remaining distributions through year 220 and the year-220 own-funds mark. It is neither a perpetual-growth appraisal nor an observed market price.

Commercial intellectual property, brands, software, licences, unallocated service-package capital and complete circulating inventories remain unvalued real assets. Consequently, the alternate cash-claim equity mark can exceed the included enterprise assets and produce a negative enterprise residual. That valuation difference remains visible in the checkpoint accounts; the model does not invent intellectual property to eliminate it. Geological potential likewise receives no financial asset value without qualified rights and a usable asset valuation.

## U balances, reserves and the external position

Under continued outside ownership in the worked case, year-200 U transaction-money value changes from **15.147 to 14.777** trillion. The revised stock comprises 10.332 trillion held locally and 4.445 trillion held outside the zone. Monetary base is 1.478 trillion, and the separate programme reserve is 10.709 trillion.

The largest cumulative U-credit draw across the 18 financial paths remains below one USD2041 equivalent. These primary results therefore do not demonstrate a large loan-created money stock or a new credit-financing benefit. Their money balances chiefly follow retained cash, outside holdings and the stated external-asset closure. The net external position changes from -32.498 trillion under outside ownership to 296.758 trillion under the negotiated transfer, because ownership and retained community payouts change who holds the claims.

The inherited holding rules retain 90% of programme cash, 50% of household savings and 5% of uninvested community funds in U transaction deposits. Outside investors retain 1% of their cumulative cash receipts in U; customers hold seven days of the purchases settled in U. The settlement share rises from 25% to 85% over the first ten operating years. These are unchanged scenarios for payment balances and saving, not calibrated money demand.

Bank settlement reserves, set at 10% of transaction deposits, are reported as monetary base and are not added to nonbank transaction money. The separately funded programme reserve remains an enterprise-owned restricted claim on the reserve institution. Its institutional location does not turn it into an unencumbered community endowment. Domestic credit has a matching loan and deposit; supplier-held U term claims remain financing outside transaction money.

Consolidated local net wealth equals included real assets plus external financial assets minus external liabilities, including outside equity. Domestic claims cancel. The household, community, enterprise, bank and reserve-institution accounts remain separate in every annual row. Outside actors contain the counterparty positions represented here, not a complete balance sheet of Earth investors or customers. Their dated cash returns are reported separately.

The banking account solves outside backing as the residual needed to reconcile the selected holdings. This establishes an accounting identity; it does not independently demonstrate available foreign exchange, a balance-of-payments path or stable currency prices. A 50/30/20 reporting split among three illustrative U-related constituent currencies allocates the same financial claims and money stock without adding another basket-issued currency. The [revised financial analysis](../enclosed_returns/revised.md) treats operating returns, monetary constraints and resource conditions separately.


The [year-200 production and income study](../production_income/README.md) now uses these ownership histories alongside the revised financial and physical products to estimate GDP and GNI distributions. Its household service imputations and accrued foreign earnings are annual accounting entries, distinct from this study's cash saving and balance sheets. The new study preserves actual dilution and purchase shares in its matched ownership comparison and identifies the additional assumptions needed for national accounting.

## Evidence, checks and reproduction

[revised_inputs.json](revised_inputs.json) pins the original ownership settings, retained historical result, revised financial flows, revised physical asset stock and accounting code. The cash and asset products must identify the same physical source, and cases join by explicit baseline identity and the combined intervention. Both the nested physical-case hash and the complete intervention-record hash must also match. Every year must be present; debt-sector balances, repayments and custody changes must reconcile before an ownership path is calculated.

The sector identities and monetary classification retain the [existing source record](sources.json) and accounting kernel. The admitted 2025 System of National Accounts PDF was available locally and its exact identity was verified again; no new monetary standard or ownership policy was substituted. The physical and financial producers own their respective engineering and market assumptions. Resource access, preservation release, deployment chronology and long-run markets remain conditions, rather than probabilities used to weight this wealth account.

The full monetary/wealth suite passed **51 tests**, including 11 revised-consumer tests. These cover source and per-case hash tampering, dated debt and custody reconciliation, unchanged payout allocation, separate terminal equity/custody, and the absence of invented cash receipts from restored service values. A separate historical control recomputed all 54 original ownership paths and all 270 checkpoints with a largest numerical change of exactly zero. The final revised run completed all 54 paths and their annual balance controls; programme receipts, capital calls, cash, reserves, debt and total payouts remain those of the supplied financial export.
