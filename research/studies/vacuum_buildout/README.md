# Completing the enclosed build by year 200

The prescribed slow-start programme can reach the established **6,468,289-person enclosed architectural capacity by elapsed year 200** in a conditional engineering calculation. It requires selectively enlarged extraction, refining, fabrication, power, transport and construction equipment. The new machinery makes all nine inherited export configurations pass the annual base-feed capacity screen. The calculation does not establish financing, passenger migration, qualified mineral reserves or operational reliability.

The mining result is asymmetric. Lunar bulk-material requirements occupy a modest geometric footprint under the stated depth assumption. Near-Earth feed requirements exceed the existing narrow catalogue screen well before completion. Maintaining the reference extraction cost therefore depends on qualifying additional bodies with suitable composition and delivery opportunities. Poorer asteroid feed can exceed the enlarged plant's capacity; a claim of constant affordable supply through year 200 would go beyond the evidence.

The [year-100 and year-200 returns appraisal](returns.md) uses this committed physical schedule and reports the external funding required to complete it. U retains its established meaning as the general economic unit, potentially a currency or basket of U-related currencies, serving the planned off-Earth economic zone. The boundary includes the enclosed lunar build and its current industrial and transport support. Earth counterparties remain financial and socioeconomic stakeholders. The Open Moon and later large-scale programmes supply no assets, costs or receipts here.

## A gradual increase followed by completion

The existing 0.1% crater-area/site-quota programme, together with existing cities, supplies the capacity ceiling through the pinned provisioning economy product. The percentage is not reinterpreted as a population fraction or extraction allocation. A 12,500-person productive module opens in year 8. Occupied capacity stays at that level through year 10; subsequent capacity follows

\[
P(t)=12{,}500\exp\{\log(6{,}468{,}289.238/12{,}500)(3u^2-2u^3)\},
\quad u=\operatorname{clip}((t-10)/190,0,1).
\]

This assumed occupancy path makes both endpoint growth rates zero. Annual construction orders are the increase in capacity due two years later, so machinery and construction throughput precede the resulting residents. Growth orders end in year 198; commissioning completes in year 200. Year 201 checks continuing operation and replacement at the same cap, with no further expansion.

Orders peak at **5.313% of installed capacity per year in year 104**. Absolute construction later peaks at roughly 111,000 people of capacity per year. Neither demographic growth nor immigration demand is independently forecast. Fabrication and industry are assumed to replicate in proportion to occupied capacity; replacement continues after growth ends.

## Machinery and its cost

Simply multiplying the entire original plant cannot support the peak rate: source-metal machinery would consume too much of its own reproduction capacity. The tested design instead doubles lunar excavation, hauling, bulk and metal processing, fabrication, and source metal-processing/fabrication assets. It adds 10% source chemistry, solar and radiator capacity; increases lunar landers from 782 to 978; and increases construction robots and rated floor installation by 15%. Additional lunar process power, receivers, radiators and the existing industrial beam-power fleet are sized from the inherited engineering equations. Added machinery itself requires new structures, imported components, renewal, processing and freight.

The final maximum annual equipment utilization is **97.78%**, in the two manufacturing-heavy configurations. An additional allowance of 10,000 technical staff per 250,000-person industrial scale is charged in the financial consumer at 250,000 constant USD2041 per staff-year. Base physical accounts keep technical employment below 40% of residents, leaving the inherited 20% civic/other workforce allocation intact. Cultural platforms, service kits and premium-process additions still require a joint screen in the financial consumer.

One billion tonnes equals 10¹² kg. The raw-resource price case physically uses the inherited refined-source-goods configuration; it is not a qualified raw-ore export design. The separate initial-module material proxy adds approximately 0.086–0.088 billion tonnes of source feed and 0.0075–0.0077 billion tonnes of lunar feed. It estimates seed stocks from marginal product requirements before applying the common-feed maximum. Initial surveying and a complete bootstrap transport campaign remain outside these feed totals.

The lunar footprint assumes 1,500 kg/m³ bulk density and five metres of worked depth. If 25% of a surveyed district can be used, the prospective area is four times the excavated area: about 2,160–3,380 km². Neither area includes all facilities, transport or buffer land. Regional composition, conservation and actual depth determine whether suitable districts exist; a small fraction of lunar surface area does not prove accessible ore.

The 17-body dark, favorable-orbital-geometry catalogue screen contains **3.119 billion tonnes** at assumed density. The base programme crosses that entire hypothetical mass in years **88–93**; a hypothetical 10% extraction allowance is crossed in years **34–40**. These are comparisons with assumed screening inventories, not dates of exhaustion of actual mines. The much broader dark catalogue contains about **8,511 billion tonnes**, so year-200 reference requirements equal 2.37–3.13% of that hypothetical mass, or 23.7–31.3% of a 10%-extractable allowance. That broader set includes routes and compositions not qualified for this programme. Catalogued material is neither automatically affordable nor automatically available.

## Would extraction become more expensive?

The [mining evidence review](mining-evidence.md) examines measured lunar sample composition, Bennu nitrogen, patchy polar ice and trajectory-dependent access. It supports testing poorer feed and more difficult access; it supplies no empirical depletion curve. The model therefore gradually shifts the share of output obtained from poorer feed from zero in year 50 to all output in year 200. This is a scenario, independent of the catalogue-crossing dates.

Halving lunar usable-metal yield from 10% to 5% and reducing bulk yield from 90% to 80% increases year-200 cumulative lunar feed to roughly **5.95–10.04 billion tonnes**. The gradual transition remains within enlarged equipment capacity in these runs. A full immediate shift during peak construction can exceed handling capacity; the timing matters. Added feed incurs the inherited handling-energy and resource-provision costs. Refining-energy dependence on mineralogy, deeper excavation and longer hauling remain unqualified, so this is a bounded cost sensitivity rather than a complete delivered-price forecast.

The asteroid stress reduces water-equivalent yield from 7% to 2.5% of feed, and nitrogen and metal yields to 1/2.4 of their reference values. Required feed through year 200 rises to roughly **484–644 billion tonnes**. At the endpoint water-linked feed is 2.8 times the reference. The gradual stress exceeds source capacity in every portfolio, first in years **64–86**, with peak utilization of **140–181%**. Extra excavation, processing, power and possibly different routes would need another priced design. Those unmet requirements cannot be converted into a financed, feasible return scenario by multiplying operating costs alone.

The reference programme is therefore materially plausible at a broad inventory scale, while its economic continuity depends strongly on source qualification and delivered volatile costs. A useful next engineering decision is to qualify multiple replenishment sources well before the narrow-screen crossing, investigate lower-propellant transport and increase recovery where its full equipment and energy cost is favorable. Until then, reference-cost returns are conditional; the poor-source case is a demonstrated capacity risk within the model.

## Reproduction and evidence

The [engineering exporter](exporter.py) runs against pinned provisioning commit `146a558275f486c9fd9a5cf79908b7c6fdf21888`, verifies source identities and writes a versioned frozen response product. The consumer reads that product without importing the provisioning implementation. Original zero-growth profiles reproduce the earlier strategy snapshots. Responses are interpolated only between saved growth rates from zero to 6%; midpoint numerical controls give maximum relative discrepancy 8.56×10⁻¹⁶. This establishes numerical reproduction, not qualified hardware.

```sh
python research/studies/vacuum_buildout/exporter.py --provisioning-root /path/to/pinned/provisioning
python -m research.studies.vacuum_buildout.run
python -m pytest -q research/studies/vacuum_buildout/tests
MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/terluna-mpl python visualization/vacuum-buildout/render.py
```

Ordinary runs write to ignored `research/runs/vacuum_buildout/`. Updating the reviewed result requires `--output research/studies/vacuum_buildout/results/buildout.json` and review of the resulting changes.
