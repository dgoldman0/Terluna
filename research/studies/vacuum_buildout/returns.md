# Returns on completing the enclosed build by year 200

The prescribed ramp produces a much larger economy by year 100 and completes the 6.468-million-person enclosed build in year 200. Under the inherited strong worldwide-demand case, sponsor cash net present value at a 3% real discount rate is **+$2.76–5.11 trillion at year 100** and **−$2.13 to +$2.48 trillion at year 200**, depending on the export portfolio. These are conditional returns on a completed programme supplied with additional equity when earnings and admissible credit are insufficient. They do not establish a self-financing route to completion.

The corresponding funding requirement is substantial: **$384–613 trillion of additional, undiscounted external equity through year 200**, beyond the seed. At completion, every strong-demand portfolio has an annual operating deficit, ranging from **$6.57 trillion to $10.94 trillion** before new expansion. Early receipts can outweigh very distant costs at a 3% discount rate even while the completed economy requires continuing support. Funding, operating sustainability and discounted value consequently need separate judgments.

All amounts below are constant USD2041 equivalents. U remains the established general economic unit and possible currency or basket of U-related currencies. The accounts cover the planned off-Earth economic zone and the Earth-based investors, suppliers, customers, workers and other beneficiaries affected by it. They prescribe neither an exchange-rate peg nor an Earth-currency basket.

## The physical and financial comparison

The [committed build-out and mining study](README.md) supplies the dated schedule: first production in year 8, 222,240 people of occupied capacity in year 100 and 6,468,289 in year 200. Two-year advance construction and an enlarged refining/fabrication system replace the earlier cash-constrained 1% growth policy. Additional capital, annual renewal, resource provisions and technical staffing are charged. The matched $14.501 trillion establishment/$41.892 trillion campaign remains the accounting source; its legacy recurring allowances are replaced by actual model-year operations rather than charged a second time.

The nine industrial configurations retain their worldwide sports, entertainment, licensing, research and professional exports, shared customer budgets, competition, acquisition costs and product qualification assumptions. The cultural platform includes four sports and eight organizations per sport. Tourism and hospitality remain in the inherited destination account. Population does not cap Earth audiences. Service facilities are added one per year when their two-year commissioning reservation fits common power and workforce capacity; customer demand and the existing 20-kit ceiling limit expansion.

The augmented seed, including carrying costs and working cash, is **$2.610–2.840 trillion**, with a separately funded **$130–142 billion monetary reserve**. Capital orders after opening total **$15.18–15.60 trillion through year 100** and **$171.30–182.89 trillion through year 200** in the strong market. These are dated investment requirements; a positive book value is not counted as a factory sale at the checkpoint.

Two financing arms share exactly the same physical and customer paths:

- **Equity completion:** retained operating cash and explicit external equity supply the prescribed investments, deficits and liquidity requirements.
- **Latest U completion:** eligible capital loans can substitute for part of that funding, with 2.5% real interest on local U balances, 3% on outside loans and supplier-matched U claims, and 50% supplier-U eligibility for identified external capital. Remaining needs still require external equity.

Credit retains 1.5-times coverage from 70% of positive operating cash, a 75% maximum loan share, a debt ceiling fixed at 25% of the original seed, two years of principal grace and 20-year amortization. A separate positive-value test prices an order against a constant current incremental margin over 40 years, discounted and delayed for commissioning and production maturation. This is a level-margin underwriting screen, not a forecast of 40 years of qualified contracts. Negative marginal value or insufficient coverage prevents new borrowing. Existing covenant breaches suspend payouts while the completion mandate continues to require funding.

Initial monetary reserves are externally supplied. Subsequent reserve contributions use 20% of positive cash after debt service, toward one year of core imports plus conversion/loss buffers equal to 40% of outstanding U credit. Supplier claims remain outside local circulation. The inherited price-pressure test compares the additional effect of U credit against the same equity-funded build; it can replace rejected U credit with equity and defer intended local owner spending into separate custody. Passing that test does not demonstrate low absolute inflation during the much larger construction programme.

## Sponsor cash returns and continuing support

Sponsor cash NPV charges all dated equity contributions, initial working cash and monetary-reserve funding, then credits actual owner payouts. Cash, monetary reserves, owner custody and remaining debt appear separately as terminal book claims. No perpetual continuation value, factory resale, goodwill or automatic reserve redemption is included in the headline figures.

The following results use strong worldwide demand and the latest U financing. All financial columns are **trillions of constant USD2041**; the last column is **trillions per year**.

The premium case's approximately $5 billion positive year-200 NPV is economically close to zero relative to the model's uncertainty and funding scale. No confidence interval or probability of success is assigned to any result. Repeated contributions and payouts produce multiple cash-flow sign changes, so the calculation declines to report a single nonnegative internal rate of return for these year-200 paths.

The propellant/logistics example shows what the additional funding purchases. Of **$398.606 trillion**, approximately **$248.278 trillion** replenishes cash used by operating deficits, **$142.096 trillion** supplies capital shortfalls and **$8.232 trillion** meets increased liquidity requirements. These categories use opening cash above the protected buffer; they do not relabel the same deficit as both operating expenditure and funding. Debt service receives no separate rescue contribution in this example. At year 200 the programme retains approximately $9.802 trillion of operating cash and $1.181 trillion of monetary reserves, with no remaining modeled debt. Those are conditional book claims, separate from realized payouts.

The earlier finding that one portfolio could have a positive operating residual at the cap used a much later market-age assumption. Reaching the cap in year 200 exposes a different stage of the inherited industrial-budget growth curve. This comparison therefore finds all nine strong-demand cap accounts negative without revising the historical result.

## Time valuation and market uncertainty

Discounting changes the importance of the second century. The strong propellant/logistics case gives year-200 sponsor cash NPV of approximately **−$365.61 trillion at 0%**, **−$55.44 trillion at 1%**, **+$2.48 trillion at 3%**, and **−$0.11 trillion at 7%**. The intermediate positive result reflects the timing of early distributions, later capital and still later deficits. Neither a high discount rate nor a positive intermediate-rate NPV pays a later operating bill.

Across all nine portfolios, the strong case's undiscounted sponsor balance through year 200 is **−$596.53 to −$363.93 trillion**; every portfolio is negative at 1% as well. At 7%, none has a positive sponsor cash NPV at either checkpoint. The financing commitment is therefore central to the interpretation, especially for stakeholders responsible for sustaining completed capacity after year 200.

| Worldwide customer scenario | Year-100 sponsor cash NPV range at 3%, trillion USD2041 | Year-200 range |
|---|---:|---:|
| Limited | −5.73 to −4.33 | −11.88 to −9.09 |
| Middle/global | −4.31 to −2.75 | −10.28 to −7.34 |
| Strong/breakthrough | +2.76 to +5.11 | −2.12 to +2.48 |

These are alternative assumptions, not likelihood-weighted forecasts. They preserve the current worldwide customer and contract ceilings. In particular, household and institutional service budgets do not automatically compound for two centuries simply because lunar infrastructure grows. The resulting deficits identify a mismatch between replicated production costs and admitted external revenue. They do not establish a physical ceiling on future sports audiences, research demand or digital business.

## Socioeconomic value and switching values

A separate account values final exports and resident services, removes identified resource-funding provisions and embedded resident payroll, and adds residents' alternative earnings as an opportunity-cost proxy. Known initial preservation and stock-restoration allowances are also removed where they are treated as unspent financial provisions. Actual extraction and transport purchases remain costs. This treatment improves the separation of financial transfers from consumed inputs relative to the earlier synthesis; it does not value completed restoration work or erase environmental losses.

Supplier turnover, wages, GDP, finance proceeds and research spillovers are not added again as independent benefits. The resulting numbers remain purchaser-price and resident-service proxies for socioeconomic value. Unpriced science, strategic resilience, cultural meaning, ecological effects, preservation losses and displaced Earth production require measured counterfactuals before entering a total.

| Strong-demand valuation method, at 3% real | Year-100 result across portfolios | Year-200 result |
|---|---:|---:|
| Full export and resident-service price proxies | +$4.32–6.68 trillion | +$1.80–6.39 trillion |
| Half of both benefit proxies | −$7.78 to −$6.30 trillion | −$12.66 to −$10.34 trillion |
| Export value needed for break-even, retaining full resident-service value | 74.7–81.1% of modeled receipts | 79.2–93.1% |

The full-price positive results depend on substantial realized purchaser value and time valuation. At zero discount, even the full-price strong socioeconomic proxy is negative through year 200, by roughly **$55–298 trillion**. In the middle market, a 3% appraisal needs an additional **$64–124 billion of net social benefit per operating year** to close the year-100 gap, or **$145–250 billion per year** over the year-200 appraisal. Those are switching values for additional net benefit, not a claim that research or entertainment already supplies it.

A complementary operating threshold asks what would sustain completed capacity. Strong-case year-200 deficits require an additional **$6.57–10.94 trillion of annual net operating contribution**, or equivalent real cost savings. At an explicitly hypothetical 50% contribution margin, that would mean **$13.15–21.89 trillion of additional annual gross service receipts**. Such receipts would still need customers, production capacity, staff, energy and delivery; the threshold does not add them to the returns. Wider non-material demand and less costly industrial configurations can therefore be evaluated against a concrete requirement rather than an arbitrary revenue multiplier.

## What U financing changes

On a fixed build and customer path, finance changes timing and stakeholder claims. It does not add output. The strong cases originate at most about **$1.38 billion of local U credit** and **$0.44 billion of supplier-held U credit** under the retained underwriting and reserve limits; total peak borrowing, including outside loans, remains below $24 billion. Four strong portfolios take no capital credit because the relevant investment packages fail the admission conditions or do not need borrowing at admissible dates.

At a 3% discount rate, loans priced at 3% largely substitute funding without changing the completed programme's discounted cash value once repaid. The local 50-basis-point reduction adds up to approximately **$51.6 million** of sponsor NPV on the matched balances. The same amount reduces local lenders' gross interest income. The socioeconomic and unlevered operating-minus-capital accounts are identical between the two financing arms, as their real activities are identical. There is no second interest-saving benefit added to social value.

No modeled incremental price-limit breach occurs. That narrow finding depends on the original transaction-balance model, reserve availability, supplier term acceptance and payout deferral. It does not measure actual U purchasing power or establish that all required construction can be financed through monetary expansion. Moreover, residual equity guarantees payment in this experiment: lender losses and the probability that such a guarantee fails are not estimated. The prior receivables-bridge experiments remain separate; no avoided-disruption benefit is added here.

## Mining and the next economic design decision

The returns use the mining study's reference yields and routes. Through year 200 those require roughly 202–267 billion tonnes of near-Earth feed and substantial new source qualification. The adverse asteroid-grade scenario exceeds the enlarged equipment capacity and has no fully priced remedy, so it cannot yet support a completed-build return estimate. The financial base case is therefore conditional on obtaining suitable replacement sources at approximately reference delivered cost.

The main economic improvement to investigate is the relationship between industry, demand and life-support logistics. The present model replicates the complete founding-scale system with population, continues nameplate commercial production even when buyers constrain receipts, and retains inherited replacement and import intensities for two centuries. Dispatching unsold production, changing the industrial mix, reducing transport propellant demand and selectively replacing Earth inputs could reduce both the external deficit and mining pressure. Each change needs its own capital, maintenance, power and capacity account before a return improvement is credited.

The completion schedule now supplies a common basis for that work. It preserves the year-200 objective while exposing the source surveys, productive redesign and external earning power required to sustain the resulting society.

[The runner](returns.py) reads the committed build-out product and pins its financial dependencies in [returns_inputs.json](returns_inputs.json). The [reviewed results](results/returns.json) retain the annual physical/operating and financing accounts, 108 checkpoint comparisons, funding purposes and terminal claims.

```sh
python -m research.studies.vacuum_buildout.returns
python -m pytest -q research/studies/vacuum_buildout/tests
MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/terluna-mpl python visualization/vacuum-buildout/returns.py
```

Ordinary runs write to ignored `research/runs/vacuum_buildout/`; updating the reviewed product requires an explicit `--output research/studies/vacuum_buildout/results/returns.json`.
