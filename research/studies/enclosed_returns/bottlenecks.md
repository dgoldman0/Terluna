# What limits the returns from staged off-Earth development?

The current returns are limited by the timing of commercial expansion, the share of receipts spent delivering services, the cost of sustaining the completed system and the treatment of retained funds. Which constraint matters most depends on the portfolio and the stage of development. The new experiments distinguish those effects: faster propellant sales can improve discounted returns substantially without increasing the eventual customer budget, while faster manufacturing expansion exceeds the available workforce in several cases. Longer-lived source-tug components improve recurring costs, but do not by themselves fund the services portfolio's entire residential programme.

The approximately $12 trillion net present value is also measuring a different quantity from a cumulative investment multiple. In the optimistic propellant-and-services case, investors receive $347.65 trillion in real distributions through year 200. Discounting those dated distributions at 3% gives $15.82 trillion; subtracting $3.88 trillion of discounted contributions leaves $11.94 trillion NPV. The resulting internal rate of return is 6.20% real annually. The estimate is conditional on a very large worldwide market and sustained real demand growth; its uncertainty remains substantial even though its cumulative payout is much larger than three times the initial investment.

This study preserves the accepted baseline and adds an attribution of all 81 combinations of nine portfolios, three market scenarios and three demand-growth assumptions. It then compares 28 physical cases covering four representative portfolios, six demand/rollout settings and a component-life intervention. All four unchanged controls reproduce the existing physical and financial accounts exactly. Twenty-one cases pass the stated workforce and equipment screens. The other seven receive no completed financial-return estimate.

U retains its established meaning as the general economic unit, potentially one currency or a basket of U-related currencies. The economic zone includes the planned off-Earth system before Open Moon, planetary-array and outer-Solar-System development. Earth investors, customers, suppliers, workers and other affected people remain stakeholders. Population still reaches 6,468,289 in year 200, with the year-100 checkpoint and sustaining check through year 220. Monetary quantities below are constant USD2041 purchasing-power equivalents; they establish no U exchange rate.

## Reconciling investment, distributions and NPV

The optimistic propellant-and-services example uses the breakthrough market, with a potential audience of two billion and 1% annual growth in real spending and funded contract volumes until year 200. It retains the 35% capital allowance, the U credit and reserve rules, and all priced residential and industrial requirements. Its initial funding is larger than the priced physical deployment:

| Initial requirement | Trillion USD2041 |
|---|---:|
| Priced physical seed deployment | 2.728 |
| Commercial seed facilities and carrying costs, additional | 0.029 |
| Capital-cost allowance, treated as spent | 0.948 |
| Working cash | 0.400 |
| Initial monetary reserve | 0.205 |
| **Total initial funder contributions** | **4.311** |

That initial contribution finances the opening deployment and balances. Subsequent construction and replacement are paid from operating receipts or additional finance when required. Initial cost is therefore an incomplete denominator for judging how much productive activity occurs over two centuries. The model also retains explicitly unpriced deployment requirements; the $2.728 trillion is a priced scope, not a complete executable construction quote.

| Return measure through year 200 | Result |
|---|---:|
| Gross receipts, undiscounted | $1,297.98 trillion |

| Distributions divided by all contributions, undiscounted | 80.65 times |

NPV is already net of contributions. Dividing it by an undiscounted initial construction subtotal mixes valuation dates and scopes; interpreting the quotient as the full 200-year payout multiple then loses the timing and amount of distributions. The separate measures above make those distinctions visible. None measures a probability-weighted expected return: the market and technical conditions remain scenarios.

At a 3% real discount rate, one unit received in year 100 contributes 0.0520 units to initial present value; in year 200 it contributes 0.002707 units. The large final economy consequently adds much less to initial NPV than an equally profitable business operating earlier. The following period account uses the same optimistic propellant portfolio. All entries are trillion USD2041 present value at 3%; cash after capital precedes financing and distributions.

The same timing effect makes the value of a prospective additional business depend on when it begins. An extra $10 trillion of initial present value requires about $0.529 trillion of additional annual **net cash** from years 20–200, or $5.90 trillion annually from years 100–200. These are algebraic targets at 3%, not assumed sales or a proposal that such cash can be generated without further cost.

## Where the discounted receipts go

The optimistic propellant portfolio's $43.84 trillion of discounted gross receipts includes $6.34 trillion of propellant sales, $0.41 trillion of mission support, $20.09 trillion of sports, entertainment and licensing, and $16.95 trillion of research and professional services. Accommodation contributes about $0.05 trillion. This portfolio is already a broad service economy; its name does not mean that every receipt comes from fuel.

Its largest expenditure category in present value is **$22.31 trillion of commercial operating costs**. The inherited variable-cost shares spend 50% of cultural receipts, 55% of licensing, 65% of research and 70% of professional invoices before separately charged acquisition, fixed purchases, payroll, facilities and the shared physical system. These are explicit cost assumptions for partners, platforms, rights, purchased production and other inputs. They have not been established as the best terms achievable by future enterprises. Their prominence is one reason large worldwide sales produce much smaller consolidated profits.

Physical operation, later capital and opening deployment then reduce the remaining cash. The resulting NPV of operating cash after capital, before financing, is $13.52 trillion. Carrying zero-real-yield financial balances accounts for another $1.51 trillion of the difference; net financial claims still held in year 200 have present value $0.065 trillion and are reported separately from distributions. These items reconcile to the $11.94 trillion funder NPV. They are deductions within one account, rather than separate estimates to add as benefits.

The ranking changes when considering the completed economy's annual viability. Late replacement hardware and freight are expensive even though discounting gives their distant costs little initial weight. In the services portfolio under 0.5% demand growth, year-200 physical operation costs $4.13 trillion, including $2.24 trillion of imported replacement hardware. Source-tug components alone account for $1.35 trillion. A project can therefore have positive early cash value while facing an unaffordable sustaining bill at the population cap.

The saved stratification reports all eleven revenue sectors and fourteen annual cost categories, plus seed expenditure, separately by period. Shared residential, industrial and transport costs remain shared. Subtracting an arbitrary fraction of those costs from one export sector would not establish that sector's incremental profitability; that requires a separate physical counterfactual for the removed activity.

## The constraints change during expansion

Early services are restricted by a planning allocation before the model exhausts its overall workforce. In the services/reference case, year 20 has three installed service packages against scenario demand for 22 before customer-budget competition. The 10%-of-residents staffing allowance permits only three packages. Total modeled jobs use about 30% of the broader pool permitted to technical and commercial work. The one-package-per-year order rule and two-year lead can matter later, but the early staffing allocation is the stronger restriction in this example. Neither planning rule is a measured technological maximum.

Industrial supplier offers grow from 5% of the inherited reference scale at 4% annually, with the opening five-year production ramp. They reach that reference scale around year 85. Raising the offer-growth assumption to 8% brings that point to approximately year 47. Whether the earlier activity can occur still depends on labor, equipment, source supply and finance; increasing a supplier offer does not prove greater factory productivity.

By year 200, customer ceilings restrict several offers at the stated prices. In the diversified/reference case, the industrial budget admits 38.3% of the offered material sales. Provisioning subsequently sizes assets for admitted orders, so that fraction is **not** the utilization of a fleet of already built factories. Earlier, at year 100, industrial sales total $0.475 trillion against a $3.165 trillion scenario budget. The unused budget is potential scenario purchasing capacity, not a signed orderbook or proof of available production.

The new faster-expansion cases expose a third restriction: simultaneous staff requirements. The diversified portfolio's faster industrial capture first exceeds the total workforce allowance in year 34 and peaks at 4.87 times that allowance in year 83. Combining faster capture with faster services fails from year 26. The earlier manufacturing mix has similar industrial conflicts; even its service-only acceleration peaks 7.45% above the combined allowance in year 59. The allowance reserves 20% of residents for civic/other activity out of a 60% working share, leaving 40% for the modeled technical and commercial work. Staffing intensities remain planning proxies, especially where industrial staffing follows equipment value.

These failures identify what a faster industrial design would need to change: its labor requirement, its timing, the division of labor among activities, or provision for additional workers and their support. The calculation has not established which remedy is economical. It does establish that credit or customer budgets alone do not supply the missing labor under the stated residential schedule.

## Controlled interventions

The experiments below retain breakthrough demand with **0.5% annual real growth**, all accepted population dates, the two-year commissioning lead, the 35% capital allowance and the existing financing rules. They differ from the 1%-growth explanatory example above. Each changed order path receives a complete physical rerun, including resulting construction, component renewal, power, freight and seed fuel, followed by a finite-source assessment and financial appraisal when capacity screens pass.

The stronger-demand test increases the four customer budgets and specified funded quantities by 25%. It retains the two-billion audience ceiling, increases paid participation from 40% to 50%, licensing accounts from 400 million to 500 million, and advertising/rights spending intensity by 25%. Other unit prices remain unchanged. Faster service rollout allows four package orders per year and allocates up to 20% of residents to service staffing, while retaining the total workforce screen. Faster industrial capture changes 4% offer growth to 8%. The life test changes only source-tug active-component mean life from 20 to 30 years, holding structural life at 50 years and purchase price fixed; qualification and any extra development or component cost remain unpriced.

The table gives year-200 funder NPV at 3% real, in trillion USD2041. An unresolved entry identifies the first workforce-failure year and carries no completed return estimate.

| Intervention | Services | Diversified | Propellant / logistics | Earlier manufacturing mix |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Unchanged baseline | -4.37 | 5.86 | 5.91 | 8.02 |
| 25% stronger demand | -4.14 | 8.65 | 10.21 | 10.45 |
| Faster industrial capture | -4.37 | Unresolved, year 34 | 11.66 | Unresolved, year 26 |
| Faster service rollout | -4.29 | 7.75 | 7.81 | Unresolved, year 41 |
| Both rollout changes | -4.29 | Unresolved, year 26 | 13.25 | Unresolved, year 21 |
| Stronger demand and both rollout changes | -4.08 | Unresolved, year 26 | 19.06 | Unresolved, year 21 |
| Source-tug active life of 30 years | -4.17 | 6.85 | 7.93 | 8.58 |

The propellant case gives the clearest separation of demand and expansion speed. Faster rollout raises NPV from $5.91 trillion to $13.25 trillion at unchanged demand, while stronger demand alone produces $10.21 trillion. Their joint result is $19.06 trillion; its gain exceeds the sum of the separate gains by about $1.50 trillion. Earlier capacity can serve some of the extra customers, so the interventions interact. This comparison is specific to the tested 25% demand change and rollout assumptions; it is not a universal ranking of demand and supply elasticities.

Faster rollout alone leaves that portfolio's year-200 operating cash at approximately **-$0.38 trillion annually** because it reaches the same final market sooner. Stronger demand raises it to **+$0.53 trillion**, while the longer-life case raises it to **+$0.18 trillion**. This is why the analysis reports both accumulated value and the sustaining margin: earlier profitable activity improves NPV, but does not necessarily resolve the final operating deficit.

The services portfolio behaves differently. Every tested intervention still requires additional equity to complete the prescribed residential programme; no variant distributes cash by year 200. Stronger demand plus faster services reduces that requirement from $109.69 trillion to $55.34 trillion, while its operating deficit remains $1.84 trillion annually. Its NPV of cash before financing rises from $4.61 trillion to $9.34 trillion, but that early surplus is retained against later obligations. An unchanged negative funder-return pattern does not mean that the intervention produced no useful cash; it means the prescribed completion programme consumes the available cash and more.

Longer source-tug life reduces year-200 physical operating expenditure by $0.54 trillion for services, $0.77 trillion for diversified exports, $0.56 trillion for propellant/logistics and $0.64 trillion for the earlier manufacturing mix. Its modest reduction in annual source feed at year 200, approximately 1.4%, does not qualify a two-century source reserve. Every physical variation retains the seven catalogue-mass comparisons, source-access conditions and original absence of qualified reserves.

## Service margins and retained financial balances

A separate financial target reduces purchased commercial-input costs by **five percentage points of covered gross revenue** from sports, entertainment, licensing, research and professional services. It preserves delivered quantities, physical capacity, seed funding and all other cost categories. The reduction is bounded against the covered variable input bill after the existing internal-computing credit. No engineering efficiency or better supplier contract is assumed to have been secured.

| Portfolio, breakthrough / 0.5% growth | Direct input-cost saving, PV at 3% | Baseline funder NPV | Funder NPV at the cost target |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Services | 1.48 | -4.37 | -4.20 |
| Diversified | 1.49 | 5.86 | 7.72 |
| Propellant / logistics | 1.48 | 5.91 | 8.46 |
| Earlier manufacturing mix | 1.48 | 8.02 | 9.59 |

All quantities are trillion USD2041 through year 200. The funder improvement can differ from the direct saving because smaller operating bills also change cash buffers, reserves and distribution dates. A supplier-price concession can transfer income from suppliers to project funders; an actual production efficiency can save resources. Until that distinction is specified, this experiment reports financial results and leaves its socioeconomic change unvalued in the saved product.

The baseline's zero-real-yield balances produce a separately identifiable drag. In the diversified/reference case, $7.80 trillion NPV of cash after operations and capital becomes $5.86 trillion of distributed funder NPV. The difference consists of $1.87 trillion of financial carry cost and $0.068 trillion of net financial claims retained at year 200. For the services/reference case, the carry cost is $8.95 trillion: cash peaks at $49.08 trillion in year 122, then pays for later deficits and is eventually insufficient.

This carry cost is the discount-rate opportunity cost of holding balances. It is neither another consumed physical resource nor a demonstrated saving available for immediate distribution. It indicates the value of examining dated outside financing commitments, reserve composition and investments compatible with liquidity requirements. Assigning a return to all reserves would be unjustified; reserves, working cash and long-dated construction funds have different liquidity and risk requirements. A lower lending rate has little effect in the selected baseline cases because they draw essentially no credit. The current U package cannot improve productivity merely by changing the denomination of a future payment.

The saved calculation reconciles this attribution. If A is closing cash plus reserves and owner custody, D is debt, J is interest, and r is the appraisal rate, then through horizon H:

`unlevered NPV − (distributed funder NPV + PV of terminal A−D)`

`= PV of interest + r × sum of prior-year (A−D), discounted to each following year.`

At the 3% lending benchmark, the debt carry and benchmark interest cancel. What remains is the carry cost of financial assets less any local-U interest concession. This identity holds independently of whether a project has a positive final operating margin.

Raw and commodity Earth-return offers still fail the price floor; their remaining returns arise from other admitted activities. The earlier manufacturing mix retains its $2,000 USD2026/kg lunar quote. The analysis does not establish commodity exports at a loss as a growth strategy, nor qualification of the expensive manufactured product. The model also contains an inherited $8 billion common premium-development item in an earlier strategy input that is not separately itemized in the enclosed seed account; its overlap with generic qualification expenditure and the capital allowance remains unresolved.

The off-Earth system could create value beyond these invoices and distributions: new research methods, knowledge used by Earth firms, cultural participation, technical capability, resilient infrastructure and options for later development. Those opportunities are not disproved by a moderate sponsor NPV. They also do not automatically accrue to the funders of this programme. The existing socioeconomic account uses explicit purchase-price valuations for exports and resident services, including all affected stakeholder groups within its stated scope. In the optimistic propellant example it gives $16.44 trillion NPV at full prices and -$6.85 trillion at half prices. That sensitivity is evidence of unresolved valuation, not an uncertainty interval or an estimate of all the project's benefits.

No factory sale or perpetual enterprise value is included. Adding an arbitrary terminal multiple would conceal the need for replacement, markets and qualified sources beyond the modeled period. At 3%, even a hypothetical additional $1 trillion of annual net cash from year 201 forever contributes only about $0.090 trillion to initial present value. A more complete societal appraisal may therefore differ markedly from this sponsor account through its valuation of spillovers, distribution of benefits and treatment of distant generations. That work needs an explicit counterfactual and evidence for additional value rather than a multiplier applied to sales.

The immediate implication for the design is to examine earlier profitable activity where actual staffing permits it, improve the retained margin on worldwide services, and reduce the recurring supply bill before committing to costs that later receipts cannot sustain. The experiment supports earlier propellant and service capacity under the stated workforce assumptions; it requires a materially different labor plan for equally fast manufacturing expansion. Treasury policy then needs to match the dates and risks of those real investments. These are distinct design questions with measurable consequences, rather than a single demand bottleneck.

## Method, evidence and reproduction

The attribution uses five complementary methods: dated discounted cash flow; revenue and expenditure decomposition by period; an exact financing reconciliation; controlled demand/supply interventions with their interaction; and annual-net-cash targets. It retains undiscounted totals, real internal rates of return, years 100/200/220, additional funding requirements and final operating margins. Sector receipts are not added to profits or treated as independent social benefits.

The selected methodological reading is HM Treasury's *Green Book 2026*, sections on discounting, sensitivity analysis and switching values, and consideration of unmonetised effects. They support keeping timing, uncertainty and wider value visible. The constant 0%, 1%, 3% and 7% rates remain project sensitivities; this analysis does not adopt the Green Book's declining social-rate schedule or claim that it validates a 200-year forecast. [HM Treasury, *The Green Book 2026*](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-green-book--2/the-green-book-2026#shortlist-appraisal).

The original market, physics and finance implementations remain unchanged. The producer at `provisioning/enclosed_industry/bottlenecks.py` owns altered physical requirements; `resources/enclosed_mining/bottlenecks.py` owns their source assessment.
