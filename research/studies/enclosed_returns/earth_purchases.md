# Earth purchases over the planned 100 and 200 year buildout

Under the existing diversified portfolio and breakthrough worldwide market, cumulative purchases comprise about **5.52 million tonnes of goods delivered to Earth by year 100**, increasing to **191–274 million tonnes by year 200**. The year-200 range compares the existing 0.5% and 1% annual real demand-growth assumptions. The breakthrough market is the existing strong-demand scenario with a two-billion-person potential audience and its associated industrial, institutional and mission budgets. External customers also purchase **11.66 million tonnes of propellant delivered in space by year 100**, increasing to **272–471 million tonnes by year 200**. These are alternative commercial scenarios, conditional on buyers purchasing the modeled quantities at the modeled prices.

The dated orders retain the regular expansion: 12,500 residents at first production in year 8, approximately 222,240 in year 100, and 6.47 million in year 200. The industrial model sizes capacity for those customer orders and domestic requirements. This account adds no accelerated refinery ramp. It decomposes the unchanged orders underlying the [revised returns](revised.md), with 81 alternatives covering nine portfolios, three market levels and three growth assumptions.

**U remains the general economic unit**, potentially a currency or basket of U-related currencies, serving the planned off-Earth economic zone before Open Moon, the habitat array and outer-system expansion. Earth is outside that zone. Customer invoices below use constant USD2041 purchasing-power equivalents, without fixing a U exchange rate.

## Approximate material breakdown

The diversified portfolio is the useful mixed-industry example because it sells manufactured goods, asteroid-derived products and propellant alongside worldwide services. The table counts purchased product mass in **million metric tonnes**, after the original quality, acceptance and return-loss allowances. Packaging and mine feed are excluded. Year-100 material purchases are the same at the displayed precision in both growth cases because production offers remain below the relevant buyer limits.

| Purchased product | Through year 100, both growth cases | Through year 200, 0.5% growth | Through year 200, 1% growth |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Standard lunar manufactured products | 2.81 | 126.77 | 166.44 |
| Qualified premium lunar products | 1.57 | 23.57 | 50.02 |
| Asteroid-derived refined products | 1.13 | 40.45 | 57.35 |
| **Total goods purchased on Earth** | 5.52 | 190.79 | 273.81 |
| Propellant purchased for delivery in space, separately | 11.66 | 272.07 | 471.48 |

“Standard” is the product's commercial category relative to the premium offer. Both the standard lunar goods and asteroid products in this portfolio sell at the inherited **$750 USD2026/kg**, equivalent to about **$1,168 USD2041/kg**. Qualified premium products sell at **$3,000 USD2026/kg**, about **$4,674 USD2041/kg**. These prices assume substantial product value, purity, processing or manufacturing. They cannot be applied indiscriminately to ordinary iron, aluminium or nickel shipments.

The source and value classes are the material breakdown the economic orders actually specify. The lunar factory uses generic local metal plus imported inputs; the asteroid route supplies generic refined products. Neither carries a bill of materials specifying how much of its sold mass is iron, nickel, titanium, cobalt, platinum or rare-earth material. Accordingly, the table does not identify all lunar manufactured mass as newly recovered lunar metal.

The precious-metal and rare-earth assessment, `resources/source_access/coproducts.md` at resources revision `84886850`, supplies a separate composition account. Its CI-like asteroid analogue contains quantified nickel, cobalt, precious metals and other specialty elements; selected KREEP-like lunar material supplies rare earths, yttrium, scandium and other constituents. Those assays estimate recovery potential from selected feed. They do not assign the existing customers' invoices to individual elements. **Platinum-group metals, gold, silver and rare earths therefore have unspecified planned Earth purchases, rather than a planned purchase of zero.**

An element-level purchase plan needs a chosen product mix, domestic retention, processing capacity, delivered quality and element-specific sales volumes and prices. Its receipts must replace the corresponding generic-product receipts or occupy an additional funded customer budget. Adding the entire recoverable inventory to the existing invoices would count potential output as sold exports and could count embedded materials twice.

The zero commodity shipments follow the inherited **$150 USD2026/kg Earth ground-handling floor**: the raw and commodity offers quote $3/kg and are rejected before dispatch. Their surviving sales come from other activities. These zeroes describe this transport-and-price case; they do not indicate absent ore or an absence of future commodity demand.

The strong propellant/logistics example therefore combines **706 million tonnes of customer propellant delivered in space** with worldwide service purchases over 200 years. It contains no separate Earth-return cargo offer. Its approximately 1,298 trillion USD2041 of cumulative commercial receipts cannot be interpreted as that much metal sold to Earth. The manufacturing-heavy alternatives make materially different customer and product assumptions.

The premium portfolio's large standard-product mass is also significant. Unaccepted premium attempts return 80% of their mass to the standard offer. In the 1% case, only about 66.46 million tonnes of its cumulative 347.42 million tonnes sell as qualified premium goods; the remainder sells at the standard quote. Production growth eventually exceeds the dedicated premium market, so its label does not describe every purchased kilogram.

Services account for most early receipts. In the 1% diversified case, goods delivered to Earth contribute about $12 trillion of $178 trillion purchased through year 100; by year 200 they contribute about $495 trillion of $1,798 trillion. This is why cumulative invoices alone provide a poor estimate of returned material mass.

The 1% diversified case reaches annual year-200 purchases of **8.99 million tonnes of Earth goods** and **11.07 million tonnes of in-space propellant**. Its annual gross customer invoices are **34.08 trillion USD2041**. The cumulative totals sum the actual gradual sales path; they do not multiply these mature annual rates by 100 or 200.

## Calculation and interpretation

The [purchase-account runner](earth_purchases.py) reads the existing annual orders, price and acceptance assumptions, then reconciles its invoices to the revised financial results. Sales begin in dated year 8; the year-100 checkpoint sums 93 annual rows and the year-200 checkpoint sums 193. That inclusive convention matches the existing returns exactly. Seed construction supplies neither additional commercial invoices nor repeated founding output.

For ordinary goods, expected purchased mass is gross export-product mass multiplied by 98% quality yield and 99% return survival. A premium attempt is split between accepted premium output and standard fallback recovery. Because the saved orderbook omits the accepted premium mass, the calculation recovers it algebraically from its two-price invoice and checks it independently against the original acceptance and demand limits. Propellant already records delivered sales and receives no Earth-capsule loss factor.

The [input manifest](earth_purchases_inputs.json) pins all consumed records. The [saved result](results/earth_purchases.json.gz) retains both cumulative and checkpoint-annual quantities for every alternative, including the smaller worldwide markets. All 486 matching financial checkpoints reconcile, across 81 alternatives, two dates and three financing modes; the financing modes add no customer sales. The [verification record](earth_purchases_checks.md) describes the numerical and document checks.

The central commercial requirement is therefore measurable: the diversified plan calls for millions of tonnes of valuable finished goods purchased by Earth customers, substantial in-space propellant purchases, and sustained worldwide service spending. The remaining material uncertainty concerns which specific products and elements earn those invoices. That commercial allocation has to accompany further coproduct development before its supply potential can alter the return estimates.
