# Revised returns, monetary development and retained wealth

Costed recovery and refurbishment substantially improve the ability to sustain the enclosed economy. With the breakthrough worldwide market and 0.5% annual real demand growth, the propellant-and-logistics portfolio changes from a year-200 operating deficit of **0.38 trillion USD2041 per year to a surplus of 0.75 trillion**. Its year-200 pooled funder cash net present value (NPV) rises from **5.91 to 8.79 trillion USD2041**. The diversified portfolio's corresponding operating surplus rises from **0.16 to 1.54 trillion per year**, and its funder NPV rises from **5.86 to 7.35 trillion**.

Earlier commercial earnings make a further difference. With 1% annual real demand growth, the propellant portfolio's recovery/refurbishment case produces **415.57 trillion USD2041 in cumulative payouts and a 12.90 trillion funder NPV through year 200**. Combining those changes with the tested faster rollout raises the respective values to **507.91 trillion and 23.16 trillion**, with a **7.55% real cash internal rate of return (IRR)** and **4.17 trillion of initial funding**. This accelerated case passes the represented capacity screens; two accelerated diversified cases do not. These are conditional commercial and engineering results, with source qualification and complete deployment still unresolved.

The revision compares the same commercial programme before and after explicitly costed material recovery and equipment refurbishment. It also brings the broader resource distributions, monetary accounts and ownership alternatives into one appraisal. **Cash returns, continuing operating capacity and locally retained wealth answer different questions.** The tables retain those distinctions rather than treating a large cumulative sales total, a discounted return and an asset balance as interchangeable measures.

## Scope, valuation and comparison

**U is the established general economic unit**, potentially one currency or a basket of U-related currencies. It serves the planned off-Earth economic zone. That zone includes its supporting lunar and near-Earth industry; Earth remains an external trading partner and a source of investors, suppliers and customers. The economic appraisal includes those stakeholders. Open Moon, the habitat array and outer-Solar-System development remain outside the scenario.

The residential schedule starts at 12,500 people, reaches 222,240 in year 100 and reaches the planned 6,468,289 capacity in year 200. Population remains at that cap through year 220. Industrial capacity follows actual household, construction and customer requirements. The founding fleet is built once; its surviving components, additions, retirements and repairs determine later capacity.

Monetary amounts are **constant USD2041 purchasing-power equivalents**. This common valuation does not establish a U exchange rate, dollar peg or Earth-currency backing basket. Unless specified otherwise, tables use the U-financing arm, a 35% allowance on modeled capital cost and a 3% real discount rate. A trillion is 10¹² dollars in that valuation. Annual operating cash is receipts less operating costs, equipment renewal and provisions, before new capital, debt service and reserve contributions.

The full primary grid contains nine portfolios, three worldwide market levels and three demand-growth assumptions: 81 distinct orderbooks. Each has its previous physical account and a new combined-recovery account, three financing alternatives and checkpoints at years 100, 200 and 220. The earlier financial engine and its inputs remain unchanged, and all 729 previous primary financial checkpoints reproduce exactly. Matched primary comparisons change supporting physical production costs without adding receipts. Construction funding consolidates participating enterprises and funders and assumes an arrangement that makes their retained earnings available to the shared programme. This is an institutional financing assumption, not an adopted tax system or a claim that all activity belongs to one owner.

Worldwide demand retains four sports and eight organizations per sport, wider entertainment, licensing, research, computing, professional services, industrial products and space logistics. Visiting participants need not be permanent residents. The limited, global and breakthrough market levels retain potential audiences of 150 million, 600 million and two billion people, with separate spending and institutional assumptions. Headcounts do not grow indefinitely: annual demand growth of 0%, 0.5% or 1% changes real spending and funded contract volumes until year 200. Demand then remains constant. These are commercial scenarios, not committed sales.

Buyer budgets, competing products and service synergies are preserved. Internal research and computing consume resources without creating a second external invoice. Visitor accommodation carries facilities, utilities, consumables and staffing costs; passenger transport and fares remain unqualified requirements. Raw and commodity Earth-return offers still fail the inherited ground-handling price floor. Their other businesses can earn revenue, but this does not establish profitable bulk commodity exports to Earth.

## What the physical revision changes

The original model already recovered much of its water and material. The revision raises domestic and industrial water recovery from 98% to 99.5%, crop-water recovery from 99.5% to 99.7%, and fish-purge recovery from 95% to 98%. Goods achieve 98% collection followed by 97% quality acceptance, or 95.06% usable return; structures achieve 99% collection and 99% acceptance, or 98.01% usable return. Initial inventories, stock growth and losses still require fresh material.

Refurbishment collects 80% of retiring eligible machines and accepts 60% of those collected. Each accepted unit receives 20% new repair parts, with an equal old mass discarded. A year of inspection precedes two years of work. Workshops at the equipment's location require machines, staff, electricity, consumables and transport. The calculation solves the resulting interactions among freight, propellant, extraction and manufacturing. Restoring the original expected service life after requalification, including at later refurbishment cycles, remains an engineering assumption requiring demonstration.

All 81 primary physical cases pass the represented workforce, equipment and material-capacity checks. Through year 200, their cumulative fresh asteroid-feed requirement falls **11.05–18.55%** relative to their matching baselines. Year-200 physical operating outlay falls **9.37–26.64%**, including the represented resource provision and before commercial costs, financing and the financial capital allowance. The corresponding year-100 reductions are 8.80–14.67% in cumulative feed and 5.30–16.51% in annual physical operating outlay. These ranges compare orderbooks; they are not confidence intervals for engineering performance.

The cost gain is larger than the direct material saving in several cases because requalification avoids expensive active-equipment purchases. Lower freight requirements then reduce propellant demand and the mines, power systems and vehicles needed to supply that propellant. The resulting saving comes from the full recalculated system, rather than adding independent percentage discounts to the same expense.

The main account retains the inherited 5 kg per person-day landscape/agriculture water allowance. A separate maximum-credit endpoint removes it because later crop and fish accounts may overlap with part of that allowance. Its full removal is not an established design saving. Chemical rocket exhaust receives no recovery credit. Food nutrients, metabolic carbon dioxide and carbon return do not yet have a complete elemental balance; neither full closure nor free additional oxygen recovery is assumed.

## Why large cash receipts still produce a much smaller NPV

A payment in year 200 contributes only about 0.27% of its undiscounted value to a year-zero NPV at 3% real. Cash retained at zero real yield to fund later construction also has an opportunity cost. Neither effect implies that a completed factory, an institution or a later generation's income has disappeared. They explain what this particular dated investor-cash measure values.

In the revised strong propellant case, 205.12 trillion USD2041 of payouts arrive in years 151–200. Their year-zero present value is only 1.13 trillion at 3% real. By contrast, the 4.78 trillion paid in years 8–25 has a present value of 2.76 trillion. Earlier revenue capture can therefore have a greater NPV effect than a much larger late saving.

Through year 200, this case has 14.44 trillion USD2041 of unlevered cash NPV. Retaining cash, reserves and custody at zero real yield costs 1.48 trillion in present-value terms. The remaining amount reconciles to 12.90 trillion of distributed funder NPV plus 0.06 trillion of terminal net financial claims. Physical assets and subsequent unmodeled earnings are separate accounts. The financing reconciliation closes to numerical roundoff.

The accounts separate seven periods, each with sector receipts, external commercial inputs, equipment renewal, resident payroll, resource provisions and new capital. The reconciliation then distinguishes physical cash surplus from debt service, reserves, custody and working cash retained for later obligations. This permits a weak discounted return to be traced to its timing and costs rather than attributed to a generic lack of demand.

## Resource potential, source selection and preservation

The revised resource study integrates catalogue measurements, inferred sizes, spectral classifications, population estimates and explicit composition priors. In its central population model, the wider near-Earth orbital screen contains a conditional 5th–95th percentile interval of **25–73 billion tonnes of water equivalent**, with a median of 39 billion tonnes. The broader near-Earth population excluding Don Quixote contains **504–2,931 billion tonnes**, with a median of 961 billion tonnes. Recovery and allocation restrictions still apply. These nested pools overlap and cannot be added.

Near-Earth asteroids orbit the Sun. Neither an orbital screen nor a large estimated mass puts that material at a depot, qualifies a recurring transport route or establishes legal and physical access. The revision compares cumulative feed and recovered-water requirements against the paired resource draws, retaining population, diameter and grade branches separately. It does not use a stock-sufficiency frequency as a probability of financial success or multiply NPV by it.

In the services/reference case, the central model's 95th-percentile joint requirement falls to about 2.7% of that broader stock at year 200, compared with 53.8% of the wider screen. This is a requirement on useful access, with both feed and recoverable water satisfied in the same draw. It does not mean 2.7% is already surveyed, mineable or allocated.

The difference between those conditions shows why neither the narrow characterized sample nor the entire theoretical inventory should stand in for an actual source plan. The saved product retains all seven unweighted population/size/grade branches, both population pools and all three checkpoints.

The physical primary cases still select sources with 10% water-equivalent grade and 70% recovery. The estimated average wider-pool mixture is only 1.41–3.84% water equivalent, with a median of 2.22%. Indiscriminately processing that mixture would require roughly 2.6–7.1 times the feed at the same recovery. Targeting hydrated bodies could avoid that penalty, but requires a demonstrated sequence of suitable sources. The separate poor-source cases rerun the full plant and transport requirements at 5% water grade and 50% recovery, with poorer nitrogen and metal coefficients as well.

Lunar bulk material has a different constraint. The regional analysis gives approximately 7.84–9.21 billion tonnes in a prospective 1,000 km² footprint worked to five metres. Global bulk scarcity is therefore a weak explanation for rising costs at this build scale. Particular grades, workable depth, contamination, processing quality, site access and transport can still become more expensive. Coarse regional oxide rankings guide prospecting; they do not certify a deposit or a finished alloy.

The existing preservation rules remain conditions of every source comparison. Survey precedes irreversible disturbance, including landing, exhaust and dust effects. Scientific targets stay excluded. Representative samples retain native conditions and an unopened fraction, with provenance, distributed archives and long-term access. Recording data cannot replace physical heritage or continuing natural functions. Remote braking, Earth exclusion and safe capture remain cargo requirements, alongside accessible stocks for future use and scarce-element recovery. Assumed availability fractions do not authorize extraction. The inherited cash resource provision is retained, but the resource study does not establish a complete price for these obligations. Uncosted site qualification and preservation work remains outside the claimed margin.

### U credit and reserves

U financing keeps the existing real local loan rate of 2.5%, against 3% external borrowing; twenty-year terms; two-year principal grace; repayment-coverage, project-value and budget limits; and a seed-relative debt ceiling. Reserves cover core imports and added conversion and credit-loss requirements. The price rule limits the incremental effect of the credit arm relative to an equity-financed version of the same physical economy. It is **not a forecast of absolute U inflation or its exchange rate**.

Nine of the 81 revised primary cases draw some U credit by year 200. The largest cumulative draw is **1.026 billion USD2041**, and the largest incremental price level is **0.361%** above the matched equity account; no relative-price rule is breached. The cases that fund the build without additional equity are the same under the equity and U arms. The fixed schedule is therefore not chiefly constrained by the monetary price limit. Strong cases largely finance their work from retained receipts; weak cases cannot satisfy the repayment requirements merely by issuing more claims.

Among the eighteen breakthrough/reference-or-wider paths, removing the local interest-rate concession and halving or doubling the transaction basket leaves the displayed main financial results unchanged to the reported precision. The model's modest rate relief is not the source of the multi-trillion-dollar gains. Physical efficiency and commercial timing produce those gains. U can still provide a useful payment bridge where temporary timing, collateral and external liquidity permit it.

Money issuance and cheaper local credit change claims, financing costs and liquidity. They do not create a second sale or a new real socioeconomic benefit. The isolated no-rate-relief comparison, half/double transaction-basket tests and the external-funded credit arm show which effects actually arise in this fixed construction schedule. A broader programme that changes investment timing through credit would require newly solved physical orderbooks.

The payment-delay experiments are separate monthly episodes at years 100, 200 and 220. All invoices issued during a six- or twelve-month interruption are delayed by that interval and then collected. The tests compare full access with access to only 25% of programme cash, while excluding owner custody. New investment, ordinary payouts and reserve contributions pause during each episode. The U bridge assumes voluntary supplier acceptance of half the relevant external-supplier gap in U term claims and willing holders of locked local claims; the externally funded benchmark assumes a 6% real loan offer. Thus a successful bridge protects current payments under that interruption; it does not prove uninterrupted construction, permanent-loss insurance or unlimited currency conversion.

There are 54 dated portfolio/growth/checkpoint situations and sixteen payment episodes per situation. With full access to programme cash, all 54 survive the six-month interruption without a facility, and 53 survive twelve months; the assumed U or funded-external facility covers the remaining case. With access to only 25% of programme cash, cash alone covers 6 of 54 six-month situations, while either facility covers 18. For twelve-month interruptions under that cash restriction, only 6 of 54 are covered even with the facility. These counts are conditional tests, not default probabilities.

The largest bridge balance reached in any episode is 0.989 trillion USD2041. Facility limits, repayment coverage and accessible external reserves still bind. Halving access to reserve assets leaves the success counts unchanged in this grid; it remains a separate restriction in the saved results. Monthly cash, receivable, loan and reserve identities close within 0.12 USD2041 at the largest modeled balances.

## Monetary stocks, productive assets and ownership

## Implications for further development

The revision identifies three distinct economic improvements. Recovery and refurbishment lower the cost of maintaining the full population and industrial system. Faster feasible rollout brings paying customers and cash forward, which has a larger effect on investor NPV. Ownership and saving arrangements determine how much of the resulting wealth remains with residents and institutions within U's economic zone.

The strongest tested combined rollout is the propellant/logistics case. At 1% annual real demand growth, its checkpoints are:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 142.39 | 17.74 | 3.39 | 7.52% |
| 200 | 507.91 | 23.16 | 6.60 | 7.55% |
| 220 | 637.94 | 23.42 | 6.51 | 7.55% |

It requires 4.17 trillion USD2041 of modeled initial funding and no further equity under its declared scenario. That outcome does not extend to every portfolio or sales level. The broader resource evidence supports substantial opportunity, but selective-source quality, transport, repair performance, workforce and commercial margins still determine whether the opportunity can be supplied profitably. Monetary headroom supports eligible financing and temporary payment needs; it does not remove those real requirements.

The next decisions can therefore be tied to measurable requirements: qualifying source grades and routes; testing repair acceptance, spare-part costs and restored lifetime; resolving the landscape/agriculture allowance; specifying complete nutrient and carbon balances; and establishing the commercial and ownership agreements that turn potential receipts into retained wealth. These investigations address separate limits. A finding of abundant material, a successful repair programme and a large paying audience can reinforce one another, but each needs its own evidence.
