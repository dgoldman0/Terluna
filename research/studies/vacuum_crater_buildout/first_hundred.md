# Building the first 100 crater communities

The first 100 complete towns have a reference construction allowance of **about 130 billion U and 18 years** if each starts with 500 residents. They would house 50,000 people in approximately 238 m craters, under the adopted representative architecture. Both figures are conditional on an advanced space economy able to supply machinery, specialist components, controlled volatile deliveries and dependable power. The schedule begins with regional mobilisation, includes establishing and qualifying the local construction system, and ends when all 100 towns can be occupied. It assigns no present-day starting date.

The larger comparisons preserve the same floor-space standard, civic landscape and single contiguous enclosure. They change the population and span of every town; they are alternatives to starting with the smallest communities.

| Residents per town | Total residents | Crater diameter | Reference initial funding | Reference time from mobilisation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 | 50,000 | 238 m | 129 billion U | 17.8 years |
| 750 | 75,000 | 291 m | 186 billion U | 20.7 years |
| 1,000 | 100,000 | 336 m | 247 billion U | 26.0 years |

**U is the project's common real-price unit**, inherited from the [world-economy construction account](../world_economy/construction.md). Its reference Earth output is 100,000 U per resident-year. No conversion to current dollars is established. The cost cases are planning allowances in that unit; the evidence supports the quantities more firmly than the prices or commissioning dates. The first 100 actual sites remain to be chosen. This estimate assumes a few regional groups with shared works and a 500 km road allowance, rather than treating the global density ranking as an approved construction order.

## What is built and what the clock assumes

Each town includes the permanent homes, workshops, education and basic clinical spaces, planted terraces, walks, public rooms and lake that will remain after the enclosure is removed. Food production, major repairs, bulk storage and higher-level services can be shared regionally, with pressurised travel and docking. Refuge rooms and redundant services support emergencies within the continuous community. Occupation follows environmental commissioning and garden establishment; planting matures after opening, as it does in any new town.

The budget includes the full initial regional infrastructure requirement attributable to these 100 towns. Future towns receive no advance share of that cost. Local construction plants and fleets must be commissioned within the lead period, while their normal capital recovery is included in installed supply prices. An additional establishment allowance pays for regional mobilisation, temporary construction facilities, commissioning and start-up losses above normal supplier charges. The model also funds incremental enclosure development, conservation work, contingency and an initial reserve for eventual removal. These allowances need engineering estimates as designs mature.

The supplier network beyond the region is assumed to accept orders. Its delivered prices recover the attributable cost of manufacture, freight and controlled capture, while the dedicated power allowance buys the programme's required supply capacity. Building a civilization-wide transport, volatile extraction or orbital power industry from today's starting point is outside this clock and bill. If those services are unavailable, their development becomes an additional predecessor whose cost and duration are not established here. The finished Open Moon's atmosphere, seas, global protection programme and complete orbital array are separate projects.

The [annual maintenance account](maintenance.md) now estimates recurring physical upkeep, operating supplies and component renewal alongside this construction bill. The monetary total covers construction and initial inventories. Resident consumption, migration fares, financing and long-vacuum operation and replacement require continuing budgets. Services already supporting occupied towns must be funded while the rest are built. Reusable factories and utilities continue to support the subsequent programme, with no assumed resale receipt reducing the first-100 bill.

## Local material, imported material and conservation

For 100 towns of 500 residents, the reference calculation uses 1 atmosphere and 293.15 K inside the enclosure. Its reinforcement comparison assumes a 600 MPa effective allowable and 1,600 kg/m³ density, with the complete material/joint system still to qualify. The resulting quantities are:

| Quantity, all 100 towns | Amount | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Built floor | 3.50 million m² | 70 m² per resident across homes and other buildings |
| Curved enclosure area | 5.16 km² | A spherical cap rising by 20% of crater diameter |
| Loose shielding comparison | 24.75 million tonnes | A uniform 3 m equivalent at 1,600 kg/m³ |
| Pressure-system procurement allowance | 410,000 tonnes | Twice the ideal 600 MPa reinforcement mass, plus assumed roof and ground barriers |
| Local finished solids | 34.64 million tonnes | Buildings, roads, shielding, extra supports/foundations and radiator metal |
| Raw local feed | 43.07 million tonnes | Before bulk-process rejects and metal separation |
| Retained process tails | 8.43 million tonnes | Kept on the Moon; no reuse credit in this estimate |
| Specialist and equipment imports | 0.84 million tonnes | Pressure components, machinery, utility hardware, vehicles/docking and biosphere inputs |
| Initial imported air and water | 2.90 million tonnes | Includes 2.22 million tonnes for 2 m average lake depth |

About **90% of delivered mass is local** in this case. The raw-feed ledger closes as finished local solids plus retained tails. A separate grading pass increases handled tonnage without claiming another mineral reserve. Manufactured ordinary stock is held at the existing 100 tonnes per resident reference across all price cases, separating price uncertainty from material quantity. Its assumed split is 90% local bulk, 5% local metal and 5% imported goods. The 90% finished bulk yield and 10% recovered metal mass per raw feed are process assumptions awaiting mineral assays and qualified separation routes.

Common lunar material supplies shielding, blocks, ceramics, glass feed and suitable metals. Carbon-rich reinforcement, polymers, selected alloy constituents, copper, electronics and other specialist goods retain import allocations. **Water, nitrogen and oxygen come from the Solar-System resource operation**; oxygen from local processing remains an uncredited by-product. Polar ice is preserved for its scientific record. Imports follow controlled delivery, with capture and placement included in the supply boundary. The programme gives no extraction credit to protected major bodies, including Saturn's rings, Titan's atmosphere and ocean worlds.

At 10 m working depth and the assumed raw density, the reference feed occupies roughly **2.7 km² of quarry footprint**. The towns' circular estates occupy 17.8 km², the assumed 20 m road corridors another 10 km², and the exterior radiators have about 5.9 km² of emitting surface. These are different area measures, not a surveyed land allocation. Quarries also need working room, stockpiles, tailings management and buffers; receivers, farms, ports and workshops need their own spatial designs. The [conservation programme](../conservation/README.md) and the [distribution programme](../distributions/README.md) govern the eventual spatial fit, including the protected wild land, parks and dark sky.

Resource use follows the [future-reservation account](../world_economy/development.md#early-lunar-mining-with-future-resources-reserved): survey the relevant deposits, reserve future settlement and industrial needs, retain ecological nutrients and useful coproducts, and assess each disturbance before extraction. The earlier synthetic export pool grants no quarry allocation here. Scientific recording and sampling precede the loss of a record; heritage treatment requires written evaluation and the relevant communities' judgement. Craters, quarries, corridors, utilities, landing areas and waste sites all enter that review. The available heritage coordinates are not approved exclusion polygons.

The reference budget includes 0.7 billion U for survey and preservation and about 1.3 billion U for disturbed-ground restoration. These are estimating allowances rather than evidence that preservation or restoration can be purchased for those sums. Clean spoil may replace some virgin shielding after testing; contaminated or nutrient-bearing tails need identifiable, accessible storage that remains usable after the planned waters arrive. No disposal or sale of scarce constituents supplies a revenue credit.

A further reservation diagnostic makes the long horizon explicit. At a 50-year mean life for processed local assets and **95% usable return through the complete collection/reprocessing chain**, 200 years of replacement would need about **3.67 million tonnes of additional raw feed**. At 90% or 99% return it becomes 7.33 or 0.73 million tonnes. The corresponding net stock cycle times are 500, 1,000 and 5,000 years. Loose shielding is retained for recovery and excluded from that replacement stream. These aggregate scenarios define a reserve to investigate; element-specific reserves, actual lifetimes and achieved recovery still determine sustainability. The reserve is not another quantity mined immediately or charged as initial construction.

## The reference bill

The 500-resident case has the following initial funding requirement. Values are billion U.

| Work or reserve | Billion U |
|---|---:|
| Ordinary buildings, equipment and everyday services | 25.00 |
| Whole-community pressure system | 24.60 |
| Independent roof support and foundations | 5.55 |
| Loose local shielding | 2.47 |
| Regional roads, pressurised transport and docking | 2.00 |
| Firm power, reserves, exterior cooling and garden lighting | 11.71 |
| Initial air, water, biosphere and extra ordinary-goods delivery | 5.65 |
| Surveys and scientific/heritage preservation | 0.70 |
| Incremental enclosure qualification | 5.00 |
| Incremental regional industrial establishment | 8.00 |
| Construction utility operation and disturbed-ground restoration | 1.43 |
| Direct construction | 92.11 |
| 35% contingency | 32.24 |
| Initial removal/recovery funding reserve | 4.61 |
| **Total initial funding** | **128.95** |

The ordinary 500,000 U per resident allowance already includes normal buildings, productive equipment, food plants, routine services and ordinary R&D. Their mass-times-price proxy is not added to the bill. The pressure-system row is the extra community-wide enclosure, with its barriers and installation; ordinary indoor structures remain in ordinary capital. Independent roof supports and foundations have separate 100 kg/m²-of-roof and 300 kg/m²-of-plan mass allowances. Neither quantity demonstrates a complete pressure-vessel load path or a roof that remains stable after inflation is lost.

Reference unit assumptions include 60 U/kg for the delivered, installed pressure system; 3 U/kg for additional local supports and foundations; 0.1 U/kg for installed loose shielding; 3 million U/km for regional roads; 5 U/W for firm delivered electrical capacity; 0.1 U/Wh for emergency reserves; and 1 U/kg for delivered air and water. The pressure price is an average for a specified package, not a raw-fibre quotation. These future-price assumptions remain explicit in [first_hundred_inputs.json](first_hundred_inputs.json).

Installed supplier charges include their machinery, fabrication, normal operation and capital recovery. Project-provided on-site electricity and exterior cooling are excluded from the local material and road charges, because their capital is purchased separately here. Construction electricity adds only the variable utility expense; off-site power remains within delivered supplier prices. The extra 8 billion U regional establishment allowance covers start-up burdens above normal supplier recovery, while the extra 5 billion U qualification allowance addresses this enclosure programme beyond ordinary R&D. Those two unengineered allowances are substantial sources of uncertainty.

Varying the ordinary capital, new unit prices, establishment allowances and contingency together produces a much wider financial envelope:

| Residents per town | Lower prices | Reference prices | Upper prices |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 | 44 | 129 | 468 |
| 750 | 64 | 186 | 669 |
| 1,000 | 85 | 247 | 880 |

All entries are billion U for the first 100 towns and include contingency and the initial removal reserve. **These are scenarios, not a statistical confidence interval.** The 44–468 billion U range for the smallest case shows why a tighter budget needs qualified procurement packages and commissioned supplier capacity. A useful working provision is about 130 billion U for the smaller first towns, with explicit capacity and design gates before committing that programme.

## How the construction rate is obtained

The reference lead allows two years for survey and preservation, four for regional industry and power/access, and six for enclosure qualification. These tasks overlap; the maximum gives a six-year lead. Roughly 86,000 tonnes of construction-plant and dedicated-power hardware arrive during that period. At 25% of the assumed 200,000-tonne annual specialist delivery capacity, that shipment takes about 1.7 years and fits within the lead. Its mass is removed from subsequent town kits, so the productive equipment precedes the output it makes.

After the lead, production reaches 25%, 50%, 75% and then 100% of its specified annual capacity. Materials and prepared components accumulate until one complete town's requirements are available and a site team is free. Each of 16 parallel teams then takes 18 months for assembly, testing, environmental commissioning and garden establishment. Every material stream stops after supplying the priced programme; unused capacity creates no extra extraction, imports or buildings.

| Reference stream | Effective annual capacity |
|---|---:|
| Excavation, short-haul transport and placement | 8 million tonnes |
| Finished bulk construction products | 1.5 million tonnes |
| Finished refined/fabricated metals | 120,000 tonnes |
| Qualified pressure-system manufacture | 60,000 tonnes |
| Specialist imports after establishment | 200,000 tonnes |
| Controlled air and water deliveries | 500,000 tonnes |
| Prepared building floor components | 600,000 m² |
| Regional roads | 100 km |
| Site assembly and commissioning | 16 teams × one town per 18 months |

The handling capacity corresponds to 40 complete operating systems, each effectively moving 50 tonnes/hour for 4,000 available hours/year. It includes haulage and placement, rather than counting excavation alone. These are required industrial capacities, not demonstrated lunar fleet outputs. Local route length, maintenance, rework and actual equipment determine whether they can be achieved.

The reference occupation milestones are approximately **year 7.8 for the first town, 9.4 for ten, 10.8 for twenty-five, 13.2 for fifty and 17.8 for all one hundred**. Site-team availability is the slowest steady stream at 500 residents; roof procurement becomes the limiting stream at 750–1,000 residents. For a subsequent 100-town order with the reference industry and qualified design already running, removing the initial lead and ramp gives about **10.8 years** at 500 residents or **18.5 years** at 1,000. Those shorter times do not describe the first regional mobilisation.

A rapid case with a four-year lead, greater production capacity and 24 one-year site teams reaches 100 small towns in **10.8 years**. A constrained case with a ten-year lead, lower capacity and ten two-year site teams takes **32.8 years**. At 1,000 residents per town the corresponding extremes are **16.8 and 47.5 years**. Each rate case has its own construction-power and radiator quantity in the result. These dates assume the qualification programme succeeds within its allowance; a failed design or unresolved site decision adds time outside the production calculation.

## Protection, light, power and the eventual open town

The budget carries the previous study's **3 m shielding mass comparison**, alongside an explicit lighting and cooling system, without adopting an opaque roof as the final design. It compares material and service requirements before a radiation-transport solution and an architectural light design exist. A uniform loose covering would block direct sunlight; useful collected-light routes or transparent protective systems must demonstrate their radiation and optical performance before receiving a mass or electricity saving.

The electrical comparison supplies 150 W/m² to the unbuilt dry ground for 16 hours per terrestrial day. Its 43.33 m² per resident gives **217 MW average garden lighting** for the 50,000 residents. Together with 5 kW per resident for general services and 10 kW per resident for lunar food production, occupied towns average **967 MW** and peak at **1.075 GW** under simultaneous lighting. The food figure is a scenario within the existing fully lamp-lit process range of 5.2–53 kW per resident. A complete diet, vacuum farm equipment, roof and balcony planting, comfortable visual quality and all heat gains still need design.

The reference construction streams use about **27.3 TWh** across the programme and require approximately **706 MW** while their assumed equipment operates. This includes separate heating/manufacturing energies for bulk solids and refined metal; loose shielding is moved without being melted. Sizing the utility for occupied towns plus continuing construction gives **1.78 GW of firm delivered capacity**, appropriate to a region intended to keep building the later towns. Exterior heat rejection uses an ideal shaded 330 K radiator calculation with twice the ideal emitting area as an explicit allowance. Solar absorption, terrain views, fluid circuits and conversion losses remain to be resolved.

The adopted bulk-power programme uses beams, so occupation requires commissioned supply through the lunar night. The budget includes **12 GWh** of installed emergency reserve for 72 hours at 2 kW per resident, with efficiency, usable-capacity and reserve factors. A local-solar alternative keeping the full reference occupied load through half a lunar month needs about **571 GWh** under the same storage convention. At the reference storage price that store alone costs **57 billion U**, before extra daytime generation, charging losses and installation changes. That diagnostic is not a complete solar alternative or a reason to assume the bulk-power link already works.

The partial sensitivities clarify what changes the estimate. At fixed density and unit prices, increasing effective reinforcement allowable from 600 to 800 MPa reduces ideal reinforcement by a quarter, while fixed barriers and shielding remain. The 400 MPa comparison adds about 10 billion U to initial funding. The 7 m shield comparison adds raw material and handling but holds support/foundation allowances fixed; its cost is only a material sensitivity until the heavier support is designed. The 53 kW food case increases power and cooling while holding the ordinary food-plant allowance fixed, so it is likewise a service sensitivity. Doubling road allowance to 1,000 km tests a wider regional network; none of these variants selects a different town architecture.

Ordinary buildings and streets are arranged for the open landscape from the beginning. The enclosure needs independent support through low or reverse pressure difference, weather-compatible services, lifting access and a removal sequence that protects residents and gardens. The initial removal reserve is a funding provision, with no discounting or assumed recovery yield. Sustained breathable outdoor conditions trigger removal; this study assigns no date to terraforming.

Long vacuum operation also preserves import and repair obligations. At 1% annual loss, the reference gross operating air inventory needs about **1,808 tonnes/year** of makeup, a 100-year inventory cycle. The assumed 100 kg/person/day processed-water loop at 98% recovery needs **36,525 tonnes/year** of makeup before lake, crop, airlock and accident losses. Imported feedstocks, local return chains and accessible replacement components therefore remain part of a functioning community after its construction bill has been paid.

## Evidence and reproduction

The [existing vacuum study](README.md) supplies the geometry, architecture, pressure and operational comparisons. Current research supports investigating automated construction and materials processing, with a substantial scale-up still required. NASA's [RASSOR report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210011366/downloads/ASCE%202016%20RASSOR%202%20Final%20%206_8_2016.pdf) describes a conceptual 1,000-tonne/year small-robot plan for Mars, supported by terrestrial testing; it does not validate the proposed lunar fleet. Metzger and Autry's [landing-pad trade study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00378) supplies a thermal-processing comparison of about 0.517 kWh/kg from its tabulated assumptions. The present bulk-manufacturing allowance is 1.11 kWh/kg, while the 20 kWh/kg finished-metal allowance remains a separate assumption. Neither establishes lunar production performance.

NASA's [2026 molten-regolith-electrolysis record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20260006167) reports a 25 kg simulant experiment, averaging 0.07 kg/hour oxygen production over nine hours under vacuum. Its achievement establishes relevant process development, with industrial refining rates still to demonstrate. The [2025 construction programme account](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/stmd/nasa-enables-construction-technology-for-moon-and-mars-exploration/) likewise describes prototypes and tests. The study uses these sources to identify credible development paths and present limits; its prices, industrial capacities and programme lead times are explicit assumptions.

Run `python -m research.studies.vacuum_crater_buildout.first_hundred` for an ignored working result, or supply `--output research/studies/vacuum_crater_buildout/results/first_hundred.json` to reproduce the committed product. The runner binds the adopted plan, architectural design, vacuum scenarios, prior result, economic inputs and food product by schema and hash. [Source metadata](first_hundred_sources.json) preserves the reading scope; [the check record](first_hundred_checks.json) distinguishes numerical controls, bounded reviews, editorial reading and the repository checks from engineering validation. The next material improvement is a qualified pressure/protection/light package for one measured small crater, linked to a surveyed resource district and a supplier capacity and price study.
