# Maintaining a crater town through the vacuum period

A complete **500-person town needs a reference annual provision of about 36 million U** for its physical upkeep, operating services and future equipment replacement. About **18 million U** pays recurring upkeep and services, including an operating margin, and another **18 million U** funds major renewals. Its average share of the first-100 construction programme is about **1.3 billion U**. Both accounts include the town's share of regional infrastructure; a wholly isolated town would require a different service design.

The figures use the same future real-price unit U and construction-price scenarios as the [first-100 estimate](first_hundred.md). U has no established conversion to today's dollars. Lifetimes, maintenance rates and losses remain explicit assumptions. At the reference prices, longer-lived/lower-upkeep and higher-wear cases give approximately **21–67 million U per year** for the 500-person town. This range tests assumptions, rather than defining a probability interval.

| Residents in one town | Average share of initial build | Recurring upkeep/services per year, including margin | Major-renewal provision per year | Total annual funding |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 | 1.29 billion U | 17.7 million U | 17.9 million U | **35.7 million U/year** |
| 750 | 1.86 billion U | 27.5 million U | 27.5 million U | **55.0 million U/year** |
| 1,000 | 2.47 billion U | 37.8 million U | 37.5 million U | **75.4 million U/year** |

For all 100 small towns, the reference annual funding is about **3.6 billion U**. The individual-town total is roughly **71,000 U per resident-year**, or **2.8% of its initial construction allocation**. These are allocations of real work and replacement funding across the community and its businesses, with no assumption about taxes, household bills or who owns the assets.

## What the annual account pays for

Routine upkeep covers inspection, cleaning, calibration, minor repairs, seals, filters, service robots, specialist maintenance work and routine care of plant and gardens. The reference applies **2% a year to about 649 million U of maintained installed assets**. That gives **13.0 million U per year**, before operating energy and makeup supplies. This uniform percentage is a provisional allowance; an asset register and service plan should distinguish passive foundations from active machinery and pressure interfaces. Heavy automation is part of the programme, while its achieved labour savings and failure rates remain unmeasured.

Variable utilities and makeup add **1.8 million U per year**. A **20% operating margin** on upkeep plus those services adds approximately **3.0 million U**, making the recurring account **17.7 million U per year**. The margin covers unallocated operating work within this physical scope; it establishes no insurance policy or quantified protection against loss of the whole town.

The **17.9 million U renewal provision** is separate. It accumulates funds for full component replacement at the assumed lives below, holding real installed prices constant and assuming zero real return on the reserve. Replacement invoices draw on that provision. They are not added to it a second time. A single town would spend unevenly, with large work programmes in particular years, while a region can stagger replacements across its towns.

| Replacement class | Reference life, years | Annual provision, million U |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ordinary buildings and durable systems | 80 | 2.19 |
| Ordinary equipment and other shorter-lived capital | 20 | 5.00 |
| Pressure reinforcement and major connections | 50 | 2.88 |
| Roof and ground pressure barriers, combined proxy | 25 | 4.07 |
| Independent roof supports | 100 | 0.21 |
| Additional foundations | 200 | 0.20 |
| Regional roads | 40 | 0.42 |
| Pressurised vehicles and docking | 15 | 0.33 |
| Occupied-town firm power and distribution | 40 | 1.34 |
| Emergency storage | 20 | 0.60 |
| Occupied-town exterior cooling | 30 | 0.25 |
| Civic garden lighting | 10 | 0.43 |

The ordinary-capital allowance is provisionally split 70% to durable buildings/systems and 30% to equipment and other shorter-lived capital. Its original scope includes services and intangibles, so this allocation is a funding proxy awaiting a detailed asset register. The separate world-economy ordinary-depreciation assumption, 15% of gross product, is not added to component renewal. The power and storage lives of 40 and 20 years follow that study's explicit sensitivity conventions; the remaining lives are estimating assumptions rather than qualified lunar service lives.

Reinforcement and gas barriers together require roughly **7 million U per year** of renewal provision. At the assumed prices and lives, their gross replacement procurement averages about **116 tonnes per year**, though actual installation occurs in campaigns. The combined 25-year barrier life is a proxy: the exposed roof layers and buried ground barrier need separate access, ageing and repair designs. A maintenance plan must preserve the pressure volume and occupied garden landscape while those parts are renewed. Stronger fibre alone establishes neither a longer joint life nor an accessible replacement sequence.

New prices for qualified replacement packages would supersede these allowances. The model repeats the extra vacuum freight for ordinary imported equipment at that equipment's life. Local support, foundation, road and radiator replacements also buy their required processing electricity and cooling as a regional service. Its separate reference allowance is 50 U/MWh, including supplier utility capital and operation; that supplier capital is not also assigned to the town. Material recovery can reduce fresh extraction while fabrication, inspection and installation still require work, so a 95% usable material return is not treated as a 95% discount on installed replacement cost.

## Occupied services and continuing imports

One 500-person town uses **9.67 MW on average** under the retained service assumptions: 5 kW per person for general services, 10 kW for lunar food-production processes, and explicit civic-garden lighting. Annual electrical use is **84,738 MWh**. At the assumed variable utility-operation price of 5 U/MWh, that costs **423,690 U/year**, with generation/distribution upkeep and renewal funded separately. A complete purchased-power tariff would replace that ownership account rather than being added to it.

The town's attributable firm capacity is **10.75 MW**, its occupied peak. The construction programme's additional regional industrial load is excluded from this maintenance account. The same allocation removes the growth industry's share of exterior radiators. Replacement factories recover their normal costs through their installed replacement and processing-service charges, so the town pays for the repair work it uses without renewing the entire original growth industry.

The reference annual makeup quantities are:

| Stream, one 500-person town | Annual quantity | Annual delivered allowance |
|---|---:|---:|
| Air at 1% of gross operating inventory | 18.1 tonnes | 18,100 U |
| Processed-water loop at 98% recovery | 365.3 tonnes | 365,300 U |
| Additional landscape/agriculture water | 913.1 tonnes | 913,100 U |
| Biosphere-related imported material | 5 tonnes | 50,000 U |

The air inventory cycle is 100 years at this loss rate. The processed-water comparison handles 100 kg per resident-day. Additional landscape/agriculture makeup is an explicit **5 kg per resident-day allowance**, introduced because the previous domestic/process loop does not close all garden, lake and food-production losses. It is not a measured loss rate. Biosphere makeup assumes 2% of the initial imported biosphere material per year; it represents lost feedstocks requiring resupply, rather than annual replacement of the living landscape. Actual nutrient collection, treatment, usable return, crop water and airlock transfers need their own balances.

For the complete 100-town reference, these air and water allowances require **about 130,000 tonnes/year** of continuing deliveries. That is an additional contract beside the construction schedule's **500,000 tonnes/year** of initial-fill delivery capacity. If both draw on one fixed-capacity transport service, the construction timetable must be recalculated. The present comparison keeps construction and occupied-service capacity separately funded and available.

The initial lake water, air and loose shielding have no automatic full-inventory depreciation. Makeup maintains air and water stocks. The shielding remains recoverable; its annual work allowance rehandles 0.1% of that retained material and purchases no new complete cover. Preserving future local resources still requires the [construction study's reservation and conservation programme](first_hundred.md#local-material-imported-material-and-conservation). Its coarse 200-year material-reserve diagnostic and these component cost lives are separate approximations; a component bill of materials should reconcile them before setting an extraction allocation.

## Scope, uncertainty and a long vacuum

The recurring total pays for the physical town and the stated services. Ordinary food-plant capital and its process electricity are included, while a complete food-production operating budget remains to be closed. Household consumption, teachers, medical staffing, general public administration, debt service, migration, new-town growth and extraordinary accidents have separate accounts. Maintenance labour and ordinary technical servicing are included in routine upkeep. The result therefore describes a funded physical-maintenance provision, with the human service and household economy still to be added.

The first programme's qualification, mobilisation, construction contingency, initial scientific surveys, initial disturbed-ground restoration and prefunded dome-removal reserve are not physical assets renewed every few decades. Routine inspection and recertification continue within upkeep. Later conservation monitoring, disturbance and access requirements must be retained in the relevant maintenance work programmes. The maintenance design must retain pressure supports, barriers, utilities and removal interfaces that can handle changing external pressure and weather, followed by dome removal when sustainable breathable conditions exist.

At fixed reference construction prices, the longer-life case combines 1% routine upkeep with longer component lives and smaller losses. The higher-wear case uses 4% upkeep, shorter lives and greater losses, including 20 kg per resident-day additional water makeup. Price uncertainty widens the result further:

| Construction-price case | Longer life/lower upkeep | Reference maintenance | Higher wear |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Lower | 8.4 | 14.0 | 25.7 |
| Reference | 20.8 | 35.7 | 67.0 |
| Upper | 56.9 | 106.4 | 218.4 |

Values are million U per town-year for 500 residents, including renewal funding. All rates and service lives are scenarios; no contemporary maintenance guideline establishes them for lunar communities. The most useful reductions come from accessible durable pressure boundaries, longer equipment life, reliable automated upkeep and closed water/nutrient returns. Whether those improvements justify the lower case is a qualification question, rather than an assumed consequence of automation.

Over an illustrative **200-year vacuum period**, constant reference prices and routine spending plus replacements strictly before the endpoint sum to about **6.54 billion U per town**, excluding its initial build and final removal. The calculation pays each replacement invoice once and does not also add the annual renewal fund. At exactly the endpoint it avoids another replacement of an enclosure that is being retired. Buildings and ordinary services then continue into the open-town budget. The 200-year interval is a sensitivity, with no prediction of the date at which the atmosphere becomes usable.

## Evidence and reproduction

[maintenance_inputs.json](maintenance_inputs.json) identifies the construction result, construction inputs and annual economic conventions by schema and hash. The [result](results/maintenance.json) contains every population, price and maintenance case, component contributions, physical service flows and the 50-, 200- and 500-year event accounts. Run `python -m research.studies.vacuum_crater_buildout.maintenance` for an ignored working result, or pass `--output research/studies/vacuum_crater_buildout/results/maintenance.json` to reproduce the committed product.

The [verification record](maintenance_checks.json) reports the numerical controls, bounded reviews, substantive reading and repository checks. The maintenance-rate and service-life evidence remains at the scenario level. An ancillary review could not retrieve the substantive legacy National Research Council maintenance-budget chapters; no claim about their detailed cost boundaries or a validated lunar maintenance rate is admitted from those unread chapters. The next estimate should replace the uniform upkeep fraction and mixed capital split with a maintainable component design, service tasks, crew/robot availability and supplier renewal prices.
