# H2 — Alkaline habitats and distinctive seafood chemistry

**Status: literature-supported hypothesis and proposed study, 4 November 2026.** No Terlunan organism, flavor result or breeding gain has been demonstrated. The target is a distinctive, desirable food produced by a viable lineage in its habitat. Increased stress metabolites alone do not meet that target.

## Biomes before organisms

The [sea-chemistry study](../sea_chemistry/README.md) separates three environments that the food study must keep separate. Its chemistry is a model, including older thermal inputs; its composition is a starting scenario to reconcile with the current climate, not measured lunar water. The [aquatic-biogeochemistry plan](../aquatic_biogeochemistry/plan.md) identifies the missing magnesium floor in the inherited managed sink route; a complete culture medium must resolve that deficiency.

| Habitat | Defining chemistry | Food-system role |
|---|---|---|
| Maintained common-level fresh seas | Model baseline about 0.14 g/kg dissolved salts and pH 8.3; calcium-bicarbonate water, with active mineral management; the original managed route leaves zero magnesium and a physiological Mg floor remains unresolved | Reference habitat for ordinary freshwater founders; pH alone gives no reason to expect shellfish-like sweetness |
| Moderately concentrated lakes, spring-fed margins and controlled finishing waters | Site-specific mixtures of bicarbonate/carbonate, sodium, calcium, magnesium and minor ions | Best initial H2 test space, after complete-life-cycle and food-web suitability screening |
| Mature closed soda lakes | Sodium carbonate/bicarbonate, roughly pH 10 in the model; calcium removed during concentration; concentrations depend on age and water balance | Specialist ecosystem and comparative physiology; no assumption of edible shellfish or large fish production |

Salinity measures dissolved salts, alkalinity acid-neutralizing capacity, hardness mainly calcium and magnesium, and pH hydrogen-ion activity. These quantities interact but are not interchangeable. A sodium-chloride experiment is not a direct test of Terlunan carbonate water. A high pH does not supply calcium for shells. Extremely alkaline waters may also require very different nitrogen-excretion physiology.

**Proposed ecological route:** mildly concentrated basin → compatible periphyton/plankton and benthic prey → an alkali-tolerant omnivore or predator → heritable tissue chemistry and texture → identifiable regional food. Establish each arrow separately. A productive algal mat can feed grazers without making their flesh desirable, and an experimentally attractive finishing treatment may remain an aquaculture technique rather than a naturally reproducing wild fishery.

## What the Earth evidence actually supports

| Evidence | Observed result | Consequence for H2 |
|---|---|---|
| Largemouth bass, 10 weeks at 0, 3 and 9 g/L salinity [A1] | Changes in texture and taste-active compounds; the 9 g/L group had higher IMP, and sweet/umami free amino acids increased | A useful positive comparator for chemical plasticity; these waters are far saltier than the modeled fresh seas, and this is a salinity experiment |
| Largemouth bass, 80-day alkalinity experiment [A2] | Authors report muscle-quality changes and favor 15–20 mmol/L within their tested conditions | A more directly relevant lead; retrieve the full methods, alkalinity convention and ion recipe before adopting an exposure |
| Giant freshwater prawn, saline-alkali versus freshwater comparison [A3] | Reported higher taste-active compounds and more than twice the **calculated equivalent umami concentration** | Supports testing a chemical mechanism; EUC is a model index, not a finding that people experience twice the umami or prefer the product |
| Separate giant freshwater prawn comparison [A4] | Higher IMP but lower umami amino acids; no significant difference in umami reported | The response is conditional. The two studies must be reconciled by chemistry, diet, stock, life stage and handling, not combined into a universal enhancement claim |
| Mozambique tilapia under separate salinity, alkalinity and combined stresses [A5] | Gill metabolomics changed, including amino acids and osmolytes | Supports mechanistic screening of this lineage; a gill response does not establish edible-muscle flavor or successful reproduction |
| Magadi tilapia, *Alcolapia grahami* [A6] | Urea excretion persists after gradual dilution to essentially freshwater | Demonstrates specialized nitrogen physiology; urea production/excretion does not establish high muscle urea, a sweet flesh profile or an ammonia-like food odor |

The prawn comparison [A4] is particularly valuable because it prevents selecting only favorable flavor reports. An increase in one nucleotide can coexist with a decrease in amino acids. Neither the taste of isolated compounds nor an electronic/chemical index substitutes for sensory evaluation in the finished food.

## Founder and comparator portfolio

These are **candidates for contained evaluation**, not approved introductions or newly established species.

| Lineage | Proposed habitat and role | Breeding objective | Culinary direction and decisive limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Oreochromis niloticus* | Fresh-sea baseline and moderate carbonate-lake omnivore; test periphyton-supported diets | Reproductive performance, efficient growth, low off-flavor, firm fillet and favorable measured taste chemistry | Pale-flesh cooked fish and possible qualified raw product; Earth juvenile pH work does not validate extreme soda water [A7] |
| *O. mossambicus* | Chemistry-tolerant comparison and prospective moderate-lake founder | Whole-life-cycle performance under the target ion mixture, then muscle traits | A possible regional savory whitefish; preserve separate lineages initially because stress tolerance is not yet sensory merit [A5] |
| *Micropterus salmoides* | Contained experimental predator and positive literature comparator | Determine whether quality effects reproduce in carbonate water and current thermal regimes | Firm slices or steamed/grilled fillet; do not infer permission for ecosystem release or suitability throughout warm Terlunan basins [A1–A2] |
| *Macrobrachium rosenbergii* | Managed freshwater/moderately concentrated grow-out; benthic omnivore linked to a separate hatchery | Growth, molt success, survival and desired muscle chemistry under compatible hardness/alkalinity | Cooked ebi, grilled tails and broth; larval development normally needs brackish water, so ordinary fresh seas do not close its life cycle [A8] |
| *Alcolapia grahami* | Extreme-chemistry research comparator | Understand tolerance and nitrogen disposal before considering any food objective | No food-production assumption; specialized ecology and conservation/acquisition review precede a founder proposal [A6] |

For tilapia trials, use screened periphyton, phytoplankton, detrital and formulated-feed treatments with measured nutritional composition. For prawns, use benthic diets and shelters while recording sediment oxygen demand and nutrient losses. These are proposed trial food webs, not evidence that all named organisms coexist successfully. Keep toxin-producing blooms and geosmin/2-MIB exposure distinct from nutritional composition.

Prawns warrant a restrictive first screen. A 2017 postlarval study reports lethal outcomes in some high-alkalinity treatments [A9]. The accessible abstract does not establish the concentration convention and full exposure conditions sufficiently for a numerical threshold here. Its condition-specific evidence directly challenges treating “carbonate-rich” as automatically favorable. Retrieve and harmonize the complete water chemistry, alkalinity-equivalent convention, exposure duration and life stage against the later flavor studies before live comparisons. High hardness and high alkalinity can have different effects.

## Selective breeding and lineage evaluation

Start with multiple documented Earth populations or established farm families and retain unselected reference stocks. Use genomic relatedness and pedigrees to separate family effects from common rearing history, and preserve sufficient family representation. Compare the same families across reference freshwater and target chemistry; a family that tastes good only while physiologically impaired is a poor candidate.

The proposed selection index includes full-life-cycle survival and reproduction, welfare, growth/feed use, fillet yield, appropriate texture and measured sensory preference. Flavor measures include free glutamate/aspartate, glycine/alanine, IMP/AMP/GMP, lipid composition and oxidation; screen urea, ammonia and off-flavor compounds rather than assuming their direction. Measure muscle separately from gill, liver, hemolymph or plasma.

Common-garden offspring and reciprocal rearing distinguish inherited differences from acclimation. A persistent association between a genomic marker and phenotype remains a prediction until validated across families and generations. Keep environment-induced finishing effects as a separate product route. No gene-editing target is selected here; engineering becomes a later comparison only if founder choice and breeding fail a defined requirement.

## A staged, falsifiable experiment

1. **Chemistry and life-cycle gate.** Obtain full methods for [A2–A4, A9]. Characterize actual modeled target waters with pH, total alkalinity in charge equivalents, inorganic carbon, osmolality, conductivity, Na/K/Ca/Mg/Cl/sulfate and trace elements. Run precipitation/speciation checks. Select exposures from demonstrated non-injurious ranges for each life stage; do not transfer a bass treatment to prawn postlarvae. Predefine welfare stopping rules and require normal feeding, growth and reproduction before flavor selection.
2. **Separate the mechanisms.** Within feasible chemistry, compare reference freshwater, chloride-dominated salinity controls, carbonate-enriched water, and carbonate water with altered pH/CO₂. Match osmolality and major ions where chemically possible and document what cannot be matched. Carbonate alkalinity, pH and CO₂ are coupled; this is a constrained design, not an arbitrary independent three-factor recipe. Keep temperature, oxygen, diet, stocking and handling comparable.
3. **Separate water from food.** After the direct-water screen, cross the supported water treatments with a standardized diet and a habitat-derived diet of measured composition. Otherwise a favorable algal diet may be misattributed to alkalinity. Add the lunar light cycle only after the chemistry effect is estimable, using the habitat's underwater light trace.
4. **Replicate biological units.** Randomize independent tanks and distribute families among tanks. Tank is the treatment replicate; fish within a tank are subsamples. Use pilot variance and a preregistered minimum sensory/production effect to size the confirmatory trial. Sample matched size, sex/maturity, lunar phase and postharvest interval. Record family-by-environment interactions.
5. **Test actual food.** Begin with identically cooked, blinded samples. Measure sensory preference, texture, aftertaste, aroma and yield alongside chemistry. Compare steamed fish with grilled fish, and steamed prawn with grilled prawn: heat can change which precursor differences matter. Only independently qualified material enters raw sensory work, with matched freezing/thawing and storage.

**H2 succeeds** if a tolerable habitat produces a repeatable desirable culinary difference, and breeding can preserve or improve it without unacceptable reproductive, nutritional or ecological tradeoffs. A direct-water mechanism weakens if effects disappear under matched diet; that outcome can instead support an indirect food-web mechanism. The broader culinary proposal weakens if changes reflect stress or processing artifacts, fail sensory preference, or require a chemistry absent from the proposed habitat. A finishing-only benefit is still useful, but supports a different claim from a naturally distinctive lake lineage.

## Culinary outcomes to carry forward

The leading proposed product is a moderate-lake whitefish with a firm bite and a measurable savory or sweet profile. Its first dishes can be steamed fillets, lightly grilled fish and cooked sushi toppings; qualified sashimi is a later independent option. The prawn route begins with cooked ebi, grilled tails and stock. Extreme-lake fish remain research organisms until edibility, harvest scale and processing are established.

No pink, green, blue or translucent flesh phenotype follows from alkaline water in this evidence. Measure color and pigments, but describe any novel coloration as an untested trait. “Alkaline sweetfish” is a working culinary hypothesis, not the taxonomic Earth sweetfish (*Plecoglossus altivelis*) or a promise that lake minerals will taste sweet.
