# Lunar-cycle fisheries: from stored sunlight to edible muscle

4 November 2026, revised 7 November 2026. The study design for H1, the lunar-cycle fatfish, within the [aquatic-food study](aquatic_novelty.md): Earth evidence on how light cycles become stored energy, and the trials that would show whether the Moon's 29.5-day cycle can be tasted in its fish.

## The hypothesis and its alternatives

**H1:** in suitable productive waters, the 708.7-hour light cycle favours a food web that stores substantial energy, and some fish lines store part of it in edible muscle, so that harvests have a rich and a lean phase that recur. The long dark could instead favour starch storage, dormancy, mixotrophy, metabolic suppression, continued feeding on heterotrophs, or storage in organs outside the fillet; a fatty phase could be weak, delayed or absent, or come with losses of growth and health. The trials are built to tell these outcomes apart.

Earth field measurements show marine plankton communities accumulating triacylglycerol (TAG) by day and using it at night [LC1]. That mechanism is the one to investigate in warm, dilute fresh water, where the response to the Moon's 29.5-day period is measured in its own right. In *Chlorella*, storage depends on strain and conditions and includes both starch and lipid [LC2], and in another strain a few days of darkness shifted the fatty acids towards saturated forms and slightly lowered the oil content [LC3]. More storage can therefore mean less EPA and DHA, harder fat or a different flavour.

The cycle is 29.53059 Earth days ([shared constants](../../../shared/constants.json)), and the underwater light study on domain/hydrosphere gives the light a phytoplankton community actually receives through it (*product*; register, 4 November). Just below the surface the photosynthetic light stays under 1 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ for 255 hours of each lunar day at the equator, 208 hours at 45° and 123 at 60°, and from 65° poleward the long twilight never lets it fall that low. The twilight carries 0.7% of the lunar day's photosynthetic photons at the equator, 1.5% at 45° and 12% at 75°, and photosynthetic light reaches 71 m in the open sea at the equator as a lunar-day dose. The dark interval of an H1 food web is therefore a property of its latitude, depth and water, and each trial takes it from the site's trace, cloud and mixing included.

## Habitats and the route into food

The habitats come from [the seas' oxygen](../sea_oxygen/README.md) with its mixing brackets, [the sea chemistry](../sea_chemistry/README.md) and [the phosphorus accounts](../../../resources/sea_phosphorus.md), which bound where an H1 food web can live and leave its edible biomass to the trials. The oxygen study ran on forcing interpolated to 301 K on 18 October; the turned design years at the target, with the ground at 301.3 K, now set the climate, and the waters' studies await their rerun on it ([habitats](aquatic_biomes.md)). A warm, oxygenated surface layer above a deep basin can carry the food web whatever the state of the basin's floor. Any added production fits the same phosphorus and oxygen budgets that shellfish and other harvests draw on: the waters' life can spare 21–56 kt of phosphorus a year in all.

| Habitat | Energy pathway | Founder screen | Culinary question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clear, moderately productive lake or sea margin with an oxygenated upper layer | Algae, zooplankton, small schooling fish | *Limnothrissa miodon*; *Stolothrissa tanganicae* as a more specialized comparison | Does a whole-fish lipid cycle give distinct grilled, smoked or [qualified](aquatic_novelty.md#terms) raw products? |
| Productive managed freshwater pond or enclosed bay | Algae and periphyton with measured feeds, to an omnivorous fish | *Oreochromis niloticus* families | Can a freshwater line develop useful muscle fat and a harvest phase that repeats? |
| Flooded forest, river margin or connected lake | Cyclic fruit and seed fall from the land with aquatic prey, to an omnivore | *Colossoma macropomum* as a separate ecological comparator | Can cyclic land production support rich cooked portions without a pelagic food web? |
| Clear carbonate habitat beside productive water | Benthic grazers and invertebrates with a pelagic subsidy, to fish | The carbonate chapter's shortlisted fish | Does the carbonate habitat buffer food scarcity, or synchronize a distinctive pulse of prey? |

The placements are proposals for contained trials. The Tanganyika sardines show that fresh water can hold a pelagic food web of this shape: *Stolothrissa* is the more specialized zooplankton feeder, *Limnothrissa* has a broader diet that changes with life stage, and their nursery habitats differ from the adults' [LC4]. Their complete life cycles, culture methods and performance in each site's temperature and chemistry are the first gates, and each site's 29–32 °C is a test of its own for fish from a tropical lake. A wild line among them meets the rules for what lives wild, returning the phosphorus its harvest takes ([aquatic foods](aquatic_novelty.md#wild-lines-and-the-rules-for-what-lives-wild)).

Tambaqui gives a different comparison, since experiments already examine its physiology under altered food supply and temperature [LC8]. A river and floodplain line passes the carbonate-water screen before any trial in the seas, and whether long fasting changes its meat is one of the questions the trials ask.

Jade perch is a parallel first candidate for the edible-fat trials: the [carbonate chapter](reef_shellfish_cuisine.md#jade-perch-as-the-first-test-animal) records a feeding trial in which its muscle lipid followed dietary lipid. That evidence motivates the choice of founder, and its response to the lunar light cycle is the H1 question.

For early common-garden work, Nile tilapia is an accessible fish with published responses to diet and photoperiod; its fillet omega-3 rose with dietary oil from *Schizochytrium*, a marine heterotroph used as a source of feed [LC5]. Feed for the Moon's trials starts from verified freshwater algal strains with measured fatty-acid profiles, and contained feed production is evaluated on its own.

## What makes the flesh distinctive

Edible fat is a trait of place in the body. Histology across ten teleost species found different distributions of adipocytes and muscle-associated lipid, with Pacific herring and Pacific saury showing analogous deposits from separate ancestries [LC6]; they serve as anatomical comparators for warm freshwater founders. The trials therefore measure the tissue that is served, since whole-body fat says little about white-muscle marbling.

| Trait to measure | Possibility sought | Competing outcome kept in view |
|---|---|---|
| Lipid mass in a standard edible portion; its distribution between myosepta, skin and cells | Fine fat distribution or a naturally richer cut | Fat gathers in the liver and viscera, or in belly fat that is trimmed away |
| TAG, membrane lipid and individual fatty acids | A pleasant mouth-coating and suitable melting | The added storage is saturated fat with a waxy mouthfeel |
| Lipid oxidation and aroma after processing | Clean flavour after freezing, thawing and cooking | Richer portions oxidize faster or keep off-flavours |
| Fibre structure, connective tissue, water retention and gaping | Distinct silky, springy or succulent portions | High lipid comes with poor slice integrity |
| Free amino acids, IMP and related compounds after death | Repeatable savoury differences between cuts or phases | An apparent phase effect is a difference in time since death |

These endpoints are measured beside the fish's welfare, reproduction and feed efficiency, which stay selection traits: a fish can survive a hard cycle and still make poor food or fail to breed.

The night keeps zooplankton, benthic prey, detritus and some chances for visual feeding, and fish can keep daily rhythms of their own: in a tilapia experiment a melatonin rhythm persisted in constant darkness [LC7]. Lunar timing, residual daily timing and the feeding schedule are therefore measured separately, since altered access to food or stress can mimic a lunar storage adaptation.

## A programme the results can refute

**Stage A: primary producers and prey.** Several identified freshwater algal strains and mixed communities are screened before any lipid producer is favoured. An Earth control runs beside the site's trace from the underwater light study, with one treatment matching the cycle's integrated photons and another reproducing the site's actual energy, since the two answer different questions. Nutrient inventory and temperature are held explicit, self-shading is measured, and the light absorbed is told from the light supplied. Each run follows several complete 708.7-hour cycles after acclimation, its length chosen from observed settling.

The measurements are carbon fixation, respiration, viable biomass, starch, TAG, fatty acids, nutrient ratios and recovery after the dark. Grazers enter only after this screen, and the next step tests whether prey production and food quality reach the next trophic level. A high lipid percentage that comes from losing all other biomass gives no more harvestable lipid per area and time. A failure to recover, oxygen depletion or a community shift away from suitable prey rejects the assemblage tested, even if one surviving strain is oily.

**Stage B: light separated from food.** Replicated independent tanks with randomized families cross Earth against lunar light with steady against cyclic food. In the controlled comparison, total ration, protein, energy and fatty-acid supply are matched over the cycle, with ingestion and waste recorded; in a separate ecological treatment, prey availability comes from Stage A. Withholding feed is a treatment of its own, distinct from the lunar environment.

Repeated non-destructive observations are used where possible, and matched siblings for destructive tissue analysis. Tank and family effects are estimated, with the tank as the unit of replication and the fish within it as subsamples. Sex, age, maturity, size, health, activity and intake are recorded. Sampling covers the whole cycle, transitions included, and fits both amplitude and lag, since the transfer from prey to fish and the turnover of tissue may shift the richest flesh well past local sunset or smooth the cycle away. The primary endpoint and analysis are registered before the main trial, and replication is chosen from pilot variance and a difference that matters in practice.

**Stage C: selection with measured pedigrees.** Diverse founder families are established, their identity and parentage verified, and each family tested across environments. Genomic relatedness and estimated breeding values are used beside tissue and sensory phenotypes. Selection takes useful muscle deposition, desirable texture, efficient growth, reproduction over the full cycle and robust performance at night together. An unselected control population is kept and inbreeding limited. A family's performance in one environment may not predict another, so the reaction norm is kept, and genetic associations are predictors that need validation. The programme begins with breeding alone.

**Stage D: preparation and preference.** Standard cuts are compared at the same time since death, chilling history and serving temperature. Raw samples, once their safety process is validated, are paired with fully cooked samples processed identically. Sensory panels are blind to line and harvest phase and record preference, richness, aroma, firmness, juiciness and aftertaste beside the chemistry, telling a detectable difference from a desirable one.

| Result | Decision |
|---|---|
| More prey lipid, unchanged edible-muscle lipid | Keep the ecological finding; reject that line's fatty-fillet mechanism |
| A muscle cycle only under forced cycling of ration | A response to food supply; the natural fishery mechanism remains to be shown |
| A muscle cycle that persists with stable intake under lunar light | Investigate the endocrine and timing mechanism; verify health and reproduction |
| A richer phase that also brings poor growth, liver pathology or loss of texture | Reject the combination or change the breeding objective |
| A repeatable desirable flavour or texture that survives processing, with heritable family differences | Advance the line to larger contained trials |
| No useful phase effect, but a consistently desirable fish | Keep it as a food candidate and withdraw the lunar-phase product |

## Products worth testing

A small pelagic fish that works could give a month of foods: richer grilled and smoked fish in one phase and leaner preparations in another. A larger farmed line could give a prized rich belly and a firmer dorsal cut from the same animal. Sashimi and nigiri are candidate uses once qualified, and H1 succeeds on cooked products as fully as on raw.

For rich flesh that cooks well, the trials compare steaming then grilling, charcoal grilling and glazed preparations in the kabayaki manner, each fish keeping its own species name. Unagi comes from eel, whose founders and spawning route are in `eel_habitat_hatchery.md`. Liver, roe and other coproducts are evaluated separately from the edible yield.

The distinctive result H1 seeks is a freshwater food whose best cut and harvest phase follow a measured lunar energy cycle. It need not imitate tuna, and its richest product may be cooked. Names that fix the phase of peak quality, such as "dusk-fat fish", wait until the trials find the phase and its lag.
