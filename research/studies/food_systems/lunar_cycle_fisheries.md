# Lunar-cycle fisheries: from stored sunlight to edible muscle

Study design, 4 November 2026. **Evidence state: Earth literature synthesis and
testable Terlunan hypotheses.** No lunar fish, sensory trials or fishery yields
have been measured. This note develops option (1), the proposed lunar-cycle
fatfish, and its connections to the other food-system niches.

## The hypothesis and its alternatives

**H1:** In suitable productive waters, the long light cycle could favor a food web
with substantial energy reserves; some fish lineages could store part of that
energy in edible muscle and show repeatable phase-dependent culinary qualities.
Each link needs testing. Long darkness could instead favor starch storage,
dormancy, mixotrophy, metabolic suppression, continued feeding on heterotrophs,
or energy storage in organs outside the fillet. A desirable fatty phase might be
weak, delayed, absent, or accompanied by unacceptable losses of growth and health.

Earth field measurements establish daytime triacylglycerol (TAG) accumulation and
nighttime use in particular marine plankton communities [LC1]. They provide a
mechanism to investigate. Their daily response cannot be multiplied by 29.5 or
assumed to hold in warm, dilute freshwater. Experiments on *Chlorella* show that
storage depends on strain and conditions, and includes both starch and lipids
[LC2]. In another strain, several days of darkness changed fatty-acid proportions
and slightly reduced oil content [LC3]. Greater total storage therefore does not
establish greater EPA/DHA content, more fluid fat, or better flavor.

The period comes from [shared constants](../../../shared/constants.json):
29.53059 Earth days. Approximately 354 hours with the Sun below the horizon does
**not** mean 354 hours without usable light. The [biosphere's twilight
calculations](../../../biosphere/README.md) already distinguish these quantities.
Use local spectral irradiance, latitude, cloud, Earthlight, water attenuation and
mixing trajectories when deriving phytoplankton exposure. Terrestrial canopy
thresholds cannot define an aquatic dark interval.

## Biomes and the route into food

Start with the [301 K sea-oxygen study](../sea_oxygen/README.md), including its
mixing brackets, and the [sea chemistry](../sea_chemistry/README.md) and
[phosphorus accounts](../../../resources/sea_phosphorus.md). Their outputs constrain
habitat; they do not predict edible biomass. The oxygen study's October 18 forcing
is an inherited scenario, distinct from the later GCM and pending reorientation
rerun; its regional numbers are not refreshed forecasts. A warm freshwater surface layer above
a deep basin can support this study without assuming that the basin floor is
habitable. Additional production must fit the same phosphorus and oxygen budgets
used by shellfish and other harvests.

| Biome | Proposed energy pathway | Founder screen | Culinary question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clear, moderately productive lake or sea margin with an oxygenated upper layer | Algae → zooplankton → small schooling fish | *Limnothrissa miodon*; *Stolothrissa tanganicae* as a more specialized comparison | Does a whole-fish lipid cycle produce distinct grilled, smoked or validated raw products? |
| Productive managed freshwater pond or enclosed bay | Algae/periphyton and measured feeds → omnivorous fish | *Oreochromis niloticus* families | Can a viable freshwater lineage develop useful muscle fat distribution and a repeatable harvest phase? |
| Flooded forest, river margin or connected lake | Cyclic terrestrial fruit/seed inputs plus aquatic prey → omnivore | *Colossoma macropomum* as a separate ecological comparator | Can cyclic terrestrial production support rich cooked portions without forcing a pelagic food web? |
| Clear carbonate habitat beside productive water | Benthic grazers and invertebrates plus pelagic subsidy → fish | The reef study's shortlisted fish | Does the reef buffer food scarcity or instead synchronize a distinctive prey pulse? |

These are proposed placements, not release recommendations. The Tanganyika
species demonstrate a freshwater pelagic architecture: *Stolothrissa* is the more
specialized zooplankton feeder; *Limnothrissa* has a broader, life-stage-dependent
diet. Nursery habitats must accompany adult habitat [LC4]. Their complete life
cycles, culture methods and performance at each Terlunan site's temperature and
chemistry remain screening gates. A tropical lake origin alone does not establish
fitness at a particular 29–32°C site.

Tambaqui supplies a different comparison because experiments already examine its
physiology under altered food availability and temperature [LC8]. A river/forest
lineage must pass the carbonate-water compatibility screen before deployment.
Its inclusion does not imply that long fasting improves meat.

Jade perch is a parallel first candidate for edible-fat trials: the
[carbonate-food chapter](reef_shellfish_cuisine.md#why-jade-perch-is-a-particularly-useful-starting-point)
records a primary feeding experiment in which muscle lipid responded to dietary
lipid. That evidence motivates founder selection; its lunar photoperiod response
and any repeatable rich harvest phase remain untested.

For early common-garden work, Nile tilapia offers an experimentally accessible
fish with published dietary and photoperiod responses. Its fillet omega-3 content
responded to dietary oil in an aquaculture experiment [LC5]. The study used
*Schizochytrium* oil: this marine heterotroph is a feed-production precedent,
rather than evidence that it will populate the freshwater sea. Begin with verified
freshwater algal strains and their actual fatty-acid profiles; evaluate contained
feed production separately.

## What makes the flesh distinctive?

Edible fat is a location-dependent trait. Histology across ten teleost species
found different distributions of adipocytes and muscle-associated lipid. Pacific
herring and Pacific saury show analogous deposits despite separate ancestry
[LC6]. They are anatomical comparators, not warm freshwater founders. The evidence
supports measuring the target tissue directly; whole-body fat cannot stand in for
white-muscle marbling.

| Trait to measure | Desired possibility | Competing outcome to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Lipid mass in a standardized edible portion; myoseptal, subcutaneous and intracellular distribution | Fine fat distribution or a naturally richer cut | Fat accumulates mainly in liver/viscera; belly fat is trimmed away |
| TAG, membrane lipid and individual fatty acids | Pleasant mouth-coating and suitable melting behavior | Additional storage is saturated fat or produces waxy mouthfeel |
| Lipid oxidation and aroma after processing | Clean flavor after freezing, thawing and cooking | Richer portions oxidize faster or retain off-flavors |
| Fiber structure, connective tissue, water retention and gaping | Distinct silky, springy or succulent portions | High lipid content accompanies poor slice integrity |
| Free amino acids, IMP and related postmortem compounds | Reproducible savory differences across cuts or phases | Apparent phase effect is actually different postmortem age |

These endpoints are proposed measurements. The fish's welfare, reproduction and
feed efficiency remain selection traits alongside culinary quality. An organism
can survive a difficult cycle while producing poor food or failing to reproduce.

Nights do not remove zooplankton, benthic prey, detrital production or all visual
feeding opportunities. Fish can also retain daily endogenous rhythms: a tilapia
experiment reported a melatonin rhythm persisting in constant darkness [LC7].
Consequently, lunar timing, residual circadian timing and feeding schedule must be
measured separately. Altered food access or stress can imitate a lunar storage
adaptation.

## A falsifiable programme

**Stage A — primary producers and prey.** Screen several identified freshwater
algal strains and mixed communities before choosing a favored lipid producer.
Compare an Earth control with a site-derived lunar irradiance trace. Include a
matched cycle-integrated photon treatment and the actual site-energy treatment;
these answer different questions. Hold nutrient inventory and temperature
explicit, measure self-shading, and distinguish light absorbed from light supplied.
Follow at least several complete cycles after acclimation, with duration selected
from observed settling behavior rather than an assumed number of cycles.

Measure carbon fixation, respiration, viable biomass, starch, TAG, fatty acids,
nutrient ratios and recovery after the dark interval. Add grazers only after this
screen, then test whether prey production and food quality actually reach the
next trophic level. A high lipid percentage caused by loss of all other biomass
does not establish more harvestable lipid per area and time. Failure to recover,
oxygen depletion or a community shift away from suitable prey rejects the tested
assemblage even if one surviving strain is oily.

**Stage B — separate light from food.** Use replicated independent tanks with
randomized families. Cross Earth versus lunar light exposure with steady versus
cyclic food delivery. In a controlled comparison match total ration, protein,
energy and fatty-acid supply over the cycle, while recording ingestion and waste.
In a separate ecological treatment allow prey availability to emerge from Stage A.
Do not impose a two-week fast and call it the natural lunar environment.

Use repeated non-destructive observations where possible and matched sibling
sampling for destructive tissue analyses. Estimate tank and family effects;
individual fish within one tank are not independent treatment replicates. Record
sex, age, maturity, size, health, activity and intake. Sample across the entire
cycle, including transitions, and fit both amplitude and lag. Prey-to-fish transfer
and tissue turnover may shift the richest flesh well past local sunset or smooth
the cycle away. Register the primary endpoint and analysis before the main trial;
choose replication from pilot variance and a practically meaningful difference.

**Stage C — selection with measured pedigrees.** Establish diverse founder
families, verify identity and parentage, and test each family across environments.
Use genomic relatedness and estimated breeding values alongside tissue and sensory
phenotypes. Select useful muscle deposition, desirable texture, efficient growth,
full-cycle reproduction and robust night performance together. Maintain an
unselected control population and limit inbreeding. Family performance in one
environment may not predict performance in another; retain the reaction norm.
Genetic associations are predictors requiring validation, rather than proof of a
causal mechanism. No gene editing is required to begin this programme.

**Stage D — preparation and preference.** Compare standardized cuts at the same
postmortem age, chilling history and serving temperature. Pair identically
processed raw-service samples, only after their safety pathway is validated, with
fully cooked samples. Blind sensory panels to lineage and harvest phase. Record
preference, richness, aroma, firmness, juiciness and aftertaste alongside chemical
measurements. Distinguish a detectable difference from a desirable one.

| Result | Decision |
|---|---|
| More prey lipid, unchanged edible-muscle lipid | Retain ecological finding; reject that lineage's proposed fatty-fillet mechanism |
| Muscle cycle appears only under forced ration cycling | Food-supply response supported; natural fishery mechanism remains unestablished |
| Muscle cycle persists with stable intake under lunar light | Investigate endocrine/timing mechanism and verify health/reproduction |
| Richer phase also causes poor growth, liver pathology or texture loss | Reject the selected combination or change the breeding objective |
| Repeatable desirable flavor/texture survives processing, with heritable family differences | Advance the lineage to larger contained trials |
| No useful phase effect, but a consistently desirable fish | Retain as a food candidate; withdraw the lunar-phase product claim |

## Products worth testing

A successful small pelagic could support a local sequence of richer grilled fish,
smoked products and leaner preparations through the month. A larger farmed lineage
could produce a prized rich belly and a firmer dorsal cut in the same animal.
Sashimi and nigiri remain candidate uses subject to the separate safety programme;
raw eligibility is not a requirement for success here.

For rich flesh that performs better cooked, test steaming followed by grilling,
charcoal grilling and glazed preparations inspired by kabayaki. A non-eel fish
prepared this way should retain its own species identity. True unagi candidates
need their own founder and reproduction assessment, including any marine larval
requirements. Lipid-rich liver, roe or other coproducts require separate evaluation;
they cannot be counted automatically as edible yield.

The proposed distinctive result is **a freshwater food whose best cut and harvest
phase reflect a measured lunar energy cycle**. It need not imitate tuna, and the
richest product may be cooked. Until trials resolve the direction and lag, avoid
names such as “dusk-fat fish” that prematurely fix when peak quality occurs.
