# Culinary evaluation and product qualification

4 November 2026 · proposed product programme for [aquatic foods](aquatic_novelty.md).
No tasting trial, process validation or certification has been performed.

## What a breeding programme should deliver to a cook

The endpoint is an enjoyable, repeatable food with a viable supply chain. More
fat, more glutamate, a faster-growing fish or a mineral-rich habitat is only an
intermediate trait. The [mechanism chapters](aquatic_novelty.md#read-the-study)
give the source evidence; the table below sets proposed product objectives.

| Product | Useful tissue properties to select and measure | Preparations to compare | Main confound to control |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 rich fish, at different cycle phases | Edible-muscle fat distribution, melting behavior, oxidation stability, clean aroma, acceptable firmness | Qualified raw slice, fully cooked nigiri topping, plain skin-on grill | Size, maturity, ration, catch stress, cut and elapsed storage time |
| H2 sweet/savory pale fish | Free taste-active compounds with actual sensory benefit; clean aftertaste and bite | Plain steamed fillet, grilled fillet, qualified raw slice | Salt and sauce can hide the intrinsic difference; tissue chemistry is not a preference score |
| H3 pale fatty fish | Loin/belly contrast, slice cohesion, water retention and favorable prey-derived fat | Thick and thin qualified raw cuts; steamed and roasted portions | Different diets and species can be mistaken for a carbonate-habitat effect |
| Actual eel/unagi | Skin and connective-tissue behavior, fat retention, fillet yield, boning and reheating performance | Grilled, steamed-and-grilled, glazed; first compare unsauced controls | Sauce, cooking loss and cut thickness may dominate the apparent lineage effect |
| Other kabayaki-style fish | Same preparation objectives, judged as their own species | Catfish or jade-perch fillets with a standardized cooking/glazing sequence | A resemblance in preparation does not establish eel identity or identical flavor |
| Prawns and crayfish | Tail yield, sweetness, springiness, molt-stage consistency | Poached/steamed tail, grilled tail, cooked sushi topping | Molt stage, size, cooking loss, added salt and postharvest interval |
| Food-qualified bivalves | Edible yield, tenderness and aroma, with independently acceptable source quality | Steamed meat, broths and other fully cooked products | Filtration and pearl quality do not establish edible quality or safety |

Use plain controls to detect the organism's contribution, then assess culturally
plausible recipes. A product may be excellent with glaze or smoke even if its
plain counterpart is unremarkable. Keep those outcomes separate. Working names
such as “fatfish,” “sweetfish” and “butterperch” describe hypotheses and must not
be treated as recognized species or marketing certifications.

## Measurements and fair comparisons

Use anatomically matched samples and a standardized humane harvest/handling
sequence. Record capture or harvest stress, fish size and sex/maturity, local
light-cycle phase, feed history, sample site, time to chilling, rigor/storage
state, freezing/thawing history and serving temperature. Pair biochemical and
sensory measurements; compare edible portions rather than whole-body averages.

The proposed panel includes lipid classes and fatty acids, location of fat,
thermal behavior, free amino acids, nucleotides and their degradation products,
collagen/connective-tissue characteristics, instrumental firmness, water loss,
color, oxidation and geosmin/2-MIB. None alone measures deliciousness. Calculated
equivalent umami concentration is a chemical index whose sensory usefulness
needs testing in that product matrix.

Randomize coded samples and serving order. Separate trained descriptive testing
from consumer liking. Predefine meaningful differences and estimate uncertainty;
do not turn a significant chemical change into an unmeasured sensory claim.
Repeat across families, cohorts and harvest phases. Use both matched body size
and age-aware analyses when different growth rates make a single comparison
misleading. A useful harvest calendar must be referenced to **local solar phase
and measured food-web lag**, not just the Earth-visible lunar phase.

For raw products, compare the validated freezing/thawing process itself as a
quality factor. If freezing damages one lineage's desired texture, freeze
tolerance becomes part of the selection objective or that cut follows a cooked
route. A cooking process should be measured by its resulting food and validated
thermal exposure, not the appearance of the surface.

## Three qualification states

| State | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Raw-qualified product | A defined lineage/species, source, season or phase, cut and processing chain has the required hazard and quality evidence; qualification can be suspended |
| Cooked-qualified product | The source is acceptable and its defined cooking/handling process is supported; raw use is not established or is not the intended product |
| Food use unqualified or excluded | Insufficient evidence, unacceptable contamination, or a non-food ecological/material organism; cooking is not assumed to make every such product acceptable |

**The food-safety reference is an external benchmark, not a claim of present
Terlunan regulation.** FDA's 2026 model Food Code, §3-402.11, retains specified
time/temperature parasite-destruction schedules; one is freezing at −35 °C until
solid followed by at least 15 hours at −35 °C or colder [C1]. “Flash frozen” alone
does not specify that exposure. FDA's seafood guidance requires attention to
parasite identity, product geometry and freezing process, and notes that unusually
thick fish can need specific validation [C2].

For each product, qualify source conditions, relevant pathogens and parasites,
toxins/chemical contaminants, cold chain and the intended process. Freezing,
curing and surface searing have different functions; none establishes general
raw safety. Natural toxins can remain after cooking or freezing [C3]. Preserve
separate controls for cooked product handling and subsequent contamination.

Ongoing Earth–Terluna exchange belongs in the surveillance assumptions. Test
the relevant source populations and food webs as they change; initial absence
of a parasite is not a permanent property. A family selected for resistance may
still carry an organism without obvious illness. A healthy-looking catch and
clear water do not replace source and process evidence.

The target of Earth-comparable sushi safety needs a **defined comparison**:
species/product and process mix, illness per serving, severe outcomes, uncertainty,
surveillance sensitivity and underreporting. Current evidence does not provide one
universal “sushi risk” number. No raw equivalence is asserted by this branch.
Cooked products remain worthwhile regardless of whether a lineage reaches that
additional qualification.

  especially control discussion pp. 6-6 and 6-8. Targeted PDF reading; specific
  species/source assessment is still needed. These general controls do not imply
  that every toxin behaves identically.
