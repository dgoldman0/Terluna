# The vacuum economy, period by period

A model of the vacuum buildout's economy from 2041 to 2341 whose foundation is the technology of each period. Every year the settlement builds and renews with the engineering of that year, chosen by cost among the options the technology then offers: its towns and cities come from their design studies, its power from the cheapest supply at each kind of site, its machines from its own factories as far as they can make them, its water from the near-Earth sources nearest first, and its money from what Earth's customers pay against what it pays Earth. It is the third approach to this economy. The coupled study in [vacuum_economy](../vacuum_economy/README.md) is closed up as it stands; the compact model in [vacuum_economy_compact](../vacuum_economy_compact/README.md) is kept and not extended.

This is the second version, of 16 November 2026. The first (12c50265) held the residents at the planned 6.47 million once reached and ran to 2261. This one lets them go on growing past the plan wherever resources allow, runs to 2341 with technology held at its 2241 values after 2241, houses growth past the initial quota on the eventual set of crater sites, tests growth as a result decade by decade, keeps the project's conservation set-asides on the near-Earth sources in every case (90% of each body's water left unmined), flies no tug past a practical limit of its own mass, and projects roughly when the sites and the sources' water run out. Three of the design's five open choices (what the money counts, births and fares, towns or cities) are still set to the options recommended on 16 November and carried as switches; the fourth, the shape of growth, was settled that night; the fifth, money in U, was begun ahead of the report and given an exchange rate on 17 November.

```
python3 -m research.studies.vacuum_economy_periods.run                  # a table of every case (several minutes)
python3 -m research.studies.vacuum_economy_periods.run --case central:central:steady
python3 -m research.studies.vacuum_economy_periods.run --exhaustion     # the rough time to exhaustion (about a minute)
python3 -m research.studies.vacuum_economy_periods.run --write          # results/periods.json, about eleven minutes
```

A case on a ramp of residents takes about a fifth of a second; growth as a result searches for its two rates in ten to twenty-five seconds.

The technology of the period decides whether the settlement pays, more than the demand does. With 2041's engineering held for two centuries no scenario pays. On the central path the settlement needs 1.55 trillion dollars of funding, most of it by the opening, has repaid it by 2075 and gives its funders 2.8 trillion of net present value at 3% to 2241 (82.4 trillion undiscounted). On low demand it is repaid in the 2080s or 2090s and then falls back into deficit as its residents pass about two million, since every resident costs the account more than Earth's demand brings in; at the plan's size it runs about 0.2 trillion a year short.

In the central case the engineering changes as the roadmap says it can. Power comes from fission through the 2040s to 2060s, as the power lookup found megawatt reactors the lightest supported supply away from the poles, and from beamed stations from about 2070; the polar grid is never chosen, since its lines grow with the square of their length to sites 600 to 2,700 km away and the poles supply less than half a gigawatt on the central path. A kilowatt-hour costs 3.7 dollars in 2041, 2.7 cents in 2091 and half a cent in 2241; a kilogram landed from Earth 2,800 dollars, then 184 and 47. The settlement makes 98% of its equipment by 2071, after an imported seed and a ramp of twenty years, and buys from Earth mostly the supplies its farms and homes consume. Its tugs fly chemical propellant from the sources' water, on legs that lengthen from 1.1 km/s at the opening to 3.8 km/s by 2241 as the nearest water open to mining is drawn. Its firm power, 189 GW by 2241, comes from a constellation of beamed stations in lunar orbit carrying five times that load, about 950 GW of stations weighing about 4.7 million tonnes at 202 W/kg, with 3.8 million tonnes of receivers on the ground; the stations are first chosen in 2064, and the constellation grows from 22 GW of stations in 2070 (0.3 million tonnes) to 64 GW in 2100, 137 GW in 2150 and 357 GW in 2200 (1.8 million tonnes), most of its growth following the residents in the last fifty years. Of the 189 GW, computing sold to Earth takes about 47 GW and the industry about 47 GW. By 2241 the settlement receives 1.87 trillion dollars a year (computing 0.78, visits 0.65, sport and entertainment 0.28, hosted work 0.13) and pays Earth 1.14 trillion (what partners and Earth's services keep 0.59, the share of residents' pay spent on Earth 0.43, supplies 0.13). In the high scenario the size checks show what its demand implies, 1,000 to 8,000 GW and 1,000 to 23,000 launch pads by 2241, and it does not hold.

The front-loaded ramp, the founding system of 250,000 by year 33, costs more in every case: in the central case 6.25 trillion dollars of funding by 2241 and a net present value of −0.38 trillion, because it builds earlier with dearer engineering for residents whose upkeep the demand does not yet pay.

**Past the plan at the programme's pace.** Carried on at 3.3% a year, the central case reaches 12.4 million residents in 2261, 45.6 million in 2301 and 167 million in 2341, when the eventual set of crater sites is full. Its surplus with Earth falls from 0.73 to 0.19 trillion a year by 2261 and the account is in deficit for good from about 2261 (2266 with fast technology, 2225 with slow), reaching 43 trillion dollars a year by 2341: each resident added costs the account about 100,000 dollars a year, most of it the share of pay spent on Earth and the supplies bought there, while the receipts follow Earth's demand and not the residents. The programme's pace is therefore carried by the account with Earth for about two decades past the plan and not beyond.

### Residents as a result

When the residents are a result, the settlement grows from the opening as fast as it can while its funders still earn the programme's 3% (a net present value at 3% to 2341 that is not negative) and, once its account with Earth is repaid, no ten years together are in deficit, with its own work staffed and its sources' water not exhausted. Steady growth is solved in two stages: the highest single rate to the plan, then the highest single rate past it. Each rate is the highest below which every slower rate the solver checks also passes (since 17 November 2026): a test that a single year can fail, such as the reserve's under a policy of U, can make passing and failing alternate as the growth rate moves a lump of reinvestment from one year to another, and a search by halving alone could then land on a passing rate above a failing one. In the dollar account the answer changed only for the high scenario.

The decade test is what binds: in a first check, a test on the funders' present value alone let the same case grow 4.9% a year to 333 million by 2261 while running 31 trillion dollars a year short at the end, since two centuries of discounting shrink late deficits almost to nothing. The decade window is the study's reading of the rule agreed on 16 November, no yearly deficit once repaid: a single year's reinvestment, such as a change of power supply that the factories cannot yet make in full, would otherwise stop all growth (slow technology's change from fission to beamed stations in 2144 puts that year 0.13 trillion short), while a lasting deficit still fails. In the high scenario the steady rule reaches the plan by 2109 at 11.1% a year with central technology, on 3.4 trillion dollars of funding, and 23.8 million by 2341; the search by halving alone had landed on 16% a year with 98 trillion of funding, a passing rate above failing ones. With fast technology its sales at the depot draw down the water open to mining so fast that the residents can grow only 1.5% a year, a further sign that its demand does not hold. The coupled study's rule, the fastest steady rate period by period, spends the allowance on the first years: the central case grows 30% a year to 2053 and 20% to 2059 and then hardly at all, about 125,000 residents by 2341.

### How long the sites and the water last

The rough time to exhaustion carries each path on, technology still held at 2241, until the water open to mining runs out or lies beyond every tug, or to 3541. It reads the sources' water three ways: the project's set-asides applied to the survey's median (141 billion tonnes of contained water open to mining), the default of every case since 16 November 2026; the set-asides applied to the survey's 5th percentile (60 billion); and, as a comparison, the survey's whole conditional pool at its median (1,413 billion). The set-asides are the only stated ones in the project, the world economy study's synthetic shares from the register's rule of 31 October 2026 not to strip what is needed later: 40% kept for future use, 30% protected and 20% withheld for uncertainty, applied evenly to every body. Water lies beyond every tug where no tug could fetch it without weighing more than five times what it delivers in a year, the study's practical limit. The conservation framework draws water and gases from ordinary icy bodies, and every near-Earth body in the survey is one. The sites are the eventual set of the crater programme, 222,163 sites, the best 2.5% of catalogued craters 200–1,000 m across, beyond which the framework's sixth principle leaves the land unassigned.

### Money in U

Beside the account with Earth the model can carry U, as one unit for the accounts; Terluna's later work splits it into several zones. Under a policy of U the share of trade with Earth settled in U rises from a quarter at the opening to 85% (conservative) or 95% (liberal) ten years later, and Earth's customers and sellers keep a week of it as working balances. Paying in U leaves Earth's purchases from the zone unchanged by default, with a fall of 10% at full settlement as the case of a thin market or a widely swinging U: the evidence on shared monies measures partners who use one money, and moving the zone from the dollar to U puts an exchange between it and Earth. Earth's investors hold U assets when the real yield and U's average yearly change in value over the last five years exceed Earth's safe real rate by 3.5 points, the median premium of emerging markets' currencies, up to 30% of the zone's output; their yield is paid in U and they spend part of it in the zone. The funders take part of their returns in U, spend part of it in the zone and convert the rest over the years. Goods that holders buy with U are charged the lines' average Earth inputs, hands and power.

U's value in dollars, its rate, is a result (17 November 2026). Until the account with Earth is repaid U stays at par and the funders keep the reserve at its target. After that U floats. A year whose dollars would leave the reserve below its minimum lowers U within the year, so that the residents' purchases from Earth, a tenth of their pay valued at the rate, close the gap, down to a floor; below the floor the reserve falls short and fails its test, or in the credit-line cases the funders lend it the rest and their flows carry the loan, as in the dollar account they cover a year's deficit. A year that leaves the reserve above its target raises U back toward par, and only then pays the funders. The year's local work is priced at the year's opening rate, so a lower U makes the settlement's goods and visits cheaper for Earth. The reserve is a basket: a fifth in gold and platinum-group metals, kept first from the zone's own refining, which raises the price of what it still sells, and otherwise bought, earning nothing; the rest in Earth's currencies at Earth's safe real rate of 1%. Its target is the policy's share of Earth's holdings and months of payments, and its minimum the tests'. Months of payments are counted on the last five years' payments with the residents' purchases at par, so that neither a year's lump of equipment nor a fall in U changes what the zone must hold, and the year's tests read the reserve on the same terms. The floor is a setting, run at par, at 75% and at 50% of par. Five tests of a safe and healthy policy, agreed on 16 November 2026, are checked every year: inflation in U at most 3% a year over any decade and never above 5% in a year; reserves of at least 20% of the U held on Earth and six months of payments to Earth; a run withdrawing a quarter of Earth's holdings met from the reserve, its metals at 90% of their value; the yield no higher than the zone's growth over the twenty years before; Earth's holdings no larger than the zone's yearly output, which here is the residents' pay at the rate with what the zone keeps of its sales. Growth as a result under a policy must pass all five in every year, and the funders' measures come from their own flows. The conservative policy is that of 12–13 November (inflation 2%, no yield to investors, a reserve target of half of Earth's holdings and a year of payments, six months above the tests' minimum); the liberal one offers a real yield of 2% held within growth, allows inflation of 3%, keeps its reserve target at the tests' minimum and pays a quarter of the funders' returns in U.

The holders' behaviour rests on two bounded lookups (research drive, `vacuum-economy/research/u_monetary_policy/values.md` of 16 November, 61 sources, and `u_premia_money_trade/values.md` of 17 November, 54). Non-residents hold a mean 22% of emerging markets' local-currency government debt and about 30% of the output of reserve-currency issuers in their debt, which sets the investors' ceiling. Survey-based currency premia over 1996–2018 average 4.2 points a year for emerging markets (median 3.5) and 0.9 for advanced economies, and the smallest OECD currencies earned 5.8% a year against 2.0 for the largest, which sets the investors' threshold. Leaving a currency union cuts trade between the former partners by 0 to 50% (26% in the middle), much of it the decay of colonial ties after independence, with no step change in Ireland's trade with Britain after 1979; invoicing in one's own currency changes how prices and quantities answer exchange-rate moves, not the level of exports, and exchange-rate volatility has little or no effect on trade. Gold makes 16–27% of the world's official reserves at end-2023 and end-2025 prices and 0–7% of most small open economies', with a long-run real return of zero on Jastram's reading and 1.3% a year since 1900. No source records what portfolio holders spend in the issuer, so the 30% of their income spent in the zone is the study's own, as is Earth's safe real rate of 1%.

Letting U fall below par carries more residents, the more so the lower the floor, because a fall lets the residents' purchases from Earth close a gap that would otherwise take the reserve below its minimum. How much a fall can absorb is bounded by those purchases, a tenth of the residents' pay: they are 2% of the payments to Earth in 2091, about 15% in 2141 and about 40% from 2191. A lump of reinvestment early in the period therefore falls on the reserve whatever the floor, and the rate carries the residents' growth late. On the slow path the funders barely earn their 3% in either currency, and the reserve U holds through the build, earning Earth's 1%, costs them that margin, which is why the liberal policy carries fewer residents than dollars there.

The U cases face a stricter test than the dollar account. In dollars the funders cover a year's deficit so long as no ten years together are in deficit. Under U, once the account is repaid, the reserve must hold its minimum in every year with nothing behind it but the rate. The conservative policy, with no savings of its own beyond its reserve, is held back most: given the same backing as the dollar account, a credit line from the funders for what the reserve lacks at the floor, judged by the same decade test, it carries 16.9 million by 2341 against 11.7 million without, and the funders lend 1.4 trillion dollars over the period. The liberal policy never draws on the line.

The liberal policy's lead comes from paying the funders a quarter of their returns in U. They spend 30% of it in the zone on goods charged their Earth inputs, hands and power, and the dollars the zone kept against that U stay with it as savings, which hold the reserve above its minimum through the lumps of reinvestment. Without that spending the liberal policy carries 13.2 million by 2341 at a floor of 75%; with none of the funders' returns in U, 13.8 million; with a tenth, 16.7 million; with half, Earth's holdings pass the zone's yearly output in 2147 and no growth passes. The 30% has no source, so the lead rests on the least certain figure in the layer.

Earth's investors play no part. At the premium a new currency pays, 3.5 points over Earth's safe rate, a yield held within the zone's growth never draws them, and with the small advanced economies' premium of 1 point the liberal policy's 2% still does not. Yields of 6 and 8% in the twenty years after the opening, before any test of growth applies, draw investors who leave when the yield falls back to growth, and change the result by less than a million either way; the most liberal safe policy of the search (`central:central:steady:U_most_liberal_safe`) is the liberal one with a quarter of the funders' returns in U and a yield of 4%, which carries the same 19.6 million as no yield at all, the search taking the higher yield where residents tie. A fall of 10% in Earth's purchases at full settlement, the case of a thin market, costs the liberal policy 1.9 million residents (17.7 million). The reserve's metals are kept from the zone's own refining (3.4 trillion dollars over the period in the liberal case at 75%, against 0.1 trillion bought), and keeping them off Earth's market holds up the price of the rest, so the metals line runs until 2299 where in dollars it stops in the 2110s; against a reserve all in Earth's currencies they move the result by up to a million residents either way (18.4, 19.6, 21.2 and 17.9 million without them at par, 75%, 50% and with the credit line), about the size of the noise that the year-by-year reserve test leaves in these results. Inflation hardly touches the account: 3% rather than 2% takes only what Earth's working balances lose. On the programme's ramp carried on past the plan, U at its floor for decades cannot carry the deficits of 167 million residents, as dollars cannot.

The tests' thresholds, set on 16 November to be rechecked against the evidence, stand on it as follows. The decade bound on inflation, 3%, matches the targets of small open economies (2 ± 1% in the Czech Republic, 3 ± 1% in Chile, 1–3% in Israel and New Zealand); the yearly bound of 5% lies above all their bands, whose highest edge is 4%. Reserves of a fifth of Earth's holdings match the IMF's weights on non-residents' long-term claims (15–20%); six months of payments to Earth is cautious, holding about twice the IMF's emerging-market metric in these cases, where 100–150% of it counts as adequate and three months of imports is the traditional rule. A run of a quarter of Earth's holdings in a year is what emerging Europe lost in 2008; the worst case, Ukraine, lost three fifths, and reserved stablecoins 10–20% in days. Since the reserve is no longer topped up after repayment, the reserve and run tests can now fail; in the solved cases neither does, the reserve's minimum of six months of payments holding it well above a quarter of Earth's holdings, and it is the minimum, held through the lumps of reinvestment, that binds. The yield within growth matches the record: across up to two centuries the interest rate on government borrowing has run below growth by a median 1.3 points in advanced economies and 4.8 in emerging ones, though in advanced economies since 1980 the two have been about level, so the test leaves little room once growth slows. Holdings within a year's output is looser than anything observed, since foreign holdings of reserve-currency debt are about a third of output; Earth's holdings reach at most 0.42 of output in the liberal policy at a floor of 75% (in 2094), so a bound at half of output would not bind on it, while half the funders' returns in U passes even a whole year's output.

## The open choices

Each is carried as a switch; the cases named `central:central:constant_growth:<switch>` in the product measure them one at a time.

1. **What the money counts.** The account with Earth counts the share of residents' pay spent on Earth, 10%. Counting the whole payroll in dollars instead, as the coupled study did, raises the central case's funding needed by 2241 from 1.55 to 9.51 trillion dollars (and to 77 trillion by 2261 in the first version, held at the plan), since by 2241 3.6 million workers at Earth's projected wages cost 4 trillion dollars a year against receipts of 1.87. Raising the share spent on Earth to 20% lowers the net present value to 2241 from 2.80 to 2.72 trillion.
2. **Births and fares.** With births at 1% a year, most residents still arrive from Earth, and they arrive when a one-way passage costs tens of thousands of dollars (1.4 million in 2049, 55,000 in 2141); with no births or with migrants paying their own fares the net present value moves by at most 0.03 trillion.
3. **Towns or cities.** With the four caves of the founding system the only ones assumed, newcomers after it go to towns whatever the rule; with twenty caves the cities take 800,000 more residents and the net present value moves by 0.008 trillion. Towns of 1,000 in place of 750 fill the eventual sites nine years later, at 222 million.
4. **The shape of growth as a result** (settled 16 November 2026): the steady rule as headline with the fastest rule beside; growth past the plan wherever resources allow; the decade test; growth past the initial quota on the eventual set of sites; the projection to 2341.
5. **Money in U** (begun 16 November 2026, given an exchange rate on 17 November, above). The default is still the account with Earth in dollars alone; the conservative and liberal policies run as cases at floors of par, 75% and 50% and with the funders' credit line. Which policy and floor become the default, and whether the funders back the reserve after repayment as they back the dollar account, are open; Earth's holdings are to be split into balances, staked U, bonds and the funders' U next.

## How it is built

**Time and stocks.** The model steps year by year from 2041 (year 0) to 2341 (year 300); the founding deployment is built in years 0 to 7 and the settlement opens in 2049 with 12,500 residents. Capital wears out geometrically, as national accounts treat it: each year a share of each stock equal to one over its life retires and is replaced with that year's engineering. Cohorts retiring all at once would echo their first build for two centuries.

**Technology.** [period_technology.json](period_technology.json) holds 28 quantities as paths through 2241 in a slow, a central and a fast case, with a static case that holds 2041, and six technologies that arrive in dated windows: fusion, a grid from the polar ridges, a mass driver on the Moon, nuclear-electric tugs, chips made on the Moon and a belt of arrays around the equator. Every path holds its 2241 value after 2241. Each value carries its basis: the lookup and source key it was read from on the research drive (engineering, local content and transport of 15 November; food, power and launch, and manufacturing, automation and labour of 16 November), or the study's own projection. The lookups of 16 November changed the roadmap: output per job grows 0.7%, 1.5% or 3% a year (4, 20 or 369 times by 2241), megawatt fission falls from 75 to 12-20 kg/kWe, the industry that builds its own machines doubles in 7, 2 or 1.4 years once established, launch to low orbit floors at 13 dollars a kilogram, a lunar mass driver needs 0.5-0.8 kWh a kilogram, and beamed stations start at the lunar designs' 12 W/kg.

**Residents and settlements.** Residents follow a ramp of the programme, carried on past the plan at its final rate (3.31% a year for constant growth, 1.97% for front-loaded), or a schedule solved as a result, and never outnumber what the sites house. The founding system is the designed one: four lava-tube cities of 50,000 and a hundred crater towns of 500 ([lava_tube_cities](../lava_tube_cities/buildout.md), [vacuum_crater_buildout](../vacuum_crater_buildout/first_hundred.md)). After it, newcomers go to cities while caves remain and otherwise to towns of 750, first on the 8,886 sites of the crater programme's initial quota and then on the further sites of its eventual set, 222,163 in all ([crater_gardens](../../../habitation/crater_gardens/README.md)); the sites hold 167 million. [designs.py](designs.py) reads each design's own quantities (regolith handled, bulk products, metal, pressure components, equipment, the first air and water, floor and loads) and works them out for the year built: farms follow the food path, structures lighten, lamps and crops change the food power. With 2041's technology the founding system returns its design's quantities. Towns built after the founding system put their share of the region's farms and light industry in halls on the surface under three metres of regolith.

**Power.** [engineering.py](engineering.py) prices a firm kilowatt-year at each kind of site from arrays with storage through the 354-hour night, beamed stations, fission, fusion once it arrives, the polar grid once it arrives and within the poles' supply, and the equatorial belt; capacity is added each year from the cheapest at the cities' and the towns' representative distances from a pole.

**Industry and labour.** Six lines (regolith handling, bulk products, metal, manufactured equipment, water at the sources and propellant at the depot) each have a plant mass per tonne a year, an energy, a life and jobs that fall with output per job. Plant is sized a quarter above the work asked of it; the bulk lines are built to the year's work, the factories from a seed bought from Earth and then from their own output no faster than their doubling, and what they cannot yet make is bought finished. Hands go to the settlements' services first, the lines of sale next (sized on the year before's balance) and the industry last; the industry's work beyond the hands left is done from Earth by teleoperation, at an Earth wage times the 1.74 that a 3-second delay cost in a laboratory trial.

**Transport and sources.** Launch from Earth at the year's price, landed at the year's multiple; passengers of 270 kg with a tonne of cabin. Lunar material lifted to the depot by rocket or, once it arrives, by mass driver. Water from the source survey's bodies nearest first ([reach.py](../../../resources/source_access/reach.py)), with 90% of each body's water set aside by default, by the tug that costs least among chemical, electric on water and nuclear-electric, sized by the rocket equation on the leg of the water being mined; no tug is flown that would weigh more than five times the water it delivers in a year, since near the end of a tug's reach its mass grows without bound and the choice by cost, which prices a tug at the settlement's own manufacturing, would otherwise order fleets far faster than its factories can grow. The towns' lakes and the settlements' first stocks fill over ten years. A case's years after its water runs out are not physical, and its summary names the year.

**Sales.** The coupled study's scenarios of demand at their own date ([scenarios.json](../vacuum_economy/scenarios.json)): sport and entertainment, hosted work, computing, propellant and material at the depot, precious metals from the feed, and visits, priced by the coupled study's rule from this model's costs. A line is served in a year when its receipts exceed its annualized costs. Visits and depot sales saturate at bounds the study states, since one elasticity at every price would otherwise sell without limit as costs fall. Earth's income per person goes on growing at the scenario's rate after 2241.

**Money.** The account with Earth, in 2041 dollars: equipment, supplies and hardware bought there and landed, fares, what partners and Earth's services keep, teleoperation, and the share of residents' pay spent on Earth, against Earth's customers' payments. Work on the Moon costs hands, power and plant, which the model counts physically; every choice by cost values the hands it takes at the wage. Under a policy of U, [money.py](money.py) keeps beside the account Earth's holdings of U, U's rate, the reserve of Earth's currencies and metals, the funders' flows in dollars and in U, and the five tests (Money in U, above); the year's local work is priced at the rate, and goods bought with U are charged to the lines' costs.

## What it does not yet do

The rates of work, the plant masses, most paths after their sourced starting values, the mapping of Earth-orbit beaming designs onto lunar orbit, the bounds on visits and depot sales, the share of pay spent on Earth, the set-aside shares of the sources' water, the beamed constellation's factor of five over its load and the tugs' practical limit are the study's own, stated with their bases in [period_inputs.json](period_inputs.json) and [period_technology.json](period_technology.json). Of the central case's receipts in 2241, computing gives 42% on 5 GW of contracted computing in 2049 that no source supports, and visits 35% on a price elasticity constructed between two published values. Technology after 2241 is held, which understates later cases if it goes on improving, and Earth's demand is extrapolated at its scenario's rate to 2341 and, in the projection of exhaustion, to 3541. Money in U treats U as one unit. Its inflation is a setting, and nothing in the model raises U's prices when more is issued; the residents' own holdings of U and the zone's credit to local work are not modelled; the share of holders' income spent in the zone is the study's own. Earth's holdings are still one stock of investment beside the working balances and the funders' U, to be split into staked U, bonds and balances with a run test met together with six months of payments; the dollar case holds no dollars for its residents and firms to pay one another; and the zone trades with no one beyond Earth, a layer [zone_trade.md](zone_trade.md) proposes. The settlement's own economy beyond its needs is not modelled: in the central case only about a tenth of the workforce is needed by 2241 for the work the model counts, and the rest of the residents' work goes to one another, unseen by the account. Carbon and nutrients still come from Earth, though the sources' feed holds them. The metals line's treatment cost decides only whether the line runs and is not charged to the account, so metals kept for a reserve of U enter it at no cost, at a price that holds because they are kept off Earth's market. Power is chosen at one representative distance from a pole for towns and one for cities, and the further sites of the eventual set are taken at the same distance. Lines are served or dropped year by year on their annualized cost, without the coupled study's search for later starts. The time to exhaustion is a rough projection: the set-asides fall evenly on every body, heritage sites and science targets are not taken from the eventual set, and nothing past the vacuum period (the Open Moon, the array, the outer Solar System's buildout) draws on the same sources. The high scenario's results rest on demand the size checks show cannot be met.

## Evidence

The model runs on stated paths and stated inputs, and its results hold only on them; it qualifies no plant, market, price, site or source. The tests check that each path holds its stated values and interpolates, and that the static case holds 2041; that with 2041's technology the founding system returns its design's quantities; that improving technology lowers a resident's food power and built mass; that the power supply chosen is the cheapest offered and that the polar grid and fusion wait for their arrival; that a chemical tug draws more water the farther it flies and stops closing, and that no tug is flown past its practical limit; that the account adds up in every year, with visits within their bound and lines served within their hands; that better technology needs less funding for the plan; that a ramp carries on past the plan at its final rate until the sites are full, and that holding it at the plan reproduces the first version's years before the plan; that newcomers past the initial quota live on the eventual set of sites; that the growth found for residents as a result is the highest the decade test allows, to the plan and past it, and that growth at the programme's pace passes the present-value test and fails the decade test; that setting water aside lengthens the legs and brings exhaustion sooner; that under a policy of U the reserve changes by exactly the year's dollars and the metals kept for it, U stays at par until the account is repaid and between its floor and par after, working balances are a week of the trade settled in U and the funders' measures come from their own flows; that the settings of U leave the dollar account untouched; that a lasting shortfall lowers U so that the reserve holds its minimum while U is above its floor, fails the reserve test with U held at par, and is met by the funders' loan with the credit line; that the five tests catch an unsafe policy (the funders' whole returns in U where the zone's output is small beside its sales, a yield held above growth); that metals kept for the reserve raise the price of those sold; that goods bought with U take the lines' Earth inputs and hands; that the solver returns the highest rate below which every rate it checks passes, and that growth as a result under U is no lower with a lower floor; and that the saved product is current with its sources. With the residents held at the plan and the survey's whole pool, the model returns the first version's product to the last digit for 2041 to 2261 in 30 of its 32 ramp cases; the two high-demand cases with fast technology differ because their nuclear-electric tugs pass the practical limit.
