# The vacuum economy, period by period

A model of the vacuum buildout's economy from 2041 to 2341 whose foundation is the technology of each period. Every year the settlement builds and renews with the engineering of that year, chosen by cost among the options the technology then offers: its towns and cities come from their design studies, its power from the cheapest supply at each kind of site, its machines from its own factories as far as they can make them, its water from the near-Earth sources nearest first, and its money from what Earth's customers pay against what it pays Earth. It is the third approach to this economy. The coupled study in [vacuum_economy](../vacuum_economy/README.md) is closed up as it stands; the compact model in [vacuum_economy_compact](../vacuum_economy_compact/README.md) is kept and not extended.

This is the second version, of 16 November 2026. The first (12c50265) held the residents at the planned 6.47 million once reached and ran to 2261. This one lets them go on growing past the plan wherever resources allow, runs to 2341 with technology held at its 2241 values after 2241, houses growth past the initial quota on the eventual set of crater sites, tests growth as a result decade by decade, and projects roughly when the sites and the sources' water run out under the project's conservation set-asides. Three of the design's five open choices (what the money counts, births and fares, towns or cities) are still set to the options recommended on 16 November and carried as switches; the fourth, the shape of growth, was settled that night; the fifth, money in U, follows the report.

```
python3 -m research.studies.vacuum_economy_periods.run                  # a table of every case (about two minutes)
python3 -m research.studies.vacuum_economy_periods.run --case central:central:steady
python3 -m research.studies.vacuum_economy_periods.run --exhaustion     # the rough time to exhaustion (about a minute)
python3 -m research.studies.vacuum_economy_periods.run --write          # results/periods.json, about two and a quarter minutes
```

A case on a ramp of residents takes about a fifth of a second; growth as a result searches for its two rates in about six seconds.

## What it shows

**To the plan.** On the programme's constant-growth ramp, 12,500 residents at the opening in 2049 growing 3.3% a year to the planned 6.47 million in 2241, in trillions of 2041 dollars:

| Central | held at 2041 · slow · central · fast | 743 · 2.87 · 1.54 · 1.21 | 1.55 · 1.42 · 1.05 · 1.21 | −9.28 · −0.84 · 2.80 · 4.12 | never · 2115 · 2075 · 2066 | −26.0 · −0.05 · +0.73 · +0.95 |
| High | held at 2041 · slow · central · fast | 599 · 4.79 · 3.15 · 2.45 | 1.59 · 1.46 · 1.13 · 2.45 | −1.67 · 16.3 · 28.9 · 12.6 | 2094 · 2090 · 2068 · 2059 | −31.1 · +16.5 · +20.3 · +35.1 |

The technology of the period decides whether the settlement pays, more than the demand does. With 2041's engineering held for two centuries no scenario pays. On the central path the settlement needs 1.54 trillion dollars of funding, most of it by the opening, has repaid it by 2075 and gives its funders 2.8 trillion of net present value at 3% to 2241 (82.6 trillion undiscounted). On low demand it is repaid in the 2080s or 2090s and then falls back into deficit as its residents pass about two million, since every resident costs the account more than Earth's demand brings in; at the plan's size it runs about 0.2 trillion a year short.

In the central case the engineering changes as the roadmap says it can. Power comes from fission through the 2040s to 2060s, as the power lookup found megawatt reactors the lightest supported supply away from the poles, and from beamed stations from about 2070; the polar grid is never chosen, since its lines grow with the square of their length to sites 600 to 2,700 km away and the poles supply less than half a gigawatt on the central path. A kilowatt-hour costs 3.7 dollars in 2041, 2.7 cents in 2091 and half a cent in 2241; a kilogram landed from Earth 2,800 dollars, then 184 and 47. The settlement makes 98% of its equipment by 2071, after an imported seed and a ramp of twenty years, and buys from Earth mostly the supplies its farms and homes consume. Its tugs fly chemical propellant from the sources' water, on legs that lengthen from 0.8 km/s at the opening to 3.0 km/s by 2241 as the nearest water is drawn. By 2241 it receives 1.87 trillion dollars a year (computing 0.78, visits 0.65, sport and entertainment 0.28, hosted work 0.13) and pays Earth 1.14 trillion (what partners and Earth's services keep 0.59, the share of residents' pay spent on Earth 0.43, supplies 0.13). In the high scenario the size checks show what its demand implies, 1,000 to 8,000 GW and 780 to 7,000 launch pads by 2241, and it does not hold.

The front-loaded ramp, the founding system of 250,000 by year 33, costs more in every case: in the central case 6.02 trillion dollars of funding by 2241 and a net present value of −0.29 trillion, because it builds earlier with dearer engineering for residents whose upkeep the demand does not yet pay.

**Past the plan at the programme's pace.** Carried on at 3.3% a year, the central case reaches 12.4 million residents in 2261, 45.6 million in 2301 and 167 million in 2341, when the eventual set of crater sites is full. Its surplus with Earth halves by 2261 and the account is in deficit for good from about 2265 (2272 with fast technology, 2234 with slow), reaching 34 trillion dollars a year by 2341: each resident added costs the account about 100,000 dollars a year, most of it the share of pay spent on Earth and the supplies bought there, while the receipts follow Earth's demand and not the residents. The programme's pace is therefore carried by the account with Earth for two or three decades past the plan and not beyond.

### Residents as a result

When the residents are a result, the settlement grows from the opening as fast as it can while its funders still earn the programme's 3% (a net present value at 3% to 2341 that is not negative) and, once its account with Earth is repaid, no ten years together are in deficit, with its own work staffed and its sources' water not exhausted. Steady growth is solved in two stages: the highest single rate to the plan, then the highest single rate past it.

| Case | Growth to the plan · past it | Reaches 6.47 million | Residents in 2241 · 2341 | Funding needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Central demand, central technology | 6.69% · 0.57% a year | 2146 | 11.1 million · 19.4 million | 1.66 |
| Central demand, fast technology | 9.58% · 0.61% a year | 2118 | 13.7 million · 25.1 million | 1.28 |
| Central demand, slow technology | 2.35% · 0.94% a year | 2319 | 1.08 million · 7.94 million | 2.37 |
| Low demand, fast technology | 1.89% a year | not reached | 0.45 million · 2.93 million | 0.91 |
| Low demand, central or slow technology | none | not reached | 12,500 · 12,500 | 1.15 · 1.68 |

On central demand and technology the settlement reaches the plan 95 years before the ramp does and goes on growing to about 19 million by 2341, where its account with Earth comes to balance. The decade test is what binds: a test on the funders' present value alone would let the same case grow 4.9% a year to 333 million by 2261 while running 31 trillion dollars a year short at the end, since two centuries of discounting shrink late deficits almost to nothing. The decade window is the study's reading of the rule agreed on 16 November, no yearly deficit once repaid: a single year's reinvestment, such as a change of power supply that the factories cannot yet make in full, would otherwise stop all growth (slow technology's change from fission to beamed stations in 2144 puts that year 0.13 trillion short), while a lasting deficit still fails. In the high scenario the steady rule spends the funders' whole margin on reaching the plan, at 16% a year with central technology (103 trillion dollars of funding) and at the search's 30% ceiling with fast; these are further signs that its demand does not hold. The coupled study's rule, the fastest steady rate period by period, spends the allowance on the first years: the central case grows 30% a year to 2053 and 21% to 2059 and then hardly at all, about 127,000 residents by 2341.

### How long the sites and the water last

The rough time to exhaustion carries each path on, technology still held at 2241, until the water open to mining runs out or lies beyond every tug, or to 3541. It reads the sources' water three ways: the survey's whole conditional pool at its median (1,413 billion tonnes of contained water), the project's set-asides applied to the median (141 billion tonnes open to mining), and the set-asides applied to the survey's 5th percentile (60 billion). The set-asides are the only stated ones in the project, the world economy study's synthetic shares from the register's rule of 31 October 2026 not to strip what is needed later: 40% kept for future use, 30% protected and 20% withheld for uncertainty, applied evenly to every body. The conservation framework draws water and gases from ordinary icy bodies, and every near-Earth body in the survey is one. The sites are the eventual set of the crater programme, 222,163 sites, the best 2.5% of catalogued craters 200–1,000 m across, beyond which the framework's sixth principle leaves the land unassigned.

At the programme's pace the eventual sites fill in 2341, or in 2350 at 222 million in towns of 1,000, and with the set-asides the water open to mining runs out within a few years of the sites filling, or forty years before on the low estimate; without them it lasts into the 2900s. Growth as a result never fills the sites, since the account stops it at 8 to 25 million, and at that size the water lasts more than a thousand years without set-asides and about 250 to 450 years past 2341 with them (on growth solved under the set-asides themselves, which make water dearer: 16.3 million by 2341 on the median and 8.2 million on the 5th percentile in the central case). In every path the account with Earth binds before any resource does. Lunar regolith and metal do not bind: the buildout handles a few hundred tonnes of rock a resident, small beside the Moon's crust. Carbon and nutrients are bought from Earth in this model and so draw no lunar or near-Earth stock.

## The open choices

Each is carried as a switch; the cases named `central:central:constant_growth:<switch>` in the product measure them one at a time.

1. **What the money counts.** The account with Earth counts the share of residents' pay spent on Earth, 10%. Counting the whole payroll in dollars instead, as the coupled study did, raises the central case's funding needed by 2241 from 1.54 to 9.35 trillion dollars and by 2261 to 77.4 trillion, since by 2241 3.6 million workers at Earth's projected wages cost 4 trillion dollars a year against receipts of 1.87. Raising the share spent on Earth to 20% lowers the net present value to 2241 from 2.80 to 2.72 trillion.
2. **Births and fares.** With births at 1% a year, most residents still arrive from Earth, and they arrive when a one-way passage costs tens of thousands of dollars (1.4 million in 2049, 55,000 in 2141); with no births or with migrants paying their own fares the net present value moves by at most 0.03 trillion.
3. **Towns or cities.** With the four caves of the founding system the only ones assumed, newcomers after it go to towns whatever the rule; with twenty caves the cities take 800,000 more residents and the net present value moves by 0.008 trillion. Towns of 1,000 in place of 750 fill the eventual sites nine years later, at 222 million.
4. **The shape of growth as a result** (settled 16 November 2026): the steady rule as headline with the fastest rule beside; growth past the plan wherever resources allow; the decade test; growth past the initial quota on the eventual set of sites; the projection to 2341.
5. **Money in U** (after the report). Money here is the account with Earth in 2041 dollars and no U is carried. The vacuum economy's money moves from the dollar toward U during the period, and a study of U's policy, from the conservative case studied so far to a more liberal one that stays safe and healthy, follows the report: it will count the instruments that could lead Earth's investors to hold U, such as interest-bearing bonds or staking, and what those holdings buy from the U zone, so it bears on the account itself. In the coupled study credit in U changed the funding by at most 0.25 trillion dollars.

## How it is built

**Time and stocks.** The model steps year by year from 2041 (year 0) to 2341 (year 300); the founding deployment is built in years 0 to 7 and the settlement opens in 2049 with 12,500 residents. Capital wears out geometrically, as national accounts treat it: each year a share of each stock equal to one over its life retires and is replaced with that year's engineering. Cohorts retiring all at once would echo their first build for two centuries.

**Technology.** [period_technology.json](period_technology.json) holds 28 quantities as paths through 2241 in a slow, a central and a fast case, with a static case that holds 2041, and six technologies that arrive in dated windows: fusion, a grid from the polar ridges, a mass driver on the Moon, nuclear-electric tugs, chips made on the Moon and a belt of arrays around the equator. Every path holds its 2241 value after 2241. Each value carries its basis: the lookup and source key it was read from on the research drive (engineering, local content and transport of 15 November; food, power and launch, and manufacturing, automation and labour of 16 November), or the study's own projection. The lookups of 16 November changed the roadmap: output per job grows 0.7%, 1.5% or 3% a year (4, 20 or 369 times by 2241), megawatt fission falls from 75 to 12-20 kg/kWe, the industry that builds its own machines doubles in 7, 2 or 1.4 years once established, launch to low orbit floors at 13 dollars a kilogram, a lunar mass driver needs 0.5-0.8 kWh a kilogram, and beamed stations start at the lunar designs' 12 W/kg.

**Residents and settlements.** Residents follow a ramp of the programme, carried on past the plan at its final rate (3.31% a year for constant growth, 1.97% for front-loaded), or a schedule solved as a result, and never outnumber what the sites house. The founding system is the designed one: four lava-tube cities of 50,000 and a hundred crater towns of 500 ([lava_tube_cities](../lava_tube_cities/buildout.md), [vacuum_crater_buildout](../vacuum_crater_buildout/first_hundred.md)). After it, newcomers go to cities while caves remain and otherwise to towns of 750, first on the 8,886 sites of the crater programme's initial quota and then on the further sites of its eventual set, 222,163 in all ([crater_gardens](../../../habitation/crater_gardens/README.md)); the sites hold 167 million. [designs.py](designs.py) reads each design's own quantities (regolith handled, bulk products, metal, pressure components, equipment, the first air and water, floor and loads) and works them out for the year built: farms follow the food path, structures lighten, lamps and crops change the food power. With 2041's technology the founding system returns its design's quantities. Towns built after the founding system put their share of the region's farms and light industry in halls on the surface under three metres of regolith.

**Power.** [engineering.py](engineering.py) prices a firm kilowatt-year at each kind of site from arrays with storage through the 354-hour night, beamed stations, fission, fusion once it arrives, the polar grid once it arrives and within the poles' supply, and the equatorial belt; capacity is added each year from the cheapest at the cities' and the towns' representative distances from a pole.

**Industry and labour.** Six lines (regolith handling, bulk products, metal, manufactured equipment, water at the sources and propellant at the depot) each have a plant mass per tonne a year, an energy, a life and jobs that fall with output per job. Plant is sized a quarter above the work asked of it; the bulk lines are built to the year's work, the factories from a seed bought from Earth and then from their own output no faster than their doubling, and what they cannot yet make is bought finished. Hands go to the settlements' services first, the lines of sale next (sized on the year before's balance) and the industry last; the industry's work beyond the hands left is done from Earth by teleoperation, at an Earth wage times the 1.74 that a 3-second delay cost in a laboratory trial.

**Transport and sources.** Launch from Earth at the year's price, landed at the year's multiple; passengers of 270 kg with a tonne of cabin. Lunar material lifted to the depot by rocket or, once it arrives, by mass driver. Water from the source survey's bodies nearest first ([reach.py](../../../resources/source_access/reach.py)), less any share set aside, by the tug that costs least among chemical, electric on water and nuclear-electric, sized by the rocket equation on the leg of the water being mined; the towns' lakes and the settlements' first stocks fill over ten years. A case's years after its water runs out are not physical, and its summary names the year.

**Sales.** The coupled study's scenarios of demand at their own date ([scenarios.json](../vacuum_economy/scenarios.json)): sport and entertainment, hosted work, computing, propellant and material at the depot, precious metals from the feed, and visits, priced by the coupled study's rule from this model's costs. A line is served in a year when its receipts exceed its annualized costs. Visits and depot sales saturate at bounds the study states, since one elasticity at every price would otherwise sell without limit as costs fall. Earth's income per person goes on growing at the scenario's rate after 2241.

**Money.** The account with Earth, in 2041 dollars: equipment, supplies and hardware bought there and landed, fares, what partners and Earth's services keep, teleoperation, and the share of residents' pay spent on Earth, against Earth's customers' payments. Work on the Moon costs hands, power and plant, which the model counts physically; every choice by cost values the hands it takes at the wage.

## What it does not yet do

The rates of work, the plant masses, most paths after their sourced starting values, the mapping of Earth-orbit beaming designs onto lunar orbit, the bounds on visits and depot sales, the share of pay spent on Earth and the set-aside shares of the sources' water are the study's own, stated with their bases in [period_inputs.json](period_inputs.json) and [period_technology.json](period_technology.json). Of the central case's receipts in 2241, computing gives 42% on 5 GW of contracted computing in 2049 that no source supports, and visits 35% on a price elasticity constructed between two published values. Technology after 2241 is held, which understates later cases if it goes on improving, and Earth's demand is extrapolated at its scenario's rate to 2341 and, in the projection of exhaustion, to 3541. Money is in dollars and carries no U, its policy or Earth's holdings of it. The settlement's own economy beyond its needs is not modelled: in the central case only about a tenth of the workforce is needed by 2241 for the work the model counts, and the rest of the residents' work goes to one another, unseen by the account. Carbon and nutrients still come from Earth, though the sources' feed holds them. Power is chosen at one representative distance from a pole for towns and one for cities, and the further sites of the eventual set are taken at the same distance. Lines are served or dropped year by year on their annualized cost, without the coupled study's search for later starts. The time to exhaustion is a rough projection: the set-asides fall evenly on every body, heritage sites and science targets are not taken from the eventual set, and nothing past the vacuum period (the Open Moon, the array, the outer Solar System's buildout) draws on the same sources. The high scenario's results rest on demand the size checks show cannot be met.

## Evidence

The model runs on stated paths and stated inputs, and its results hold only on them; it qualifies no plant, market, price, site or source. The tests check that each path holds its stated values and interpolates, and that the static case holds 2041; that with 2041's technology the founding system returns its design's quantities; that improving technology lowers a resident's food power and built mass; that the power supply chosen is the cheapest offered and that the polar grid and fusion wait for their arrival; that a chemical tug draws more water the farther it flies and stops closing; that the account adds up in every year, with visits within their bound and lines served within their hands; that better technology needs less funding for the plan; that a ramp carries on past the plan at its final rate until the sites are full, and that holding it at the plan reproduces the first version's years before the plan; that newcomers past the initial quota live on the eventual set of sites; that the growth found for residents as a result is the highest the decade test allows, to the plan and past it, and that growth at the programme's pace passes the present-value test and fails the decade test; that setting water aside lengthens the legs and brings exhaustion sooner; and that the saved product is current with its sources. With the residents held at the plan the model returns the first version's product to the last digit for 2041 to 2261.
