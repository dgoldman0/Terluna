# The vacuum economy, period by period

A model of the vacuum buildout's economy from 2041 to 2341 whose foundation is the technology of each period. Every year the settlement builds and renews with the engineering of that year, chosen by cost among the options the technology then offers: its towns and cities come from their design studies, its power from the cheapest supply at each kind of site, its machines from its own factories as far as they can make them, its water from the near-Earth sources nearest first, and its money from what Earth's customers pay against what it pays Earth. It is the third approach to this economy. The coupled study in [vacuum_economy](../vacuum_economy/README.md) is closed up as it stands; the compact model in [vacuum_economy_compact](../vacuum_economy_compact/README.md) is kept and not extended.

This is the second version, of 16 November 2026. The first (12c50265) held the residents at the planned 6.47 million once reached and ran to 2261. This one lets them go on growing past the plan wherever resources allow, runs to 2341 with technology held at its 2241 values after 2241, houses growth past the initial quota on the eventual set of crater sites, tests growth as a result decade by decade, keeps the project's conservation set-asides on the near-Earth sources in every case (90% of each body's water left unmined), builds no tug past a practical limit of its own mass, and projects roughly when the sites and the sources' water run out. Three of the design's five open choices (what the money counts, births and fares, towns or cities) are still set to the options recommended on 16 November and carried as switches; the fourth, the shape of growth, was settled that night; the fifth, money in U, was begun ahead of the report, given an exchange rate on 17 November and made the default the same day: the liberal policy of U at a floor of 75% of par with the funders' credit line behind its reserve, the account in dollars alone kept as the comparison. Since 17 November the tug fleet also turns over as fleets of heavy equipment do: a new kind of tug comes in as fast as the factories learn to build it, and the old tugs fly on until they wear out or are sold. Every figure below is under that default unless it is named as dollars alone or as another policy. The report written from these results for informed general readers, *The enclosed lunar buildout, 2041–2341: how its economy could work*, is [scoping_study.md](scoping_study.md) (since 18 November in that general form, in dollars of 2026; earlier a scoping study for prospective partners), its measures [scoping.py](scoping.py) and `results/scoping.json`. The measures include the funders' repayment year and rate of return for every case and sensitivity, the returns by growth path, Earth's purchases a tenth, a quarter and a half fewer when paid in U, the base case's account by period and what the founding capital buys. Beside it, [rd_fund.md](rd_fund.md) proposes a fund of 100 million dollars for the research and development of 2027–2041 that comes before the build: a short document standing on its own, on what is known, what remains to be learned and how the fund closes each gap, whose budget and earnings rest on a bounded lookup of 17 November (research drive, `vacuum-economy/research/rd_fund/`, 80 sources). [visualization/vacuum-economy-periods/report.py](../../../visualization/vacuum-economy-periods/report.py) draws the study's figures and builds both as PDFs.

```

python3 -m research.studies.vacuum_economy_periods.run --case central:central:steady
```

The technology of the period decides whether the settlement pays, more than the demand does. With 2041's engineering held for two centuries no scenario pays. On the central path the settlement needs 1.63 trillion dollars of funding, most of it by the opening, has repaid it by 2076 and gives its funders 2.65 trillion of net present value at 3% to 2241 (82.6 trillion undiscounted). In dollars alone it needs 1.55 trillion, is repaid by 2075 and gives 2.81 trillion: under U the funders build the reserve during the build, where it earns Earth's 1% against their 3%, and every case on the ramp needs a little more funding for it. On low demand the settlement is repaid in 2083 with fast technology or 2100 with central and then falls back into deficit, from 2141 at a quarter of a million residents on the central path and from 2207 at 2.1 million on the fast, since every resident costs the account more than Earth's demand brings in; at the plan's size it runs about 0.15 trillion a year short.

In the central case the engineering changes as the roadmap says it can. Power comes from fission through the 2040s to 2060s, as the power lookup found megawatt reactors the lightest supported supply away from the poles, and from beamed stations from about 2070; the polar grid is never chosen, since its lines grow with the square of their length to sites 600 to 2,700 km away and the poles supply less than half a gigawatt on the central path. A kilowatt-hour costs 3.7 dollars in 2041, 2.7 cents in 2091 and half a cent in 2241; a kilogram landed from Earth 2,800 dollars, then 184 and 47. The settlement makes 98% of its equipment by 2071, after an imported seed and a ramp of twenty years, and buys from Earth mostly the supplies its farms and homes consume. Its tugs fly chemical propellant from the sources' water, on legs that lengthen from 1.0 km/s at the opening to 3.8 km/s by 2241 as the nearest water open to mining is drawn. Its firm power, 190 GW by 2241, comes from a constellation of beamed stations in lunar orbit carrying five times that load, about 950 GW of stations weighing about 4.7 million tonnes at 202 W/kg, with 3.8 million tonnes of receivers on the ground; the stations are first chosen in 2064, and the constellation grows from 23 GW of stations in 2070 to 67 GW in 2100, 141 GW in 2150 and 362 GW in 2200, most of its growth following the residents in the last fifty years. Of the 190 GW, computing sold to Earth takes about 47 GW and the industry about 47 GW. By 2241 the settlement receives 1.94 trillion dollars a year (computing 0.78, visits 0.65, sport and entertainment 0.28, hosted work 0.13, metals 0.07) and pays Earth 1.16 trillion (what partners and Earth's services keep 0.58, the share of residents' pay spent on Earth 0.43, supplies 0.12, and 0.02 for the Earth inputs of what U's holders buy in the zone). In dollars alone the engineering is the same to within the metals line's power (188.5 GW), the metals line stops in 2204 without the reserve's demand for its metals, and the settlement receives 1.87 trillion and pays 1.15 trillion, 0.01 of it for the dollars its residents and firms add to what they hold. In the high scenario the size checks show what its demand implies, 1,000 to 8,000 GW and 1,000 to 30,000 launch pads by 2241, and it does not hold.

The front-loaded ramp, the founding system of 250,000 by year 33, costs more in every case: in the central case 6.26 trillion dollars of funding by 2241 and a net present value of −0.40 trillion, because it builds earlier with dearer engineering for residents whose upkeep the demand does not yet pay.

**Past the plan at the programme's pace.** Carried on at 3.3% a year, the central case reaches 12.4 million residents in 2261, 45.6 million in 2301 and 167 million in 2341, when the eventual set of crater sites is full. Its surplus with Earth falls from 0.78 to 0.44 trillion a year by 2261 and the account is in deficit for good from about 2260 (2270 with fast technology, 2228 with slow), reaching 40 trillion dollars a year by 2341 with U at its floor (48 trillion in dollars alone). Each resident added after 2261 raises the payments to Earth by about 280,000 dollars a year by 2341: half of it is the share of pay spent on Earth, and most of the rest is tugs and drives bought from Earth, since the water left by then lies near the end of every tug's reach. The receipts follow Earth's demand and not the residents, rising by about 19,000 dollars a year for each resident added. U stands at its floor for the last 61 years and the funders lend its reserve 766 trillion dollars by 2341. The programme's pace is therefore carried by the account with Earth for about two decades past the plan and not beyond.

### Residents as a result

When the residents are a result, the settlement grows from the opening as fast as it can while its funders still earn the programme's 3% (a net present value at 3% to 2341 that is not negative) and, once its account with Earth is repaid, no ten years together are in deficit, with its own work staffed and its sources' water not exhausted. Steady growth is solved in two stages: the highest single rate to the plan, then the highest single rate past it. Each rate is the highest below which every slower rate the solver checks also passes (since 17 November 2026): a test that a single year can fail, such as the reserve's under a policy of U, can make passing and failing alternate as the growth rate moves a lump of reinvestment from one year to another, and a search by halving alone could then land on a passing rate above a failing one. In the dollar account the answer changed only for the high scenario.

On central demand and technology the settlement reaches the plan 99 years before the ramp does and goes on growing to about 20 million by 2341, where its account with Earth has fallen into deficit and U stands at 0.81 of par; U's lowest, 0.77, came in 2137–2144, while the settlements built fastest toward the plan. With the survey's whole pool of water (the `whole_pool` comparison) it would reach the plan in 2139 and 24.4 million by 2341. In dollars alone it reaches the plan in 2150 and about 16 million by 2341, where its account comes to balance. U carries more residents wherever the settlement grows at all except on the slow path, where the funders barely earn their 3% and the reserve U holds through the build, earning Earth's 1%, costs them that margin, and on high demand with fast technology, below. The gap is widest with fast technology. In dollars alone the decade of fastest building toward the plan, 2115 to 2124, comes out barely in deficit, since the tug fleet must grow by two-thirds in six years as the legs lengthen; with no reserve to carry that decade almost no growth past the plan passes the decade test, and the settlements stop at about 6.6 million. Under U the reserve carries the same decade and they reach 30.2 million. The decade test is what binds: in a first check, a test on the funders' present value alone let the central case grow 4.9% a year to 333 million by 2261 while running 31 trillion dollars a year short at the end, since two centuries of discounting shrink late deficits almost to nothing. The decade window is the study's reading of the rule agreed on 16 November, no yearly deficit once repaid: a single year's reinvestment, such as a change of power supply that the factories cannot yet make in full, would otherwise stop all growth (slow technology's change from fission to beamed stations in 2144 puts that year 0.25 trillion short), while a lasting deficit still fails. In the high scenario the steady rule reaches the plan by 2111 at 10.7% a year with central technology, on 3.9 trillion dollars of funding, and 20.2 million by 2341, no more than central demand carries, since its sales at the depot draw down the water open to mining (in dollars alone 2109 at 11.1% a year, 3.4 trillion and 20.1 million; there the search by halving alone had landed on 16% a year with 98 trillion of funding, a passing rate above failing ones). With fast technology those sales draw the water down so fast that no growth passes under U and the residents can grow only 1.6% a year in dollars alone, a further sign that its demand does not hold. The coupled study's rule, the fastest steady rate period by period, spends the allowance on the first years: the central case grows 30% a year to 2053 and 20% to 2059 and then hardly at all, about 130,000 residents by 2341.

### What moves the result

Moved one at a time from the base case, the inputs change the residents carried by 2341 (20.1 million) as follows (`results/scoping.json`, 17 November 2026). Computing contracted at the opening moves it most: 1 GW in place of 5 carries 8.1 million and 20 GW 48.2 million, since the computing line grows with Earth's output from what is contracted at the opening; the computing line and the price response of visits now take the options `computing_W_2049` and `visits_price_elasticity`, and the response of visits within its published range of 0.5 to 2 carries 19.4 and 20.6 million. The whole technology stack (slow 5.9, fast 30.2 million), Earth's demand as a whole (low: no growth; high: 20.2 million, held to the central case's residents by the water open to mining) and the share of pay spent on Earth (20%: 11.8; 5%: 30.9 million) follow.

The technology stack is now also moved group by group ([technology.py](technology.py) `GROUPS`: launch; tugs and the sources; power; local industry with equipment and output per job; food and life support; computing), a case such as `central|power=slow` taking one group's slow or fast path with the rest central. On the slow side, launch alone stops all growth (the founding system repays in 2108 but the funders earn 2.9%), local industry alone delays the plan to 2174 (12.9 million by 2341), food and life support alone costs 1.4 million residents (18.7 million, plan 2143) and a fifth of the funders' present value at 3% (0.64 trillion dollars against 0.79), power alone barely changes the residents (19.2 million, plan 2148) but cuts that present value to 0.36 trillion, and tugs and the sources move little (19.7 million). Computation per watt changes nothing because the model sells computing by the watt and reads no such path. On the fast side, local industry alone brings the plan to 2130 and carries 22.6 million by 2341. Launch alone brings the plan to 2134 and nearly doubles the funders' present value (1.54 trillion), but carries 18.9 million by 2341: its growth past the plan, 0.52% a year against the base case's 0.57%, lies at the edge of the spread the solver returns for neighbouring cases (0.54% to 0.60% for every other group's slow and fast paths but slow industry's). The other groups' fast paths move the residents by less than half a million. Under the fleet of 16 November, which switched all new building to a new kind of tug at once and bought what the factories could not yet make finished from Earth, slow food and life support nearly stopped growth past the plan (7.7 million by 2341) and fast launch or local industry carried fewer residents than the base case (15.9 and 16.6 million); both results changed with the fleet's gradual turnover and the smoothly lengthening legs of 17 November.

### How long the sites and the water last

The rough time to exhaustion carries each path on, technology still held at 2241, until the water open to mining runs out or lies beyond every tug, or to 3541. It reads the sources' water three ways: the project's set-asides applied to the survey's median (141 billion tonnes of contained water open to mining), the default of every case since 16 November 2026; the set-asides applied to the survey's 5th percentile (60 billion); and, as a comparison, the survey's whole conditional pool at its median (1,413 billion). The set-asides are the only stated ones in the project, the world economy study's synthetic shares from the register's rule of 31 October 2026 not to strip what is needed later: 40% kept for future use, 30% protected and 20% withheld for uncertainty, applied evenly to every body. Water lies beyond every tug where no tug could fetch it without weighing more than five times what it delivers in a year, the study's practical limit. The conservation framework draws water and gases from ordinary icy bodies, and every near-Earth body in the survey is one. The sites are the eventual set of the crater programme, 222,163 sites, the best 2.5% of catalogued craters 200–1,000 m across, beyond which the framework's sixth principle leaves the land unassigned.

### Money in U

The model carries U beside the account with Earth, by default since 17 November 2026, as one unit for the accounts; Terluna's later work splits it into several zones. Under a policy of U the share of trade with Earth settled in U rises from a quarter at the opening to 85% (conservative) or 95% (liberal) ten years later, and Earth's customers and sellers keep a week of it as working balances. Paying in U leaves Earth's purchases from the zone unchanged by default, with a fall of 10% at full settlement as the case of a thin market or a widely swinging U: the evidence on shared monies measures partners who use one money, and moving the zone from the dollar to U puts an exchange between it and Earth. Earth's investors hold U assets when the real yield and U's average yearly change in value over the last five years exceed Earth's safe real rate by 3.5 points, the median premium of emerging markets' currencies, up to 30% of the zone's output; their yield is paid in U and they spend part of it in the zone. The funders take part of their returns in U, spend part of it in the zone and convert the rest over the years. Goods that holders buy with U are charged the lines' average Earth inputs, hands and power.

U's value in dollars, its rate, is a result (17 November 2026). Until the account with Earth is repaid U stays at par and the funders keep the reserve at its target. After that U floats. A year whose dollars would leave the reserve below its minimum lowers U within the year, so that the residents' purchases from Earth, a tenth of their pay valued at the rate, close the gap, down to a floor; below the floor the funders lend the reserve the rest on a credit line and their flows carry the loan, as in the dollar account they cover a year's deficit, judged by the same decade test (the default since 17 November 2026), or in the cases without that backstop the reserve falls short and fails its test. A year that leaves the reserve above its target raises U back toward par, and only then pays the funders. The year's local work is priced at the year's opening rate, so a lower U makes the settlement's goods and visits cheaper for Earth. The reserve is a basket: a fifth in gold and platinum-group metals, kept first from the zone's own refining, which raises the price of what it still sells, and otherwise bought, earning nothing; the rest in Earth's currencies at Earth's safe real rate of 1%. Its target is the policy's share of Earth's holdings and months of payments, and its minimum the tests'. Months of payments are counted on the last five years' payments with the residents' purchases at par, so that neither a year's lump of equipment nor a fall in U changes what the zone must hold, and the year's tests read the reserve on the same terms. The floor is a setting, run at par, at 75% and at 50% of par. Five tests of a safe and healthy policy, agreed on 16 November 2026, are checked every year: inflation in U at most 3% a year over any decade and never above 5% in a year; reserves of at least 20% of the U held on Earth and six months of payments to Earth; a run withdrawing a quarter of Earth's holdings met from the reserve, its metals at 90% of their value; the yield no higher than the zone's growth over the twenty years before; Earth's holdings no larger than the zone's yearly output, which here is the residents' pay at the rate with what the zone keeps of its sales. Growth as a result under a policy must pass all five in every year, and the funders' measures come from their own flows. The conservative policy is that of 12–13 November (inflation 2%, no yield to investors, a reserve target of half of Earth's holdings and a year of payments, six months above the tests' minimum); the liberal one offers a real yield of 2% held within growth, allows inflation of 3%, keeps its reserve target at the tests' minimum and pays a quarter of the funders' returns in U.

Earth's holdings of U are kept by four groups, each with its own instrument (17 November 2026). Earth's customers and sellers keep a week of the trade settled in U as working balances. Earth's investors buy bonds with dollars, of ten years, a tenth falling due each year, so that they can leave no faster than their bonds mature. People on Earth who spend in the zone on its culture and visits, the holders for use, keep three months of that spending in U (one to six as the range, the study's own) and, where the policy pays a yield, stake a third of it for two years, the share of ether staked at its 2.6% reward, spending the reward in the zone. The funders keep their U liquid, spending part of it in the zone and converting the rest, with their staking a case. The run test asks the reserve's currencies and metals, less the metals' haircut, to meet a quarter of what could leave within the year (the working balances, the liquid U of the holders for use and of the funders, and the stakes unlocking) together with the bonds falling due, in full as the Greenspan–Guidotti rule asks, and six months of payments; the reserve test still asks a fifth of all Earth's holdings and six months of payments, and the rate and the funders' line hold the reserve at the larger of the two, which is the run test's in every year of the solved cases. Without U the dollar account now pays Earth for the dollars its residents and firms hold to pay one another, 6% of the zone's output, the middle of the 4–8% that replacing a domestic currency has cost; the account of the earlier versions is the case `no_dollars_held`, and the new payment lowers the central case's residents as a result from 16.3 to 16.2 million by 2341.

The holders' behaviour rests on two bounded lookups (research drive, `vacuum-economy/research/u_monetary_policy/values.md` of 16 November, 61 sources, and `u_premia_money_trade/values.md` of 17 November, 54). Non-residents hold a mean 22% of emerging markets' local-currency government debt and about 30% of the output of reserve-currency issuers in their debt, which sets the investors' ceiling. Survey-based currency premia over 1996–2018 average 4.2 points a year for emerging markets (median 3.5) and 0.9 for advanced economies, and the smallest OECD currencies earned 5.8% a year against 2.0 for the largest, which sets the investors' threshold. Leaving a currency union cuts trade between the former partners by 0 to 50% (26% in the middle), much of it the decay of colonial ties after independence, with no step change in Ireland's trade with Britain after 1979; invoicing in one's own currency changes how prices and quantities answer exchange-rate moves, not the level of exports, and exchange-rate volatility has little or no effect on trade. Gold makes 16–27% of the world's official reserves at end-2023 and end-2025 prices and 0–7% of most small open economies', with a long-run real return of zero on Jastram's reading and 1.3% a year since 1900. No source records what portfolio holders spend in the issuer, so the 30% of their income spent in the zone is the study's own, as is Earth's safe real rate of 1%.

Under other technology and demand, the liberal policy at a floor of 75% against dollars only: with fast technology 30.2 million residents by 2341 against 6.6, the plan reached in 2115 against 2123 (above); on low demand with fast technology 6.7 million against 2.9, the plan reached in 2337 and not in dollars; with slow technology 5.9 million against 7.5, the plan not reached under U and reached in 2318 in dollars; on low demand with central technology neither grows.

Letting U fall below par carries more residents, the more so the lower the floor, because a fall lets the residents' purchases from Earth close a gap that would otherwise take the reserve below its minimum. How much a fall can absorb is bounded by those purchases, a tenth of the residents' pay: they are 2% of the payments to Earth in 2091, about a quarter in 2141 and about 40% from 2191. A lump of reinvestment early in the period therefore falls on the reserve whatever the floor, and the rate carries the residents' growth late. On the slow path the funders barely earn their 3% in either currency, and the reserve U holds through the build, earning Earth's 1%, costs them that margin, which is why the liberal policy carries fewer residents than dollars there.

The funders' credit line puts U on the backing the dollar account has: there the funders cover a year's deficit so long as no ten years together are in deficit, and under U they lend the reserve what it lacks at the floor, judged by the same test. Without it, once the account is repaid, the reserve must hold its minimum in every year with nothing behind it but the rate, a stricter test than the dollar account's. The conservative policy, with no savings of its own beyond its reserve, depends on the line most: at a floor of 75% the funders lend its reserve 0.55 trillion dollars after repayment, and without the line it reaches the plan in 2158 in place of 2147, with 10.7 million residents in 2241 against 11.1, though both carry 19.6 million by 2341. The liberal policy never draws on it.

With the holders for use keeping three months of their spending in U, the liberal policy carries 20.1 million residents by 2341 at a floor of 75%, against 19.6 million with one month and 20.2 with six. Buying U with dollars as their spending in the zone grows, they lend the zone what they hold without interest, about 0.22 trillion dollars' worth in 2241; their staking changes nothing (20.1 million without it). Earth's investors buy bonds, though never for the yield: they come while U recovers toward par after a fall, expecting the appreciation of the last five years to go on, up to 0.33 trillion dollars' worth under the liberal policy (in 2149) and 0.49 trillion under the conservative, which pays no yield. Bonds of ten years keep them through the recovery's end, yet with bonds falling due in a year the liberal policy carries the same 20.1 million, and with the small advanced economies' premium of one point more of them come (up to 0.39 trillion dollars' worth), again with no change in the residents. The funders staking a third of their U, a case, keeps it on Earth's books long enough that Earth's holdings outgrow the zone's output if the zone grows less than about 0.7% a year to the plan. Every faster steady rate checked, up to 7.5%, passes all five tests, but the solver asks every slower rate, no growth included, to pass as well, so the case reports no growth: the policy is safe only while the zone grows.

Paying the funders a quarter of their returns in U adds about half a million residents by 2341: with none of them in U, or with a tenth, the liberal policy carries 19.6 million. With half, Earth's holdings outgrow the zone's yearly output unless the zone grows at least about 5% a year to the plan, and the case reports no growth for the same reason as the funders' staking. Their spending of 30% of that U in the zone, which has no source, is worth about half a million too: without it the liberal policy carries 19.6 million.

A yield above growth in the twenty years after the opening, before any test of growth applies, draws investors who leave when the yield falls back, and lowers the result a little (19.6 million at 4%, 19.8 at 6% and 8%); the most liberal safe policy of the search (`central:central:steady:U_most_liberal_safe`) is the liberal policy itself, a quarter of the funders' returns in U and a yield of 2%, which carries the same 20.1 million as no yield, the search taking the higher yield where residents tie. A fall of 10% in Earth's purchases at full settlement, the case of a thin market, costs 2.3 million residents (17.8 million). The reserve's metals are kept from the zone's own refining (3.0 trillion dollars over the period in the liberal case at 75%, against 0.06 trillion bought), and keeping them off Earth's market holds up the price of the rest, so the metals line runs, with gaps, until 2335 where in dollars it stops in 2111; without them the liberal policy carries 19.7 million. Inflation hardly touches the account: 3% rather than 2% takes only what Earth's working balances lose. On the programme's ramp carried on past the plan, U at its floor for decades cannot carry the deficits of 167 million residents, as dollars cannot. Differences of up to about a million residents between nearby cases lie within the noise that lumps of reinvestment leave in the year-by-year tests, and several cases land on the same pair of growth rates.

The tests' thresholds, set on 16 November to be rechecked against the evidence, stand on it as follows. The decade bound on inflation, 3%, matches the targets of small open economies (2 ± 1% in the Czech Republic, 3 ± 1% in Chile, 1–3% in Israel and New Zealand); the yearly bound of 5% lies above all their bands, whose highest edge is 4%. Reserves of a fifth of Earth's holdings match the IMF's weights on non-residents' long-term claims (15–20%); six months of payments to Earth is cautious, holding about twice the IMF's emerging-market metric in these cases, where 100–150% of it counts as adequate and three months of imports is the traditional rule. A run of a quarter of Earth's holdings in a year is what emerging Europe lost in 2008; the worst case, Ukraine, lost three fifths, and reserved stablecoins 10–20% in days. Since the reserve is no longer topped up without limit after repayment, the reserve and run tests can now fail; in the solved cases neither does, since the rate and the funders' line hold the reserve at the run test's need. The yield within growth matches the record: across up to two centuries the interest rate on government borrowing has run below growth by a median 1.3 points in advanced economies and 4.8 in emerging ones, though in advanced economies since 1980 the two have been about level, so the test leaves little room once growth slows. Holdings within a year's output is looser than anything observed, since foreign holdings of reserve-currency debt are about a third of output; Earth's holdings reach at most 0.53 of output in the liberal policy at a floor of 75% (in 2093), so a bound at half of output would bind on it, and half the funders' returns in U passes even a whole year's output.

## The open choices

Each is carried as a switch; the cases named `central:central:constant_growth:<switch>` in the product measure them one at a time.

1. **What the money counts.** The account with Earth counts the share of residents' pay spent on Earth, 10%. Counting the whole payroll as spent on Earth instead, as the coupled study did, lowers the central case's net present value to 2241 from 2.65 to 2.00 trillion dollars. Under U its funding needed by 2241 stays at 1.64 trillion, since the reserve built in the surplus decades and U at its floor from 2221 carry the deficits until then, but past 2241 the funders' position falls to −5,605 trillion by 2341. In dollars alone the same switch raises the funding needed by 2241 from 1.55 to 9.84 trillion (and to 77 trillion by 2261 in the first version, held at the plan), since by 2241 3.6 million workers at Earth's projected wages cost 4 trillion dollars a year against receipts of 1.87. Raising the share spent on Earth to 20% lowers the net present value to 2241 from 2.65 to 2.57 trillion.
2. **Births and fares.** With births at 1% a year, most residents still arrive from Earth, and they arrive when a one-way passage costs tens of thousands of dollars (1.4 million in 2049, 55,000 in 2141); with no births or with migrants paying their own fares the net present value moves by at most 0.02 trillion.
3. **Towns or cities.** With the four caves of the founding system the only ones assumed, newcomers after it go to towns whatever the rule; with twenty caves the cities take 800,000 more residents and the net present value moves by about 0.01 trillion. Towns of 1,000 in place of 750 fill the eventual sites nine years later, at 222 million.
4. **The shape of growth as a result** (settled 16 November 2026): the steady rule as headline with the fastest rule beside; growth past the plan wherever resources allow; the decade test; growth past the initial quota on the eventual set of sites; the projection to 2341.
5. **Money in U** (begun 16 November 2026, given an exchange rate on 17 November, above). The default since 17 November is the liberal policy at a floor of 75% of par, with the funders' credit line behind the reserve after repayment and Earth's holdings kept by four groups; the account in dollars alone runs as the comparison for every scenario and technology, on the ramp and as a result, and the conservative and liberal policies run as cases at floors of par, 75% and 50% and without the credit line. Trade within the zone beyond Earth is designed ([zone_trade.md](zone_trade.md)) and waits to be built.

## How it is built

**Time and stocks.** The model steps year by year from 2041 (year 0) to 2341 (year 300); the founding deployment is built in years 0 to 7 and the settlement opens in 2049 with 12,500 residents. Capital wears out geometrically, as national accounts treat it: each year a share of each stock equal to one over its life retires and is replaced with that year's engineering. Cohorts retiring all at once would echo their first build for two centuries.

**Technology.** [period_technology.json](period_technology.json) holds 28 quantities as paths through 2241 in a slow, a central and a fast case, with a static case that holds 2041, and six technologies that arrive in dated windows: fusion, a grid from the polar ridges, a mass driver on the Moon, nuclear-electric tugs, chips made on the Moon and a belt of arrays around the equator. Every path holds its 2241 value after 2241. Each value carries its basis: the lookup and source key it was read from on the research drive (engineering, local content and transport of 15 November; food, power and launch, and manufacturing, automation and labour of 16 November), or the study's own projection. The lookups of 16 November changed the roadmap: output per job grows 0.7%, 1.5% or 3% a year (4, 20 or 369 times by 2241), megawatt fission falls from 75 to 12-20 kg/kWe, the industry that builds its own machines doubles in 7, 2 or 1.4 years once established, launch to low orbit floors at 13 dollars a kilogram, a lunar mass driver needs 0.5-0.8 kWh a kilogram, and beamed stations start at the lunar designs' 12 W/kg.

**Residents and settlements.** Residents follow a ramp of the programme, carried on past the plan at its final rate (3.31% a year for constant growth, 1.97% for front-loaded), or a schedule solved as a result, and never outnumber what the sites house. The founding system is the designed one: four lava-tube cities of 50,000 and a hundred crater towns of 500 ([lava_tube_cities](../lava_tube_cities/buildout.md), [vacuum_crater_buildout](../vacuum_crater_buildout/first_hundred.md)). After it, newcomers go to cities while caves remain and otherwise to towns of 750, first on the 8,886 sites of the crater programme's initial quota and then on the further sites of its eventual set, 222,163 in all ([crater_gardens](../../../habitation/crater_gardens/README.md)); the sites hold 167 million. [designs.py](designs.py) reads each design's own quantities (regolith handled, bulk products, metal, pressure components, equipment, the first air and water, floor and loads) and works them out for the year built: farms follow the food path, structures lighten, lamps and crops change the food power. With 2041's technology the founding system returns its design's quantities. Towns built after the founding system put their share of the region's farms and light industry in halls on the surface under three metres of regolith.

**Power.** [engineering.py](engineering.py) prices a firm kilowatt-year at each kind of site from arrays with storage through the 354-hour night, beamed stations, fission, fusion once it arrives, the polar grid once it arrives and within the poles' supply, and the equatorial belt; capacity is added each year from the cheapest at the cities' and the towns' representative distances from a pole.

**Industry and labour.** Six lines (regolith handling, bulk products, metal, manufactured equipment, water at the sources and propellant at the depot) each have a plant mass per tonne a year, an energy, a life and jobs that fall with output per job. Plant is sized a quarter above the work asked of it; the bulk lines are built to the year's work, the factories from a seed bought from Earth and then from their own output no faster than their doubling, and what they cannot yet make is bought finished. Hands go to the settlements' services first, the lines of sale next (sized on the year before's balance) and the industry last; the industry's work beyond the hands left is done from Earth by teleoperation, at an Earth wage times the 1.74 that a 3-second delay cost in a laboratory trial.

**Transport and sources.** Launch from Earth at the year's price, landed at the year's multiple; passengers of 270 kg with a tonne of cabin. Lunar material lifted to the depot by rocket or, once it arrives, by mass driver. Water from the source survey's bodies nearest first ([reach.py](../../../resources/source_access/reach.py)), with 90% of each body's water set aside by default, by tugs of three kinds, chemical, electric on water and nuclear-electric, sized by the rocket equation on the leg of the water being mined; within each band of the survey the leg lengthens in step with the water drawn from it. No tug is built that would weigh more than five times the water it delivers in a year, since near the end of a tug's reach its mass grows without bound. The fleet turns over as fleets of heavy equipment do, buying the newest kind while the old fly on, selling used tugs and keeping parts (since 17 November 2026; `transport.fleet_turnover`, from a bounded lookup of aircraft, ships and heavy machinery on the research drive, `vacuum-economy/research/fleet_turnover/`, 44 sources): a pool for each kind carries its mass and average age, delivers what its mass can on the present leg (so older tugs deliver less as legs lengthen, and fly beyond the limit on building until their leg no longer closes), flies its share of the water at its own propellant and water drawn, and wears out at one part in the tugs' life a year. New tugs are of the kind that costs least on the leg expected halfway through their life, changed only for a saving of a tenth; a kind new to the factories takes a rising share of last year's tug building in its first five years (12, 32, 62, 86 and 96%, as the A320neo did of its family's deliveries), each line otherwise grows at the industry's doubling pace, the rest of a year's additions are of the kinds already being built, and beyond the lines the tugs are bought finished from Earth as the kind that costs least bought there. A kind the factories no longer build is retired early, at most 8% a year, when its running cost alone exceeds the whole cost of the kind being built; tugs retired early or whose kind no longer closes the leg are sold to operators elsewhere in space, a fifth of them, at a value falling 10% a year of age to a floor of 14% of the cost new, and those that wear out or find no buyer are taken apart, 15% of their mass reused as spares for their kind and 90% of the rest recycled as metal. The model of 16 November kept each tug's delivery fixed from the year it was built, flew every tug at the newest kind's efficiency, and switched all new building to a new kind at once. The towns' lakes and the settlements' first stocks fill over ten years. A case's years after its water runs out are not physical, and its summary names the year.

**Sales.** The coupled study's scenarios of demand at their own date ([scenarios.json](../vacuum_economy/scenarios.json)): sport and entertainment, hosted work, computing, propellant and material at the depot, precious metals from the feed, and visits, priced by the coupled study's rule from this model's costs. A line is served in a year when its receipts exceed its annualized costs. Visits and depot sales saturate at bounds the study states, since one elasticity at every price would otherwise sell without limit as costs fall. Earth's income per person goes on growing at the scenario's rate after 2241.

**Money.** The account with Earth, in 2041 dollars: equipment, supplies and hardware bought there and landed, fares, what partners and Earth's services keep, teleoperation, and the share of residents' pay spent on Earth, against Earth's customers' payments. Work on the Moon costs hands, power and plant, which the model counts physically; every choice by cost values the hands it takes at the wage. Under a policy of U, [money.py](money.py) keeps beside the account Earth's holdings of U, U's rate, the reserve of Earth's currencies and metals, the funders' flows in dollars and in U, and the five tests (Money in U, above); the year's local work is priced at the rate, and goods bought with U are charged to the lines' costs. Without U the account pays Earth for the dollars the residents and firms hold, 6% of the zone's output.

## What it does not yet do

The rates of work, the plant masses, most paths after their sourced starting values, the mapping of Earth-orbit beaming designs onto lunar orbit, the bounds on visits and depot sales, the share of pay spent on Earth, the set-aside shares of the sources' water, the beamed constellation's factor of five over its load and the tugs' practical limit are the study's own, stated with their bases in [period_inputs.json](period_inputs.json) and [period_technology.json](period_technology.json). Of the central case's receipts in 2241, computing gives 40% on 5 GW of contracted computing in 2049 that no source supports, and visits 33% on a price elasticity constructed between two published values. Technology after 2241 is held, which understates later cases if it goes on improving, and Earth's demand is extrapolated at its scenario's rate to 2341 and, in the projection of exhaustion, to 3541. Money in U treats U as one unit. Its inflation is a setting, and nothing in the model raises U's prices when more is issued; the residents' own holdings of U and the zone's credit to local work are not modelled; the share of holders' income spent in the zone is the study's own. The months of their spending that the holders for use keep in U are the study's own; investors expect U's change in value over the last five years to go on, which brings them in while it recovers; and the zone trades with no one beyond Earth, a layer [zone_trade.md](zone_trade.md) designs and that waits to be built. The settlement's own economy beyond its needs is not modelled: in the central case only about a tenth of the workforce is needed by 2241 for the work the model counts, and the rest of the residents' work goes to one another, unseen by the account. Carbon and nutrients still come from Earth, though the sources' feed holds them. The metals line's treatment cost decides only whether the line runs and is not charged to the account, so metals kept for a reserve of U enter it at no cost, at a price that holds because they are kept off Earth's market. Power is chosen at one representative distance from a pole for towns and one for cities, and the further sites of the eventual set are taken at the same distance. Lines are served or dropped year by year on their annualized cost, without the coupled study's search for later starts. The time to exhaustion is a rough projection: the set-asides fall evenly on every body, heritage sites and science targets are not taken from the eventual set, and nothing past the vacuum period (the Open Moon, the array, the outer Solar System's buildout) draws on the same sources. The high scenario's results rest on demand the size checks show cannot be met. The tug fleet is carried as one pool for each kind with an average age, in place of cohorts; a new kind's share of the factories' tug building in its first five years follows one airliner family's record; the leg a new tug will fly halfway through its life is projected from the last decade's lengthening; and the operators elsewhere in space who buy a fifth of the retired tugs are assumed, with no market modelled for them. The path of computation per watt is not read, since computing is sold by the watt of capacity at the price of the same capacity on Earth.

## Evidence

The model runs on stated paths and stated inputs, and its results hold only on them; it qualifies no plant, market, price, site or source. The tests check that each path holds its stated values and interpolates, and that the static case holds 2041; that with 2041's technology the founding system returns its design's quantities; that improving technology lowers a resident's food power and built mass; that the power supply chosen is the cheapest offered and that the polar grid and fusion wait for their arrival; that a chemical tug draws more water the farther it flies and stops closing; that no tug is built past its practical limit, while one already built flies on beyond it, heavier for each kilogram it delivers, until its leg no longer closes; that the tug fleet can always carry the year's water, that a new kind holds under a quarter of the fleet when the factories first build it and the old kind still carries most of the water five years on, and that used tugs are sold only in years some are retired; that the account adds up in every year, with visits within their bound and lines served within their hands; that better technology needs less funding for the plan; that a ramp carries on past the plan at its final rate until the sites are full, and that holding it at the plan reproduces the first version's years before the plan; that newcomers past the initial quota live on the eventual set of sites; that the growth found for residents as a result is the highest the decade test allows, to the plan and past it, and that growth at the programme's pace passes the present-value test and fails the decade test; that setting water aside lengthens the legs and brings exhaustion sooner; that under a policy of U the reserve changes by exactly the year's dollars and the metals kept for it, U stays at par until the account is repaid and between its floor and par after, working balances are a week of the trade settled in U and the funders' measures come from their own flows; that the settings of U leave the dollar account untouched; that a lasting shortfall lowers U so that the reserve holds its minimum while U is above its floor, fails the reserve test with U held at par, and is met by the funders' loan with the credit line; that the five tests catch an unsafe policy (the funders' whole returns in U where the zone's output is small beside its sales, a yield held above growth); that investors leave no faster than their bonds mature and their stakes unlock, and the run test reads what could leave within a year; that the holders for use keep their months of spending in U and stake only where a yield is paid; that the dollar account pays for the dollars its residents hold; that metals kept for the reserve raise the price of those sold; that goods bought with U take the lines' Earth inputs and hands; that the solver returns the highest rate below which every rate it checks passes, and that growth as a result under U is no lower with a lower floor; that a case can move one group of the technology stack while every other quantity and arrival keeps the base case's path; that the options for computing at the opening and the price response of visits replace only the scenario's values; that the funders' rate of return is recovered from known flows; and that both saved products, the periods' and the scoping study's, are current with their sources. With the residents held at the plan and the survey's whole pool, the model of 16 November returned the first version's product to the last digit for 2041 to 2261 in 30 of its 32 ramp cases, the two high-demand cases with fast technology differing because their nuclear-electric tugs passed the practical limit; the fleet's gradual turnover since 17 November changes every case's account, so that comparison no longer holds.
