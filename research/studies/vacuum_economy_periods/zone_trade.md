# Trade within the zone beyond Earth: a design for the period model

*Proposed on 17 November 2026 for the author to read before it is built. Nothing in this note is built yet. It follows the exchange rate for U and the split of Earth's holdings, which come first.*

## Why the model needs it

The period model's settlement earns only from Earth, and Earth's purchases are sized by Earth's economy: world output, the world's wealthy and activity in space. No line of sale grows with the settlement's people except through the hands it needs. On central demand and technology, with growth as a result, the counted work fills 59% of the workforce in 2141, 19% in 2191 and 11–12% from 2241. Each added resident brings housing, supplies and a tenth of an Earth-level wage spent on Earth, and no receipts, so the account with Earth limits growth.

The settlement also trades with no one else beyond Earth. U is meant to be the shared money of that wider zone. The evidence on shared monies measures trade among partners who would otherwise use different monies, and the model has no such partners. This layer adds the zone's other members as trading partners, lines of work whose quantity can follow the residents, and the accounts that carry both.

## What the project already holds

- **The U transition plan** (`research/studies/monetary_system/trade_transition.md`, 31 October 2026) covers the whole planned economic zone beyond Earth through enclosed development, before the Open Moon, the array and the outer Solar System. It targets at least 90% of the zone's eligible internal payments in U.
- **The world economy study's account of early production** (`research/studies/world_economy/development.md`) names what can pay before the finished world exists:
  - rock and metal products for orbital customers;
  - research and engineering services;
  - fabrication, repair and logistics;
  - computing;
  - maintenance and transport for other orbital systems;
  - volatiles, propellant and processed material for other users.

  It separates purchases paid by the same construction programme, which are internal and bring no new money, from independently funded Earth-orbital customers, other settlements and businesses with their own earnings, which do. It identifies the array's habitat shielding, 4,950 Gt, as a customer stock for lunar bulk material.
- **The population study** (`provisioning/population.md`) houses the Open Moon's build workers in the array's habitats and the enclosed bases. It takes 10 million people on the Moon in 2526 as an assumption, so the build either overlaps the later part of this model's horizon or follows it.
- **The product-allocation study** (`research/studies/product_allocation/README.md`) puts small orbital factories inside this phase. It finds space-made spare parts best suited to equipment already in space, and names specialty optical fibre, selected crystals and research licences as early products whose value lies in knowledge more than mass.
- **The coupled study** sizes activity in space from the space economy: about a trillion dollars in 2040, growing 5% a year in the central case to 2100 and with world output after. The period model sells that activity only propellant and structural material at the depot, 450 t of propellant a year in 2049 in the central case.

## The partners

Each partner group has three things to settle: what it buys from the settlement, what it sells to it, and where its money comes from.

1. **Independent operators in space.** Transport, stations, orbital workshops and factories, and satellite servicing outside the programme's accounts.
   - **They buy** mass that leaves the Moon's shallow gravity well more cheaply than Earth's: propellant and structural material (already sold), water and food for crews, and metal parts, spares and assemblies. They also buy services: repair, assembly, engineering, research, training and health care for their crews.
   - **They sell** the settlement microgravity products and orbital services.
   - **Their money** comes from their own customers, mostly on Earth, so their purchases bring dollars into the zone when they buy U.
2. **Other settlements.** Outposts and settlements outside the programme's accounts, on the Moon and in cislunar space; the world economy study's "other settlements".
   - **They buy** the same goods and services.
   - **They sell** what they specialise in.
   - **Their size** is a scenario, since nothing in the project sizes them yet.
3. **The Open Moon's build and the array,** if they begin inside the horizon.
   - **They buy** lunar materials, and housing and services for the build's workers; the array buys shielding and structure.
   - **Their scale** would dominate everything else once they start.
   - **Their money** comes from their sponsors and their own borrowing, so it is purchasing power from outside the settlement's account.

## How the trade is sized

- **Goods for space** are priced the way the depot's propellant already is, against the same kilogram launched from Earth. Their quantity grows with the partners' activity, and the settlement sells what it can deliver for less.
- **Services** are bought in proportion to the partners' crews and activity. Each service line has a demand from the partners and a staff drawn from the residents' spare hands, so its quantity is set by whichever runs out first.
- **Purchases from the partners** replace the imports from Earth that the partners can supply, wherever their price beats Earth's.
- **Work for Earth at a distance.** The settlement's people can sell knowledge work to Earth over the 1.3-second link: engineering, research, design, software and teaching, together with the orbital factories' products and licences. The model's institutions line is a fixed share of world output today. This line would grow with the spare workforce, up to Earth's demand for such work. It is the part of the fix that does not depend on partners existing.
  - The line pays only where the settlement's wage, at U's exchange rate, is no higher than what Earth pays for the same work.
  - At par it isn't: the wage is set at 1.5 times a high-income average.
  - A lower U, down to the floor you set, brings it into reach. That is how a currency of the zone's own turns its people's time into earnings.

Even so, earnings grow with the residents only up to what the partners and Earth will buy. Beyond that, more people still add costs alone; the design widens the set of buyers but cannot remove that limit. Sizes come from the coupled study's growth of activity in space, the world economy study's accounts where they reach this period, and a bounded lookup on spending in space by kind and on trade in remote services.

## What U adds

Trade within the zone gains from U only against partners who would otherwise use different monies. If every partner paid in dollars, U would add nothing over dollars there either. The proposed reference without U has each partner paying in its home Earth currency, with the dollar as the go-between; that fits a zone whose people descend from all of Earth's nations. The gain then applies to the share of the zone's trade that would cross currencies. Inside the zone both sides use U, so U's exchange rate against Earth's money plays no part in that trade.

The size comes from the union evidence already gathered (`vacuum-economy/research/u_monetary_policy/values.md` on the research drive):
- **The euro,** the closest analogue for firms with deep currency markets, has estimates from not significant (the newest methods, and the correction for publication bias) to 40–50% (Glick and Rose 2016).
- **Unions of small, poor partners** show 60% or more.

Proposed: 15% as the default, with 0 and 30% as cases.

## The accounts

The settlement keeps two external accounts: the account with Earth in dollars, and the account with the zone in U. Partners pay in U.
- **A partner that earns from Earth** buys its U with dollars, which reach the reserve.
- **A partner that earns from the settlement** pays with the U it earned there.

The account with Earth therefore gains from the dollars the partners bring and from the Earth imports their goods replace. The exchange rate, built in the layer before this, settles the rest. The funders' test, the floor on U's value and the residents' purchases from Earth all read the combined position.

## What it delivers

- residents as a result with the zone's trade included, against the same tests;
- the counted work's share of the workforce, year by year;
- the settlement's earnings by partner and line, in U and in dollars;
- U's gain within the zone, against the reference in home currencies and against dollars throughout;
- if the build and the array fall within the horizon, their demand on the settlement by year.

## Decisions for the author

1. **Which partners, and from when.**
   - **Options:**
     - (a) independent operators and other settlements only;
     - (b) also the Open Moon's build and the array, from a start year that is a setting;
     - (c) a single rest-of-zone economy as a share of the space economy.
   - **Recommendation:** (a) as the default, with (b) as a scenario whose start year you set. The build's timing is yours to decide, and once it begins it decides everything after it. Making it the default would turn the period model into a model of the build's base, which is a larger design question.
   - **Cost:** (a) is in the estimate below; (b) adds about two hours, reading the world economy study's construction accounts for this period.
2. **The reference without U, and the size of U's gain within the zone.**
   - **Options:**
     - (a) partners in their home currencies, with the gain at 15% and cases at 0 and 30%;
     - (b) dollars throughout, with no gain.
   - **Recommendation:** (a), with (b) beside it as the comparison, since a zone drawn from all of Earth's nations would otherwise trade across currencies.
   - **Cost:** none beyond the build.
3. **Work for Earth at a distance.**
   - **Options:** (a) include it, paid at Earth's price for the work and staffed from the spare workforce; (b) leave it out.
   - **Recommendation:** (a), because it is the side of the fix that needs no partner, and it shows what U's exchange rate does for the settlement's people.
   - **Cost:** about an hour inside the build.

## Cost

About five hours of my work after the exchange rate and the holdings split, and one regeneration of about five minutes of machine time. A bounded lookup by one agent, of about an hour, covers spending in space by kind (crews' supplies, servicing, assembly and manufacturing in space) and trade in services delivered at a distance. It can run while the exchange rate is built.
