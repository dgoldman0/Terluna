abstract: |
  The enclosed lunar buildout would put towns in craters and cities in lava tubes on the Moon from 2041, with the industry, power and water supply that keep them. A year-by-year model of their economy finds that, on central assumptions about technology and about Earth's demand for what they sell, the settlements repay their founding capital, 1.14 trillion dollars at its deepest, by 2081. They reach the plan's 6.47 million residents in 2142 and carry about 20 million by 2341. How fast they grow decides what their funders earn: 5.3% a year in real terms at the plan's own pace, and 3.9% at the fastest growth their account can pay for. Amounts are in dollars of 2026.

# The answer

On central assumptions the enclosed buildout pays its own way and grows well past its plan. Its founding capital peaks at 1.14 trillion dollars, 0.77 trillion of it spent by the end of 2049, the year the settlements open with their first 12,500 residents, and the settlements repay it by 2081 from what they sell to Earth. Growing as fast as that account can pay for, they reach the plan's 6.47 million residents in 2142, about a century ahead of its schedule, and carry about 20 million by 2341 (Figure 1).

Growth is paid for from the same account, so it costs the funders return. Growing at the plan's own pace and holding at 6.47 million, the settlements repay their funders by 2076 at a real return of 5.3% a year; growing as fast as the account allows, they repay them by 2081 at 3.9%. The settlements' own money, U, lets them grow about a quarter larger than dollars alone would, for about half a point of the funders' return (Section 4).

Three inputs decide most of the answer. The computing that buyers on Earth contract when the settlements open comes first: with 1 GW in place of the 5 GW assumed, the settlements carry 8.1 million residents by 2341, and with 20 GW, 48 million. The pace of technology comes next, above all of launch from Earth and of the settlements' own industry; with technology held at its 2041 state the settlements stay at their founding size, and their funders earn 1.3% a year. The share of their pay that residents spend on Earth comes third: at a fifth, the settlements carry 11.8 million residents by 2341, and at a twentieth, 30.9 million. The first and the third rest on the study team's own estimates, and settling them is the first work of a companion proposal for a research and development fund.

# What would be built

## The settlements

The founding system comprises four cities in lava tubes, each designed for 50,000 people, and a hundred towns in craters, each designed for 500. Built from 2041, it opens in 2049 with its first 12,500 residents, 10,000 in the cities and 2,500 in the towns, and fills as newcomers arrive. Later towns, of 750 residents each, take the 8,886 crater sites of an initial quota and then a larger set of 222,163 sites, the best 2.5% of the catalogued craters between 200 m and 1 km across. Each settlement comes from its own design study and is built with the engineering of its year.

## Industry, power and water

The settlements make most of their structures and machines from lunar material. Their factories grow from a seed bought from Earth and then from their own output, and by 2070 they make 98% of the settlements' equipment by mass; from then on Earth supplies mostly electronics, consumables and what residents choose to buy. Firm power comes from megawatt fission reactors through the opening decades and, from 2064, from stations in lunar orbit that beam it down. The firm load reaches 173 GW in 2141 and 257 GW in 2241.

Water for air, lakes and propellant comes from near-Earth asteroids, nearest first, with 90% of each body's water left in place under the project's conservation set-asides, for future use, protection and uncertainty. Chemical tugs fetch it until about 2210, and tugs driven by solar arrays carry all of it from 2230. The fleet turns over as fleets of heavy equipment do: a new kind comes in as fast as the factories learn to build it, and old tugs fly until they wear out or are sold.

The engineering cheapens steeply over the first century and slowly after it:

| | 2041 | 2091 | 2141 | 2241 |
|:------------|----:|----:|----:|----:|
| A kilowatt-hour on the Moon, dollars | 2.36 | 0.017 | 0.0049 | 0.0032 |
| A kilogram landed from Earth, dollars | 1,800 | 118 | 54 | 30 |
| A resident's passage from Earth, dollars | 1.7 million | 89,000 | 35,000 | 18,000 |

# How the settlements pay their way

## What they sell

The settlements sell computing, visits, sport and entertainment, hosted research and professional work, propellant and material at a depot in space, and precious metals recovered from the asteroid feed. Computing is the largest line throughout. The model sells it by the watt of capacity at the price of the same capacity on Earth, starting from 5 GW of demand contracted at the opening, the study team's own figure, and growing with Earth's output. Sales begin in 2053, once the settlements' power can carry them, at about 74 billion dollars a year. That rule ties the line's size to the opening contract for three centuries, which is why the contract moves the result more than any other input.

In the base case receipts from Earth's customers reach 0.22 trillion dollars a year in 2091, 0.39 trillion in 2141 and 1.26 trillion in 2241 (Figure 2), when the lines stand as follows:

## What they buy from Earth

The settlements pay Earth 0.10 trillion dollars a year in 2091, 0.30 trillion in 2141 and 1.21 trillion in 2241. Early on, the largest payment is the share that industrial partners and services on Earth keep of the sales they help make. The residents' own purchases from Earth, a tenth of their pay, draw level with those shares in the 2190s and lead from 2211, 40% of the total in 2241 and half by 2341. Since the residents' pay follows Earth's income per person, those purchases grow faster than the settlements' sales, and late in the horizon they set the limit on growth.

![What the settlements pay Earth and receive from Earth's customers, by kind, in the base case, with the total paid drawn over the receipts.](figures/flows.pdf){width=100%}

The work the model counts, in services, operations, industry and the lines of sale, fills 84% of the workforce in 2091 and about a tenth from 2241. The residents' other work goes to one another and stays within the settlements, and turning more of it into receipts, through trade within the zone beyond Earth or work sold to Earth at a distance, is the largest opportunity the study leaves to later work.

## The account over time

The account with Earth runs in deficit through the build and the settlements' first decade, while the funders carry it, and in surplus from the 2060s. In trillions of dollars over each period:

| Years | Receipts from Earth | Paid to Earth | Funders' flow |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| 2041–2048, the build | 0.00 | 0.57 | −0.57 |
| 2049–2060, the first years | 0.95 | 1.39 | −0.48 |
| 2061–2085, to repayment | 3.65 | 2.21 | +1.49 |

The funders' flow in the first years exceeds the account's deficit by the reserve they build for U.

The funders' position is deepest in 2053, at 1.14 trillion dollars, and they are repaid by 2081 (Figure 5). Their last receipts come in 2265. From then on the residents' purchases from Earth, growing with Earth's income, hold the account in deficit, which the settlements' own savings in U's reserve carry at first and U's fall to 0.81 of par carries after.

# Their money, U

U is the money of the settlements and the operations around them, the zone beyond Earth. A quarter of their trade with Earth is settled in U at the opening, and 95% ten years later. Until the funders are repaid, U stays at par with the dollar. After that its value floats, down to a floor of three-quarters of par. A year whose shortfall with Earth would draw the reserve below its minimum lowers U within the year, so that the residents' purchases from Earth cost less, and a year of surplus raises U back toward par before the funders are paid. In the base case U stands below par for 41 of the 300 years: at 0.77 of par from 2137 to 2145, while the settlements build fastest toward the plan, and at 0.81 from 2310 (Figure 3).

![U's value in dollars in the base case, against its floor (above), and its reserve against what a run on U would take (below).](figures/rate_reserve.pdf){width=100%}

The institution that issues U holds a partial reserve against it: a fifth in gold and platinum-group metals, taken first from the settlements' own refining, and the rest in Earth's currencies at Earth's safe real rate of 1%. Most of the reserve is the settlements' own savings. When the funders spend the U they are paid in the zone, the dollars behind it stay with the reserve; about 1.4 trillion dollars arrive that way and compound at 1% a year, so that the reserve stands at 4 to 6 trillion dollars through 2230–2280, several times what a run on U would take. Those savings carry the account's deficit after 2265, and the funders stand behind the reserve with a credit line the base case leaves unused.

Four groups hold U on Earth. Customers and sellers keep a week of their trade in it. Investors buy ten-year bonds, which they hold while U recovers after a fall, up to 0.21 trillion dollars' worth around 2149. People who spend in the zone on its culture and visits keep three months of that spending, partly in two-year deposits, and the funders take a quarter of their returns in U. Earth's holdings reach 0.22 trillion dollars in 2241 and 0.55 trillion in 2341, most of them with the people who spend in the zone (Figure 4).

![Earth's holdings of U by group in the base case, in dollars at each year's value of U.](figures/holdings.pdf){width=100%}

U is designed to five conditions of a safe and healthy money, which every case the study reports meets in every year: inflation of at most 3% a year over any decade and at most 5% in any year; a reserve of at least a fifth of the U held on Earth and six months of payments to Earth; enough reserve to meet a run on what could leave within a year; a yield within the zone's growth; and Earth's holdings within the zone's yearly output.

U lets the settlements grow about a quarter larger than the same settlements using dollars, 20.1 against 16.2 million residents by 2341. Its fall absorbs late shortfalls through the residents' purchases from Earth; the people who keep U to spend in the zone lend it their balances free of interest; and the funders' spending leaves savings behind. In dollars alone the funders are repaid two years sooner and earn 4.5% a year, because the settlements grow less. Paying in U could also cost the settlements some of Earth's custom: the evidence on partners who leave a shared currency puts the fall in their trade between zero and about a half. The base case takes Earth's purchases as unchanged. A tenth fewer would leave the settlements 17.8 million residents by 2341 at a return of 3.8% a year, a quarter fewer 15.1 million at 3.4%, and a half fewer 6.6 million at 3.0%, so how Earth's customers take to paying in U matters as much as any setting of U itself.

# What the funders put in and get back

## The arrangement the study assumes

The funders provide the founding capital and cover every shortfall with Earth until the cumulative account is repaid. After repayment they receive what the settlements earn above the reserve's required level, a quarter of it in U, and they stand behind the reserve with a credit line, which the study leaves uncapped and the base case leaves untouched. The pace of growth follows the study's rule: the fastest growth the account can pay for while the funders' return stays above the study's discount rate (Appendix A). The instrument the funders hold, their priority among partners, who sets the pace of growth and how they can leave over a payback of thirty to forty years are terms for the partners to set.

| The base case's growth to the plan, holding there | 2142 | 6.5 million | 1.14 | 2081 | 4.0% a year |
| The base case, growing on past the plan | 2142 | 20.1 million | 1.14 | 2081 | 3.9% a year |

The base case's speed to the plan costs the funders 1.3 points of return against the plan's pace and brings the plan a century sooner. Its growth past the plan costs them a further 0.1 point and carries three times the plan's residents by 2341. Carried on past 2241 at the same 3.3% a year, the plan's pace would fill the crater sites with 167 million residents by 2341, and its account turns to lasting deficit in the 2260s. The opening decades decide most of any return: three-quarters of a trillion dollars is spent by the end of 2049, before the settlements sell much, and the funders' position is deepest in 2053.

By residents, computing contracted at the opening moves the answer most, then the pace of technology and Earth's demand, then the residents' spending on Earth. Of the groups, launch matters most to the funders: slow launch alone leaves them 2.9% a year, a return just below the study's discount rate, so the growth rule holds the settlements at their founding size, while fast launch alone raises their return to 5.1%. Computation per watt leaves the result as it is, because the model sells computing by the watt of capacity and leaves that path unread. Differences of up to about a million residents between nearby cases lie within the model's resolution.

## Health over a lifetime

People in the settlements would live their whole lives at a sixth of Earth's gravity, under cosmic radiation, beside abrasive dust, and inside air, water and food systems they run themselves. A review of 34 published sources made for this report finds radiation the best understood of these. A lander measured about 1.4 millisieverts a day on the lunar surface in 2019, some two hundred times Earth's average natural dose. Transport models put the cosmic-ray dose near a negligible level under about three metres of soil, and below one millisievert a year deep inside a lava tube. The designs give each crater town a roof of three metres of local soil and the cave cities tens of metres of rock, and each roof's lifetime dose, openings included, remains to be calculated and measured.

Lunar dust inflamed and scarred the lungs of rats at high concentrations, and crews in space work to a six-month exposure limit; limits for a lifetime and for children remain to be set. Closed ecological trials have kept crews of four to eight for up to two years, and in one of them the air's oxygen fell to 14%, so the settlements' air needs continuous measurement. Crews in polar stations and simulated missions show disturbed sleep and mood, and the settlements form a larger social world than any of those has tested. Every town in the designs has a clinic, and a full health system, with Earth's specialists a 2.6-second round trip away, remains to be planned.

Gravity is the open question. Astronauts in orbit lose about 1% of their hip and spine bone a month, and heavy resistance exercise has held hip bone density through flights of four to six months. The longest human stay at lunar gravity lasted 75 hours, and every mammal studied so far was born under Earth's gravity, so the course of pregnancy and childhood at lunar gravity is unknown. Studies of animals at lunar gravity during the construction years and monitoring of the first adult residents can narrow the question before families arrive, and rotating rooms at Earth's gravity would offer protection meanwhile. Much of the rest can be learned on Earth: chronic dust studies with imitation lunar soil, larger closed trials, heavy-ion biology at particle accelerators, and studies of remote communities.

# What is known, what remains open, and what comes next

Much of the answer rests on firm ground. The engineering paths start from sourced values for megawatt fission, beamed power, launch prices, asteroid water and the equipment's lifetimes (Appendix C), and the settlements come from the project's own design studies. The shape of the account follows from them: a capital-heavy founding decade, repayment about three decades after the opening, and late growth limited by the residents' own purchases from Earth. Within the horizon the account limits growth before any resource does. With the set-asides, the water open to mining lasts at the base case's size to about the 2650s, and the crater sites hold 167 million residents.

Five questions remain open, and each moves the answer far: the computing that buyers would contract at the opening; how the residents of such settlements spend their pay; the institutions that would issue U, hold its reserve and clear its payments; the trade within the zone beyond Earth that would bring the residents' other work into the account; and health over a lifetime at lunar gravity. The pace of technology, above all of launch, lies with the wider space industry, and the settlements' case rises and falls with it.

The companion proposal sets out a fund of 100 million dollars for 2027–2041 that settles these questions, follows the pace of launch, and turns the work into businesses with buyers on Earth whatever is decided about the Moon.

# Appendix A. Method {-}

The model builds the settlements year by year from 2041 to 2341. Each year it houses the newcomers in cities and towns built with that year's engineering and renews what wears out; sets the year's needs for water, air, supplies, local goods and power from the residents' mix of cities and towns; prices the lines of sale from the year's costs and sells where they pay; solves power and industry together, adding capacity from the cheapest option at each kind of site; fetches water with the tug fleet; buys from Earth what the settlements have yet to make; and counts the work against the workforce, industry's remaining work being done from Earth by remote operation. Under U, the money's ledger then sets the year's value of U, the reserve, Earth's holdings and the funders' flows. Technology follows sourced paths to 2241 and holds its 2241 values afterwards, and Earth's demand follows three scenarios, each judged at its own date from Earth's projected population and income.

Growth, as the study reports it, is the fastest steady growth, first to the plan and then past it, at which four conditions hold: the funders' flows, discounted at 3% a year in real terms, the study's own discount rate, have a present value of zero or more to 2341; over every ten years after repayment the funders receive at least as much as they put in; the settlements' own work is staffed and the water open to mining lasts; and under U the five conditions of a safe money hold in every year. Each rate is the highest below which every slower rate checked also passes. Amounts are computed in the model's own unit and converted to dollars of 2026 for the text.

The model is a runnable calculation on stated inputs, and its 37 tests check its accounts, its rules and its saved results. Its empirical validation awaits built settlements.

# Appendix D. Terms {-}

**Account with Earth.** What Earth's customers pay the settlements, against what the settlements pay Earth, each year.

**Credit line.** The funding partners' undertaking, once repaid, to lend the reserve what it lacks of its minimum when U is at its floor.

**Floor.** The lowest value U may fall to, three-quarters of par.

**Funding partners.** The participants who provide the founding capital and cover the settlements' shortfalls with Earth until their funding is repaid.

**Par.** One dollar for one U.

**The plan.** The buildout's planned 6.47 million residents, reached on its own schedule in 2241 by growing 3.3% a year from the opening.

**Reserve.** The currencies and metals held against U.

**Set-asides.** The share of each near-Earth body's water left unmined, for future use, protection and uncertainty.

**U.** The money of the zone beyond Earth.

**Zone beyond Earth.** The settlements and the operations around them that use U.
