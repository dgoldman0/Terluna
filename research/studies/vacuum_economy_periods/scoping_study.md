# Summary

## The central result

On central assumptions the enclosed buildout pays for itself and goes on growing past its plan. With central paths of technology, central demand from Earth and U as their money, the settlements reach the planned 6.47 million residents in 2142, about a century ahead of the programme's own schedule, and carry 20.1 million by 2341 (Figure 1). The funding partners put in 1.77 trillion dollars, 1.21 trillion of it before the settlements open in 2049; they are repaid by 2081 and earn a real return of 3.9% a year, above the 3% the programme requires. U passes all five of the study's tests of a safe and healthy policy in every year, and once the funders are repaid the settlements carry every later shortfall themselves, through U's rate and its reserve.

Kept in dollars alone, the same settlements carry 16.2 million residents by 2341 and return 4.5% a year to funders repaid by 2079, so U buys about a quarter more growth for about half a point of the funders' return. Two paths fail to pay. The programme's own pace of 3.3% a year, carried on past 2241, leaves the account with Earth in deficit for good from about 2260. With technology held at its 2041 state, the settlements stay at their first 12,500 residents under every demand, and their funders earn less than 3%.

## The decisive inputs

Four inputs decide whether the settlements pay and how large they grow; Figure 7 ranks them by their effect on the residents carried by 2341. Earth's demand comes first, and within it the computing contracted at the opening: low demand holds the settlements at their first 12,500 residents, while 1 GW of computing contracted in place of 5 carries 8.1 million and 20 GW carries 48.2 million. The pace of technology follows. Slow technology leaves 5.9 million, and within the technology stack launch and local industry decide most of the residents while power decides much of the funders' return (Section 9). The share of their pay that residents spend on Earth carries 11.8 million at a fifth and 30.9 million at a twentieth. The arrangement of the settlements' money comes last of the four: the preferred policy of U carries 3.9 million more residents than dollars alone, and a floor at half of par 4.8 million more again.

## Where the evidence is thinnest

The computing contracted at the opening carries the most weight on the least evidence. Computing sold to Earth is the largest line of sale, two-fifths of receipts in 2241, and it rests on 5 GW under contract at the opening, a figure of the study team's own; between 1 and 20 GW the settlements carry from 8.1 to 48.2 million residents by 2341. The share of their pay residents spend on Earth and the months of spending that holders keep in U are likewise the study's own figures. U's institutions exist as requirements, described by what they must do and held to five tests of a safe policy, and await design; the model sets U's inflation by policy. Since the settlements trade with Earth alone, Earth's own demand sets their receipts, and the work residents do for one another stays outside the account; trade within the zone beyond Earth is designed as the model's next layer. After 2241 technology holds at its 2241 state, and Earth's demand follows its scenario's rates to 2341.

## Recommendation

The study team recommends that the partners proceed to a pre-feasibility study concentrated on the inputs that move the result most and rest least on evidence: demand for computing and visits at the opening, the residents' purchases from Earth, the institutions of U and trade within the zone beyond Earth, together with engineered designs for the first settlements, the power supply and the tugs (Section 11).

# The buildout

## What it builds

The buildout creates enclosed settlements on the Moon, towns in craters and cities in lava tubes, together with the industry that makes most of their structures and machines from lunar material, firm power for homes, farms and factories, and a supply of water fetched from asteroids that pass near the Earth. Its founding system comprises four lava-tube cities designed for 50,000 people each and a hundred crater towns of 500; it is built from 2041 and opens in 2049 with its first 12,500 residents. Later towns, of 750 residents each, take the 8,886 crater sites of an initial quota and then a larger set of 222,163 sites, the best 2.5% of the catalogued craters between 200 m and 1 km across. Each settlement comes from its own design study and is built with the engineering of its year.

The settlements earn their way by selling computing in lunar facilities, visits, sport and entertainment, hosted research and professional work, propellant and structural material at a depot in space, and precious metals recovered from the asteroid feed. From Earth they buy what their factories have yet to make, the electronics of their machines, some supplies and the seed of the factories themselves, along with whatever goods and services their residents choose.

## Phases

The founding build runs from 2041 to 2048 and is paid for wholly by the funding partners. Growth to the plan follows: the programme sets 6.47 million residents as its planned size, reached on its own schedule in 2241. Growth then continues past the plan wherever the settlements' money and resources allow, to the horizon in 2341, so the plan serves as a milestone and the number of residents carried by 2341 becomes one of the results.

## Participants

The buildout is a joint undertaking. Its funding partners provide the founding capital, cover the settlements' shortfalls with Earth until that capital is repaid, and then receive what the settlements earn above their needs. Industrial and operating partners, research partners, customers on Earth and in space, holders of U on Earth and the residents themselves take part on the terms set out in Section 8.

# The model and its evidence

## The model

The model builds and runs the settlements year by year from 2041 to 2341, each year with that year's engineering. Newcomers are housed in towns and cities taken from their design studies; power comes from the supply that costs least at each kind of site; the settlements' factories make what they can and Earth supplies the rest; and tugs fetch water from the near-Earth sources, nearest first. Sales follow the prices that costs and Earth's demand allow, and the work the year needs is counted against the residents who work (Appendix A gives the year's steps in order). Capital wears out geometrically and is replaced with the engineering of the year of replacement. The settlements' money is counted as their account with Earth and, under a policy of U, as the ledger of U beside it (Section 6).

## Cases

Each case combines a path of technology, a scenario of Earth's demand and a schedule of residents. The technology paths are slow, central and fast, beside a control that holds the engineering of 2041 throughout. Each of 28 quantities follows its path to 2241 and holds its 2241 value afterwards, and six technologies arrive in dated windows: fusion, a power grid from the polar ridges, a mass driver on the Moon, nuclear-electric tugs, chips made on the Moon and a belt of solar arrays around the equator. The demand scenarios are low, central and high, each judged at its own date and price from Earth's projected population and income. The residents follow either the programme's ramp, 3.3% a year from 12,500 in 2049 to 6.47 million in 2241 and the same rate afterwards, or a schedule found as a result.

## Growth as a result

The study's main measure is the fastest growth the settlements can sustain while they still pay. Their funding partners must still earn the programme's 3% a year in real terms, so that the present value of their flows at 3% to 2341 stays at zero or above. Once the funders are repaid, every ten years taken together must leave the account with Earth in balance or surplus. The settlements' own work must be staffed and their sources' water must last, and under U the five tests of a safe and healthy policy (Section 6.5) must hold in every year. Growth is solved as two steady rates, the highest to the plan and then the highest past it. Each is the highest rate below which every slower rate the study checks also passes, since a single year's lump of reinvestment can make passing and failing alternate as the growth rate moves it from one year to another.

## Evidence

The inputs come from twelve bounded lookups of published evidence, made between 15 and 17 November 2026 and drawing on 471 sources (Appendix C), from the project's own design studies, and, where the evidence ran out, from the study team's own construction. Every input carries its basis, and Appendix B lists the main ones with their standing. The team's own values, among them the share of residents' pay spent on Earth, the computing contracted at the opening and the months of spending held in U, carry stated ranges, and Section 9 shows how far each moves the result.

The figures follow from stated assumptions about technology two centuries ahead and about markets that have yet to form. Differences of up to about a million residents between nearby cases lie within the variation that lumps of reinvestment leave in the year-by-year tests, and a single decade of heavy building can move a case much further: in dollars alone with fast technology, one decade barely in deficit stops growth past the plan (Section 7). The model is a runnable and tested calculation, whose checks Appendix A lists; its empirical validation awaits built settlements.

## Power

Firm power comes from megawatt fission reactors through the opening decades, the lightest supported supply away from the poles in the study's power lookup. From 2064 it comes from stations in lunar orbit that beam it down, once their mass per watt has fallen far enough. A grid from the polar ridges loses to both wherever it is offered, because its lines grow with the square of their length and the settlements lie 600 to 2,700 km from the ridges. The base case's firm load reaches 18 GW in 2091, 173 GW in 2141, 257 GW in 2241 and 484 GW in 2341. The constellation is sized at five times its firm load, an allowance of the study's own, from which follow about 1,300 GW of stations in 2241, some 6.4 million tonnes at 202 W/kg.

## Local manufacture

The settlements' factories grow from a seed bought from Earth and then from their own output, at the pace their doubling time allows. By 2070 they make 98% of the settlements' equipment by mass, and from then on Earth supplies mostly electronics, consumables and what residents choose to buy.

## Water from near-Earth sources

The settlements' air, lakes and propellant come from water on near-Earth asteroids, mined nearest first, with 90% of each body's water left in place under the project's conservation set-asides, for future use, protection and uncertainty. Chemical tugs fetch the water until about 2210, on legs whose velocity change lengthens as the nearer water is drawn: 1.0 km/s at the opening, 2.2 km/s in 2091, 3.5 km/s in 2141 and 4.9 km/s by 2210. Tugs driven by solar arrays take over from about 2210, carrying a quarter of the fleet's capacity in 2214, three-quarters by 2220 and all of it from 2230, on legs of 6.2 km/s by 2241 and 7.1 km/s by 2300. Every tug is built to weigh at most five times the water it delivers in a year.

The fleet turns over as fleets of heavy equipment do, on values taken from aircraft, ships and heavy machinery. A tug delivers less as the legs lengthen, and old tugs fly until they wear out or their leg stops closing. New tugs are of the kind that costs least on the leg they will fly halfway through their life, and a new kind takes a rising share of the factories' tug building over five years, as the A320neo did of its family's deliveries. Tugs retired early are partly sold used, at prices set by age and condition, and the rest are taken apart for spares and metal. The old chemical tugs retire when their legs stop closing, in about 2230, and a fifth of them go to operators elsewhere in space. The largest purchase of finished tugs and equipment from Earth, 0.68 trillion dollars in 2210, comes as the solar tugs begin.

## Sales

Demand comes from three scenarios judged at each year's date from Earth's projected population and income, and prices follow the year's costs. ## Purchases from Earth

The settlements pay Earth 0.16 trillion dollars a year in 2091, 0.47 trillion in 2141, 1.88 trillion in 2241 and 5.6 trillion in 2341, and the largest payment changes over the period. Early on it is the share that industrial partners and services on Earth keep of the sales they help make. From about 2210 the solar tugs and the drives bought for them add about 0.33 trillion dollars a year by 2241. The residents' own purchases from Earth, a tenth of their pay, draw level with the partners' shares in the 2190s and are the largest single payment from 2211, 40% of the total in 2241 and 52% in 2341. Since the residents' pay follows Earth's income per person, these purchases grow faster than the settlements' sales, and late in the horizon they set the limit on the settlements' growth.

![What the settlements pay Earth and receive from Earth's customers, by kind, in the base case.](figures/flows.pdf){width=100%}

The work the model counts, in services, operations, industry and the lines of sale, fills 84% of the workforce in 2091, 34% in 2141 and 10% to 11% from 2241. The residents' other work goes to one another and stays within the settlements, while Earth's purchases follow Earth's own demand at any size of settlement. Trade within the zone beyond Earth and work sold to Earth at a distance would turn that work into receipts, which brings both into the next stage (Section 11).

# Money

## The account with Earth

The settlements' money is counted first as their account with Earth: what Earth's customers pay them against what they pay Earth. Until the account is repaid, the funding partners cover its shortfalls. Afterwards they receive what the settlements earn above their needs, a quarter of it in U under the preferred policy.

## U, the settlements' money

U is the money of the zone beyond Earth, treated here as one economy. Under the preferred policy the share of trade with Earth settled in U rises from a quarter at the opening to 95% ten years later. U's value in dollars, its rate, follows each year's flows. Until the account with Earth is repaid, U stays at par, one dollar a U. After repayment, a shortfall that would take the reserve below its minimum lowers U within the year, and the residents' purchases from Earth, valued at the lower rate, close the gap. The fall stops at a floor of 75% of par under the preferred policy, so that a resident's pay loses at most a quarter of its buying power on Earth. A surplus that lifts the reserve above its target raises U back toward par before the funders receive anything. Because the year's local work is priced at the rate, a lower U also makes the settlements' goods and visits cheaper for Earth.

In the base case Earth buys as much from the zone paying in U as it would paying in dollars. The evidence on shared monies concerns partners who use one money, while Earth and the zone each keep their own. Where partners have left a shared currency, estimates of the fall in trade between them run from zero to about a half, the largest from small economies leaving the currency of a former colonial power; studies of invoicing find that selling in one's own currency leaves export volumes as they were and changes how prices answer a move in the exchange rate. A fall of a tenth in Earth's purchases runs as a case.

## The reserve

The institution that issues U holds a partial reserve against it: a fifth in gold and platinum-group metals, taken first from the settlements' own refining and otherwise bought, and the rest in Earth's currencies, earning Earth's safe real rate of 1%. Keeping the reserve's metals off Earth's market holds up the price of those the settlements sell. During the build the funding partners keep the reserve at its target. After repayment, surpluses rebuild it before the funders are paid, and the funders stand behind it with a credit line, lending what it lacks of its minimum when U is at its floor, on the terms on which they cover a year's deficit in dollars.

In the base case U stands below par for 41 of the 300 years (Figure 3). From 2137 to 2145, while the settlements build fastest toward the plan, it stands at 0.77 of par for eight years and at 0.93 in the ninth. From 2310 to the horizon it stands at 0.81, once growth past the plan has put the account in deficit and drawn the reserve, 9.6 trillion dollars at its peak in 2276, down to what the run test asks of it. The rate carries both spells, and the funders' credit line stays unused.

## Holders of U on Earth

Four groups hold U on Earth, each with its own instrument. Earth's customers and sellers keep a week of their trade in U as working balances. Investors buy ten-year bonds with dollars that reach the reserve, a tenth falling due each year, so they can leave only as fast as their bonds mature. People on Earth who spend in the zone on its culture and visits, the holders for use, keep three months of that spending in U and, where U pays a yield, stake a third of it for two years and spend the reward in the zone. The funding partners take a quarter of their returns in U, spend part of it in the zone and convert the rest.

In the base case Earth holds 0.34 trillion dollars' worth of U in 2241 and 0.85 trillion in 2341, most of it with the holders for use (Figure 4). Investors buy bonds only while U recovers toward par after a fall, expecting its appreciation to continue, and they hold up to 0.33 trillion dollars' worth around 2149. That expected gain is what brings them: investors ask a new currency for a premium of about 3.5 points over Earth's safe rate, the median for emerging markets' currencies, and the yield the zone's growth allows stays below it.

![Earth's holdings of U by group in the base case, in dollars at each year's rate.](figures/holdings.pdf){width=100%}

## Tests of a safe and healthy policy

Five tests are checked in every year from the opening:

1. inflation in U of at most 3% a year over any decade, and at most 5% in any single year;
2. a reserve of at least a fifth of the U held on Earth and six months of payments to Earth;
3. a run on what could leave within a year, met from the reserve together with six months of payments, where the run takes a quarter of the working balances, liquid holdings and unlocking stakes, and the bonds falling due in full;
4. a yield at most equal to the zone's growth over the previous twenty years;
5. Earth's holdings at most equal to the zone's yearly output.

Growth as a result must pass all five in every year, and the base case does. The policy sets U's inflation, and U's prices in the model stay independent of how much U is issued, so the first test checks the policy's own setting. The run test is the one that binds the reserve, in every year.

## The account in dollars alone

Beside U the study keeps the account in dollars alone, in which dollars serve for everything and the funders cover a year's deficit so long as every ten years together balance. The settlements then pay Earth for the dollars their residents and firms hold to pay one another, 6% of the zone's output, the middle of the 4% to 8% that replacing a currency has cost.

# Results

## The base case

With central technology and demand and the preferred policy of U, the settlements grow 7.0% a year from the opening to the plan, which they reach in 2142, and 0.57% a year after it, to 20.1 million residents by 2341. The funding partners' position (Figure 5) is deepest at 1.77 trillion dollars, 1.21 trillion of it by the opening. They are repaid in 2081, and by 2341 they have received 26.7 trillion dollars more than they put in, undiscounted, a present value of 0.79 trillion at 3% and a real return of 3.9% a year. Their last receipts come in 2265. From then on the residents' purchases from Earth, growing with Earth's income, hold the account in deficit, which the reserve carries at first and U's fall to 0.81 of par carries after.

![The funding partners' cumulative position, in the base case and in dollars alone.](figures/funders.pdf){width=100%}

## U against dollars alone

Kept in dollars alone, the same settlements reach the plan in 2150 and carry 16.2 million residents by 2341. The funders need less, 1.65 trillion dollars, and are repaid two years sooner; they earn more, 4.5% a year and 44.0 trillion dollars undiscounted, because the settlements grow less.

Three mechanisms let U carry more residents. The rate absorbs late shortfalls by lowering what residents buy from Earth, shortfalls that in dollars the funders would cover and the growth test tolerates only briefly: held at par, U carries 18.9 million, and with its floor at half of par, 24.9 million. The holders for use lend the zone their U free of interest as their spending grows, 0.22 trillion dollars' worth in 2241; holding one month of spending in place of three, they leave 19.6 million. The funders' U spent in the zone leaves dollars with the zone as savings and adds a little, since with all the funders' returns paid in dollars the result is 19.6 million. Against these, U's reserve earns Earth's 1% while the funders require 3%.

## The programme's pace carried on

On the programme's own ramp, 3.3% a year from 2049 to the plan in 2241, the settlements need 1.63 trillion dollars of funding up to 2241, repay it in 2076, return 5.3% a year to their funders to 2241, and in 2241 receive 0.78 trillion dollars more from Earth than they pay it. Carried on at the same rate past 2241, the ramp fills the eventual crater sites with 167 million residents by 2341, while the settlements' account stays in deficit for good from about 2260; by 2341 U stands at its floor, and the funders would have lent some 770 trillion dollars. The programme's pace pays its way to the plan and for about two decades beyond it.

U carries more residents than dollars wherever demand and technology let the settlements grow (Figure 6), with two exceptions. On slow technology and central demand the funders barely earn 3% in either currency, and U's reserve, held through the build at 1%, costs them that margin. On high demand the scenario's depot sales and launches outgrow any supply the study's own size checks can support. With fast technology its sales at the depot draw the water open to mining down so fast that the settlements can hardly grow in either money; with central technology the same draw caps growth past the plan, since faster growth would exhaust that water before 2341, so high demand carries about as many residents as central demand.

The widest gap comes with fast technology. In dollars alone the decade of fastest building toward the plan, 2115 to 2124, comes out barely in deficit, since the tug fleet must grow by two-thirds in six years as the legs lengthen. The decade test meets that deficit directly, almost every rate of growth past the plan then fails it, and the settlements stop at about 6.6 million. Under U the reserve carries the same decade, and they reach 30.2 million.

# Sensitivities and risks

## Sensitivities

Figure 7 moves one input at a time from the base case and shows the change in residents by 2341.

![Change in residents carried by 2341 when one input is moved from the base case, largest first.](figures/sensitivities.pdf){width=100%}

Computing contracted at the opening moves the result most. With 1 GW in place of 5 the settlements carry 12.0 million fewer residents, and with 20 GW, 28.1 million more, because computing is the largest line of sale and grows with Earth's output from what is contracted at the opening, so that the contract sets its size for three centuries. Earth's demand as a whole matters through its low end: low demand holds the settlements at their first 12,500 residents, while high demand adds about 0.1 million, since its sales at the depot draw the water open to mining so fast that faster growth past the plan would exhaust it before 2341. Slow technology takes 14.2 million residents away and fast technology adds 10.1 million. The residents' spending on Earth comes next: a fifth of their pay in place of a tenth takes 8.3 million away, and a twentieth adds 10.9 million.

The money and the water move the result less. Held at par, U carries 1.2 million fewer residents; floored at half of par, 4.8 million more, since a lower floor lets the residents' purchases from Earth absorb more of the late shortfalls. Lifting the conservation set-asides would add 4.3 million residents, the cheaper water bought at the price of the protection the set-asides give. Dollars alone take 3.9 million away, and the conservative policy of U, with its zero yield and a year of payments in reserve, takes 0.5 million. Each remaining input moves the result by 2.3 million or less. A fall of a tenth in Earth's purchases when paid in U costs 2.3 million; the response of visits to their price costs 0.7 million at 0.5 and adds 0.5 million at 2, the ends of its published range; the funders' returns paid in U, the holders' spending of their U in the zone and the months of spending held in U each move it by about half a million, and the metals in the reserve by 0.4 million, while the bonds' term and the investors' premium leave it as it is. The base case leaves the funders' credit line unused, and the conservative policy carries 19.6 million both with the line and in its absence.

On the slow side, launch alone stops all growth, and the funders earn less than their 3%. Slow local industry delays the plan by 32 years and carries 12.9 million by 2341. Slow power barely changes the residents but more than halves the funders' present value, and slow food and life support costs 1.4 million residents and a fifth of that value. Computation per watt leaves the result unchanged, because the model sells computing by the watt of capacity, at the price of the same capacity on Earth, and leaves that path unread. On the fast side, local industry alone brings the plan to 2130 and carries 22.6 million by 2341. Launch alone brings the plan to 2134 and nearly doubles the funders' present value, while its residents by 2341, 18.9 million, fall a little short of the base case, within the variation between neighbouring cases.

## Risks

The largest risk lies in demand at the opening. The computing contracted in 2049, 5 GW, is the study team's own figure and the input that moves the result most, and visits rest on an elasticity of demand constructed between two published values; together the two lines make nearly three-quarters of the receipts in 2241. The residents' purchases from Earth carry the next risk. They are the largest payment from 2211, late growth answers to them more than to any other input, and their share is again the study's own figure, tried at 5%, 10% and 20%.

The settlements pay only if their engineering improves on roughly the central paths, and held at its 2041 state technology leaves them at their first residents. Launch prices, which the wider space industry sets, form a gate of their own. Lumps of reinvestment, such as a change of power supply, a change of tug kind or a surge of building, can put a single year deep into deficit. Under U the reserve, the floor and the funders' credit line carry such years, while in dollars alone with fast technology one such decade stops growth past the plan.

U's institutions await design: the issuer and its decision rights, the reserve's custody, the clearing of payments within the zone and with Earth, and the response of prices to issuance. The model's investors expect U's recent change in value to continue, which brings them in during recoveries; how real investors treat a new currency remains to be measured. At the far horizon, growth past the plan brings the account to its limit by 2341, by construction of the growth test. With growth stopped in 2341, the base case's account would reach a lasting deficit only around 2646.

# Resource limits

With the project's set-asides, the base case's settlements, held at their 2341 size afterwards, draw the water open to mining until about 2658 on the source survey's median, and until about 2374 on its 5th percentile, where growth as a result is itself slower (17.6 million by 2341). With the survey's whole pool, the water lasts beyond the study's projection to 3541. The eventual set of crater sites holds 167 million residents in towns of 750, far beyond what growth as a result reaches. The programme's pace, carried on past 2241, fills the sites in 2341 and exhausts the water open to mining in 2343, or by 2312 on the low estimate. Regolith and metal stay ample, since the buildout handles a few hundred tonnes of rock per resident.

On the survey's median, in every path the account with Earth limits growth before any resource does. Growth as a result stops where the account allows, far short of the sites, and the programme's pace runs into lasting deficit eight to eleven decades before its water runs out.

# Recommendation and next stage

The study team recommends that the partners proceed to a pre-feasibility study of the enclosed buildout. On central assumptions the settlements repay their funding, return more than the programme requires and grow well past the plan, but the inputs that decide those results rest unevenly on evidence. The next stage would narrow the uncertainties in the order they matter:

1. **Markets at the opening.** Gather evidence for the computing and visits the settlements would sell in their first decades, with prospective customers' terms, and for Earth's demand projections behind them.
2. **The residents' economy.** Establish what share of their income residents of a settlement of this kind spend on goods and services from Earth, and build the model's next layer, trade within the zone beyond Earth, with operators in space and other settlements as partners and with knowledge work sold to Earth at a distance. Its design is complete, and a bounded lookup has gathered its first evidence.
3. **The institutions of U.** Design the issuer, the reserve and its custody, the clearing of payments and the rules that bind them, together with a price level that answers issuance, and test the holders' behaviour against evidence: the months of spending they hold, and the share of their income they spend in the zone.
4. **Engineering.** Engineer designs to a pre-feasibility standard for the founding settlements, firm power (fission, then beamed stations), the seed and growth of local manufacture, and the tugs and sources.
5. **Conservation.** Assign the sources' set-asides body by body, and settle the reservation of sites for heritage and science.

# Appendix A. Method {-}

**A year in the model.** Each year runs these steps in order:

1. Newcomers are housed: the founding system first, then cities while caves remain and towns of 750 on the crater sites, built from their design studies with the year's engineering.
2. Every stock wears out at a share a year and is renewed.
3. The year's needs are set from the residents' mix of towns and cities: water, air, supplies, local goods, firm power and the hands of services and operators.
4. The lines of sale are priced from the year's costs and served where they pay.
5. Firm power and the industry's energy are solved together, and capacity is added from the cheapest option at each kind of site.
6. Industrial plant is sized to the work asked of it.
7. Water is fetched by the tug fleet on the leg of the water being mined. New tugs are of the kind that costs least on the leg expected halfway through their life and phase in as the factories can build them; old tugs fly on until they wear out, or are retired early and sold used or taken apart for spares and metal.
8. What the settlements have yet to make is bought from Earth.
9. The hands the work needs are counted against the workforce, and industry's work beyond the hands available is done from Earth by teleoperation and paid there.
10. Under U, the ledger sets the year's rate, the reserve, Earth's holdings and the funders' flows.

**Growth as a result.** The schedule of residents is solved as two steady rates, to the plan and past it. A rate passes when four conditions hold:

- the funders' present value at 3% to 2341 is zero or more;
- the account is repaid, and every ten-year window after repayment leaves it in balance or surplus;
- every year has the hands its own work needs, and the water open to mining lasts;
- under U, all five tests hold in every year.

Each rate is found by halving and then checked against twenty slower rates; where one of those fails, the search repeats below it.

**Checks.** The study's 35 tests confirm that:

- the paths of technology hold their stated values;
- with 2041's engineering, the founding system returns its design's quantities;
- the power chosen is the cheapest offered;
- tugs draw more water the farther they fly, and each is built within its practical limit;
- the account adds up in every year, and the reserve changes by exactly the year's flows and the metals kept for it;
- U stays at par until repayment, and between its floor and par after;
- the rate absorbs a lasting shortfall above its floor, the reserve test fails when U is held at par, and the credit line covers it;
- investors leave at most as fast as their bonds mature;
- holders for use keep their months of spending in U;
- the dollar account pays for the dollars its residents hold;
- the five tests catch unsafe policies;
- every slower rate the solver checks below a solved rate passes;
- the saved results, and this study's measures drawn from them, are current with the model and its inputs;
- the funders' rate of return is recovered from flows whose rate is known;
- a case can move one group of the technology stack to another path while every other quantity keeps its own;
- the options for computing contracted at the opening and for the visits' response to price leave a case unchanged at the scenario's own values and move their own lines otherwise;
- the tug fleet always carries the year's water, changes kind gradually, and sells used tugs only in years when some are retired;
- a tug flies on beyond the limit on building it, delivering less, until its leg stops closing.

# Appendix D. Terms {-}

**Account with Earth.** What Earth's customers pay the settlements, against what the settlements pay Earth, each year.

**Credit line.** The funding partners' undertaking, once repaid, to lend the reserve what it lacks of its minimum when U is at its floor.

**Floor.** The lowest rate U may fall to, 75% of par under the preferred policy.

**Funding partners.** The participants who provide the founding capital and cover the settlements' shortfalls with Earth until their funding is repaid.

**Growth as a result.** The fastest steady growth at which the settlements still pay their funders 3% a year and the tests hold.

**Holders for use.** People on Earth who keep U to spend in the zone.

**Par.** One dollar of 2041 for one U.

**The plan.** The programme's planned 6.47 million residents, reached on its own schedule in 2241.

**Present value at 3%.** The funders' yearly flows discounted at 3% a year to 2041.

**Rate.** U's value in dollars.

**Reserve.** The dollars, other currencies and metals held against U.

**Run test.** The test that the reserve can meet a run on what could leave within a year, together with six months of payments.

**Set-asides.** The share of each near-Earth body's water left unmined, for future use, protection and uncertainty.

**U.** The money of the zone beyond Earth.

**Working balances.** The U that Earth's customers and sellers keep for a week of their trade.

**Zone beyond Earth.** The settlements and operations that use U.
