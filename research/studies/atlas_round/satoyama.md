# Satoyama-like landscapes: what the atlas round looks for

The atlas round looks for satoyama-like landscapes beside the ecosystems already considered: mosaics of managed woodland, fields, ponds, irrigation channels, wetlands and settlements that support one another, adapted to the Moon's warmer climate, with satoumi along the salt South Pole–Aitken Sea's shores ([register](../../decisions.md#the-atlas-rounds-frame-16-november-2026), 16 November). Their criteria are set now, beside the drainage's rebuild, and their candidates are mapped with it and refreshed on the design climate's run on the final surface; their methods are chosen to limit methane. This note turns the Earth evidence into criteria that the round's products can test. The evidence is a bounded literature check of 16 November, saved with its sources and working extracts on the research drive (`atlas-round/satoyama_literature.md`); the figures this note relies on from the IPCC's tables, the Sri Lankan heritage proposal and Japan's mapping rule were checked against those extracts. What grows in these landscapes and how they are run stays with the ecology lane; the atlas round finds where the land and the water suit them.

## What the evidence shows

Every system the check found keeps the same order down the slope: woodland on the crests and upper slopes, settlements, gardens and rain-fed fields on the mid-slopes, flooded fields on terraces or valley floors, and a drainage line below, with water moving down through them by gravity and reused on the way. Japan's mapping rule for a satoyama cell asks that farmland, secondary grassland and secondary forest together cover at least half of a square kilometre, with at least two of the three present (Ministry of the Environment 2014). The woodland carried the fields: in the green-manure era a village needed forest and grassland about ten times its farmed area for green manure, fodder and fuel (Takemoto 2024; Ohsawa and Kitazawa 2009).

The closest match to the Moon's warmth is Sri Lanka's dry-zone cascade of village tanks, a recognized agricultural heritage system: an annual mean of about 27.7 °C at its heritage site, against the design climate's 301 K, and 800–2,000 mm of rain a year with a long dry season. A cascade is a chain of 4–10 small reservoirs in a catchment of 15–26 km², each tank's paddy draining into the next; at the heritage site 53 working tanks store about 1.2 m of water over the paddy they irrigate, which covers about 6% of the land, and every tank is ringed by named buffers: a tree belt, a sediment meadow and trap, and an interceptor wetland below the bund (FAO and Sri Lanka's Ministry of Agriculture 2017). The mountain terraces, Hani in Yunnan, Ifugao and Bali's subak, run on continuous outflow from forested upper catchments, with forest about half the land at Hani and terraces about 28%.

## Methane sets the scale of the flooded fields

The flooded paddies and the ponds are the mosaic's methane sources, and the Moon's methane budget is thin. The register holds methane at 1.9 ppm by biological means with technology making up the difference, because the Moon's own sinks would let it settle at 22–29 ppm (domain synthesis). With the lakes and seas kept nutrient-poor, the lake methane study leaves 0.35 Tg of methane a year of room under 1.87 ppm at the removal ledger's high-cost end, and 2.5 Tg at its low-cost end ([lake methane](../lake_methane/README.md)). At the IPCC's default rate for continuously flooded rice without organic inputs, 1.19 kg of methane a hectare a day, a 113-day season gives about 134 kg a hectare, so a square kilometre cropped twice a year gives about 27 t (arithmetic on the IPCC 2019 Refinement's Table 5.11). The whole room would then hold about 13,000 km² of such paddy at the high-cost end and 93,000 km² at the low-cost end, beside whatever the food systems already count, and less once ponds (183 kg a hectare a year) and channels (416) are added. The landscape eligible by terrain and water is far larger, so the methane budget, not the land, bounds the flooded part of these landscapes. The woodland, gardens and rain-fed fields emit little.

The methods that cut it, with the IPCC's scaling factors and measured costs:

- **Drain the paddies**, more than once a season (×0.55 of continuous flooding) or at least once mid-season (×0.71), keeping the drying mild, the water table no lower than 15 cm, so that yields hold; a meta-analysis found yields fall only where the drying is severe.
- **Keep fields dry between crops**: more than 30 days of flooding before planting multiplies methane by 2.4, and rice after a year of upland crops or dry fallow takes ×0.59. The Sri Lankan and Balinese calendars, upland crops on the drained paddies in the off-season, are of this kind; Ifugao's year-round flooding, which keeps its earthen terraces from cracking, is the highest-emitting of the analogues.
- **Compost the straw** or plough it in early: per tonne, compost carries 0.17 and straw ploughed in more than 30 days ahead 0.19 of the methane of fresh straw ploughed in just before flooding.
- **Hold the nitrogen with the water**: drying raises nitrous oxide, by 105% in a meta-analysis and up to 33 kg a hectare a season on some Indian farms, which can outweigh the methane saved; shallow flooding of 5–7 cm with nitrogen managed alongside keeps both down.
- **Keep the ponds clean and oxygenated**: livestock fenced out of farm ponds cut their methane by 56% or more, and aeration and mixing reduce it in aquaculture ponds; ponds under a tenth of a hectare give about 40% of the world's diffusive open-water methane, so the size of the ponds matters too.
- **Methane-eating biology at the sources**, which the register's methods and the aquatic programme already rely on: inoculated paddies have shown 17–44% less methane in early trials.

A Moon-specific question follows from the light cycle. The paddy's microbes make methane through the 15-day night as by day, so a drainage schedule fitted to the 29.5-day cycle is for the round and the ecology lane to work out.

## What the candidate map will hold

With step 2's drainage on the redrawn floor and the present design climate, the first map gives candidate catchments that meet the order down the slope, the slopes, the water and the rain, with their woodland share and size; the shallows along the south sea that suit satoumi; and, for each candidate, the flooded area it could carry and the methane that area would give under the methods above, summed against the room under 1.87 ppm. The refresh after the climate run on the final surface replaces the climate, and the falls survey adds the falls on finer heights where they meet the candidates.

## Gaps the evidence leaves

No slope beyond which only woodland stands; no measured methane budget for a whole satoyama-type landscape; no tank water-surface areas for the Sri Lankan site; no numbers for Japan's irrigation ponds; and several heritage dossiers, among them the Japan Satoyama Satoumi Assessment and UNESCO's nominations for Hani, Ifugao and Bali, which could not be opened, so their figures came through search summaries and are flagged in the saved report.
