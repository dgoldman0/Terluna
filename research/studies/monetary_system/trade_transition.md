# Transitioning trade and finance toward U

U should become the principal unit in which the planned off-Earth economy sets prices, contracts, receives payment and finances production. A substantial transition away from USD requires all four uses to develop together. Large worldwide demand for lunar goods, sports, entertainment and services provides an opportunity to establish U outside the resident population, while the currencies of suppliers, creditors and investors determine how much external liquidity remains necessary.

The planning alternatives are **one U currency** and **a common U formed from a basket of participating U-related currencies**. In the basket alternative, its constituents belong to that monetary system. Earth currencies remain external trading currencies. Separately, a **partial reserve system**, funded from an explicit share of initial investment and later earnings, can support settlement and external payments. Its holdings do not define U's basket or establish a USD peg.

This proposal covers the whole currently planned economic zone beyond Earth through enclosed development, before the Open Moon, planetary array and subsequent outer Solar System buildout. It extends U's established role as the general economic valuation unit into a proposed contract and payment system. The earlier real-U accounts remain reproducible; an operational U currency would need its own nominal conventions, institutions and opening valuation. The provisioning account's illustrative dollar normalization supplies no adopted exchange rate.

## The monetary architecture

The two alternatives share a common outward-facing U price and payment convention, but allocate monetary powers differently.

| Question | Single U currency | Basket of U-related currencies |
|---|---|---|
| What defines the unit? | One nominal unit under a common monetary mandate | Published quantities of participating currencies define one basket U |
| Who issues money? | A common settlement issuer; banks may issue U deposits under agreed rules | Constituent issuers supply their currencies; a clearing institution may additionally issue basket-denominated payment claims |
| What can vary inside the zone? | Credit quality and local prices; ordinary bank deposits are claims in the same currency | Constituent exchange rates as well as credit quality and local prices |
| What coordinates policy? | A common policy framework with representation and fiscal/loss arrangements | Joint rules for the basket, clearing, constituent policies, liquidity and allocation of losses |
| How does trade with Earth work? | U exchanges with Earth currencies at executable market quotes or an explicitly chosen intervention rule | The basket and its payment claims exchange with Earth currencies; an Earth currency is not automatically a constituent or anchor |

Different banks' deposits in the same U are not, by themselves, different currencies. Constituent currencies need genuinely distinct denomination and issuance arrangements. The plan therefore does not assign a currency to every town, company or sport. Participation should follow an actual monetary constituency and a reason to retain separate issuance, with institutions capable of maintaining its payments and accounts.

The European Currency Unit offers a relevant structural comparison: it combined fixed amounts of participating European currencies, whereas the euro became a single currency. This supports distinguishing the two designs, not importing their membership, governance or historical exchange-rate rules into the U economy. [ECB glossary](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/services/glossary/html/act4e.en.html), [Duisenberg's account of the transition](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/key/date/1998/html/sp980116.en.html).

For the basket alternative, write

\[
1\,U=\sum_i a_i u_i,\qquad E_{U/S}=\sum_i a_i E_{u_i/S},
\]

where the constituent currencies are u_i, their fixed quantities are a_i, and S is an external comparison currency. This gives the basket's reference valuation. A particular issuer's U payment claim can trade at a different price because of its credit quality, liquidity and redemption terms. Expressing the reference value in S does not promise S redemption or make S its backing asset. Fixed quantities also differ from permanently fixed percentage value weights: the percentages change as constituent exchange rates move. Scheduled changes to quantities should preserve the basket's value at the changeover. Admission, withdrawal, default and rebalancing rules must nevertheless be agreed in advance: continuity at that instant does not preserve the holder's future currency exposure.

The basket definition needs three further agreements. First, specify whether a U contract can be discharged in constituents, a common U settlement claim or another currency at an agreed quote. Second, identify the issuer and redemption terms of each payment claim: a bank balance denominated in basket U is not automatically a segregated holding of every constituent. Third, establish a domestic price-stability framework. The currency constituents of U and the goods and services used to measure its purchasing power are different baskets. Defining every constituent only by a peg to U, while defining U only by those constituents, would leave the overall price scale undetermined. A coordinated domestic price mandate and compatible constituent policies provide a direction for analysis; basket arithmetic alone does not stabilize purchasing power.

## Moving substantially away from USD

Four adoption measures should be reported separately: the share of prices genuinely fixed in U between revisions, the share of invoices denominated in U, the share of payments settled in U, and balances voluntarily retained in U by outside users. A USD price converted into U at checkout advances payment capability while retaining USD price exposure. Empirical invoicing research also shows that settlement statistics need not measure the currency of invoices. [IMF invoicing study](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/wp/2025/english/wpiea2025178-source-pdf.pdf).

The following are proposed implementation stages. Their percentage goals are planning targets for evaluation, not demand forecasts or adopted legal requirements. Advancement depends on the stated conditions, rather than a population threshold or fixed calendar.

| Stage | Work and proposed adoption objective | Condition for advancement |
|---|---|---|
| Establish the U contract system | Define current-U amounts, dated real-U reporting, quotes, payment finality, disputes and the single-currency/basket alternatives. Trial genuine U prices and contracts with willing suppliers and customers. | Reconciled accounts, executable conversions and accepted contract terms; a chosen issuance/clearing constitution before new payment liabilities are issued |
| Make U usable throughout the zone | Move new procurement, service charges, payroll arrangements and working-capital contracts toward U by agreement. Target at least 90% of eligible internal payments in the common U system. Establish the separately funded reserve account and payment continuity arrangements. | Reliable settlement, funded operational losses, clear constituent convertibility where applicable, and protection of essential external payments |
| Make U the normal export unit | Seek a majority of export prices and invoices genuinely set in U, and an 85–95% share of export payment value settled through U. Offer Earth-currency checkout through processors where customers prefer it. | Repeat customer use, measured conversion costs and cancellations, sufficient dealer inventory, supplier acceptance and manageable redemption concentrations |
| Deepen U finance and reduce residual USD exposure | Expand U deposits, trade credit and term funding matched to U receipts; negotiate more external procurement in U or constituents. Refinance legacy USD obligations when commercially justified. | Currency- and maturity-specific funding stress coverage, credible loss allocation and a demonstrated market for U claims; USD liquidity falls as uncovered USD obligations fall |

Targets distinguish the common U system from aggregate constituent use. In a federation, reports should show basket-U payment shares and payments in each constituent separately; a constituent payment discharging a U invoice must retain its conversion record. Financial reporting should also disclose the share still economically indexed to USD, even where the invoice or settlement label is U.

Existing contracts retain their promised denomination until counterparties agree otherwise or contractual terms provide for change. New U debt can match U revenues and reduce currency mismatch, but creditors will price the associated risks. The transition should compare that financing cost with avoided exposure rather than assume local-currency borrowing is cheaper. A banking system also needs asset quality, supervision, liquidity access and a funded resolution process; international currency use benefits from credible institutions and markets in assets outsiders are willing to hold. [ECB, international role of the euro](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/other-publications/ire/html/ecb.ire202606.en.html).

## Using the nine export portfolios

The worldwide service opportunities are common to all nine portfolios. Their role in U adoption can be much larger than the resident population because customers, distributors, sponsors, research institutions and mission operators can all transact in U.

| Portfolio | Practical route toward U pricing and retained balances | Exposure that remains |
|---|---|---|
| Raw-resource price proxy | U quotations and settlement for contracts whose buyers accept them | Commodity benchmarks and competing supply may keep prices economically linked to an Earth currency |
| Commodity refined materials | Repeated U supply contracts; supplier balances reusable for other purchases in the zone | Thin margins can make conversion charges and currency risk decisive |
| Specialty products | U prices for qualified specifications, certification and delivery contracts | Buyer acceptance, qualification and imported production inputs |
| Premium lunar manufacturing | U-denominated purchase commitments and milestone payments for differentiated output | Product claims must be demonstrated; prepayments remain delivery obligations |
| Propellant and logistics | U mission accounts covering fuel, servicing, storage and recurring operations | Route compatibility, mission budgets, and external equipment or financing costs |
| Computing and digital exports | U contracts and processor settlement, with recurring institutional balances | Earth price competition, hardware imports and customers' willingness to bear currency exposure |
| Services with domestic support | U rights, subscriptions, research access and professional-service contracts; convenient Earth-currency checkout | Platform costs, audience retention and the difference between automatic conversion and balances actually retained |
| Diversified portfolio | Reuse U receipts among material, mission, cultural and research businesses | Shared capacity and credit exposures; internal turnover must not be counted as new exports |
| Earlier manufacturing mix | U pricing for accepted products, linked to U working capital and supplier contracts | Maintaining the assumed sale prices and reducing imported-input mismatch |

Sports and entertainment can introduce U to a worldwide audience without requiring every fan to manage a currency account. A platform can display the U price, give a firm local-currency checkout quote and deliver U to the producer. Repeat users may choose U balances where they offer convenient purchases across several activities. Sponsors and rights buyers can hold larger working balances. Retention should follow usefulness, reliable redemption and competitive costs; inducements that reduce revenue or subsidize conversion must enter the commercial account.

Prepaid subscriptions, research deposits and reserved mission slots require separate accounting. They can supply liquidity before delivery, but also create performance or refund liabilities. They are not simultaneously earned revenue, freely distributable reserves and a second source of monetary wealth.

## Partial reserves funded by investment and earnings

A partial reserve system can complement either monetary design. Constituent settlement balances help discharge payments inside a U federation; liquid Earth-currency assets help meet external invoices and conversions; loss-absorbing capital covers losses. These perform different functions and need separate accounts. A reserve portfolio may include USD while USD obligations remain, without making USD a basket constituent, backing every U claim with dollars or promising a dollar exchange rate.

The proposed funding rule starts with a separately identified allocation from investment capital, then adds agreed contributions from cash receipts after operating costs, renewal and prior obligations. Contributions might be contractual payments from participating enterprises or another explicitly authorized revenue arrangement; foreign export income does not automatically belong to the monetary authority. Reserve investment income, custody costs, losses and withdrawals belong in the same account. Existing operating buffers must be reconciled before adding a reserve allowance, so their assets are neither pledged twice nor charged twice.

For example, out of an illustrative investment of 100 external-value units, allocate 5 to reserves and 95 to productive capital. If three subsequent years each provide 10 units of available cash and 20% of that cash is reserved, reserves become 11 and the remaining cash for expansion or distributions is 24. The full account is 130 of funding and earnings = 95 productive capital + 11 reserve assets + 24 other uses. This example supplies no actual U/USD rate. The existing industrial seed budget is already allocated; adding such reserves requires explicit additional funding or a revised deployment programme, rather than silently keeping both the original build and the money diverted from it.

Against an illustrative 40 units of relevant external-conversion liabilities, 11 units of liquid assets cover 27.5%. A simultaneous conversion demand of 25% requires 10 and leaves 1; a 40% demand requires 16 and leaves a gap of 5. A 10% haircut to reserve value leaves 9.9, already 0.1 short of the smaller withdrawal. These are stress arithmetic, not a recommended reserve ratio. Coverage of settlement money also differs from coverage of all bank deposits, loans and contractual obligations.

Reserve sufficiency should be tested for each institution, currency and stress horizon:

\[
\text{unencumbered liquid assets + usable committed funding + stressed receipts}
\geq
\text{essential payments + debt service + conversions + margin calls}.
\]

The right-hand side must trace each underlying payment once: an importer's conversion request and its subsequent payment of the same invoice are not two independent external outflows. Likewise, an asset pledged to a settlement facility cannot also be counted as unrestricted industrial funding. Short hedges and external credit lines require maturity, collateral and access checks; a currency-matched asset can still be illiquid when payment is due. [BIS, foreign-currency funding risk](https://www.bis.org/publications/cgfs-paper-71-foreign-currency-funding-risk-and-cross-border-liquidity.pdf).

Partial external reserves support market making and continuity, but do not establish unlimited conversion at a fixed Earth-currency price. If the U basket is redeemable into constituent currencies at specified quantities, those internal obligations need their own liquidity arrangements. A common institution cannot assume it can issue every constituent's currency or draw on an Earth central bank. The monetary agreement must allocate liquidity authority and losses explicitly.

## Why exports can support U without determining its exchange rate

When a customer acquires U to buy an export, somebody supplies the U. It may come from a dealer's existing inventory, another user's sale or newly issued claims. The exporter may then hold U, spend it within the zone, or sell it for outside currency. U invoicing therefore creates a payment need; its effect on outstanding balances and exchange rates depends on the entire sequence.

The [transaction calculation](trade_transition.py) holds the real trade identical in three cases: exports of 100 and imports of 60 at an illustrative quotation, with an unchanged opening stock of 120 U. It gives the issuer no foreign-currency backing requirement or exchange-rate commitment.

| End of the trade sequence | Immediate exporter reconversion | Exporter retains U | Customer also retains U |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Dealer's U inventory | 120 | 80 | 60 |
| Exporter's U balance | 0 | 40 | 40 |
| Customer's U balance | 0 | 0 | 20 |
| Exporter's outside-currency balance | 40 | 0 | 0 |
| Total U stock | 120 | 120 | 120 |
| Change in the zone's net external financial assets | +40 | +40 | +40 |

The customer's additional balance in the third case comes from the dealer's inventory. It is economically relevant demand, but it neither creates new U automatically nor adds another export receipt. Repeated inventory depletion could change dealers' prices or induce new supply; the transaction ledger deliberately leaves that market response open.

For steady payment flow X, U settlement share s and average balance holding time t days, a first transaction-balance approximation is H = sXt/365. The companion calculation applies shares of 25%, 60% and 85% and holding periods of 1, 7 and 30 days to each funded portfolio/date. These are alternatives, not additive sources of demand. Netting, credit, existing balances and dealer inventories can change the incremental requirement. For illustration, 85% of an annual flow valued at $1 trillion implies average transaction balances worth about $2.3 billion at one day or $69.9 billion at thirty days, at the same source valuation. Neither amount is annual income, seigniorage or a forecast U money stock.

At year 100 in the strong worldwide-service market, the same 85% settlement experiment gives the following **average transaction-balance stocks, in billion USD2041 source-value equivalents**. Each column is a separate holding-period assumption. These valuations permit comparison with the financial source; they are not nominal U balances or an adopted USD/U conversion.

| Portfolio | Seven-day holding period | Thirty-day holding period |
|---|---:|---:|
| Raw-resource price proxy | 15.5 | 66.4 |
| Commodity refined materials | 13.4 | 57.5 |
| Specialty products | 13.7 | 58.6 |
| Premium lunar manufacturing | 11.1 | 47.5 |
| Propellant and logistics | 16.3 | 69.9 |
| Computing and digital exports | 15.6 | 67.0 |
| Services with domestic support | 15.5 | 66.4 |
| Diversified portfolio | 14.2 | 60.8 |
| Earlier manufacturing mix | 11.2 | 47.9 |

Longer-term U deposits and securities could attract additional outside holdings. Their demand depends on returns, liquidity and risk and needs a separate asset-demand account. The transaction-balance table neither includes those holdings nor limits the eventual international use of U.

Under floating external exchange rates, stronger desired holdings relative to available U and constituent balances can support appreciation. More issuance, exporter conversions, foreign purchases and outward investment can offset that demand. Appreciation can reduce imported prices in U while making genuinely U-priced exports more expensive to Earth buyers; customer quantities and firms' margins then matter. Under an explicitly chosen intervention rule, reserve purchases or sales change part of the adjustment. An internal basket and partial reserves select neither a dollar peg nor a guaranteed appreciation path.

Domestic inflation remains another outcome. Issuance and credit that increase U expenditure can raise domestic prices when supply cannot respond, even while U appreciates externally. Productivity can improve domestic purchasing power without requiring appreciation against every Earth currency. The [domestic price study](../complex_returns/prices.md) provides conditional spending/supply comparisons; it does not yet connect these monetary transactions to endogenous prices or exchange rates.

## Decisions and measurements for the next integrated model

The next model should use the basket of U-related currencies as an explicit scenario alongside a single U currency. It needs participating issuers and basket quantities, a common domestic price objective, clearing and liquidity rules, reserve funding and ownership, and contract-specific conversion rights. Those are proposed institutional choices, distinct from the established U accounting convention and the direction toward substantially greater U use.

For each export activity and external obligation, record the price-setting unit, invoice unit, payment currency, reset/indexation rule, maturity, counterparty residence, conversion route and intended balance holding period. Track customer adoption and willingness to pay after conversion costs, including cases where a U requirement loses orders. Record import currencies and legacy debt with the same care. Then model money and credit issuance, member clearing positions, dealer inventories, foreign assets and reserve policy together with production, investment and spending.

This would permit comparisons of inflation, external appreciation or depreciation, reserve depletion and financing costs under the nine portfolios. Until those relationships are specified, substantial U adoption is a concrete development strategy whose monetary outcomes remain conditional. Its success should be judged by useful circulation, purchasing-power stability, manageable funding risk and sustained production, rather than by currency appreciation alone.
