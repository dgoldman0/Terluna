# Monetary institutions for the Open Moon and its trading partners

31 October 2026. **Every monetary arrangement performs the same functions: it issues the claims that circulate, settles payments, extends credit, bears losses and risk, and takes its collective decisions through whoever holds the decision rights.** How an arrangement assigns these functions determines how an early lunar settlement can develop credit, keep essential payments going and absorb shocks while it grows into a large economy. The arrangements compared here form one family, the currency areas, banks, fiscal authorities and central banks of present economies, and the choice among arrangements stays with the communities' collective decisions ([register](../../decisions.md#automation-the-network-and-food-12-october-2026)). The Earth, Open Moon and array are territories in the production comparison; their physical boundaries set neither issuers nor monetary jurisdictions, several currencies could circulate within a world, and a monetary union could span parts of several worlds.

This synthesis accompanies the [study overview](README.md) and [external accounts](external_accounts.md). It develops the mechanisms and their conditional lunar applications for readers familiar with the three-world economy. Present institutions supply the evidence of how each arrangement operates, and the lunar arrangements are proposals for comparison, with the currency arrangement, collective revenue, any mandate and any interworld guarantee still to be chosen. The population cases remain Earth nine billion, Moon eight billion and array five billion, with the colonies outside those three totals, and equal future technology applies throughout.

## Credit, collective finance and the institutions that connect them

Monetary arrangements govern which claims circulate, how obligations are discharged and who bears losses when anticipated production fails. Their consequences reach construction schedules, household security and industrial continuity. Sports, manufacturing and other exports contribute receipts within this system; each remains a distinct business whose commercial prospects require its own analysis.

Local purchasing power can support additional real production when usable lunar capacity stands idle because customers, firms or collective budgets lack finance. Keeping repairs, local supply contracts or housing completion funded through a financial interruption could prevent avoidable losses. The benefit depends on what additional expenditure buys. Available domestic capacity can respond; imported inputs also require willing suppliers, an acceptable means of payment and delivery. Monetary autonomy therefore deserves analysis through the composition and responsiveness of supply. Its usefulness changes as the domestic economy develops.

The common real unit U used in the economic comparison remains an accounting numeraire. A monetary scenario additionally needs nominal prices, contract currencies, exchange rates and price indices. Changing a currency's exchange value must not automatically change the physical production assumptions or be interpreted as an equivalent change in domestic living standards. Likewise, the construction model's patient real financing rate does not establish a monetary authority's future policy rate.

Each function stays visible even where one body performs several. The present-day family assigns them as follows:

| Function | In the present-day family | Consequential boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Collective spending and revenue, construction appropriations, transfers and accepted guarantees | A fiscal authority or Treasury | Assigns collective costs and losses to an identified budget |
| Issuing the settlement asset, setting its stance and lending against collateral | A monetary authority or shared central bank | Its powers depend on its currency arrangement and mandate |
| Payment deposits, credit assessment, working capital and longer-term lending | Commercial banks | Require capital, funding, settlement access and arrangements for failure |
| Long-horizon infrastructure loans, guarantees or equity | A development-finance institution | Investment losses and subsidies need explicit collective treatment |
| Bearing losses when an institution fails, protecting designated claims and keeping critical functions running | A deposit insurer and resolution authority | Promises require credible funding in the currency owed |
| Clearing, final settlement, custody and transfers against payment | Payment and securities infrastructure | A payment instruction, final payment and delivery are separate events |
| Maintaining habitats, utilities, transport and environmental services | Essential-service operators | Financial provision must connect to operating capability |
| Deciding the rules and holding the decision rights over all of the above | The bodies the communities charge with these functions, deciding collectively ([register](../../decisions.md#automation-the-network-and-food-12-october-2026)) | Each mandate names who decides, who bears which losses and how the rules change |

Commercial-bank lending creates a loan asset and a matching deposit liability. Payments subsequently made to customers of other banks can require settlement-asset transfers. Profitability, regulation and funding constrain lending; a fixed multiplication of pre-existing reserves does not describe the process. [Bank of England, *Money creation in the modern economy*, pp. 14–17](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/quarterly-bulletin/2014/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy.pdf)

Consequently, lunar banks could finance a substantial domestic economy without collecting an equal new saving deposit before every loan. The expenditure pattern still matters. A borrower paying local contractors presents different settlement and currency needs from one immediately purchasing array equipment. Consolidating the Moon's accounts conceals these operational differences unless the institutions and payment flows remain visible.

A development bank financing atmospheric delivery makes a long-duration investment decision. Its capital, guarantees and expected losses belong in the development and fiscal accounts. Central-bank lending to an eligible institution against collateral serves a different purpose. Placing both transactions on a public balance sheet does not make their risks or policy justifications interchangeable.

## Four arrangements within the family

Within this family, four currency arrangements differ in who issues the settlement asset, who can lend it in an emergency and who stands behind its losses.

### Use an established external currency

Early communities could denominate wages, procurement, deposits and taxes in a currency already used by their main suppliers and sponsors. This arrangement reduces one source of mismatch when imported equipment and essential services are priced in that same unit. It also concentrates dependence on access to institutions issuing, clearing and lending it. The comparison must specify those contractual relationships, rather than infer them from currency use alone.

Unilateral use does not establish membership in the issuer's central bank or entitlement to its facilities. An Earth bank's lunar branch, a separately capitalized lunar subsidiary and an independent lunar bank could have different support arrangements. Their legal form, eligible collateral and access rights would determine whether an interworld banking group actually provides useful diversification during a lunar crisis.

Local deposit lending could operate in the external currency, while conversion and interbank payments would require funding in it. An external-currency liquidity pool, committed credit facilities and diversified payment relationships could support the system. Their costs and conditional availability would enter the lunar financial account. The [Federal Reserve's swap arrangements](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/bst_swapfaqs.htm) illustrate why access must be specified: even standing facilities retain approval conditions, and receiving central banks determine onward lending rather than automatically transmitting an unconditional guarantee.

The early attraction is operational continuity with the existing economy financing settlement. The vulnerability is a shock that simultaneously reduces external receipts and increases demand for liquid external-currency claims. A local institution cannot resolve that mismatch merely by issuing additional promises in the same denomination. This architecture can nevertheless persist into maturity if support, governance and resident preferences make it satisfactory; population growth supplies no automatic reason to abandon it.

### Join or establish a monetary union

A negotiated union would establish shared monetary institutions and membership rights. Eligible lunar banks could obtain common settlement assets and facilities under the union's rules, while several fiscal authorities retained their own budgets. The arrangement would need to settle representation, supervision, bank resolution, collateral eligibility, guarantees and the distribution of monetary income. A common name or unit of account would establish none of these rights by itself.

A union removes nominal exchange-rate adjustment among its members. A lunar construction pause could therefore coincide with strong array computing demand while the common monetary stance reflects conditions across the area. Local stabilization would then depend more heavily on fiscal action, cross-world asset income, credit, price adjustment or migration. The ECB distinguishes capital-income, credit and fiscal-transfer channels through which members can share shocks; its historical estimates describe the euro area. [ECB, *Risk sharing in the euro area*, sections 1–2](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/economic-bulletin/articles/2022/html/ecb.ebart202207_01~f71f0eaa4a.en.html)

Physical mobility needs particular attention. Cheaper transport would not instantly provide housing, serviced habitat capacity or suitable work elsewhere. Gravity adaptation, occupational specialization and family ties could also limit relocation, although their quantitative effects remain unestablished. A union relying principally on migration for adjustment would need evidence that movement can occur on the timescale of its shocks. Funded transfers or cross-world ownership can distribute losses without requiring immediate relocation.

Currency sharing also leaves cross-world debts and income claims consequential. Residents of the Moon could owe residents of Earth or the array substantial amounts in the same currency. Banking integration might ease refinancing while exposing several territories to a common creditor's retrenchment. Shared supervision and a credible allocation of resolution losses would therefore be central features of the comparison.

### Issue a currency under a board or peg

A currency board would combine a local unit with a restrictive conversion commitment. Hong Kong's official description illustrates base-money backing with reserve-currency assets and the contraction of clearing balances as outflows are converted. The monetary base it identifies is distinct from the commercial banking system's total customer deposits. [HKMA, operation of the linked exchange-rate system](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/key-functions/money/linked-exchange-rate-system/how-does-the-lers-work/); [components of the monetary base](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/key-functions/money/linked-exchange-rate-system/components-of-the-monetary-base/)

For a lunar board, the backing assets would serve the conversion promise and could not simultaneously be freely committed to construction. Nor would base backing insure banks whose deposit liabilities were matched mainly by local loans. Emergency support would require resources and rules consistent with the conversion obligation.

The adjustment mechanism matters during development. A reduction in external receipts or confidence could withdraw liquidity precisely when local firms need working capital to finish construction or retain productive capacity. Fiscal buffers, bank liquidity and flexible installation schedules could reduce the disruption. They would be parts of the architecture, rather than evidence that the exchange-rate commitment itself guarantees stability.

A discretionary peg differs from a board: the authority has more latitude over intervention and domestic liquidity, while the durability and possible revision of the peg become additional expectations to manage. A basket could spread exposure where counterparties genuinely use several currencies, at the cost of more complicated contracts and reserve management. Neither arrangement permits unrestricted capital flows, a fixed exchange rate and fully independent monetary policy simultaneously. Floating allows additional policy autonomy while retaining exposure to international financial conditions. [IMF, *Two Trilemmas for Monetary Policy*](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2017/07/24/sp072417-two-trilemmas-for-monetary-policy)

### Maintain an independent floating currency

An independent currency issuer could supply its own settlement asset and choose monetary policy for domestic conditions within its mandate. Domestic-currency public debt would avoid an automatic rise in local-currency principal when the exchange rate depreciates. Exchange-rate changes could also encourage substitution and additional export sales where production and demand can respond.

These capacities become more useful as a broader domestic economy develops. An early settlement heavily dependent on externally priced equipment might face sharply higher replacement costs before a depreciation produced appreciable additional exports. A mature Moon with extensive domestic housing, food, services and manufacturing could have more scope to redirect demand toward local capacity. The comparison should test that structural change rather than apply one import dependence assumption throughout settlement.

Currency mismatches determine who initially bears the adjustment. Foreign-currency obligations financed from local receipts become more expensive in local units after depreciation. Foreign receipts can supply a partial natural hedge. When outside investors instead hold lunar-currency debt, they bear exchange-rate exposure; their required return, lending volume and hedging choices may respond. Creditor residence and contract currency must therefore remain separate variables.

A float would require a credible nominal policy framework, functioning settlement, bank oversight and a fiscal relationship capable of surviving adverse conditions. It would offer tools for managing shocks, while their effectiveness would still depend on supply, external acceptance and balance-sheet exposure. Neither mature population nor technical sophistication selects this arrangement over a union.

## Monetary transmission with automation and unequal shocks

Array computing could improve forecasting, credit monitoring, administration and payment processing. It would not decide who should absorb losses or make every future contingency contractible. All three economies also receive the same technological capabilities; financial advantage cannot be assigned to the Moon merely because its institutions use advanced software.

Even when machines perform much of production, financing conditions affect the timing of equipment, inventories, habitat construction and replacement. Long-lived assets remain sensitive to anticipated demand and discount rates. Serviced space, desirable locations, ecological establishment and transport capacity can remain scarce. Monetary transmission should consequently include capital spending and allocation of physical capacity, with human employment channels conditioned on the future labor organization rather than imported unchanged from today's economy.

The distinction between financial and physical shocks governs the response. If private spending falls while repair facilities, materials and useful projects remain available, continued credit or public expenditure could preserve output. If an essential route fails, additional money cannot deliver its missing cargo. Finance could still support alternative sourcing, local substitutes and reconstruction, provided those activities are physically possible. A policy comparison needs both the available capacity and the currency needed to mobilize it.

A common shock may also have unequal effects. Disruption to the array could impair its export earnings while depriving lunar firms of computing, power or protection services. Support to lunar buyers alone would not restore the supplier's capacity. Coordination would need to connect financial relief, repair procurement and the allocation of scarce service, without counting transfers as additional production.

### The same lunar shocks under different arrangements

These prospective comparisons hold the initial physical capacities and purchasing needs fixed across regimes. In the funding case, available imports and maturing import-related debt are payable in S, the main supplier's currency; the union includes its issuer. Emergency lending assumes eligible, solvent borrowers. Fiscal transfers and guarantees require explicit commitments. The funding interruption most directly distinguishes unilateral currency use from negotiated union membership: identical denomination can accompany different access rights. The construction slump instead tests whether policy can maintain demand for resources already available on the Moon. A float offers a separate monetary response, while a union could provide stronger fiscal insurance if its members commit to it. Neither advantage follows from the regime's name alone.

The array failure exposes the common physical limit. Financial arrangements can determine who keeps operating, who pays for repairs and who bears losses during restoration. Their contribution depends on repair capability and substitute services. Comparing these responses therefore requires both institutional access and the affected production chain; ranking currencies without those conditions would conceal the actual adjustment.

## Emergency liquidity, insolvency and public losses

A solvent institution holding illiquid assets has a different problem from one whose expected assets cannot cover its liabilities. The ECB's emergency-liquidity framework expressly concerns solvent financial institutions facing temporary liquidity problems. [ECB, emergency liquidity assistance](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/mopo/ela/html/index.en.html) The lunar comparison needs separate procedures for liquidity lending, bank recapitalization, resolution and continuing critical services when shareholders or creditors incur losses.

An own-currency central bank could provide domestic settlement liquidity under its rules. Foreign-currency obligations would instead require reserves, borrowing or a negotiated facility. Present Federal Reserve swaps illustrate a further boundary: the receiving central bank controls local lending and bears the associated private-credit exposure. The facility is a financial obligation, not a fiscal transfer. [Federal Reserve, liquidity-swap FAQ, operation and risk allocation](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/bst_swapfaqs.htm)

Any proposed interworld facility should therefore identify amount, currency, maturity, collateral, permitted uses and renewal conditions. Temporary liquidity can bridge a payment disruption; a persistent shortfall between expenditure and sustainable resources requires another adjustment. A short facility cannot be assumed to fund a centuries-long construction programme through indefinite renewal.

Central-bank accounting also needs its own treatment. Losses or negative equity do not mechanically prevent a currency issuer from operating, unlike the usual constraints on a commercial bank. Its mandate, credibility and fiscal relationship remain consequential. [BIS Bulletin 68, *Why are central banks reporting losses? Does it matter?*](https://www.bis.org/publications/bulletin-68-why-are-central-banks-reporting-losses-does-it-matter)

Moving development losses onto a monetary authority's balance sheet would leave public exposure and potentially reduce future remittances or alter policy incentives. Seigniorage likewise requires an explicit account of monetary liabilities, remuneration, asset income and public transfers. Treating every newly created monetary balance as free construction revenue would omit these relationships. Public guarantees should name their fiscal bearer and payment currency before they enter financing scenarios.

## Payment continuity and institutional development

Payment rules must identify final settlement, the issuer of the settlement asset and the handling of participant or communications failure. The BIS financial-infrastructure principles treat settlement finality, settlement-asset risk and operational continuity as separate requirements. [BIS, Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures, principles 8, 9 and 17](https://www.bis.org/committees/cpmi/pfmi/overview)

A prospective lunar implementation could retain local settlement capability, redundant records and limited emergency payment authorization during an external link outage. Its rules would have to distinguish locally final payments from claims awaiting external settlement. Unlimited offline acceptance would risk conflicting obligations. Redundancy should also account for dependence on array computing, rather than place every recovery system in the same failure domain.

Financial liquidity and physical reserves serve different continuity needs. A utility can possess acceptable money yet lack a working route for replacement equipment; an operable supplier can face interrupted payment because an intermediary fails. Liquid assets, essential stocks, delivery contracts and emergency operating authority would address different parts of that problem. Their adequacy requires operational scenarios, not a universal reserve percentage.

Development should follow demonstrated institutional capabilities. Early settlement needs dependable denomination, payment access, responsibility for banks and funded essential services. Growth adds the need for credible fiscal accounts, supervision, resolution and an explicit allocation of infrastructure risk. If a currency transition is selected, existing contracts, deposits, public debt and conversion rules require a coordinated transition account.

Each arrangement can last, and none follows from another by necessity. Established-currency use could become durable union membership; a broad lunar economy could adopt an independent currency; a board could remain its chosen commitment. The discriminating evidence concerns domestic supply, invoicing, cross-world shocks, fiscal sharing, mobility and financial exposure. Comparing those conditions sets out what each arrangement would require, and the choice stays with the communities' collective decisions.
