# Annual biomass production compared with Earth

31 October 2026. **The Open Moon's sunlight-supported biosphere produces about 20 Gt C of new biomass a year, with 15–25 Gt C as a screening estimate**: 14–24% of Earth's 104.9 Gt C a year (Field et al. 1998), or 13–25% of a rounded 100–120. Production here is net primary production (NPP), the carbon built into new biomass after the producers' own respiration over a 365.25-day year; it feeds food webs and replaces tissue, and it can far exceed the change in standing biomass or in long-term carbon storage. At 45% carbon in dry biomass on both worlds the estimate is 33–56 billion tonnes of dry biomass a year, 44 at its centre. The figures describe an established engineered biosphere, with the land on the older climate's screen; the 301 K design run supplies the temperature and water fields the next land calculation needs.

**Per square metre the Moon's computed cases produce 2.0–2.9 times Earth's mean, for two reasons.** Its land is 62% of its surface against Earth's 29%, and that share alone, at Earth's own rates, would give 1.4 times Earth's mean. Its land also grows 650–860 g C/m² a year, 1.7–2.3 times Earth's land average of 380, through the engineered canopy's water use and the deep air's diffuse light; the equatorial band of the light-limitation screen grows about 1,440 g C/m². The nutrient-poor waters grow 18–170 g C/m² against Earth's ocean's 134, so at Earth's land share the Moon's own rates would give 1.0–1.8 times Earth's mean. Earth's figures take Field et al.'s land and ocean parts, 56.4 and 48.5 Gt C a year, over its 148.9 million km² of land and 361.1 million km² of water.

The numerical basis is [biomass_comparison.py](biomass_comparison.py), its [inputs and source records](biomass_comparison_inputs.json) and the generated [comparison](results/biomass_comparison.json). The calculation keeps the existing land production, brings the waters to the nutrient-poor design and reports the sky separately. Its low and high cases are chosen parameter sets.

The dry land is 23.4 million km²: the food study already sets aside the common-level seas (28% of the Moon, 10.62 million km²) and the rain-fed lakes above them (3.92 million km²), and the wet margins take their place within that land. Sky organisms can use light above clouds or above unproductive ground, and their interception also takes light and rain from the surface below, so their production is counted against an unchanged surface only in the aerial study, net of those losses.

The older [light-limitation calculation](README.md) gave 16.2–25.5 Gt C a year with lake rates of 100–400 g C/m² a year, mesotrophic to eutrophic. The trophic design of 19 October holds the open lakes and seas nutrient-poor and gathers richer communities at the margins, shelves, deltas and farms, so this comparison takes the oxygen study's oligotrophic primary-production bracket of 50–300 mg C/m² a day, central 200, recorded secondarily from Wetzel; at 200 mg the year's production is 73.05 g C/m². The oxygen model prescribes this whole-cycle mean and calibrates its light response to reach it, so the rate is an assumption about lunar organisms. An illustrative NPP/GPP fraction of 0.4–0.6 turns it into new biomass, 36.5 g C/m² a year at its middle; the oxygen study's euphotic respiration includes consumers and decomposers and cannot supply that fraction. The source record calls the bracket primary production, and the book was not read; if the bracket is a near-net 14C rate, the fraction discounts it twice, by about 0.5 Gt C a year in the middle case.

The constructed aquatic cases apply the oligotrophic rate to every lake and to the open part of the seas, with productive coasts at the old screen's guessed 300–1,000 g C/m² a year over 5–15% of the seas, the coast and open-sea shares summing to one. They give 0.26–2.44 Gt C a year, 1.18 in the illustrative middle. A productive aquatic lake margin has no separate area yet. The earlier screen paired its endpoints independently, giving combined sea areas of 90% and 110% of the actual area; its historical result stands as committed.

The 301 K design climate's design years, `A28R_301k_s0897`'s years 5–14 at sunlight 0.897, read 301.2–301.3 K, and the design run that follows rebalances the sunlight about 3.5–4.5% above 0.897 for its convection, rain and gust changes, with a short run at 0.929 finding the value (register and plan on main, cited in the inputs). The biomass calculation's weather, `A28_dim5_moon`, and its source spectra use scale 0.950; the 295 K lock's later 0.970 is a different reference. Relative to 0.950 the provisional design sunlight is 1.3–2.3% lower and the completed 0.897 case 5.6% lower. If surface PAR fell in the same proportion with everything else fixed, the old screen's whole-biosphere light elasticity of 0.07–0.295 would take 0.1–0.7% of production in the provisional case and 0.4–1.6% in the completed one. These light-only sensitivities stay apart from the totals: the design run's clouds, spectra, temperatures and rain set the final figure.

Water and respiration weigh more than that small direct light change. The older interpolation's 25% more mean land rain at 301 K would not raise production by 25%, because the rain's distribution, soil water, transpiration demand, warmer respiration and acclimation all enter; the design run supplies the temperature and water fields for the next canopy and land calculation. Nitrogen and phosphorus supply, tolerance of continuous light, survival of the long dark and complete life cycles are conditions of the estimate. The plant budgets include the long lunar night, and the aquatic rates are whole-cycle averages, so neither takes a further factor of one half; the aquatic assumption still needs a physiological test of night survival and daytime production.

The large land share, warm growing conditions and efficient use of diffuse light give high production per unit of planetary area in the existing models. Low gravity changes architecture and supplies no photosynthetic energy. With water everywhere the older land screen gives 46.8–54.5 Gt C a year, and with the waters 47–57, about half Earth's total: a resource potential that needs ample nutrients, adapted organisms and delivered water, and whose changed vegetation would itself alter the albedo and the water cycle. Powered microbial food and lamp-lit cultivation lie outside these sunlight totals, and none of the totals sets a sustainable harvest.

For Earth, [Field et al. (1998)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9657713/) give 104.9 Pg C a year, with land and oceans contributing roughly equally, and [Behrenfeld et al. (2001)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11283369/) 111–117 over 1997–2000; together they set the rounded comparison scale. [Zhang et al. (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-025-02375-1) find different production trends on land and in the ocean. The abstracts were read for this note, and the land and ocean parts of Field et al. in a secondary summary; the inputs record each source's access.

Reproduce the arithmetic with `python -m research.studies.light_limitation.biomass_comparison` and check it with `python -m pytest research/studies/light_limitation/test_biomass_comparison.py`. The checks cover area conservation, the carbon units, the GPP/NPP distinction, the per-area split, separate sky reporting and regeneration of the recorded result, so they establish the arithmetic and records; the climate and biological assumptions rest on the cited products.
