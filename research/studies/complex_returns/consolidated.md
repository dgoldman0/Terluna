# Integrated returns with staged industry, worldwide services and U finance

13 November 2026. **The latest combined package supports positive conditional returns under strong worldwide demand, with a pronounced dependence on the required return and the extent of physical expansion.** Across the nine portfolios, the project and initial monetary-reserve sponsors receive a modeled **2.64–4.47 trillion USD2041-equivalent cash net present value (NPV) over 100 elapsed years at 3% real**. Their finite-horizon internal rates of return are **5.23–6.59% real**. All nine remain negative at a 7% real hurdle. Middle-market demand produces negative discounted sponsor returns at 3%, including at year 800.

These are returns across the planned off-Earth economic zone, including its industrial, source, power, logistics and service operations and the people and institutions who finance and use them. U remains the general economic unit and potential single currency or basket of U-related currencies. Earth counterparties and beneficiaries participate in the appraisal; the Open Moon, planetary array and subsequent outer Solar System buildout remain outside the physical boundary. Constant USD2041-equivalent valuations establish no U exchange rate.

The study consolidates the [staged investment](staged.md), [nine export strategies](strategies.md), [credit-supported expansion](credit.md) and [U monetary-policy](policy.md) studies. The intended reader is a project reviewer assessing what the improved design earns, which stakeholders receive it, and what would change the conclusion. Source prices, engineering replication, customer demand and financial contracts retain their conditional status.

## The updated package and the investment being recovered

Every portfolio retains worldwide sports, entertainment, licensing, research and professional-service opportunities alongside its industrial specialization. The cultural platform supports four sports and thirty-two organizations; the strong scenario reaches a two-billion-person worldwide audience independently of resident numbers. Shared buyer budgets, equipment, power, staff, qualification, acquisition, renewal and competing offers constrain the combined business. Destination services remain in the inherited receipts, with visiting personnel costs already included where specified; no separate tourism multiplier is added.

The latest package uses commercial lending, funded monetary reserves, the existing low-price-pressure admission rules, and eligibility for capital suppliers to retain U term claims against 50% of identified external capital purchases. A 50-basis-point rate reduction applies only to the locally circulating portion of U loans. Outside-lender rates and matched supplier-claim rates stay unchanged. An explicit new release assumption transfers each year's borrower interest saving into owners' external saving assets, creating no additional modeled domestic spending. The physical investment schedule is unchanged by that release: further gains from reinvesting the savings remain unestimated.

The modeled seed supports 5% of founding capacity, with a 10% common-infrastructure floor and operating runway. Commercial receipts begin in year eight. All horizons below are measured from first funding. The model retains a 1% desired initial growth rate, engineering limits, commissioning delays, tapering and the enclosed architectural cap. Financing and demand can stop expansion before those physical limits are reached.

It charges hardware and carrying costs over years zero through seven, working cash in year seven, and monetary reserve capital in year zero. It then receives the simulated owner distributions plus the explicitly released interest savings. This is **sponsor incidence**, not the combined return to every customer, supplier, bank and lender.

The limited-demand cases fail before the requested appraisal horizons and receive no invented later receipts. Middle-demand cases eventually recover their nominal constant-value cash outlay without discounting, yet all remain negative at 3% through year 800. By contrast, most of the strong cases' 3%-discounted value is already present by year 100. Century-scale operation matters physically and to later stakeholders even when it adds little to today's discounted investor claim.

Net project liquid assets after debt, separately owned custody assets and monetary reserve assets remain visible as terminal **book claims**. Adding those claims at year 100 raises strong-case sponsor NPV by approximately **15–37 billion**, subject to continued asset value and settlement. No factory resale or franchise goodwill is valued. Failure-resolution claims remain unvalued rather than assuming that every reserve asset can be refunded while leaving liabilities unpaid.

## What the improvements actually contribute

The study reconciles the latest number to the original strategy result. It first removes the old result's optional terminal-cash addition, then identifies the reserve contribution, matched-controller changes, commercial finance, wider supplier acceptance and interest-saving release. This prevents combining different conventions as if they were successive production gains.

For computing under strong demand at year 100, the reconciliation is:

| Contribution to sponsor cash NPV at 3% | Billion USD2041 |
|---|---:|
| Original strategy, distribution-only convention | 3,714.14 |
| Initial monetary reserve funding | −132.85 |
| Matched controller and price/payout scheduling | −8.80 |
| Commercial finance with 25% supplier-U eligibility | +10.41 |
| Increasing supplier-U eligibility to 50% | +9.63 |
| Explicit release of U-local interest savings | +0.081 |
| **Latest sponsor cash NPV** | **3,592.62** |

The initial reserve lowers immediately distributable return while providing a financial asset and potential liquidity protection. Counting its face as current income would defeat the purpose of funding it. The lower headline compared with an account that omits that contribution is consequently compatible with improved resilience.

Wider supplier acceptance adds **11.54 billion** of operating-minus-capital NPV in the strong computing case at 100 years; the sponsor receives the different **9.63-billion** cash-NPV increase because claims and payout timing differ. In the middle computing case, wider acceptance instead reduces that operating-minus-capital measure by **6.34 billion**. Other portfolios chiefly substitute funding sources. More U-denominated finance therefore requires project selection, rather than an assumption that every permitted draw improves returns.

Interest relief contributes **zero** to consolidated transfer value: the bank's gross margin falls by exactly the owner's dated saving. Across the strong year-100 latest packages, that saving is zero to approximately **138 million NPV**. The bank account is before operating costs and losses, so it is not a profit estimate. The [Green Book's appraisal framework](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-green-book--2/the-green-book-2026) supports separating such transfers and incidence from social value; its rates and institutional rules are not imposed on U.

## Socioeconomic return and the value needed to change the conclusion

The socioeconomic screen applies the latest physical paths to a different account:

\[
PV\left[\alpha X_t+\beta C_t-
\left(O_t+K_t-R_t-W_t^{\rm included}\right)
-W_t^{\rm opportunity}\right].
\]

Here \(X_t\) is final export revenue used as a value benchmark, \(C_t\) is inherited resident-service imputation, \(O_t\) is operating expenditure, \(K_t\) is capital expenditure, \(R_t\) is identified resource provision and \(W_t^{\rm included}\) is resident payroll already present in the service, premium and logistics costs. The opportunity-cost term then values the whole working resident population against the inherited alternative-earnings assumption. Removing the embedded payroll first prevents counting those workers' labour twice. External supplier prices remain resource-cost proxies; a full decomposition and shadow-price account are unavailable.

Initial working balances and monetary reserves are financial assets and are excluded from socioeconomic resource costs. Identified resource provisions in initial commissioning and bootstrap carry are also removed. The remaining startup procurement and carrying expenditures retain their price proxies. Resource provisions remain outgoings in the financial account, because the model has no recoverable resource-fund asset or ownership schedule. They are not silently added to monetary reserves at the end.

The export-value fraction \(\alpha\) represents net value relative to the without-project alternative, expressed against purchaser prices. The resident fraction \(\beta\) independently qualifies the inherited imputation. Each is tested at zero, one-half and one. Customer surplus beyond the priced transaction, additional knowledge benefits, worker gains beyond the chosen opportunity costs, environmental losses and heritage effects are left unvalued. The [MCC economic and beneficiary guidance](https://www.mcc.gov/publications-sections/story-cdg-guidelines-for-economic-and-beneficiary-analysis/) informs the separation of aggregate gains, realistic alternatives and beneficiary incidence; no terrestrial impact coefficient is transferred.

| Year-100 valuation at 3% | Strong worldwide demand | Middle worldwide demand |
|---|---:|---:|
| Full export and resident-service price proxies: net value | +3.48 to +5.12 trillion | −2.02 to −0.78 trillion |
| Full-price benefit/cost ratio, strong cases | 1.29–1.38 | — |
| Half of both export and resident-service proxies: net value, strong cases | −4.60 to −3.72 trillion | — |
| Additional annual net benefit needed with full price proxies, middle cases | — | 30.7–79.7 billion |

At full resident-service valuation, strong cases require **71.6–76.8% of the export purchaser-price benchmark** to break even socially. Middle cases require **117.4–182.2%**, meaning additional value beyond the full price benchmark or lower resource costs would be necessary. These are switching values, not estimates of actual consumer surplus or research impact. Labour opportunity-cost multipliers of one-half, one and two are also reported in the data product.

The financial shortfall is a separate requirement. Middle-demand sponsors need an additional **55.9–100.5 billion of annual collectible net cash**, from year eight through year 100 at 3%, to close their funding-return gap under a fixed-path allocation. The socioeconomic switching requirement of 30.7–79.7 billion concerns additional **net benefit**. Useful research or cultural value can help satisfy the latter without automatically paying a lender or investor. A financing institution would still need a way to collect or fund the cash.

The positive full-price screen should be read with its qualifications: purchase prices are assumed to represent benefit and resource costs, missing local opportunity costs are not independently measured, and unpriced harms can reduce the result. A large global audience creates a substantial potential benefit base; the current calculation establishes the value thresholds that need evidence rather than assigning every possible spillover an income stream.

## Payment resilience alongside long-run returns

The liquidity experiment is rerun on the latest package's actual checkpoints, including reduced U-local debt service. It retains the 3% receivables-facility rate, voluntary term holdings, actual foreign-asset requirements, collateral haircuts and additional local-balance limits from the policy study. A six-month payment delay produces these strong-demand outcomes:

| Checkpoint | Cash alone: complete payments | U facility with 50% outside operating-supplier acceptance | Same facility with half the foreign assets accessible |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 30 | 8/9 | 9/9 | 9/9 |
| Year 100 | 8/9 | 9/9 | 9/9 |
| Year 300 | 3/9 | 9/9 | 5/9 |
| Year 800 | 0/9 | 6/9 | 1/9 |

Each successful facility repays within its tested episode. These are counts of alternative portfolios under specified shocks, not default probabilities. The year-100 propellant/logistics example still requires a **144.14-billion** peak bridge and repays by month eight. With 50% operating-supplier acceptance, it uses approximately **36.37 billion** of foreign assets and **107.68 billion** of voluntary term holdings. Its financing expense is **1.129 billion**, approximately **0.769 billion** below the assumed 6% outside-funded alternative.

The episode pauses owner payouts and new orders, including through the experimental collection tail. In that example the twenty-four-month payout exposure is **746.13 billion**, with a **20.31-billion** present-value timing cost if the same amount is released at episode end. That cost is common to the two funded bridge comparisons; foregone production from deferred orders is not simulated. The facility's real economic benefit depends on avoided shutdown and other consequences of interrupted payment, which remain unvalued.

No probability-weighted resilience benefit is added to the long-run NPV. Doing so would require dated events, replacement of the affected baseline flows, implementation and commitment costs, and an estimate of the disruption avoided. The narrow price-balance allowance also remains a conditional closure, not a ceiling on U's entire monetary capacity: business working balances and international demand for liquid U are not calibrated.

## Expansion, the eventual cap and the limits of the result

The strong year-100 financial results support only **16,500–25,700 residents' physical capacity**, together with the industrial and service systems serving worldwide customers. By year 800 their modeled capacity is **66,000–406,000 residents**, still below the **6.468-million** enclosed architectural reference. Residents measure one physical replication requirement; they are not the universe of investors or beneficiaries. The financial controller avoids many expensive expansions whose additional output lacks adequate funded demand.

The preserved sustained-cap operating test exposes the difference between profitable staged operation and completing every architectural possibility. Under its fixed full-density replication and finite buyer assumptions, **eight of nine strong-demand portfolios have annual operating deficits of 6.15–10.54 trillion USD2041 at the cap**. The earlier manufacturing mix has a positive **2.83-trillion annual residual** there, yet its staged strong-market path does not finance a route to that cap within 800 years. Operating feasibility at a hypothetical endpoint does not establish the capital and demand path required to reach it.

The cap result is a test of the existing replicated industrial design and market assumptions. It does not rule out a different balance of population, production intensity, prices, services or source use. It does mean that today's profitable small-system results cannot be extrapolated into a fully funded, profitable mature build-out. The current return case is strongest for staged facilities with qualified worldwide demand, while the mature enclosed economy still needs its own integrated design and funding case.
