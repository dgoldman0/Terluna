# Credit-supported expansion with low additional price pressure in U

**Credit improves the timing and returns of six strong-demand export portfolios in the specified commercial case, while low additional domestic price pressure can be maintained through explicit spending controls.** The main gains come from bringing qualified service-and-support capacity online earlier. Most recorded industrial capital requires external resources, so the comparison distinguishes outside lending, local U-created credit and supplier agreements that accept U for external capital purchases. Expansion remains subject to the same physical limits and worldwide customer budgets.

The result supports selective financing of productive projects. It does not establish that broad monetary stimulus improves every portfolio. Some middle-demand cases borrow without improving the complete investment path, and patient development finance can increase capacity while reducing commercial returns. The distinction matters because debt service can be affordable even when a new project uses resources that would have produced a better result elsewhere.

The six borrowing portfolios improve incremental operating-minus-capital NPV by **7.3–22.8 billion USD2041**. Their project-equity cash-distribution NPV improves by about **4.4–20.0 billion**, reflecting debt service and payout timing; these are different return measures. They draw approximately **50–97 billion USD2041-equivalent** in total credit, of which **2.1–3.8 billion** is local U-created credit. Commercial borrowing adds four to eight service-facility years by year 100. In the commodity, specialty and diversified cases, the second service facility opens two years earlier. Physical industry remains constrained by its 1% desired growth rate and shared construction resources; the extra installed capacity at year 100 is modest.

Premium lunar products, propellant/logistics and the earlier manufacturing mix take no additional credit in the strong-demand comparison. Their absence from the borrowing group does not establish a poor business model. It means this credit policy finds no qualifying financing gap that improves their current programme under its retained constraints. Raw-resource and computing labels likewise do not identify the main borrower revenue: all portfolios retain the same strong worldwide cultural and institutional service opportunities.

The price-control cases admit at most 0.5% annual growth in an above-control price premium and 5% cumulatively. The useful strong-market commercial cases remain within **1.51–2.24% maximum premiums**. Achieving that result can postpone discretionary capital orders and defer a portion of intended domestic payout spending into separate owner-held custody assets. The calculation therefore does not attribute low prices to credit creation alone. Its precise rule and the difference from a household inflation target are set out below.

## International use of U and longer horizons

The international-U case permits negotiated supplier prepayments in U term claims for up to 25% of identified external capital costs. Suppliers retain those claims outside domestic circulation, and their repayment schedules match the project loans. This tests an actual financing mechanism for a transition away from outside currencies. It does not assume that a source value recorded in USD2041 fixes a supplier's invoice or settlement currency.

For the diversified strong-demand case, the same approximately 97.3 billion financing programme can comprise about **72.1 billion of other externally funded loans and 25.2 billion of U-created loans**. Approximately 21.4 billion of the U funding prepays participating outside suppliers. The productive outcome and price premium remain the same as in the ordinary mixed case because total investment, physical capacity and repayment terms remain unchanged. International acceptance changes which claims fund the goods; it does not increase their real economic return merely by changing denomination. The supplier arrangement is conditional on negotiated acceptance, delivery and retained U holdings. Other portfolios need not follow an identical investment path under different eligible funding pools: the computing case’s incremental NPV is about 11.2 billion with supplier-U financing, compared with 22.8 billion under ordinary mixed credit.

By year 30, the strongest commercial improvement is approximately 7.6 billion USD2041 of incremental operating-minus-capital NPV. The maximum rises to 22.8 billion by year 100, 24.6 billion by year 300 and remains about 24.6 billion by year 800 at the 3% discount rate. These are separate reporting horizons of continuous paths. The flattening reflects discounting and the limited early timing advantage; it is not a perpetual new source of income. No open-Moon or array growth is used to repay the loans.

## Weaker demand, patient capital and sensitivity

In the middle-demand cases, the commercial borrowing paths produce about **0.2–7.0 billion USD2041 less** operating-minus-capital NPV by year 100. A positive project appraisal at origination does not guarantee a better complete sequence of investments: debt repayment, protected funds, future capacity competition and the price-control schedule can postpone later facilities. The appropriate inference is to keep the retained-earnings option available and require a portfolio-level comparison before approving the loan programme.

Development finance offers 1.5% real interest with 40 principal installments after grace, and permits projects with a positive annual operating increment but negative incremental project NPV. In the middle market it can add service-facility years while reducing operating-minus-capital NPV by as much as about 23.7 billion USD2041. The larger active programmes also involve substantial concessional value relative to 3% commercial terms. That cost belongs to the patient capital provider. It may be justified by separately demonstrated social benefits, but this calculation does not assign an invented monetary value to those benefits or count the concession as profit.

The selected demand stresses produce no new defaults in the continuing middle/strong cases; lower-demand paths still fail. This is evidence about the specified cost response, debt caps and protected cash, rather than a measured default probability. The quarter-budget stress retains modeled variable-cost adjustment, and the commercial debt loads in successful strong cases have substantial cash coverage. A separate numerical control drives demand to zero to verify the default and loss accounting.

The local-content sensitivity is consequential. Treating more capital as local increases the procurement demand falling on the domestic supply base and can prevent early credit-financed orders under the low-price rule. Treating less as local reduces that domestic pressure while increasing reliance on externally supplied resources. Interest, coverage and annual price-limit sensitivities therefore supplement the headline comparison; no single money-creation allowance is inferred from the successful cases.

## Scope and the matched comparison

U remains the established general economic unit and the proposed currency or basket of U-related currencies serving the planned off-Earth economic zone. The boundary includes the existing industrial, source, power and transport operations through enclosed development; Earth counterparties remain external. The Open Moon, planetary array and subsequent outer Solar System buildout are excluded.

The study uses the current provisioning architecture, including its 14.5006 trillion USD2041 establishment and 41.8919 trillion USD2041 campaign accounts, through the pinned nine-portfolio financial product. It keeps the small productive seed, worldwide service markets, customer budgets, current premium qualification, joint physical limits, two-year commissioning, five-year industrial production ramp, 1% desired annual growth and eventual taper. Credit supplies no additional assumed productivity. All nine portfolios continue to include sports, entertainment, licensing, research and professional services.

The new financing controller shares one investment plan across six financing arrangements. It reproduces the 27 original financial paths separately and retains their saved products unchanged. Its matched retained-earnings control adds the common monetary-reserve account and explicit planning rules. It saves for an admissible service kit before funding industry, reserves simultaneous construction capacity, and evaluates a host-plus-service package when an additional kit cannot fit. It avoids the original allocator's additional package reservation immediately after an admissible kit purchase. These scheduling differences and the reserve funding appear in `historical_comparison`; their effects are not attributed to credit. At year 100 under strong demand, the new control’s project-equity distribution NPV is 5.6–26.5 billion USD2041 below the original result, before the separate initial monetary-reserve contribution. The reported credit gains use this new matched control, so they must not simply be added to the earlier published NPVs. Long-horizon installed capacity also differs under the planning rules; the original paths remain available for that comparison.

Every monetary amount is valued in the source's constant USD2041 accounts. A debt contract is modeled in constant purchasing-power equivalents at a reference conversion quote; the calculation selects neither a nominal U/USD rate nor a nominal U lending yield. Interest and returns below are real. Other currencies used in trade or partial reserves do not define the U-related currency basket.

## Financing institutions, project selection and debt service

| Arrangement | New funding | Terms and qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Retained earnings | Accumulated operating cash | The matched control; no loans |
| Funded loans | Outside lenders supply convertible purchasing power | 3% real interest, two-year principal grace, then 20 annual principal installments |
| Local U credit | Banks create deposits against eligible local-capital loans | Same commercial terms; cannot pay identified external capital invoices |
| Mixed credit | Funded lending plus eligible local U credit | Same total credit envelope and commercial tests; changes its financing composition |
| International U mix | Outside suppliers accept U term claims for part of capital procurement | Up to 25% of identified external capital is eligible; term claims match the financing loan’s principal and interest schedule |
| Development mix | Mixed financing with a patient lender | 1.5% real interest, two-year principal grace, then 40 annual principal installments; permits a positive operating increment with negative incremental project NPV |

Interest is paid during principal grace; draws occur at the annual financing boundary and first accrue interest the following year. The lender tests the whole debt burden against 70% of current positive operating cash, requiring a debt-service coverage ratio of at least 1.5. Loan proceeds cannot repay old loans, fund distributions or disguise operating losses. New borrowing is limited to 75% of the package budget, supported by actual own cash, and total outstanding debt to 25% of the original seed. Existing coverage below the covenant suspends dividends and discretionary expansion. The main comparison therefore tests investment finance after positive operations, rather than credit extended against unearned future audience growth or loss-making startup operations.

Commercial approval also requires positive incremental project net present value at 3%, over 40 operating years after the conservative construction/commissioning/ramp delay. The project is valued against current qualified demand and current customer budgets. A larger future audience or successful future qualification cannot make today's loan admissible. Development finance relaxes this commercial return condition while retaining the debt-service, physical and price tests. Its interest concession is valued against the 3% benchmark. That is a financing cost or income concession, distinct from the project's operating gain, and not an assumed market offer.

Bank lending creates deposits and corresponding loan assets. The [Bank of England's account](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy) supports separating this process from the transfer of outside savings through funded loans. The [World Bank's project-finance discussion](https://ppp.worldbank.org/financing/issues-in-project-financed-transactions) informs the revenue, coverage and distribution tests. The numerical thresholds are design sensitivities rather than coefficients drawn from those sources.

## Capital eligibility and external payments

Identified external purchases account for approximately 94–95% of the modeled post-founding industrial financing bill. This figure comes from the source accounts for expansion purchases and commercial commissioning, rather than treating all prices expressed in USD as external obligations. Below founding scale, the calculation conservatively allows the entire prepaid common floor to have covered external hardware before calculating the remaining external-purchase lower bound. Both stages use the same piecewise cost and multiplier as the inherited expansion model.

The residual is unclassified cash cost. The central experiment treats half of it as eligible for local U credit, with zero and full-residual sensitivities. Service-kit capital has no supplier split; its central assumed local share is 10%, with zero and 50% sensitivities. These are eligibility scenarios, not measurements of lunar content. The source's separate imputed domestic capital formation is not appended to the cash bill because its overlap with wages, civil installation and financing has not been reconciled.

In the local-credit and ordinary mixed cases, U-created proceeds remain in a separate restricted account and can pay only eligible local capital. Outside loans and convertible earned receipts cover the remainder. Restricted proceeds stay outside earned profit and cannot simultaneously cover an operating buffer and a dividend. A full host-plus-service package retains its capital, carrying-cost and liquidity requirements; a partial credit promise cannot make an unfunded package start.

The monetary fund receives additional initial capital equal to 5% of the original seed. Contributions from post-debt-service earnings can replenish it toward one year of identified core external purchases, plus separate allocations equal to 25% of U credit exposure for conversion liquidity and 15% for credit-loss capital. These allocations must fit together before a U loan is approved. Core imports are a partial external-payment measure, so this is a specified liquidity screen rather than proof of reserve adequacy. Pooled operating receipts are assumed convertible at the reference quote; the model does not infer that access from U invoice denomination.

The international-U case tests a further negotiated arrangement. At loan origination, participating suppliers accept prepayment into U term claims for eligible capital contracts and retain those claims outside domestic circulation. The project receives a restricted capital-prepayment asset that is consumed as construction orders are supplied. Each supplier claim follows its financing loan’s principal and interest schedule. This supplies real external resources against U-denominated claims; it does not supply free capital or infer supplier acceptance from the currency label.

Outside supplier claims are separate from customer transaction balances. They do not add to domestic circulating money, and the capital prepayment is not also counted as a liquid U deposit. Unused supplier prepayments have no assumed cancellation recovery at the reporting horizon or default. The supplier term-claim NPV is valued at origination face value; it is not the supplier’s production profit. Matched borrowing and supplier rates leave the bank zero interest margin on this portion before its costs and losses. An actual arrangement therefore needs a fee, spread or explicit public funding for those services. Supplier acceptance, delivery risk and the financial-services cost remain qualification requirements.

## Keeping additional domestic price pressure low

The price calculation uses the existing broad domestic final-use basket, including civic-service and housing imputations and domestic capital formation. It does not establish a household consumer-price index. Construction's assumed local cash content is disbursed across the two commissioning years. The intended additional domestic payout spending remains 1% of distributions. There is no separate 5% productivity bonus: installed physical capacity enters only when commissioned.

Let N* be planned domestic expenditure, k = 90/365 years the domestic balance-holding period, and F the customer balances required for U-settled trade. Customer holdings cover seven days of receipts while U settlement rises from 25% to 85% over the first ten productive years. New U loan balances remaining in project or contractor escrow are excluded from circulating credit. Other monetary issuance supplies required balances residually: credit first replaces money that would otherwise have been issued to meet growth in kN* + F. Only circulating loan money beyond that growth adds a monetary spending impulse. Repayments retire loan-created balances, and the residual issuance rule maintains required payment liquidity. This is an explicit monetary-policy closure, not proof that aggregate currency demand is otherwise unsatisfied.

With supply elasticity one, the pressure index solves P² = N/B. The current price and the unavoidable remainder of construction prepayments are screened before a new commitment. The policy then limits the credit case's price premium relative to the matched control to 5% cumulatively. Its annual rule is

    relative_price[t] / max(1, relative_price[t−1]) <= 1.005.

This is a limit on growth of an **above-control price premium**. A price initially below the control can return toward parity without being forced into further deflation. The product reports ordinary annual relative-price changes alongside this measure. Neither statistic is a forecast that actual annual U consumer inflation stays below 0.5%. The annual premium limit is tested at 0.25% and 1% as well.

Maintaining that constraint involves a distribution policy as well as a loan limit. If intended domestic payout spending would exceed the price allowance, its owners retain the amount in a separate custody account of externally valued assets with an assumed zero real return. It can be released when room becomes available. The assets belong to the payout recipients, cannot also finance construction or issuer reserves, and remain visible in terminal claims. Other distributions continue. The model can also postpone discretionary investment orders; it preserves previously committed construction payments and records any irreducible breach. This policy is conditional on authority over the designated payout programme and actual access to conversion and custody services.

## Returns, losses and evidence limits

The primary comparison is the difference in operating cash after renewal, less capital orders, discounted at 3%. Interest, principal and loan draws are financial transfers and are excluded from that incremental operating-and-capital measure. It can improve because useful capacity operates earlier, even when financing leaves the ultimate installed scale nearly unchanged. Additional productive scale-years and service-kit-years record that timing; they are not monetized welfare benefits.

Separate accounts report project-equity cash distributions, externally funded lender cash returns, U-bank loan faces and deposit liabilities, interest income, terminal debt, owner-custody assets and reserve funding. Equity cash returns exclude the reserve provider's initial contribution; the combined project-and-reserve-funder account includes it. The optional terminal claim explicitly liquidates cash, escrow and protected liquidity, nets outstanding debt and includes custody assets while excluding nonliquid supplier prepayments. It is not a continuing operation's freely distributable terminal balance, and it assigns no resale value to factories or intangible businesses.

Default is a payment failure with limited-liability recovery from remaining project cash and liquid U escrow, including accrued interest in creditor claims. Supplier prepayments are excluded from that recovery; outstanding supplier principal and accrued interest remain separate bank-resolution claims. Recovery is credited to lenders at the default date. Physical collateral resale and bailouts are zero in this stress convention. A U-loan writeoff reduces bank capital while previously created deposits remain; allocated loss capital is deducted from the reserve funders' recoverable claim, and any loss beyond that allocation is reported separately. [World Bank guidance on public support](https://ppp.worldbank.org/government-guarantees) motivates recording such costs and contingent exposures explicitly.

The moderate stress halves buyer-budget ceilings for years 40–44. A separate severe stress reduces those ceilings to one quarter for years 20–29. Operating costs respond through the existing production model, so these are not equivalent to imposing the same percentage cut on net cash. Neither stress has an assigned probability. The broader default controls test loss accounting rather than assigning a probability to total market failure. Sensitivities also raise commercial interest to 6%, require coverage of 2.0, vary local capital eligibility and vary the annual price-premium limit.

The results remain conditional on the inherited engineering, market and staffing assumptions. Current source-price costs do not adjust endogenously to the calculated local price index, and floating exchange rates, actual supplier contracts, bank operating costs and credit spreads, nominal debt indexation, asset prices and household spending behavior require further work. The low-pressure rule is an admissibility experiment in the current accounts. It does not authorize a currency constitution, guarantee an inflation outcome or establish a reserve multiplier.
