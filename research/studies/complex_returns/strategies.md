# Industrial exports and worldwide services in one investment account

The U socioecosystem can sell several kinds of output whose economics differ sharply. Raw and commodity materials compete on delivered cost; specialty products require exact specifications and qualified buyers; a technical lunar premium requires a useful production advantage. Propellant and logistics serve missions with particular routes and compatible equipment. Sports, entertainment, research, computing and digital products can reach customers far beyond the people who live in the complex. A financing strategy must compare these activities while charging their shared infrastructure once and allocating its limited capacity among them.

This model extends the [staged financing study](staged.md) with nine explicit industrial portfolios, three worldwide-service markets and joint resource and customer-budget constraints. It retains the founding off-Earth complex, its source operations and transport network, and the **$14.501 trillion establishment / $41.892 trillion campaign** account. Earth customers, capital providers, suppliers and research users remain stakeholders. The physical scenario is enclosed; no planetary array or Open Moon market is introduced.

Under the strong worldwide-service assumptions, the propellant-and-mission-support portfolio produces the highest tested **100-year capital-recovery net present value: +$4.599 trillion at 3% real**. Services with domestic support give +$3.781 trillion; the diversified portfolio gives +$3.320 trillion. Under the middle service assumptions, every tested strategy has negative 100-year NPV, although several sustain operation and expansion. These are conditional comparisons of specified businesses and funding policies. They establish neither a market forecast nor an optimal portfolio.

## Comparable industrial portfolios

The comparison uses new joint evaluations of the existing provisioning producer. Each retains the same founding support system and its domestic obligations. Commodity and specialty cases reproduce the earlier saved accounts; an additional control reproduces the earlier larger manufacturing portfolio exactly. The [frozen response](inputs/strategy_profiles.json) records producer and input hashes, physical arguments, costs, commissioning and joint expansion responses. The financial model reads that product without importing the physical implementation.

| Strategy | Planned commercial output at founding scale | Price or qualification distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Raw-resource price case | 100,000 tonnes/year of source product on the refined-product route | $3/kg in USD2026; a conservative route-cost proxy, without a raw-ore engineering design |
| Commodity refined stock | 100,000 tonnes/year of lunar goods, 50,000 tonnes of source goods, 1 GW computing and 25,000 tonnes of propellant | Both material streams at $3/kg in USD2026 |
| Specialty products | The same physical portfolio as commodity stock | Both material streams at $750/kg in USD2026, requiring corresponding products and buyers |
| Premium lunar manufacturing | 200,000 tonnes/year of attempted lunar goods | Explicit premium acceptance, extra processing and development costs, finite orders and lower-grade fallback |
| Propellant and logistics | Up to 1 million tonnes/year of external propellant; separately sold mission support | Funded compatible missions, a shared procurement budget and a competing Earth-delivered price |
| Computing and digital exports | 5 GW computing capacity | Contracted capacity, uptake, price competition and purchased external services |
| Services with domestic support | No additional industrial export programme | A control retaining the same domestic industrial system and worldwide service opportunities |
| Diversified portfolio | 100,000 tonnes/year lunar goods, 25,000 tonnes source goods, 3 GW computing and 250,000 tonnes propellant | Half the lunar goods enter the premium process; all sectors share capacity and budgets |
| Earlier manufacturing mix | 200,000 tonnes/year lunar goods, 3 GW computing and 25,000 tonnes propellant | Earlier $2,000/kg USD2026 manufactured-product assumption, without attributing that price to a lunar advantage |

The material quantities in this table precede the inherited delivery/acceptance factor of 0.9702. Lunar and source products retain separate mass and revenue accounts. The commodity/refined mix includes the same computing and small fuel business as the specialty mix, so changing material prices does not silently grant different equipment. The additional source-refinery response is retained as a control: producing inventory inside the complex creates no external receipt.

The raw-resource case deserves a narrow interpretation. The source model supplies a refined-product route, not a raw-ore excavation, handling and export plant. Applying a low bulk-resource price to that route tests exposure to shipping low-value mass through an expensive system. It does not establish the best possible raw-resource cost. Local aggregate, shielding, oxygen, water and structural stock can also avoid imports. Those uses belong to domestic production and procurement savings; their internal invoices do not become Earth export revenue.

The framework also distinguishes whole-system balance from an industry's incremental contribution. A useful specialty activity can improve the account while leaving the retained complex dependent on other income. Conversely, a worldwide media business can finance a portfolio whose materials alone lose money. Positive combined NPV must not be described as proof that its raw materials are profitable.

## A lunar technical premium must survive a product comparison

Existing project research identifies plausible tests: filled materials that must remain uniform through a particular processing interval, large blanks formed with lower support loads, vacuum fabrication of large components, and locally supplied repair or industrial subsystems. Each needs a specified recipe, optimized Earth and orbital alternatives, accepted delivered performance, full production cost and actual orders. Lunar provenance can support a distinct cultural or collectible price, but that is a different mechanism from technical superiority.

Direct evidence remains limited. Reitz and colleagues melted regolith simulant under Earth, lunar and microgravity conditions, but their small samples did not establish a statistically significant quality advantage. A reported ISS optical-fibre result concerns microgravity and selected characterization; it does not establish a lunar-gravity yield or premium. The fibre paper's institutional abstract was available, while its full-text retrievals failed; no detailed performance estimate is transferred. [Lunar-gravity experiment](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12217-021-09878-4), [ISS fibre study abstract](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/space-based-fabrication-of-zblan-optical-fibers-a-breakthrough-in/).

The financial scenario makes the missing evidence explicit. Premium production starts from an assumed equivalent-product offer of $2,000/kg in USD2026 and tests willingness to pay at 1.5 times that offer. It requires ten years after first production to qualify and then accepts 60% of the premium stream. Qualified research reuse can shorten that period by two years and raise acceptance by ten percentage points, as uncalibrated sensitivities. Premium demand is capped at 10,000, 60,000 or 150,000 tonnes per year across the three markets. Unpurchased or rejected premium output receives only an 80% fallback recovery at the specialty price; it is never all sold at the premium.

This programme pays an additional $8 billion common development allowance, $20 billion installed capital per founding-scale equivalent, $400 million annual development expenditure per equivalent, $20 per attempted kilogram, extra staff and 0.5 W of additional mean process power per attempted kg/year. The diversified case scales these allowances by its premium share. Every attempted batch carries its costs, including failed qualification. These are testable design allowances, not quotations or measured process economics.

A zero-qualified-premium sensitivity keeps development and processing costs while removing premium sales. A competing-product sensitivity reduces the accepted offer. Research, technical advantage and buyer willingness to pay therefore remain separate inputs rather than a single multiplier attached to lunar gravity.

## Customer competition and shared production

All portfolios receive the same selected worldwide-service scenario. The middle case has 600 million distinct followers and the strong case two billion, with paid-account, advertising, license, contract, acquisition, production and replacement assumptions inherited from the staged study. Four sports and 32 organizations share the cultural audience with entertainment; visiting participants need not become residents. Sports and entertainment receive a 45:55 reporting allocation of one cultural receipt total, not separate copies of the same subscriptions.

The new model places related sales within shared customer budgets. In the middle scenario, annual industrial purchases start at $500 billion; cultural and license receipts share a $90 billion revenue limit; external mission purchases share $100 billion; and non-mission research, professional work and computing share $200 billion. The strong values are $2 trillion, $600 billion, $1 trillion and $1.5 trillion. These are scenario budgets, not measured markets. The cultural limit includes advertisers as well as consumers. Industrial budgets grow at 0.5% annually to a maximum of 30 times their initial value; other stated market ceilings remain fixed.

Twenty-five percent of research invoices draw from the mission budget. Those invoices receive allocation before fuel and support purchases, so more research can reduce affordable propellant sales. The remaining research, professional and computing receipts share the institutional budget. Separate contract counts and computing capacity limits also apply: a spending allowance alone does not establish demand for a particular product.

Propellant demand additionally requires 100, 1,000 or 10,000 compatible funded missions per year, each using an assumed one million kilograms of delivered fuel. A support invoice of $30 million per mission covers separately purchased handling, inspection and mission services; fuel is not counted again inside it. Support incurs 60% variable costs, additional equipment, staff, fixed purchases, renewal and depot capacity. These mission classes and allowances need route-specific qualification. The source model already charges the complex's own propulsion and logistics; those internal uses generate no new external sales.

This treatment follows the dependencies emphasized in NASA's integrated infrastructure account—processing, storage, transfer, maintenance and power—and the delivered-cost comparison in Metzger's propellant analysis. Neither source supplies funded customers for the present project. The current source quote is $300/kg in USD2026, bounded by 80% of the modeled Earth-delivered alternative. Lower Earth launch costs reduce both the complex's identified annual launch purchases and the competing fuel offer; they do not automatically improve lunar margins. [NASA infrastructure study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240008379), [propellant cost analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09011).

Computing requires contracted external capacity of 1, 5 or 20 GW across the markets, with uptake of 50%, 75% or 100% of otherwise available capacity. This uptake is separate from the billable duty already included in the source's revenue-per-watt convention. Uncontracted equipment still incurs its installed costs. Internal media and research workloads withdraw computing capacity from outside billing before any purchased-service savings are credited.

Physical competition is equally explicit. The source model rejects a portfolio combining all individually maximal exports: its source heat rejection reaches **178.2% of capacity**. The accepted alternatives therefore use complete joint responses. Additional cultural facilities, institutional work and premium processing consume the same remaining power and staff; logistics support consumes depot capacity. Expansion is limited by what remains after those uses.

Growth also replicates commercial factories and computing equipment. Their full commissioning allowance is charged in addition to the base industrial/residential expansion bill, and the producer now evaluates simultaneous construction of both stocks. Repeating the full qualification allowance is conservative, but omitting the commercial assets would create revenue from unfunded equipment. Added service installations retain a separate commissioning reserve. These screens establish consistency with the stated allowances, not a complete staffing, passenger, communications or site design.

## Synergies with costs and displaced alternatives

Three additional mechanisms are tested individually through the joint account. Internal computing can displace 3% of purchased cultural and digital delivery costs, conditional on allocating the specified computing capacity. It loses the corresponding external computing invoice and reduces its revenue-linked Earth service purchases. Research-assisted premium qualification reserves 100 internal research slots per founding-scale equivalent of the premium programme, leaving fewer slots available to outside contracts. Finally, accepted premium goods can support 5% more separately purchased branded licenses, within the same cultural/license revenue limit and with their own acquisition and production costs.

These mechanisms are hypotheses about particular transactions. They do not multiply total GDP or make all sectors more profitable. For example, in the middle market at founding scale, the premium-only portfolio's combined synergy effect is approximately **−$0.166 billion per year**: the premium order limit and foregone research receipts offset the additional licensing benefit. The diversified portfolio gains approximately **$19.087 billion per year** under the same assumptions because its accepted premium volume and shared computing have different constraints. A measured causal estimate is still required for each effect.

The accounts also include financing cooperation. A profitable service facility may justify additional industrial support even when that support has a negative return considered alone. Before starting such a programme, the fund reserves the required host and service capital, conservative commissioning/ramp carrying costs and added operating liquidity. Construction then proceeds within the same annual physical limits. Cash assigned to that programme cannot simultaneously finance another facility or a distribution.

## Staged investment and returns across time

The model preserves the earlier campaign reconciliation: $17.171 trillion of one-time establishment/material delivery is separated from $24.721 trillion of legacy recurring support. The latter is replaced by dated operating costs. Each strategy adds its own commercial commissioning and new programme allowances. The resulting externally funded first stages range from **$2.554 trillion to $2.784 trillion**, including deployment carrying costs and five years of no-sales liquidity. First receipts arrive in year eight and industrial output ramps over five years.

Later orders use only accumulated earned cash after costs, prior losses, renewal, provision and distributions. Ordered facilities receive no revenue before commissioning. The fund keeps its operating buffer, reports insolvency rather than adding outside money, and credits no terminal sale of productive assets. Positive marginal operating contribution is a development-policy test; it is not a claim that every expansion maximizes discounted return. The model can retain a smaller profitable system when markets or physical capacity no longer justify expansion.

NPVs are measured from first investment at **3% real**, in constant USD2041. The strong service market provides much of the positive recovery in every row. The raw-resource result consequently cannot be interpreted as a profitable raw-material export business. It is a combined enterprise receiving the same ambitious worldwide-service opportunities as its competitors. The lower service-demand cases exhaust their funding; those failed paths have no assumed rescue or costed liquidation.

Time and productive scope differ across strategies. For the strong propellant/logistics case, 3% NPV is **+$0.653T at year 30, +$4.599T at year 100, +$5.324T at year 300 and +$5.327T at year 800**. The corresponding diversified values are **+$0.030T, +$3.320T, +$3.875T and +$3.877T**. Undiscounted initial-funding recovery occurs in years 21 and 24 respectively. These outcomes use prospective customer demand and long-lived operating assumptions; no probability is assigned to them.

At year 100, those two paths have built only about 10.3% and 9.3% of founding-scale industrial capacity. Most tested paths plateau below the enclosed architectural cap because specific customer markets or shared resources bind. The 6.468-million-resident reference remains a maximum and a taper condition, rather than an instruction to build unprofitable unused capacity. Retaining abundant financial cash does not itself expand power, factory throughput or a qualified customer market.

A separate stock test imposes the full enclosed cap and sustains it under the strong market ceilings, with the industrial-budget path evaluated at year 800. Annual available cash is **−$9.346T for the raw-price proxy, −$7.081T for premium manufacturing, −$6.624T for logistics, −$6.549T for diversification and +$2.835T for the earlier manufacturing mix**. This is an operating comparison, not a claim that the staged paths financed that stock. The large deficits show why successful early exports do not justify unlimited replication under unchanged product markets.

## Sensitivities and decisions

The strong diversified portfolio's 100-year NPV falls from **+$3.320T to +$3.022T without the tested synergies**, to **+$3.073T if the premium never qualifies**, to **+$0.689T with customer budgets and quantity commitments halved**, and to **+$0.949T with new-service variable costs 30% higher**. The premium-focused portfolio falls from +$3.250T to **+$2.034T when qualification fails**. Its remaining positive value comes principally from the worldwide services; failed material development has not become free or successful.

The linked Earth-competition case reduces the propellant/logistics NPV from **+$4.599T to +$4.190T** while increasing the computing case from +$3.732T to **+$3.794T**. Cheaper launch purchases help the latter account even while its computing offer faces a lower competing price. This is why a single blanket improvement factor for Earth launch costs would misrepresent the interaction. Initial establishment costs are held unchanged in this sensitivity; only the identified annual launch bill and stated competing offers change.

The model supports a staged evidence programme. Bulk resources first need a suitable product, destination and delivered-cost comparison. Specialty manufacturing needs specification, yield and orders. A technical premium needs matched process trials and independent acceptance of delivered products. Logistics needs compatible funded missions and qualified storage/transfer routes. Digital and cultural businesses need retained paying cohorts, rights and production economics. A joint strategy should then purchase the scarce capacity its profitable uses require, rather than add together each sector's standalone maximum.

These are financial comparisons within the wider socioeconomic appraisal. Customer enjoyment, research outcomes, worker opportunities and future capabilities remain relevant alongside displacement, opportunity costs and environmental or heritage effects. They must be measured against alternatives and attributed without adding supplier turnover, wages, profits and downstream output repeatedly. The earlier [stakeholder appraisal](README.md) remains the framework for those additional returns; none supplies spendable cash to this financing model without an actual funding arrangement.
