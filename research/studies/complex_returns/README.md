# Socioeconomic returns of the founding off Earth complex

The founding cities, crater towns, mines, factories, power systems and transport network form the U socioecosystem, a shared productive complex. Its return concerns the people and institutions who finance it, work in it, buy its products, use its research and experience its consequences. Most of those stakeholders need not reside on the Moon. This appraisal uses the current provisioning account for that complex, including its source operations and depots, and excludes a planetary array and the Open Moon.

Several measures are necessary. The larger tested manufacturing portfolio produces a conditional **$136.56 billion annual external surplus after renewal and resource provision**, while the matched industrial and material campaign costs **$41.89 trillion** before the separately costed commercial addition. Positive annual trade therefore establishes neither rapid capital recovery nor a complete socioeconomic return. Conversely, a financial deficit does not establish that research, customer, public or cultural benefits are valueless. Those benefits require their own counterfactual and evidence.

The calculations below distinguish external funding recovery, capital-provider returns, production and income, a socioeconomic valuation screen, and decision thresholds for unmeasured benefits. They use **constant 2041 US dollars**, with no subsequent inflation. The inherited monetary unit U is not equated with dollars; here “the complex” denotes the socioecosystem. All population, demand, pricing, longevity and valuation extensions remain scenarios. The research reader is assumed to know the founding industrial design; the accounts and comparison methods are defined here.

The [staged expansion study](staged.md) now models receipts from the first productive facilities, reinvestment constrained by earned cash, and much larger worldwide cultural and institutional-service markets. It reconciles the same campaign into one-time deployment and recurring costs, then follows a new annual schedule. A conditional $2.789 trillion seed supports a small first module; the strong service case produces +$2.947 trillion capital-recovery NPV at 3% over 100 elapsed years, while the middle case sustains itself without meeting that return. The original full-campaign timing below remains a benchmark. The alternative delivers physical capacity much later, and its minimum functioning module and markets require qualification.

Establishment is contained in the campaign and is never added to it again. The campaign also funds initial material deliveries, supplier operations and renewal provision during construction. It is more than an equipment purchase and less than a fully reconciled dollar budget for every civil, household and municipal service. Its original deployment design has not been repriced to prove that the later modular-maintenance assumptions can be achieved at startup. The return calculation preserves that limitation and tests 25% and 50% larger campaign allowances.

The larger commercial portfolio adds **$395.18 billion**: $309.29 billion in Earth purchases, $81.78 billion in civil installation allowances, $0.56 billion of local component value and $3.56 billion of resource provision. The funding screen includes all these uses, assuming they remain additional to the campaign. A procurement reconciliation could identify overlap; their internal origin alone does not make their provision free. The economic screen removes the identified $3.56-billion reserve because reserving money is not itself resource consumption.

The founding system supports 250,000 residents, four cities and 100 crater towns. That population bounds a physical operating case; it is not the population of investors, customers or beneficiaries. The principal annual comparisons are:

| Operating portfolio | Earth sales | GDP or gross value added | Net domestic product | External balance after renewal and resource provision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mixed specialty products | 170.10 | 351.01 | 34.82 | −299.34 |

| Larger tested manufacturing portfolio | 701.63 | 831.48 | 477.37 | +136.56 |

All annual amounts are **billion USD2041**. These quantities answer different questions. GDP includes domestic production; net domestic product deducts the stated consumption of fixed capital. Neither is money that can be added to the external balance. Imported equipment, domestic replacement production, sales among off-Earth producers and final Earth purchases already have distinct places in the source accounts.

The higher manufacturing prices require qualified products and substantial Earth orders. The positive external balance is a conditional combination of quantities, prices and costs, not a forecast. Its media, research, professional and destination services remain modest prescribed programmes. The earlier humanities sports and entertainment scenarios identify additional possibilities, but their receipts cannot be appended through a universal U-to-dollar conversion. A larger cultural programme must replace overlapping service receipts and bring its additional production, travel and acquisition costs into this account.

## Stakeholders and the return each receives

The physical boundary and the appraisal's beneficiary population are separate. Internal off-Earth payments cancel in consolidated trade, while an Earth-based investor or researcher can still benefit from the project. Global appraisal also includes adverse effects on Earth and losses to people displaced by new production. This is an application of the distinction between aggregate returns, beneficiary incidence and counterfactual outcomes in [MCC's economic and beneficiary guidance](https://www.mcc.gov/publications-sections/story-cdg-guidelines-for-economic-and-beneficiary-analysis/).

| Stakeholder | Relevant return or burden | Evidence needed for valuation |
|---|---|---|
| Equity investors and lenders, wherever based | Dividends, principal, interest, losses and residual ownership | Dated capital commitments and enforceable claims after senior obligations |
| Public funders and taxpayers | Public outcomes, fiscal receipts, subsidies and foregone alternative spending | Funding shares, tax incidence, service mandates and a comparison investment |
| Earth and off-Earth customers | Useful goods, computing, entertainment, access and consumer surplus | Demand, substitute products, prices, quality and willingness to pay |
| Research users and institutions | Scientific results, access, reproducibility and downstream applications | Additional outcomes relative to terrestrial or robotic alternatives, diffusion and attribution |
| Workers and residents | Earnings above alternatives, housing and services, autonomy, health, leisure and risk | Counterfactual earnings and living conditions, participation and longitudinal outcomes |
| Suppliers and competing producers | Producer surplus, learning, displacement and adjustment costs | Margins and opportunity costs; gross supplier sales are not net benefit |
| Affected communities and future cohorts | Environmental and heritage effects, resilience, future capabilities and liabilities | Physical consequences, stewardship costs, duration, distribution and ethical assumptions |

A person can appear in several rows. Their dividend, wage and enjoyment of an event are distinct outcomes, but the same wage or invoice must not be counted twice. Paying for research establishes a service purchase, not the additional value of every eventual discovery. Similarly, export receipts and the customers' full purchase value are two views of one transaction; only surplus beyond that value can be added as a separate customer benefit.

### External funding recovery and capital claims

For operating year (t), the funding screen calculates

`A[t] = Earth sales − Earth operating and renewal purchases − resource provision − expansion funding`.

Resource provision remains restricted cash in this calculation. The available recovery flow is `c × max(A[t], 0) + min(A[t], 0)`, where `c` is the fraction of positive surplus collectable for the programme. Negative balances remain funding requirements in full. A value of one is a ceiling on collection, not a promise that businesses, workers or institutions surrender all their claims.

For cash flow `F[t]`, net present value is `sum(F[t] / (1+r)^t)`. The series includes the initial campaign, commercial commissioning, annual renewal and expansion once. Loan proceeds are not revenue; interest and principal are absent from this unlevered funding envelope. An investor case then assigns explicit shares of negative funding flows and positive distributions. The remaining stakeholders receive the complementary flows, preserving the combined balance.

For example, at 3% real over 100 operating years, the large portfolio's total funding NPV is **−$25.64 trillion**. A hypothetical investor funding and receiving 50% has **−$12.82 trillion**, with the same balance assigned to the other funders. An investor funding 25% but receiving 50% has **−$5.99 trillion**, while the others bear **−$19.65 trillion**. Preferential financing reallocates the cost; it creates no additional combined return. These illustrations are not proposed contracts. Actual equity returns require taxes, debt seniority, retained earnings and exit values that the provisioning account has not specified.

### Production and income comparison

The annual GDP and net domestic product table shows productive activity that an external-sales-only measure would miss. To estimate additional income, compare the same people and assets with what they would otherwise produce, consume and earn. Moving a person from Earth raises the local headcount without automatically creating their entire new income as a global gain. Earth suppliers' alternative uses of labour and capital also matter. An input-output multiplier would need a measured supplier network and available capacity; this study introduces none.

A complete income-incidence model remains unestimated. The current calculation exposes resident opportunity earnings as one component: an assumed working share of 60%, at $100,000 per worker in 2026 purchasing power, adjusted by the inherited inflation factor. It tests half and twice that assumption. These earnings are not the return denominator and do not stand in for investor, customer or institutional benefits.

### Socioeconomic benefit and resource comparison

The monetary socioeconomic screen uses a deliberately inspectable proxy:

`net benefit = counterfactual value of exports + value of resident services + additional net spillovers − imported resource-cost proxies − local opportunity costs − expansion resources`.

Export purchase prices can represent avoided Earth production costs for equivalent output, or incremental willingness to pay for genuinely additional products. They establish neither quantity by themselves. The calculation therefore tests 0%, 50% and 100% of export purchase value as economic benefit. For substituted products, the customer's unchanged enjoyment cancels against the alternative; the relevant comparison is avoided terrestrial production cost and any quality or price benefit. Goods used by businesses are not all final consumption, and their downstream output cannot be added in full again.

Resident housing, food and civic services use the source account's imputed value, with 0%, 50% and 100% comparisons. That source relies partly on its illustrative $30/U normalization. This is not measured willingness to pay or an adopted exchange rate. Imported purchases, including replacement equipment, supply price proxies for resource cost; Earth supplier margins and market distortions still need shadow-price analysis. Resident opportunity earnings enter once. The domestic growth invoice is not added wholesale because its internal inputs and labour overlap that ledger; an explicit local-growth cost fraction can test additional resource requirements after decomposition.

The result is a **conditional purchase-value/resource-cost screen**, not a completed global welfare estimate. The initial campaign is itself a procurement and renewal-funding envelope. Construction-period opportunity costs and interim services have not been independently decomposed from its invoices. Further local resource, environmental and heritage costs are unpriced in the central calculation; the zero additional-cost input is not evidence that those costs vanish. Fiscal transfers, supplier turnover, GDP, wages and profits are not added as further benefits.

[HM Treasury's appraisal guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-green-book--2/the-green-book-2026) supports presenting counterfactual comparisons, distribution, unmonetized effects and switching values alongside summary ratios. The application here preserves those distinctions instead of presenting the proxy ratio as a single social return on investment.

## Time horizons and recovery

The campaign is spread evenly across 33 annual draws at elapsed years 0–32, with commercial additions over the final five draws. The first full operating receipt is at year 33. This timing experiment gives no credit for early operating income and is not a procurement schedule. A delayed-and-rescheduled sensitivity spreads the same capital over 43 years; it does not represent a pure operational delay with fixed earlier expenditure.

The main table holds the founding population and portfolio constant. Every amount is funding NPV in **trillion USD2041**, discounted at **3% real from the first construction draw**:

| Portfolio | 10 operating years | 30 operating years | 100 operating years | 500 operating years |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mixed specialty | −28.22 | −29.50 | −30.90 | −31.10 |
| Higher-price mixed | −27.38 | −27.59 | −27.81 | −27.84 |
| Larger manufacturing | −26.86 | −26.28 | −25.64 | −25.55 |

The larger case's positive annual external surplus recovers the specified capital without discounting only at about **elapsed year 342**, approximately 310 operating years. At 500 operating years its undiscounted cumulative cash is **+$25.99 trillion**, whereas its 1% and 3% funding NPVs are **−$26.32 trillion and −$25.55 trillion**. Discounting affects construction outlays as well as receipts, so a less negative NPV at a higher rate does not mean the project meets that rate. The study also reports 0% and 7% cases and peak cumulative funding requirements.

No perpetuity, resale price or terminal business valuation is credited. Zero residual value can penalize a short horizon containing assets still useful afterward; the annual plateau account shows the continuing service obligation separately. Renewal allowances are already included in the imported costs, so the calculation adds neither annual depreciation nor another complete fleet replacement. A future dated asset-cohort model should replace the level-renewal convention, particularly for synchronized replacement and liquidity risk.

The latest [UK discounting supplement](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ae0b34016428df537606216/Green_Book_supplementary_guidance_-_discounting.pdf) uses successive real forward rates through year 125 and recommends other approaches beyond that. This study does not adopt a UK lunar policy or extend that schedule. Its constant 0%, 1%, 3% and 7% rates are transparent project sensitivities. The very long horizons primarily expose maintenance, demand and intergenerational dependencies.

## Slow growth, taper and the sustained cap

The source's vacuum architectural reference is **6.468 million residents**. The eventual 507.549-million crater capacity belongs to a different architectural horizon and supplies no population or revenue here. The source's forty-year growth simulations also exclude commercial exports; their faster growth frontier cannot be combined with the larger export programme without sharing the same factories, power and transport twice.

For the larger export portfolio, existing spare capacity supports about **2,806 new residents per year** at founding scale, or **1.122%**. The present paths request zero growth, 0.5% or 1% initially. Construction starts consume capacity immediately; the corresponding population and industrial capacity enter operation after two years. Marginal external expansion funding follows the source's **$22.874 million per new supported resident**, with a separate **$1.067 million resource provision**. This is the cost of extending the whole supporting system, not a bill presented to the new resident.

At 70% of the enclosed cap, the requested trajectory changes from exponential growth to a quadratic approach whose initial slope matches and whose final slope is zero. The 1% and 0.5% cases reach the cap after **378 and 753 operating years**. The model then holds occupied capacity constant. Renewal, imports, services and resource demand continue; ending expansion does not end costs. Linear replication of the founding equipment and material intensities over these durations is an explicit extension, conditional on sites, staffing, permitted sources and renewed qualification.

Earth demand has its own assumption. One comparison allows sales to rise with supplied capacity at unchanged real prices. Another caps Earth sales at five times the founding portfolio while maintaining the installed system's costs. This second case tests overbuilding; it is not a model of optimal retirement or redeployment. For the 1% growth path, the eventual annual external balance is **+$3.53 trillion** with proportional demand and **−$11.11 trillion** with the five-times sales cap. The latter does not impose a ceiling on possible Earth interest; it asks what happens if physical expansion exceeds the chosen market.

Over 800 operating years at 1% real discounting, the 1% growth case gives funding NPV **−$8.61 trillion** with proportional demand and **−$90.44 trillion** with capped demand. The zero-growth comparison is **−$26.25 trillion**. Without discounting, proportional-demand growth recovers the specified funding around elapsed year 222. These are coupled capacity-and-market scenarios, not forecasts of eight centuries of contracts. They show why growth can improve the account when productive uses develop, and damage it when new capacity lacks funded uses.

Source-feed demand and continuous renewals scale with the occupied system. The supplied resource inventories do not establish permission or availability for that duration. Long-run plateau operation remains an assumption, as requested, with source access and conservation unresolved rather than priced as unlimited free resources.

## What unmeasured benefits would have to contribute

For discounted gap `G` and the discounted factor for the chosen operating window `D`, the switching value `G/D` is the constant **additional annual net benefit** required to close the socioeconomic screen. The same calculation on the financial gap gives **additional collectable cash**, a different requirement. A wellbeing or scientific gain does not pay a debt unless an institution finances it.

In the 100-operating-year larger-portfolio case at 3%, with the full inherited resident-service valuation and the stated opportunity-cost assumptions:

| Export purchase value credited economically | Additional annual net benefit required, trillion USD2041 |
|---|---:|
| 0% | 2.549 |
| 50% | 2.198 |
| 100% | 1.847 |

Those values are not divided only among residents. They could concern many Earth customers, research users, future innovators and public beneficiaries, as well as workers and residents. The study tests illustrative additional net benefits of zero, $100 billion and $1 trillion per operating year without asserting that any is established. A benefit must be additional to the priced services and net of its own costs, displacement and attribution to other investments. Unpriced negative effects raise the requirement.

Nonmarket research should proceed outcome by outcome. [OECD's environmental appraisal review](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/cost-benefit-analysis-and-the-environment_9789264085169-en.html) discusses several valuation approaches; none supplies a lunar price. Suitable next measurements include demand experiments for media and visits; competing bids for scientific and industrial access; avoided costs for a specified research capability; and repeated surveys of workers' and residents' wellbeing. [Wellbeing appraisal guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/1005388/Wellbeing_guidance_for_appraisal_-_supplementary_Green_Book_guidance.pdf) defines a WELLBY as one life-satisfaction point for one person-year. No terrestrial monetary WELLBY value is converted into the project's hypothetical units here, and wellbeing cannot be added uncritically to income or health benefits that already explain it.

Cost-effectiveness measures should follow each purpose: cost per qualified tonne or useful computing workload, per independently evaluated research result, per visitor experience, per supported resident-year, or per demonstrated resilience capability. A single resident-year denominator would omit most of the complex's purposes. Until outputs and comparators are specified, these remain measurement requirements rather than invented effectiveness scores.

## Decisions that the analysis can support

Three decisions have different evidence requirements. Continuing useful operations asks whether their additional benefits and costs justify future expenditure; sunk establishment cannot be spent again. Expanding an existing system requires incremental facilities, qualification and capacity-supported demand. Committing to the entire programme requires recovery or justification of the full campaign and the benefits of alternatives forgone. A favourable expansion margin does not retrospectively establish a favourable whole-programme return.

The incremental commercial comparison makes that distinction quantitative. Keeping the already established modest-service system is the baseline; the larger manufacturing programme costs an additional **$395.18 billion** and improves its annual external balance by **$428.40 billion**, from −$291.84 billion to +$136.56 billion. Charging the full addition over five years and then receiving that improvement for 30 operating years gives **+$7.09 trillion incremental funding NPV at 3%**. The original industrial campaign remains funded in both alternatives and cancels from this incremental comparison. The result depends on the qualified product orders and prices; it is neither a $7.09-trillion cheque to investors nor a valuation of the whole complex. Continuing a deeply subsidized baseline also makes the upgrade's improvement large. Closure, partial retirement or a different product mix require their own comparisons.

Staged qualification can preserve the ability to stop, change products or defer additional capacity. The [New Zealand Treasury working paper on investment options](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/publications/wp/contemporary-microeconomic-foundations-structure-and-management-public-sector-wp-12-01) explains why the option to learn can matter; an actual monetary option value requires information quality and probabilities. This calculation compares cost, delayed construction, growth, demand and collection scenarios without inventing such probabilities. It does not yet optimize a staging policy or estimate expected return.

The strongest next improvements are a unified campaign bill with dated asset commissioning; evidence for qualified product demand at the tested quantities; contract-specific investor claims; a counterfactual supplier and labour account; and a small set of additional customer, research and public outcomes that can be measured without duplicate credit. Together they would move the analysis from conditional envelopes toward a socioeconomic valuation of U's whole stakeholder system. The present results already establish which accounts must stay separate and how strongly conclusions depend on timing, renewal, demand and benefits beyond commercial receipts.

```sh
python3 -m pytest -q research/studies/complex_returns/tests/test_returns.py
MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/terluna-roi-mpl python3 visualization/complex-returns/plot.py
```

Normal calculation output goes to ignored `research/runs/complex_returns/`. The reviewed [results](results/returns.json) require an explicit `--output` path. The [input specification](returns_inputs.json) binds exact provisioning snapshots and states every new time, counterfactual and growth assumption. [Checks](checks.md) record numerical tests, source and substantive reading, the bounded accounting reviews and visual inspection separately from empirical validation.
