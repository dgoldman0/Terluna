# The river deltas of the young Moon and the mature one

4 November 2026. This study screens every river mouth where the terrain study's routing sends the land's rock into standing water: 23,015 mouths of channels carrying 1 m³/s and more on geography's 1.9 km drainage grid, into the seas, the small waters at their level, the rain-fed lakes and the soda lakes. It works two states of the Moon side by side at lunar gravity. The young Moon of the first centuries to millennia with water runs on regolith with bare or newly planted banks, drier rivers and soft young seas. The mature Moon runs on developed soils from sand to clay under rooted banks, with the seas held fresh. For each mouth the screen gives the bankfull channel, the split of the load into bed material and wash load, the plume through the lunar day, the balance of river, wave and tidal sediment fluxes that shapes the delta, the delta's scales and the share of its load the delta keeps. The same functions run with Earth's gravity, rotation, seawater, tides and waves on four Earth deltas of known type and on the lunar mouths. It is a screen of closed-form relations on one-dimensional arrays; morphodynamic model runs follow for the kinds of delta it picks out.

```sh
nice -n 10 env OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 -m research.studies.river_deltas.run   # 20-25 s, 0.33 GB
python3 -m pytest research/studies/river_deltas/test_run.py -q                        # TERLUNA_RIVER_DELTAS_REGENERATE=1 reruns the screen
```

The runner writes [results/river_deltas.json](results/river_deltas.json) (schema `terluna.research.river-deltas/1`) and the table of every mouth to `/media/projectspace/terluna-research/river-deltas/mouths.csv.gz`. [sources.json](sources.json) records each published value and how far it was read.

## What it finds

Every figure below is a calculation of this screen at the central case unless named otherwise: the terrain study's central erosion, geography's flows on the older climate run, the design years' temperatures and the screened channel. Medians run over mouths, with the 10th and 90th percentiles in brackets; shares of load weight each mouth by the rock it receives.

**The Moon's deltas are mostly small streams entering lakes.** Of the 23,015 river mouths, 21,967 enter rain-fed lakes, 892 the small waters at the common level, 146 the seas (115 the Near-side Sea, 26 Orientale, 2 each South Pole–Aitken and Moscoviense, 1 Smythii–Marginis) and 10 the soda lakes. The median mouth carries 2.5–3.3 m³/s (0.9–56) and floods to 13–15 m³/s at bankfull. The river mouths take 22.5% of the rock that meets standing water; hillslopes and streams below the grid deliver the rest. Lakes upstream catch most of the rock in the terrain study's routing, so even the largest rivers arrive nearly clear. The Orientale ring's chain of lakes passes 57,000 m³/s to its sea mouth with 0.47 Mt a year of rock in the young state. The rivers that reach the common level carry 227,700 m³/s, against the drainage product's 229,410, and the screen finds eleven of the drainage's twelve largest rivers to within 0.1%; the twelfth's 30 km search window catches a larger neighbour.

**The young Moon's rivers braid, and the mature Moon's rooted banks hold them to single, narrow, deep channels.** At lunar gravity a loose sand bed's bankfull stress, at Earth's dimensionless bankfull Shields number for its grain, is 4.1–5.2 Pa. Bare regolith banks resist only 1.5 Pa, so the young channels spread to 4.4–4.9 times the loose channel's width at a third of its depth: 274 m wide (172–1,030) and 8 cm deep at the median, a braidplain at every mouth. Rooted developed soils resist 4.9 Pa (sand) to 16.9 Pa (clay), and bank stress does not change with gravity, so the mature channels are 1.4–4.4 times deeper and a tenth to three fifths as wide as loose scaling. A mature loam channel is 13 m wide (8–48), 0.6 m deep and runs at 1.7 m/s; loam, clay loam and clay channels are single-thread, sandy loam mostly transitional, and sand braided. On Earth the same sand bed's loose stress is 15–16 Pa, close to the rooted banks' resistance, so bed and banks set Earth's fine-grained channels together, near the banks' threshold where Dunne & Jerolmack (2020) find them. The Moon's weaker gravity lowers the bed's stress and leaves the banks' strength, and the balance tips: bank strength sets the lunar channel. With roots at half strength the mature loam channels braid as well, and at double strength the sand channels close to single threads. The mature beds can carry 90–55,000 times their bed material at bankfull (loam: 1,700 at the median by load), so they cut down and pass their sand to the mouth; the young beds carry 4–90 times theirs.

**At lunar gravity most of the load travels as wash load.** Grains settle at a sixth of Earth's speed (a 62.5 µm grain at 0.64 mm/s against 3.6, clay at 0.7 µm/s), so the Rouse number that separates wash load from bed material moves to coarser grains. Bed material is 41% of the young regolith load (the regolith's median 72 µm, the bed's 220–270 µm). In the mature soils it is 38% for sand, 29% sandy loam, 13% loam, 11% clay loam and 4% clay. The young seas are soft, 0.14 mmol/kg of calcium and magnesium, below the 0.3–3 mmol/L at which calcium coagulates smectite and illite clays. The young rivers carry 2 mmol/L from the fast-weathering young soils, so their fines flocculate on the way: 82% of the clay and fine silt are in flocs at the median. In the mature waters the held seas' 0.84 mmol/kg and organic binding put 73–78% in flocs, which settle at 0.02–0.17 mm/s at lunar gravity. These shares rest on search summaries and a design bracket, and stay open.

**The rivers plunge into the fresh seas and lakes through most of the lunar day.** River water from the land, 1–4 K cooler than the seas' 29.6 °C surface and the lakes, is 0.4–2.1 kg/m³ denser than the water it meets. That is a tenth to a fiftieth of the 41.8 kg/m³ of sediment that fresh river water needs to sink into Earth's seawater (the screen's value for Mulder & Syvitski's 36–43). By load, 96–99% of the young rivers' water and 69–92% of the mature rivers' enters as plunging underflows through the lunar day, at mean flow and at bankfull. Most of the rest spreads at its own level where the water is too shallow to plunge in, and almost none floats. The plunge lies at 1–6 m in the young braidplains and 6–63 m in the mature channels, from a densimetric Froude number of 0.5 with lunar g′. Where plunging holds over half the day at bankfull, underflow channels cut the delta fronts as the Rhône's do in Lake Geneva: at 97% of the young mouths and 74–88% of the mature. Plunging holds across the temperature bracket. With the seas at 25 °C, no catchment cooling and lakes at the air's temperature, floating rises to 10–12% of the mature load and plunging stays at 70–80%. The soda lakes are the exception: the mature soda lakes' 35 g/kg brine is 27 kg/m³ heavier than the rivers, and every plume floats.

**No lunar plume turns into a coastal current at its mouth, though rotation may steer the largest rivers' plumes far out.** The Moon turns once in 27.3 days, so its Coriolis parameter is a twenty-seventh of Earth's at the same latitude, and the plumes' internal Rossby radius is 16–196 km for the young and 25–570 km for the mature. The plumes shed half their wash load within 0.01–0.06 km (young) and 0.02–26 km (mature), a few tenths of the Rossby radius at most, so the lunar plumes spread and settle around their mouths. In the Earth control the Mississippi's Rossby radius is 18 km against a 10 km reach, near the turning its plume makes along the Louisiana coast. The exception is 150 mature mouths, the Orientale chain's 54,000–57,000 m³/s rivers first among them, whose fines would travel about 360 km against Rossby radii of 100–280 km. Their plumes spread at their own level or plunge, which the screen's count of coastal currents (floating plumes only) leaves out, and their reach rests on mature channels 110–160 m deep, deeper than banks stand (the bank-height flag below), so whether rotation steers them stays open.

**Waves shape most of the small mouths and the seas' deltas, the rivers shape the rest, and the monthly tide shapes part of the young seas' deltas only on gentle slopes.** By load, the rivers dominate 77% of the young deltas and the waves 23%; in the mature state each dominates half. The mature land yields a sixth of the young land's rock, so the mature deltas fall to the waves more often. Across the terrain study's erosion cases the river-dominated share runs 43–91% (young) and 24–61% (mature). By count, waves dominate 59% of the young mouths and 82% of the mature: at lunar gravity the seas' light winds raise fully developed waves of about a metre on the seas and lakes wider than about 100 km and several decimetres on lakes of a few hundred km², and the small streams' load cannot outbuild them. The sea mouths are 63% wave-dominated by load in the young state and wholly in the mature. The monthly tide fills and drains each channel once in 27.55 days, so its tidal discharge is a fiftieth of what a semidiurnal tide of the same range would drive. At the screened channels it reaches a tenth of the river's discharge at the median young sea mouth, and it dominates no mouth. At a tenth of the screened slope, the gentle end that the Earth control and the channels' Froude numbers favour (below), it dominates 27% of the young seas' load and still none of the mature. With Earth's semidiurnal tide on the same ranges, 65% of the young seas' load would be tide-dominated.

**The deltas keep a tenth to two fifths of their load as delta, less than the terrain study's half to all.** A delta keeps its bed material and what of its wash load settles within its own reach while the plume floats. The young deltas keep 0.42 (0.39–0.44) of their load. The mature deltas keep 0.42 for sand, 0.34 sandy loam, 0.18 loam, 0.16 clay loam and 0.08 clay; the soda lakes' floating plumes keep 0.76–1.0. If the plunging underflows drop their fines within the delta's reach, the shares rise to 0.99 for the young and to 0.95, 0.85, 0.66, 0.55 and 0.34 for the mature soils. What the delta does not keep spreads over the lake and sea floors as prodelta and bottomset. The same mouths with Earth's constants keep 0.69 (young) and 0.45 (mature loam). Lunar settling and the plunging plumes halve what a delta keeps.

**Results that move across the channel bracket.** The Earth control found the screened channels of large sand rivers 0.8–3.6 times their observed width and 3–10 times too steep. The screen's slopes come from the bankfull Shields closure for the grain size; the routing grid's slope at the mouths is the shore's raw terrain, a median 2–2.7% at every discharge and 8–80 times steeper still on large rivers. The closure stays central. The bracket reruns the classification at half and double width, at a third and a tenth of the slope, and on the grid's slope:

The braiding of the young rivers, the single threads of the mature loam and clay rivers, the plunging plumes, the wave-shaped seas of the mature Moon and the keep shares hold across it. Three results flip with the channel: whether the mature sand rivers braid, whether a quarter to two fifths of the young sea mouths' load is tide-shaped, and how far the monthly tide floods the plains.

The screened slopes are too steep on a second count. With the screen's friction coefficient a channel's bankfull Froude number is the square root of its slope over that coefficient, whatever its banks, and the median mouth's screened slopes of 1.1–1.4% give 1.7–1.9 in every state. Mobile beds hold their flow near critical at most (Grant 1997), so these slopes overstate the real ones about three times or more. A third of the slope brings the median to about 1 and a tenth to about 0.55. The gentle end of the bracket is therefore the physically consistent one, as the Earth control also found: there the monthly tide floods 0.85–3.4 km of plain at the median sea mouth, about a quarter of the young sea mouths' load is tide-shaped at a tenth of the slope, and the mature sand rivers run single-thread. The bracket moves the slope and width that the classifications read, the depth following the width by continuity, and holds the screened velocity and bed stress, so the channel's own figures in the table below stay those of the screened slope.

**The delta's scales are small, and the young deltas switch channels within decades.** Mouth bars stand 0.9 km (0.6–3.4) from the young mouths and 0.03–0.15 km from the narrower mature ones. The backwater reach of these steep small streams is metres to a kilometre, so channels switch over the delta's own extent. Building one channel depth of superelevation takes 18 years (3–720) at the young mouths. At the mature mouths it takes 53 (sand) to 210 years (clay). Weighted by load the times fall to 7 years (young) and 18–69 (mature), since the rock-rich steep streams switch most often. Against the Mississippi's observed lobe switching of about a thousand years the Earth control gives 150, so these times carry about a factor of ten. Sand fronts stand at the angle of repose, 32°, unchanged by gravity. Cohesive mud of 0.5–5 kPa stands 29–290 m high at a 1° front and 6–59 m at 5°, six times Earth's 5–49 and 1–10 m. The monthly tide floods and drains 0.28–0.34 km of plain each fortnight at the median on the screened slopes (up to 1.7 km), from its 4.4 m median range at the sea mouths over the plains' slope, and 0.85–3.4 km on the gentler slopes the Froude number favours.

**The young deltas build into a moving sea.** At the sea mouths the young deltas aggrade 1.8 cm a year at the median by load (0.3–6.3 cm). The crust's sag lowers the deep floors 1.5 cm a year under the central mantle and up to 41 cm a year under the warm profile, and uptake would drop the seas 17 cm a year if delivery stopped (geography, "The crust's response to the water"). Wherever delivery does not hold the level, the sea moves as fast as the young deltas build or faster, drowning or stranding their plains.

## How the terrain study would read it (proposal)

The terrain study fills the nearest water at every mouth with half to all of what settles there, as a design guess. The screen supports a share by state and soil: about 0.4 for the young Moon (0.39–0.44, rising toward 1 where the underflows drop their fines on the delta front), and 0.4 (sand) falling to 0.08 (clay) for the mature soils, with 0.34–0.95 as the underflow bracket. The rest of what settles goes to the lake or sea floor. Read this way, the terrain study's young delta plains stay near its latest case, and the mature clayey soils' plains shrink to a fifth to a half of its central areas while the floors fill correspondingly faster. A per-mouth share is in the drive's table.

## How it is set up

- **Mouths.** The terrain study's saved grids (`research/runs/resources/terrain/terrain_16ppd.npz`, read and not rerun): each standing-water cell where land first meets water, its rock per Gt a year of land erosion, its depth and its delta areas. A river mouth is such a cell next to a terminal channel cell, one that no neighbouring channel cell exceeds in discharge; its discharge is the sum of its terminal cells. Receiving water from the terrain units (rain-fed or closed lake) and, for the common level, from the tide product's sea labels by connected water.
- **States.** Young: rock at each mouth is its own catchment's (lakes upstream trap) times a yield factor of 6 (3–12, paired with the latest and earliest cases). The factor is a design bracket from the storm-water study's bare dryland soils, which detach 10–23 times what crusted ones do and 2.6–12.6 times the terrain study's central lowering, and from Earth's plowed fields eroding 1–2 orders of magnitude faster than ground under native vegetation (Montgomery 2007). Flows are lowered by the 1,000-year water table's lost seepage in each band: 0.71 at 0–15°, 0.95 at 15–30°, 0.78 at 30–45°. Bankfull is 6 times the mean on humid land and 10 on dry. The seas are at 1,000 years of the sea chemistry and the soda lakes at 1 g/kg (0.1–35). Mature: the terrain study's three erosion cases, bankfull 4 and 10 times the mean, seas held at 0.142 g/kg and soda lakes at 35 g/kg (1–300), deltas at their 10,000-year areas.
- **Channels.** Loose-bank scaling: Parker et al.'s (2007) dimensionless width (fitted on gravel, used here for sand beds), the sand-bed bankfull Shields number 17 Rep^-1/2 (Trampush et al. 2014, as Hayden et al. 2019 carry it to Mars), a friction coefficient of 0.004, and Parker et al.'s depth and slope for gravel. Threshold banks: 1.2 times the larger of the bed's Shields threshold and the banks' critical stress (Dunne & Jerolmack 2020), WEPP's bare-soil critical shear (Alberts et al. 1995) times a root factor of 3 (1.5–6), and the screened slope. Mean-flow depth follows bankfull at fixed width and slope. Chezy's C, which holds g, is written as sqrt(g/Cf) wherever it appears.
- **Load and grains.** Regolith lognormal at 72 µm (Carrier 2003) and 3,100 kg/m³; the mature textures from clay, silt and sand modes at 2,700 kg/m³. Settling by Ferguson & Church (2004); wash load below a bankfull Rouse number of 0.11, Earth's 62.5 µm convention at a shear velocity of 0.07 m/s. Flocs of grains under 16 µm settle at Earth's 0.1–1 mm/s times g/g_E, in proportion to the salt (calcium 0.3–3 mmol/L, sodium in the soda lakes 30–300 mmol/L) and organic shares.
- **Plumes.** River water at the design years' land air for the mouth's aridity class in each tenth of the lunar day. It is carried to the mouth's level by cold_waters' 1.04 K/km from the land around the mouth, less half the local relief as catchment height. The seas' surface is 29.6 °C with a 1 K swing over deep water at 26 °C (25–29); lakes are the air at their level plus 1.2 K (0–2.4). Density adds 0.8 kg/m³ per g/kg of carbonate water, 0.75 for seawater, and the suspended load at the flux-weighted concentration rated to mean flow and bankfull.
- **Shaping.** Nienhuis et al.'s (2020) fluxes. Qwave is twice the maximum potential alongshore transport times the waves' directional steadiness. The CERC formula (K = 0.39) is recast into deep-water terms with g explicit: Q = K ρρs/(8(ρs−ρ)) · 2π(4π)^-6/5 · g^3/5 γb^-2/5 · H0^12/5 T^1/5 · 0.4685. Waves come from the wave study's nearside coastal power and Smythii–Marginis nodes, and elsewhere from JONSWAP growth at lunar gravity under the seas' median 2.6 m/s wind, which gives 1.0 m against the nearside's computed 1.39 m. The tide's discharge is ω a² B / S over the 27.55-day line, the channel's surface over the reach where its bed rises by the tidal amplitude. The western channels of Fecunditatis and the Crisium passage would mark strait-swept mouths; no resolved river enters them.
- **Scales.** Mouth bar three widths at Earth's typical jet, scaled by Edmonds & Slingerland's one-fifth power of momentum over grain weight. Backwater depth over slope. Avulsion time as one depth of aggradation over a belt of ten widths along the larger of the backwater length and the delta's radius (Jerolmack & Mohrig 2007; Ganti et al. 2016). Bar mode by Crosato & Mosselman (2009), b = 4 for sand. Mud fronts by infinite-slope cohesion.

## What it leaves out

- **The flows and the climate are older and coarser than the design.** Geography's drainage runs on A28_dim5_moon (years 20–29); the design years' land P − E is 1.86 times that run's, so the flows carry ×0.75–1.86. Across it the dominance and keep shares move by under two points and the plunging shares by up to four, while the sandy loam's braided share runs 4–31%. The aridity class is the most probable one in each latitude band, so 99.9% of river mouths read as humid. The turned Moon's geography (about 8°) and the atlas round's shorelines are not in; latitude and the climate inputs are parameters for the rerun.
- **What the routing does not save.** Each mouth's rock passed on by filled lakes upstream is not in the saved grids, so the mature mouths take their own catchments' rock. The routing should save the passed rock per mouth and year (a proposed change to `routing.py`). Lake chains count one river at every lake it runs through. A few lake mouths double their discharge where an outlet channel ties with its downstream neighbour: summed per lake against the lake's inflow, the mouths' discharge ran 0.76 at the median and 2.0 at the 99th percentile in a check of the first run.
- **Channels.** The hydraulic geometry carries the Earth control's factor of 2 in width and 3–10 in slope, and its screened slopes run supercritical at bankfull (above). Parker et al.'s width is fitted on gravel and the sand closure on Earth's rivers, whose banks it embeds. WEPP's critical shear is an Earth regression on texture whose small grain-weight part stays at Earth's gravity. The roots' factor, the banks' cohesion and the soils' textures are design brackets; the soils are not chosen (register, 1 and 3 November 2026). Braidplains are given as total width and mean depth, without their threads. A flag marks the 0.4–1.8% of channels deeper than a steep cohesive bank stands; Earth's big rivers keep taller banks, so it is not applied as a cap.
- **Plumes.** River and lake temperatures are design estimates whose bracket covers the plume classes. Flocculation rests on search summaries; no review of hard-water or organic flocculation was read, so those shares stay open.
- **Waves and tides.** Waves outside the two computed seas come from fetch-limited growth under the median wind, with storms left out. The tidal storage omits intertidal plains, which would add to the prism where a plain lies within the range.
- **Out of scope.** Morphodynamic models (Delft3D, SCHISM's sediment module), dry hollows' fans and playas, wind-blown dust, and the seas' level through time beyond the comparison above.
