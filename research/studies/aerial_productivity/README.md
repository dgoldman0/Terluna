# Additional production in the deep living sky

31 October 2026. **A richly occupied sky adds roughly 0.2–3.3 Gt C of primary production a year to the whole Moon at 10–20% projected biological area**, 1–18% of the surface's 18.8 Gt C, under the reference engineered-organism traits. The range runs from complete loss of production beneath the organisms' footprints to the same light loss spread over the surface habitats, and both ends carry the rain the surface loses with its sunlight. On the GCM's gas-dimming branch of the 0.950 design case, each per cent of dimming takes 0.55% of the land's rain and 0.071 Gt C a year of rain-fed growth; at that ratio the 10% and 20% skies, which intercept 9.5% and 18.1% of the sunlight, cost 0.67 and 1.29 Gt C a year and bring the light-only range of 0.8–4.6 down to this one. The rain channel is a first-order estimate. Sunlight the organisms absorb aloft heats the air above the surface and probably cuts the rain more than the same dimming does, so the cost may be larger; sunlight they reflect moves the Moon's energy balance instead, as added dimming does. That energy-balance check needs a GCM run of the rich sky, and none has been run. The cover is a prescribed scenario: a likely coverage and a carrying capacity need the population and resource calculation set out at the end.

## Light, the organisms and the ground

The inherited surface production includes the Moon's direct and diffuse light and its effect within plant canopies. The [surface-light calculation](../../../illumination/surface_light/README.md) gives 37% diffuse photosynthetic light with the Sun overhead, 56% at 30° elevation and 80% at 10°, against 7%, 12% and 30% in its Earth control, on the older clear-sky columns. The [tenant-light calculation](../sky_ecology/tenants.md) includes light scattered upward by the air below an organism, isotropic diffuse illumination from both hemispheres and obstruction by neighbouring reef modules: at 10 km a lone body's underside receives 19–30% of the light on a horizontal surface at its top over ground albedos of 0.05–0.2, and reef undersides 11–22%. That gives photosynthetic flanks and undersides a physical basis; their fixation is left out of this account.

The organisms' mutual shading uses a vertical-beam column. Strong scattering lights surfaces hidden from the direct Sun and spreads light among leaves with less saturation, so complete local loss beneath an intercepted footprint is the severe surface case. Oblique rays also cross more organisms, and absorbing or reflecting bodies change the diffuse field itself, so the diffuse fraction alone sets neither the direction nor the size of the whole-world correction. A calculation in which the organisms, the air and the ground share photons by angle comes before the table is read as attainable cover. Applying the canopy model's diffuse-light gain a second time to the inherited surface production would count it twice.

The area variable C is the sum of projected organism areas over all heights divided by the Moon's area: four levels averaging 5% each make C = 0.20, as one community with the same total projected area does. Independent opaque footprints intercept F = h[1 − exp(−C/h)] of the light, where h is the fraction of the Moon over which the organisms occur; at h = 1, 10% and 20% area intercept 9.5% and 18.1% of the column. Each layer shares what the layers above it transmit.

The main table places the most light-demanding giants above the lesser aerophytes and the reefs. Each class receives the photons transmitted through the classes above it, and the class means sum exactly to the intercepted photon budget. Within each class the mean dose and the dose at its bottom plane are checked against maintenance, hydrogen production, tissue renewal and adult replacement, and a community is credited only when both pass. The 10–20% stratified cases pass both checks, including the sensitivity that concentrates the organisms over 71% of the Moon. The 30% case passes both when spread globally; concentrated over 71%, the bottom of its giant layer falls marginally short. A randomly mixed community can pass at its mean while its lower giants fail, and at 50% globally distributed area the stratified mixture passes at its class means while the bottom of its giant layer fails, so neither is credited as a fixed vertical community. These are tests of specified traits and placement; angular light, reflected light, finite patches, shadow duration and motion come next.

The table uses the inherited mixture of 50% sky reefs, 30% round giants and 20% lesser aerophytes by projected area: 60 m reef modules, 1 km giants and 100 m lesser bodies. The reference gross fixation is 2 kg C per projected m² a year, with photolytic lifting gas, low maintenance, night suppression and an annual adult hazard of 0.003; these are engineered traits whose joint attainability is open. Height, reflection from clouds, following the Sun and the area of an underside add no production of their own here. The light multiplier is 0.982, the biomass comparison's midpoint provisional 301 K sunlight relative to its 0.950 baseline. The 5 kg C/m² a year case of the organism study is a separate physiological scenario.

## What is counted

The quantity counted is **new host tissue and structure, including replacement, plus the host's sugar supplied to its food web**: in the representative mixture 0.575 kg C/m² a year of tissue and 0.100 kg C/m² a year of sugar. Consumer and decomposer production spends this carbon, and adding it again would count the same primary production twice, so the figure is the accounted part of aerial NPP. The mature bodies' renewal and replacement stay fixed while their photosynthesis pays for them; dimming reduces their unallocated assimilate first, and the screen rejects a community whose income cannot pay its fixed programme. That is why the accounted production scales with organism area. At 10% and 20% area, allocating half the remaining assimilate to new tissue would add another 0.86 and 1.45 Gt C a year, an allocation sensitivity that needs its own nutrients, structural support and destination for the mass and stays out of the headline.

The deep atmosphere offers a vertically differentiated community. On the older design case 10 km has a mean temperature of 14.2 °C and a pressure near one atmosphere, and 20 km 5.1 °C and 0.81 atmosphere, with the mean freezing level near 25.7 km; at the 301 K design climate the freezing level stands several kilometres higher, and the design run supplies the new fields. Upper, exposed forms can gather strong light, while lower reefs keep water, soils and nutrients in pools and carry shade phototrophs on their fringes. Microbes, grazers, flyers and decomposers can occupy that structure without each needing its own sunny patch. The 20 km shell has only 2.3% more collecting area than the surface, so these habitats share almost the same incoming sunlight.

## The surface underneath

The low end of each range assumes complete loss of production beneath the intercepted area, over representative terrain, with the rain loss on the ground outside the footprints; the high end spreads the light reduction across the surface habitats, with the rain loss everywhere. Rain-fed land keeps the lower of its light-limited potential and its water, and its water falls by the rain channel's share: 4.7% of rain-fed growth at 10% cover and 9.0% at 20%. Wet margins and productive coasts respond directly to light, and nutrient-poor waters weakly. Organisms concentrated over highly productive forests can cost more surface production than these averages, and over dry ground or nutrient-poor water less. Clouds and diffuse scattering soften shadows; no cloud gain is added.

## The shared sky

The register's rules for the shared sky were set on a 1% sky. Scaled linearly from their products' 1% basis, with reefs half of the cover as in the class mixture, the rich sky meets them at these rates:

| Cover | Reef passes per sky town a day | Beam switches per receiver a day, sky towns | Beam switches per receiver a day, ground |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1% | 0.04–1.2 | 0.2–7.5 | 0.4–14 |
| 10% | 0.43–12 | 2.3–75 | 3.9–139 |
| 20% | 0.85–23 | 4.6–150 | 7.8–278 |

Towns give way to sky reefs on the shared tracks, with the reefs keeping that preference if they join the useful balancing system and a somewhat higher reef density acceptable (register, 12 October); at 10–20% cover a town meets ten to twenty times the reefs it meets at 1%, and its steering reserves, still to be sized even at 1% ([domain synthesis](../domain_synthesis/README.md)), would be sized at these rates. Beams move to another part of the ring before a reef crosses their column (register, 12 October), so receivers switch ten to twenty times as often, and the power screen's make-before-break design and the dose a late switch gives the reef ([power](../../../provisioning/power.md)) are next to be checked at that rate. The aerial life is kept wild above all (register, 11 October), and wild organisms at any of these covers belong to the wild sky. Cultivated and inhabited aerophytes, such as provisioning's crops and homes on reefs, belong to the managed share of the sky, which by the preferred rule leaves at least half of the dark sky wild; neither study yet places them.

## Water and phosphorus

Water is a matter of access and timing. Cool photosynthetic surfaces with a CO₂-concentrating mechanism need 25–92 kg of water per projected m² a year in the inherited humid and moderately humid cases, at 1.83 kg C/m² a year of net leaf assimilation, scaled here by area without a reduction for mutual shading; the concentrating trait's energy and its joint physiology with the carbon-budget organism are open.

| Projected area | Water-efficient throughput, Gt water/year, about km³/year | Phosphorus resupply before atmospheric fallout, tonnes/year | Ideal interception of the shared fallout analogue, tonnes P/year |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10% | 96–349 | 52,000–625,000 | 2,500–101,000 |
| 20% | 192–697 | 103,000–1,251,000 | 4,800–193,000 |

The water returns to the air through transpiration, so it circulates through the organisms. The warm-leaf C3 reference needs five to eighteen times as much as the efficient cases. Small reef modules can buffer only about 0.04 kg/m² of water swing within their inherited pressure-trim allowance, so they collect often, and survival between storms needs more than mean rain: sorbent surfaces, cloud collectors, routes through humid air and stable wet microhabitats are the existing study's mechanisms, still to be tested together.

Phosphorus must return upward after litter, emigrants and whole bodies fall. The table carries the existing forest-floor-like retention scenarios with their losses before rain and dust are credited, because the former sky-need figures had already subtracted a per-footprint atmospheric input; the shared fallout is then credited once. At 10–20% cover the sky's resupply, 0.05–1.25 Tg of phosphorus a year, is of the order of people's 0.18–1.44 Tg (register, 11 October). The phosphorus range keeps its inherited hazards of 0.001–0.005 a year and its trait and harvest choices, while the carbon uses 0.003. Fallout is an Earth analogue with unknown lunar rates, and the interception assumes perfect capture of the flux crossed, so it closes only some favourable corners; larger communities need biological transport by returning flyers and other migrants, managed return, or both, drawing on the Moon's existing reservoirs. Nitrogen fixation, its energy, and iron and molybdenum supply have no closed aerial account yet.

## Suspended microbes

Suspended phototrophic microbes could contribute, and the optical depth fixes how much: the calculation holds the column's optical depth fixed, so doubling its physical depth halves the concentration and leaves the total biomass unchanged. At optical depth 0.1, 1–10 µm dry particles with the reference 3.6-day division time and favourable conditions for 20% of the cycle make 0.008–0.080 Gt C a year of cells. These division and duty rates are scenario inputs; the inherited cloud-water incubation parameter describes a different organism. The 30-day removal case loses cells faster than they reproduce, and the 100-day case persists. An optimistic engineered one-day division with 50% favourable duty raises synthesis ninefold, to 0.072–0.720 Gt C a year, with its water, nutrients and physiology open. Optical depth 0.1 already removes 9.5% of the light column, so the microbes stay out of the aerophyte table: the two populations would share photons and nutrients, and the microbial screen has not yet counted the surface production it displaces.

Earth has viable airborne phototrophs, including cyanobacteria and green algae recovered from cloud water, and microbial activity that responds to cloud moisture ([Dillon et al.](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/AEM.01850-20); [Péguilhan et al.](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/22/1257/2025/)). They support exploring aerial communities, while the productivity of a stable lunar aeroplankton population is still to be measured.

## What comes next

The strongest present route to a productive deep sky is extensive aerophyte communities that keep their water and nutrients, differentiated by height and light demand. A contribution of a few tenths to a few Gt C a year fits the reference budgets. Turning it into a capacity needs a population and resource calculation along the design climate's routes, with shadows, dry gaps, upward nutrient return and the steering and switching rates above, and a GCM run of the rich sky for its own effect on the energy balance, clouds and rain.

Reproduce with `python -m research.studies.aerial_productivity.run`; check with `python -m pytest research/studies/aerial_productivity`. The tests cover photon conservation, carbon closure, the viability gates, the surface loss with and without the rain channel, the rain channel's reading of the GCM branch, the sky rules' scaling, the optical column mass and regeneration; the [check record](checks.json) holds the runs.
