# Design brief, 4 October 2026

The array will combine atmospheric protection, climate control, energy
collection and orbit control. A fleet of replaceable optical units and larger
service, industrial and inhabited nodes is the working architecture. The
holding study will determine how much mass can occupy the optical formation
and which nodes need separate trajectories.

## Optical and power functions

The surface climate case uses 1,235 W/m² after the titania–silica filter and
an additional wavelength-independent factor of 0.95, giving 1,173.25 W/m².
Across the Moon's projected disk, that extra dimming represents about 586 TW
of incident optical power; the filter and dimmer together reject about
1.78 PW relative to 1,361 W/m². These are optical energy budgets. Electrical
output requires a spectral device model, conversion efficiency, mass, lifetime
and a thermal budget. Average visible transmittance cannot determine the
bolometric attenuation or electrical yield of a solar cell.

Investigate three optical architectures:

1. A unified titania–silica protection filter and spectrally selective,
   semitransparent photovoltaic (PV) device. Place any UV-harvesting absorber
   sunward of the UV-stopping layer; assess contacts, passivation, radiation
   damage and the complete transmitted spectrum.
2. An ultralight protection filter and diffractive or reflective router that
   sends selected rejected sunlight to separate collectors. The collectors
   convert it to electricity or supply heat directly to industry.
3. A hybrid that powers tile controls locally and routes additional sunlight
   to regional collectors. Compare momentum, optical leakage, mass and heat
   with the two alternatives above.

Divide the aperture into a central climate window, an atmospheric-protection
annulus and, if worthwhile, separate steering surfaces. The central window
preserves the chosen climate spectrum. At the present 78,000-km stand-off its
radius is about 2,100 km, or 2,400 km with the twilight allowance. The protected
atmosphere can require 4–10 lunar radii; that larger aperture and the finite
angular size of the Sun determine where opaque hardware can sit without
shadowing the surface. Outer-ring visible photons could support much larger
collection than the central rejected-energy budget. Calculate the geometry
before allocating that power.

Treat 100 TW of usable electricity as a civilization design case. Track gross
generation, array holding and operation, transfer losses, industrial use and
habitat supply separately. A 100-TW generation target does not establish
100 TW available to inhabitants. Power itself also cannot specify population,
floor area, food production or supported industrial throughput.

The author's 5 October instruction makes the [collection inventory](collection.md)
part of subsequent trajectory and population modeling. Retain per-tile optical
power and energy through service and every off-service stage, including actual
orientation, finite-Sun illumination, body and mutual shadows, spectral mode
and collection hardware. Report collection potential alongside inventory and
propulsion, then electrical generation and net delivery when their device and
transfer assumptions are specified. Any change in optical momentum or hardware
mass must enter the coupled dynamics.

## Habitats and regional nodes

Investigate toroidal inhabited wheels surrounding an optical disk, with their
axes near the Sun–Moon direction. A counterrotating pair can reduce net angular
momentum; each rotating ring still requires its own load path and attitude
control. At a 5-km radius, 1 g requires about 0.42 revolutions per minute;
at 50 km, about 0.13. Floor area, pressure-vessel structure, radiation shielding,
water, soil, services, docking and rescue require independent budgets.

Keep habitat wheels available as a candidate. Compare a wheel attached to a
held tile, a shepherd station managing dynamically independent membranes, and
a habitat on a nearby economical trajectory. Attachments transmit both forces
and disturbances. A massive habitat cannot inherit the optical membrane's
area-to-mass ratio merely by sharing its disk.

Tiles supply their control, metrology and communications locally. Regional
nodes aggregate power and manufacturing. Habitat and industrial hubs receive
redirected sunlight, microwave/millimetre-wave power, laser power or short
physical links where geometry permits. Count efficiency and receiver heat
at every stage. Establish modular grids that can operate independently after
a link or node fails.

## Holding study

The September reference holds the screen about 78,000 km sunward of the Moon.
Its 50-g/m² base allocation, 4-lunar-radius protected region and assumed electric
propulsion closure give about 308 TW mean holding power. It samples a circular
lunar phase sweep. The new study will use measured ephemeris-based positions
and velocities, real orbital inclination and eccentricity, changing solar
range and finite-Sun eclipses. It will calculate vector forces, with the
optical aperture's shadow requirements enforced beside trajectory results.

Solar pressure pushes away from the Sun. Its magnitude being comparable to
required holding acceleration does not establish that it supplies the needed
direction. Reflective and diffractive optical control must obey photon momentum
conservation; passive sunlight cannot give an arbitrary sunward force. An
optimistic control envelope will serve as a lower bound on residual thrust,
with practical filter and sail cases compared separately.

Explore stand-off distance, controlled sunward motion, off-axis tile motion,
coasting during eclipses and optically steered remote-screen alternatives.
Screen trajectory families first, then propagate the candidates and test
coverage, control margins, sensitivity and failure drift. Periodic orbits near
Earth–Moon libration points require a separate coverage demonstration because
they do not automatically stay on the Sun–Moon line.

The 10-g/m² sail idea is an exploratory material target. The stored filter
already contains 10 µm of silica and 1 µm of titania, about 26 g/m² before
antireflection coatings and supports. A 10-g/m² replacement therefore requires
a different film, rather than removal of ballast from the present design.
Evaluate complete optical and propulsion mass together. Power equipment,
propellant buffers and habitat payload change the acceleration obtainable
from a given optical area.

## Closure and decisions after dynamics

Account for protection leakage, intercepted spectrum, generated electricity,
propellant, momentum sources and all heat-release locations. Check failures of
optics, pointing, power links, formation control and individual nodes. Reflected
or beamed energy requires Earth- and Moon-exclusion geometry; aperture gaps and
replacement delays must stay inside the atmospheric loss budget.

The author authorized investigation and this branch. Existing atmospheric,
climate, safety and lifetime requirements remain the governing constraints.
Optical material selection, inhabited capacity, final holding architecture and
an operational construction plan await the calculations. Numerical feasibility,
material measurements and a demonstrated integrated system are separate
stages of evidence.
