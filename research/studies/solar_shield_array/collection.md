# Solar collection inventory

The author requested collection accounting alongside the moving inventory on
5 October 2026. Every subsequent trajectory and population comparison must
carry an optical-energy ledger across service, handovers, departure, return,
coast and recovery. Include every instantiated member at simultaneous actual
dates. Preserve per-member power histories and integrated energy, then report
population totals, mean power and sampled extrema. Record service assignments
separately from illumination: a tile outside its assigned passage can still
receive sunlight.

## Required accounts

1. Incident optical power from projected clear area, finite-Sun directions and
   solar distance, with both illuminated faces distinguished.
2. Joint body and mutual visibility, without counting overlapping ray bundles
   repeatedly. A sum over family capacities does not supply this fleet ledger.
3. The intercepted spectrum, the portion retained for lunar climate and
   protection, and the portion allocated to reflection, absorption or collection.
   More collection from off-service tiles requires an explicit optical mode.
4. Electrical generation under stated spectral/angular conversion and thermal
   assumptions; power-routing efficiency, receiver limits and storage follow
   separately. Preserve unknown entries until a model supplies them.
5. Propulsion and attitude load, other operating loads, and net delivered power.
   The 100 TW ambition refers to delivery; the 238.35 TW held-screen figure is
   a propulsion comparison. Neither is a collection measurement.

Changing absorption, reflection direction, deployed collection area or hardware
mass changes the dynamics. A candidate receiving such a change needs coupled
repropagation and renewed coverage, separation and beam checks. Capturing its
existing outgoing light with an external collector still requires collector
placement, beam interception and power-transfer verification.

## Bounded first calculation

Starting remote head is `ce72224326fafb2e94033c31ad98e1e6c54a5965`; main remains
unchanged. Postprocess the saved 361-member, six-hour natural service and
six-hour prepared departure. Their actual terminal states match at the join.
Keep the 50 g/m² optical allocation, physical 10 km square and 9.89 km clear
side. No new trajectory or complete population is generated by this calculation.

The budget is ten minutes of numerical wall time, one process and one numerical
thread, 2 GiB address space and 128 MiB raw output. Evaluate every 60 s with
16 solar sources, then 32 independently rotated sources. Compare 120 s and
60 s integration using the first trace. Check the optical momentum against
the existing force model at hourly dates. Repository checks have a separate
five-minute allowance.

The first-intercept bolometric equivalent counts sunlight at the first clear
aperture along each sampled source ray. It uses the force model's centre
irradiance/incidence approximation and perspective rectangle shadow unions.
The filter's absorbed power requires its own spectral/device model. Multiplying
the geometric inventory by the force model's uniform 13.795% redirected
fraction gives the existing ideal spectral band's outgoing optical power.
This calculation gives no
collection credit to transmitted light or to the physical support edges.

The current coupled prefixes are uneclipsed. An additional whole-tile angular
envelope rejects possible Earth/Moon eclipses. A later return crossing an
eclipse needs a joint source/area visibility calculation for both force and
energy; multiplying independent body and array shadow fractions is inadequate.

Command stages label the summaries. They do not certify that every intercepted
ray contributes to the receiver's coverage, or extend the old service window.
No efficiency, outgoing-beam capture fraction or collector hardware is assumed.
Gross electrical capacity and delivered power therefore remain unfilled.

The whole interval contains **1.592 × 10¹⁷ J** in the allocated reflected
band. The incidence-only calculation, before mutual shadow exclusions, would
give 34.419 TW of mean bolometric-equivalent power. The first-intercept union
reduces that to 26.715 TW. These totals include all clear tile apertures,
including rays outside the moving 20 km receiver window. They do not estimate
a completed four-radius fleet or supply an inventory scaling factor.

The feathered coast receives a small finite-Sun flux on both faces. Increasing
the number of solar sources from 16 to 32, with an independent disk rotation,
changes its integrated energy by **4.46%**. Treat that low-power stage as a
rough estimate. The same refinement changes the whole twelve-hour integral
by **0.00430%**; the turn changes by **0.00494%**, and the service and preparation
changes are smaller. Coarsening the time integration from 60 to 120 s changes
the whole integral by **0.0000920%**, and the turn by **0.00342%**. These are
quadrature comparisons on the same saved positions, not a new dynamics replay
or a continuous energy/coverage certificate.

At thirteen hourly dates, converting the ledger's reflected-band power back
into photon momentum agrees with the existing 16-source sail acceleration
within **3.90 × 10⁻¹⁷ m/s²**. Analytic tests independently check the incidence
cosine, both faces, specular momentum, stacked-aperture double counting, the
finite-Sun feathering limit, the eclipse guard and time-weighted energy.

`results/collection.json` binds the sources, parent products and original
trajectory bytes. Its raw product retains per-tile W histories for both source
counts, allowing subsequent device assumptions to be evaluated without
reconstructing the optical inventory. Production output and delivery remain
unestimated. The sharper fall during feathering now enters the trade between
control demand, useful shielding and collection potential.
