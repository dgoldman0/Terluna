# Products and processing locations for the enclosed economy

The comparison supports an initial industrial strategy combining local production of heavy necessities, selective export of compact valuable materials, and a small group of processes whose performance benefits from microgravity. Earth remains an important place for final fabrication. Returning a refined metal or concentrate can earn more than returning a heavier finished product, especially when Earth factories supply inexpensive carrier materials, established purification, precision equipment and customer qualification.

This is a first product-selection and processing-location study for the planned economy served by **U, the established general economic unit**, potentially a currency or a basket of U-related currencies. Its boundary includes the enclosed lunar build and supporting near-Earth source, depot and industrial facilities. Small orbital factories belong to this phase; Open Moon, the habitat array and outer-system expansion remain outside it. The study retains the regular build to approximately 6.47 million residents in year 200 and the roughly fifteen years available before the planned 2041 start.

The calculations identify useful choices and the conditions that reverse them. A complete optimal percentage allocation still requires element-specific domestic requirements, customer orders, plant costs and qualification results. The [existing purchase account](../enclosed_returns/earth_purchases.md) gives the commercial targets those products would have to fulfill. Its generic $750/kg standard-product and $3,000/kg premium-product offers, in USD2026, remain conditional assumptions rather than validated prices for a specified mixture.

## Products, uses and preferred processing locations

The following allocation is a development recommendation. Local use includes lunar operations and the supporting space economy; an Earth export is a sale across that boundary. Location follows the whole process and its customer, so one product can pass through several places.

| Material or product family | Useful production | Preferred initial route and destination | What could change the choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lunar regolith and ordinary silicates | Shielding, road and landing-site material, foundations, ceramic blocks and thermal-store bulk | Excavate and fabricate near the lunar site of use | Local process performance and preservation constraints; Earth buyers do not justify shipping this bulk under the current freight assumption |
| Iron, nickel, cobalt and copper bearing material | Structural alloys, tanks, machine parts, tooling, conductors and wire and qualified additive-manufacturing feed | Separate near the source; refine and fabricate at the source, lunar industrial site or orbital customer according to transport and capacity | Alloy chemistry, missing alloying elements, fatigue/corrosion qualification and actual local demand; Earth's ordinary-metal prices leave little return-freight allowance |
| Lunar aluminium, silicon and titanium bearing minerals | Structural stock, radiator substrates, electrical conductors and selected corrosion-resistant components | Develop lunar reduction and finishing for local equipment and construction | Mixed-metal extraction is not production of separate high-purity metals; separation, electrodes and refractory lifetime may dominate cost |
| Glass, glass-ceramics and basalt-type fibres | Insulation, reinforcement, containers and optical substrates | Manufacture close to lunar use; finish specialised optics where equipment and quality favor it | Regolith variability, fibre strength, defects, annealing and chemical durability; ordinary glass has no general lunar export premium |
| Silicon and other photovoltaic constituents | Solar cells and large-area power equipment | Import initial qualified cells and active hardware; progressively localize substrates and components that pass a full cost and yield comparison | Solar-grade purification, dopants, deposition, encapsulation and reliability; abundant silicon alone does not establish a competitive solar-cell factory |
| Water, oxygen and hydrogen | Life support, process reagents and customer propellant | Recover source water and retain essential inventories; deliver compatible fuel to customers in space | Delivered route cost, storage losses, competing Earth supply and mission demand; lunar oxygen retains its role as a materials coproduct |
| Carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus and other nutrient/process constituents | Food-system inputs, atmosphere make-up, reagents and chemical feedstocks | Prioritize domestic cycles, recovery and future accessible stocks | Actual surplus after losses and reserves; these requirements cannot be inferred from gross source abundance |
| Platinum-group metals | Catalysts, electrodes, electronics, specialised glass equipment and compact refined exports | Capture and concentrate near extraction; compare an Earth refining contract with further off-Earth purification | Recovery, payable metal content, assay penalties, lot size and scarce refining capacity; fabricate most Earth-destination carrier-heavy products on Earth |
| Gold and silver | Contacts, coatings, conductors and compact metal exports | Separate selectively; retain useful domestic quantities and sell qualified surplus | Silver's lower value and low feed grade make cheap joint recovery more important; gold's value still requires recovery and buyers |
| Gallium, germanium, indium, selenium and tellurium | Compound-semiconductor inputs, infrared optics, detectors and selected photovoltaic materials | Recover suitable streams; initially sell qualified compact intermediates or metals to established fabricators | Target purity, volatile capture, reagents and small specialist markets; availability of one element does not supply all constituents of a device |
| Neodymium, praseodymium, dysprosium and terbium | Permanent magnets and magnetic components | Develop selective separation for local motors and equipment; consider Earth sales by specific oxide, metal or compound | The elements have different prices and demand. Returning an entire magnet also returns its iron, coating and binder mass |
| Other rare earths, scandium, zirconium and niobium | Ceramics, coatings, alloy additions and selected refractory/electronic components | Recover according to domestic use and named specialist buyers; keep residues identifiable | Light rare-earth abundance does not imply heavy-rare-earth value. Pure scandium small-lot prices do not apply to all scandium-bearing alloy |
| Specialty fluoride optical fibre | High-performance optical links, lasers and instruments | Draw qualifying fibres in microgravity; compare finishing, coating and integration on Earth | Flight quality is promising, but preforms, fluorine and other constituents need qualified supplies; orbital production is not equivalent to lunar-surface production |
| Selected semiconductor or semimetal crystals | Infrared/electro-optical substrates and specialised devices | Test orbital growth of valuable bulk crystals; retain Earth wafer processing, device fabrication and packaging where economical | Demonstrated improvement is process-specific. It does not establish that a complete leading-edge chip factory belongs in space |
| Large optical blanks and structures | Concentrators, instruments, trusses and other equipment used in space | Compare lunar fabrication and orbital assembly at the place of use | Launch-envelope savings, support loads, distortion, metrology and finishing; Earth-returning structures lose many of those advantages |
| Biomedical crystal work and other specialist research | Research products, process knowledge, licences and selected physical products | Use microgravity where it improves the research or product; manufacture on Earth when the knowledge transfers successfully | Some of the strongest earnings may be intellectual property and services, with little exported mass |
| Repair, remanufacture and recycling | Longer-lived machinery, recovered feedstocks and reusable components | Build capacity near the equipment and its waste streams | Inspection, remaining life, repair success and replacement lead time; these services can reduce the build's import bill without an Earth export |

ESA's lunar-simulant work demonstrates why the first metal rows require separation development: molten-salt electrolysis at about 950°C produces oxygen and a composition-dependent mixture of metals, while the usable alloys and downstream refining remain questions. It is evidence for a process family, not a quotation for pure lunar aluminium or titanium. [ESA laboratory account](https://www.esa.int/articles/esa-opens-oxygen-plant-making-air-out-of-moondust).

## Which fabrication has a demonstrated space advantage

Specialty optical fibre is a leading candidate for an orbital pilot. NASA reported approximately 12 km of ZBLAN fibre produced on the International Space Station in 2024. A 2025 NASA manufacturing presentation subsequently reported attenuation improvement of about fivefold on average and tenfold for the best fibre relative to its ground control. That presentation also reported four semimetal/semiconductor ingots with improved uniformity and yield. These are specific flight-program results; commercial throughput, independent replication, complete cost and future demand remain separate questions. [NASA production account](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/optical-fiber-production/), [NASA manufacturing presentation, slides 9–10](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/august-cop-in-space-manufacturing-2025-08-06.pdf).

The first feedstocks for such pilots should be the materials that achieve the required quality, including Earth-made preforms where appropriate. Local mineral availability becomes an advantage when a local purification route beats that imported input at equal quality. ZBLAN needs zirconium, barium, lanthanum, aluminium, sodium and fluorine in specified forms. The resource assays do not establish a complete qualified supply of that composition. For semiconductor products, crystal growth is only one part of a longer fabrication process.

Lunar partial gravity offers a different set of hypotheses. The existing [manufacturing study](../world_economy/manufacturing.md) and [slow-settling comparison](../world_economy/slow_settling.md) identify selected filled castings, spin-cast optical blanks and sintered components worth testing. Earth viscosity, cure-time, agitation and pressure controls compete with those benefits; an orbital centrifuge can also supply partial gravity. The lunar surface supplies a stationary site and nearby feedstock, but its dust, long night and transport costs enter the comparison. None of these hypotheses yet supports a general premium on lunar-made goods.

Ordinary spare-part production has a stronger rationale for serving equipment already in space than for displacing Earth factories. ESA demonstrated wire-fed laser metal printing aboard the station and returned samples for comparison with identically printed Earth controls. That supports developing repair and inventory flexibility, while leaving full industrial qualification open. [ESA sample evaluation](https://www.esa.int/articles/a-close-up-look-at-the-first-metal-part-made-in-space).

There is also a commercially useful route in which the valuable export is knowledge. NASA describes how Merck's orbital crystal research helped refine ground-based production of a pharmaceutical formulation. Research and licensing income therefore belong alongside physical manufacturing; the entire downstream Earth product value cannot be counted as U's export revenue. [NASA research-to-production example](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/space-station-research-informs-new-fda-approved-cancer-therapy/).

## Returning raw material, concentrate or refined product

“Raw material” needs a product specification. Unprocessed rock, a mineral concentrate, a mixed metal intermediate and certified pure metal carry very different amounts of payable material per kilogram shipped. Refining near the source removes unwanted freight; refining on Earth uses established plants and can achieve better recovery. The comparison must value both effects.

The inherited model charges **$150 USD2026/kg for Earth ground handling alone**. This study retains that floor, tests hypothetical total return charges of $500 and $1,500/kg, and separately examines $1–50/kg as possible future changes to the transport assumption. The cheaper cases are not adopted routes. All comparisons use dispatch mass; packaging and any uncounted freight must fit within the remaining budget.

The [USGS benchmark register](market_benchmarks.json) preserves the 2025 estimated observations, their product forms and lot sizes. For arithmetic against USD2026 costs, a disclosed 3% planning index aligns the price years; it is not an observed inflation statistic. A further factor of 1.557967 converts USD2026 amounts to the existing USD2041 accounting basis. Neither conversion sets a U exchange rate or predicts a future commodity price.

| Product benchmark | Approximate 2025 price per kg of the specified product | Initial implication at the inherited $150/kg handling floor |
|---|---:|---|
| Aluminium ingot | $4 | Ordinary Earth return cannot cover handling |
| Nickel metal | $15 | Ordinary Earth return cannot cover handling |
| Titanium sponge | $12 | Ordinary Earth return cannot cover handling |
| Cobalt cathode | $46 | Ordinary Earth return cannot cover handling |
| NdPr oxide | $69 | Ordinary Earth return cannot cover handling |
| Dysprosium oxide | $239 | Limited room remains before processing and the rest of transport |
| Gallium metal, average U.S. import value | $580 | Potential freight allowance; purity and market must match the quote |
| Scandium oxide, specified small lots | $640 | Potential specialist sale; lot prices and production scale matter |
| Terbium oxide | $1,010 | Potential specialist sale after separation and qualification |
| Silver bullion | $1,222 | Compact exports may clear freight; dilute-feed recovery still matters |
| Germanium metal | $4,100 | Compact exports merit process and buyer qualification |
| Platinum and palladium | $38,581 and $35,366 | Freight is a smaller share of value; recovery losses become costly |

These selected observations are from [USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026.pdf). They provide a scale comparison. A commodity price is not a price for a component, and a price for oxide cannot be multiplied directly by elemental assay mass.

At the same benchmark prices, nickel would need **less than about $15/kg of total return cost even if all other costs were zero**. NdPr oxide has an analogous threshold near $70/kg. Ordinary bulk exports can become candidates if a qualified return system changes those costs sufficiently. That possibility needs an explicit transport redesign rather than a larger export-volume assumption under the old floor.

For a concrete refining comparison, take one kilogram of concentrate containing 10% platinum. Both routes sell the same qualified metal, and the Earth refiner recovers 95%. With equal 95% off-Earth recovery, the maximum affordable off-Earth treatment cost is approximately **$136/kg of concentrate at $150/kg freight**, or **$453/kg at $500/kg freight**, before adding the Earth treatment cost that the route avoids. These amounts cover all incremental refining expenses, including equipment, energy, people and losses; they are thresholds, not quotations.

If off-Earth recovery falls to 90%, the first threshold becomes **negative $60/kg** and the second about **$258/kg**. At the lower freight assumption, the value of lost platinum exceeds the mass saving even with free off-Earth treatment and zero Earth treatment cost. This is a reason to obtain actual refining offers and recovery data before choosing a location.

The calculation uses

\[
C_{S,\max}=s g(r_S-r_E)P+F(1-g r_S)+sC_E,
\]

where *g* is target mass fraction in the common concentrate, *r* is refining recovery, *s* is return survival, *P* is price per kilogram of qualified target, *F* is return cost per kilogram dispatched, and *C* is treatment cost per kilogram of starting concentrate. The subscripts identify space and Earth. A profitable refining step also needs the preceding concentration stage and shared plant costs covered.

## When final fabrication belongs on Earth

Finished products often add cheap carrier material to an expensive active constituent. That can be useful for equipment consumed locally and costly for Earth exports. The following examples deliberately specify their compositions as assumptions; they isolate freight rather than claim complete manufacturing costs.

| Assumed finished product | Finished mass per kg of active constituent | Extra freight at $150/kg return | Extra freight at $500/kg return |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Catalyst containing 0.5% precious metal | 200 kg | $29,850 | $99,500 |
| Magnet containing 30% rare-earth active material | 3.33 kg | $350 | $1,167 |
| Structural alloy containing 0.2% scandium | 500 kg | $74,850 | $249,500 |

The extra charge is per kilogram of active constituent compared with returning that constituent alone. Any off-Earth fabrication advantage must cover it as well as differences in carrier supply, processing, yield, testing and inventory. Thus refined precious metal for an Earth catalyst maker can be preferable to a completed catalyst; a magnet or alloy for a lunar motor can favor local manufacture. Higher sales value per item does not by itself locate the factory.

Additional processing can also remove mass or create a product with a demonstrated performance advantage. Semiconductor crystals, specialty fibre and selected optical components warrant their own comparisons. The decision is whether the buyer's accepted premium and avoided Earth work exceed the extra lifecycle cost at the planned production rate, including rejected output and competing Earth improvements.

## Coproduct opportunity and economical scale

The resource inputs retain their conditional composition ranges. At the middle process assumptions, each **million tonnes of CI-like asteroid feed treated** yields about **1.72 tonnes of six platinum-group metals**, **0.080 tonnes of gold**, **0.110 tonnes of silver**, **5.08 tonnes of gallium**, **17.82 tonnes of germanium**, **5,948 tonnes of nickel** and **273 tonnes of cobalt**. The existing low/high grade and recovery scenarios remain in the saved result; they are not empirical probability bounds. The common recovery assumptions compare opportunities; they do not demonstrate that one plant can recover every listed element simultaneously.

Nickel and cobalt dominate these selected recovered quantities by mass. Their likely first contribution is to useful alloys within the off-Earth economy. The much smaller precious-metal and specialty streams are more plausible Earth exports. Both come from the same feed, so a joint circuit should be costed once and its products allocated among domestic use, actual exports and retained inventory. Nickel already embodied in a generic metal product is not a second new import saving.

Under full benchmark prices and hypothetical $500/kg total return cost, the selected priced streams could collectively support at most about **$0.160 USD2026 per kilogram of treated feed**, or **$0.250 USD2041/kg**, before paying incremental recovery, finishing and the other uncounted costs. At half benchmark prices that allowance falls to **$0.075 USD2026/kg**, or **$0.117 USD2041/kg**. These are optimistic joint cost ceilings conditional on selling all economically shippable, qualified output. Germanium contributes a large share; the calculation therefore particularly needs a real germanium market and process case. Osmium remains physically counted without an invented price.

Selected KREEP-like lunar feed follows a different process. Its middle result is approximately **480 tonnes of rare-earth elements plus yttrium per million tonnes treated**, including **82.8 tonnes of neodymium plus praseodymium**, together with **11.5 tonnes of scandium**. The rare-earth subtotal already includes NdPr. Separation must manage the associated thorium and uranium, preserve valuable residues and account for mineral decomposition and reagent recovery. The calculation does not assume all lunar excavations have that enriched composition, or value the entire rare-earth mixture at a magnet-element price.

Scale becomes material before treating every tonne. In the diversified 1% demand-growth case, treating just **1% of the existing year-200 asteroid feed** gives middle potential outputs of approximately **25 tonnes of platinum, 16 tonnes of palladium, 266 tonnes of gallium and 935 tonnes of germanium per year**, before domestic retention and sale qualification. After the assumed return loss, the first three correspond to about **15%, 8% and 29%** of their respective 2025 world primary-output benchmarks. The gallium comparison concerns low-purity primary output, not a matching high-purity product market; USGS does not provide a complete global germanium production total here.

Those ratios are scale diagnostics. Two centuries of new applications, substitution, recycling and competing supply can change demand substantially. They nevertheless show why sales volumes and prices must be modeled together: a plan cannot sell every recoverable specialty element indefinitely at an unchanged small-market quote. Neither today's market nor the surveyed supply is treated as a permanent ceiling.

## Local power and the cost of manufacturing

The [power comparison](inputs/basis.json) retains local generation as an active alternative. For continuous service at its reference equipment assumptions, fission's 100-year levelized supply cost is about **$0.749 USD2026/kWh with imported equipment** and **$0.342/kWh with 90% local equipment/fluid mass**. The corresponding beamed cases are about **$1.110 and $0.684/kWh**. These are calculations for 1 GW of mean service, not quotations for small standalone plants. Common local distribution, use-side heat rejection and critical reserves remain outside that incremental comparison.

At an illustrative 100 kWh per kilogram of product, imported-equipment fission therefore adds about **$65/kg** relative to a hypothetical Earth electricity price of $0.10/kWh; the largely local equipment case adds about **$24/kg**. The inputs also test 10 and 1,000 kWh/kg. This is only the energy difference. A process with inexpensive material can still become uneconomical through power, low utilization, precision equipment, maintenance or rejected batches.

Daylight production, direct solar heat, thermal storage and local fission should compete at each process's temperature and duty cycle. Vacuum removes the Open Moon atmospheric heat constraint, but reactors and machinery still need heat rejection. No new factory is assigned free power, a free thermal sink, a planetary shield benefit or a mandatory beaming network.

## Allocation and staged development

Allocation should maximize the consolidated economy's lifecycle surplus while satisfying essential supply, preservation, workforce, power, capital and transport constraints. For each next unit of recovered material, compare the net value of a domestic use, a quoted export and retention. Domestic value is the actual alternative purchase or production cost avoided, including delivery and reliability where quantified. Internal transfer prices do not create additional export receipts.

This establishes a practical sequence. First meet indispensable material flows and the required future stocks. Then use recovered bulk materials in the construction and equipment that replace costly imports. Next sell surplus specialty material under product-specific orders, choosing concentration, refining and fabrication locations by their marginal costs and recovery. Retain or recirculate the balance where immediate processing has a lower value than preserving the material for a later use. Contract quantities, plant utilization and learning alter that sequence over time, so a permanent rule such as exporting a fixed percentage of every element would be inappropriate.

| Period | Industrial emphasis | Evidence required before expanding the next activity |
|---|---|---|
| Preparation to 2041 | Survey and preserve candidate records; test representative feed circuits; qualify local-power and transport options; run Earth and orbital manufacturing controls | Mass and impurity balances, recovery and reagent cycles, duty-cycle tests, physical/data preservation plans and buyer specifications |
| First productive facilities through approximately year 32 | Local bulk material, water and repair; compact concentrate/refined-product sales; bounded orbital manufacturing pilots | Delivered cost, recurring orders, working capital and stable product quality; mature factories are not funded merely by theoretical inventory value |
| Years 32–100 | Expand profitable separation and remanufacture; localize suitable active components; scale demonstrated specialist products | Verified unit-cost improvement, sufficient market depth, affordable machinery and staffing, and resilience to a competing Earth supplier |
| Years 100–200 | Match the growing build and sustaining requirements; deepen recovery and select larger export plants where demand supports them | Product-specific price/volume paths, replacement and recycling flows, continuing source access and affordable power; today's specialist prices are not extrapolated unchanged by default |

An executable full allocation model should impose an element balance at each date: fresh recovered material plus recovered scrap equals domestic incorporation, exported content, processing losses and changes in traceable stocks. Product recipes connect those elements to jointly constrained plant capacity, electricity, heat rejection, workers and freight. Decreasing marginal sales prices or explicit customer tranches prevent unlimited sales at a small-lot price; fixed plant costs and commissioning delays prevent free instantaneous switching. The screening model supplies thresholds for that optimizer, while leaving the missing coefficients visible.

This also clarifies the financial comparison. The diversified 1% case reaches roughly 9 million tonnes of Earth goods and $13.5 trillion USD2041 of annual Earth-goods invoices in year 200. The specialty metal opportunities alone do not validate that average product value. The commercial programme needs named advanced products, accepted quality and sufficiently large orders, alongside its service exports and the savings from local industrial capability. New metal receipts must replace their share of generic exports or occupy additional funded demand; they cannot simply be added to the old totals.

## Preservation and closed material cycles

The existing source protocols apply before disturbance: scientific and heritage review, representative survey, native-condition and unopened samples, distributed physical archives, provenance, scientific review and continuing data access. Protected sources remain held. Recovery value does not authorize extraction, and this study assigns no new protected-area fraction or depletion quota.

Plant design should retain separate, assayed metal, sulfide, phosphate, silicate and volatile streams where practicable, so a future circuit can recover what today's process leaves behind. Reagents, water and manufacturing scrap need explicit return loops. Precious metals used in catalysts, magnets and electronics should have retrieval and disassembly paths; dissipative uses require their losses to be charged. Radioactive constituents from KREEP processing require separation and containment rather than a fictitious fuel or sales credit.

Future accessible stocks and essential nutrient inventories precede a surplus claim. Every Earth-return option also retains the standing cargo conditions: passive failure behavior, Earth-clear catcher geometry, remote braking, bounded packet energy, tracking/interception/corridors and verified capture before any planetary intercept. The inherited 1% commercial return-loss parameter is an accounting assumption, not certification of those conditions.

## Reproduction and next design decisions

The [runner](allocation.py), [inputs](allocation_inputs.json), [market benchmarks](market_benchmarks.json), [source register](allocation_sources.json) and [saved calculations](results/allocation.json) reproduce the 21 product screens, 54 concentrate-refining comparisons, fabrication mass penalties, power comparisons and eight annual recovery opportunities. The [verification record](checks.md) distinguishes numerical checks, source inspection and substantive reading.

The next commercial design should therefore be built from a small set of real production chains: local structural material and repair; a joint precious/specialty recovery circuit with Earth refining options; domestic magnet/alloy inputs; and orbital fibre or crystal pilots with measured Earth controls. Their product specifications and customer quantities can replace the broad commodity and premium offers as evidence matures. This approach can retain more industrial value within U's economic zone while allowing Earth factories to perform the stages they do more efficiently.
