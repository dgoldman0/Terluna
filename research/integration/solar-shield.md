## State and contribution

Its tracked diff is 500 files, 502,419 insertions and 21,843 deletions: 37 atmosphere files, 12 engineering files, 90 protection files, 354 research files, two shared-constant files and five visualization files. Much of the size is numerical JSON and retained failed-search evidence. Source worktree `/home/kir/Documents/Projects/terluna-solar-shield-habitat-array` was verified at the pinned tip and clean before and after this audit.

The branch progressed from a combined shield/power/habitat design brief, through held-screen dynamics and many rejected return/control families, to a lead ring-fleet architecture and substantially revised atmospheric-retention screen. It ends with the adopted cap and a plan for future `research/array-industry`. It is not an accepted, complete operating shield and is not a design for occupied toroidal habitats: inhabited capacity, complete services and safe transport remain unmodeled.

## What is locked in, what remains a result, and what was superseded

Author decisions are recorded in `research/decisions.md`, Solar shield and habitat array, not inferred from numerical success:

1. The 6 October relative-orbit plan replaces the expanded-cycle SQP search as the working direction; its diagnosis of energy-spread drift and orbital-plane folding is adopted. The old search and portable restart remain evidence, not the active next step.
2. Nested ring segments, each ring a natural orbit of shingled radial-facing tiles, are the local unit. This is distinct from the rejected forced Sun-following circular family.
3. The integrated comparison replaces the earlier 1.3 m/s/cycle and 23.835 TW decision gate. Every optical, safety, stability and net-positive-habitat-electricity gate must pass before resource ranking. The 23.835 TW and 100 TW figures remain comparison references, not universal ceilings.
4. S7 is adopted: everything protection releases or redirects must stay out of the escape region within an explicitly allocated share of R1. That share is still open. It is not automatically an adopted general rule for surface launches.
5. From 7 October, the ring fleet is the lead candidate, the held screen a documented fallback. The adopted form uses radial-facing centered filters, 25% reflectivity trim, a roughly 2×10^9 N m s store per tile, rings near 20,000 km, one sail loading, and annulus tiles near 13 g/m² carrying 0.1 µm titania on 4 µm silica plus coatings (10.2 g/m² film).
6. O1 uses heat traced along slant paths through the upper air instead of the former quarter-disk heat count. The Sun's spectrum is resolved by FISM2 bands. Cycle 21's 1979–80 year is the primary maximum because the post-1978 proxy basis is stronger; cycle 19's 1957–58 year and the old factor-2.5 convention are stress cases. FISM2 is a modeled irradiance record based on measurements/proxies, not direct spectrum measurement of every historical year.
7. The two-sided ring radius layout is accepted as the next architecture direction. A layout that keeps forced eccentricities without active equalization does not nest. Acceptance of the layout does not establish implemented control or a full-year held-stack replay.
8. On 8 October the branch was closed after the outer-ring question and capped with heat/computing/industry. `research/array-industry` is planned, not created or authorized to start by this cap. The trim/store hardware and power move into that plan. A joint integration is awaited.

Conditional findings should remain conditional:

- The latest integrated comparison explicitly says the lead ring fleet **passes no gate yet and fails none**. A lead architecture is not a fully validated design.
- Local coverage, point-sail orbital returns, annual single-ring flights, ideal-force corridor returns, fractional fleet-capacity counts and electrical supply are different claims. None substitutes for a safe recurring fleet with full solar-disk coverage.
- The 589-tile patch is four-orbit local evidence with explicit receiver rays through its interior; global edges, failures, handovers and Earth's shadow remain open.
- Heat-recovery/computing statements are a screen on assumed devices, literature-derived inputs and source-read scopes. They are not new device validation. This audit did not re-read external papers or verify October-2026 hardware news.
- Larger 500–1,000 km regional magnet circuits are a promising screened comparison, not a selected primary magnetic architecture. The old atmosphere input to that comparison is now stale.

Superseded or rejected results that must remain available, with their status clear:

## How this branch and the other lines inform one another

**Main → shield.** Main supplies the corrected climate design, middle-atmosphere base states, optical stack, loss framework, inherited protection/industrial accounts, constants/provenance rules and domain boundaries. This branch already descends from current main, so those tracked changes are inherited. Main's unresolved lower-air climate disagreement is not cured by the shield's upper-air calculations. Shield → main replaces some atmosphere/loss-response screens with a more specific heat geometry and infrared treatment, updates protection requirements, and establishes an industrial heat-placement argument. It does not justify rewriting every existing lower-climate or optical product without a documented changed input.

**Electricity (`24d236b`) ↔ shield.** Electricity supplies conductivity, deep-column cosmic-ray ionization, charging/lightning, aerosols, NOx and upper-atmosphere questions. Shield supplies the light/particle-filter requirements and a revised atmospheric-loss/magnetic rationale. The important joint questions are whether lightning-produced chemistry changes ozone/NO cooling or UV profiles; what conductivity/ionization boundary applies above the electricity solver; whether charge effects influence exposed tiles or high-altitude infrastructure; and how a real magnetic architecture affects particles. The electricity branch's conclusion that deep tropospheric ionization is dominated by energetic muons is not overturned simply by making magnets weaker or the UV filter stronger. Lightning and solar-wind escape concern different particles, energies and heights. No cross-branch result yet closes a coupled chemistry/electrodynamics/plasma model. Merge atmosphere README entries and condition registers by union, then mark future product interfaces; do not silently treat reduced upper-atmosphere loss as a revised lightning rate.

**Infrastructure (`625f60e`) ↔ shield.** Infrastructure's capped review explicitly reads this exact shield tip. It turns orbital heat, power, magnet routes/night refrigeration and transport into surface questions. It also identifies launch exhaust, where failed tiles go, the lit stack in the night sky and surface power delivery. Those are useful recommended interfaces, not adopted extensions of S7 or already solved array-industry requirements. Conversely, real surface settlement demand, reliable services, evacuation and transport needs can define array delivered power and maintenance access. The ring fleet's 100-TW scenario is not an established demand budget for the summit or the whole Moon. Its orbital-collection plans coexist with the infrastructure branch's conditional local fusion assumption because the three power ledgers remain separate. The infrastructure review's phrasing of regional 500–1,000 km circuits must not turn a promising, stale-input magnet comparison into a selected installation.

**Sea appearance (`46681f4`) ↔ shield.** Sea appearance supplies corrected Earth visible albedo (0.242 rather than 0.367), Earthlight, the photometric lighting calendar and water/sky appearance. These matter to the shield's still-open visible signatures, dimming/reflections, Earth shadow and the use of orbital lights near dark ecological settings. Shield supplies a physical film/aperture and the conditions under which the 5% climate dimmer stays an appropriate input. Sea appearance did not observe or compute a complete ring-fleet night sky; its deep-twilight findings cannot substitute for that. The solar UV/IR loss revision does not automatically change sea optics, but any changed visible spectral contract must flow through a new product to illumination. The albedo change is a shared-constant reconciliation item; the sea branch has no cap requirement from this audit.

## Merge hazards and concrete treatment

1. **Do not take whole hub files from one side.** The root audit’s pairwise merge-base analysis identifies these actual competing change sets: shield + infrastructure overlap in seven paths (`climate/gcm/README.md`, `engineering/README.md`, `habitation/README.md`, research README/decisions/status, `visualization/README.md`) with four textual conflicts (climate/gcm README, habitation README, research README and status); shield + electricity overlap and conflict in five paths (atmosphere README, climate/crm README, research README/decisions/status); shield + sea overlap in four paths (research README/decisions and both constants files) with no textual conflicts. These sets differ from direct tip differences against current main because the older branches have earlier merge bases. Build one current condition per topic and preserve each independent author decision and cap. Do not treat “no textual conflict” as science compatibility. The constants still need semantic reconciliation across the combined tree, even where inheritance permits a clean text merge.

2. **Shared values have real changes.** Shield changes `sun.gm_m3_s2` from `1.32712440018e20` to `1.3271244004127942e20` for DE440; this is not an additive constant. It adds proton mass `1.67262192595e-27`, a planetary-system GM map, Julian day 86400 and Julian year 365.25. `6.5e-05` versus `0.000065` is only serialization. `constants.py` redundantly defines `SUN_GM` twice. Union new constants with other branches' additions and sea's Earth-albedo correction by semantic key, select one alias each, then audit products by named values. Existing whole-file hash metadata must not be mechanically made “current”; old runs stay tied to their actual values. Named-value products using neither changed constant do not need numerical regeneration just because the file gained keys. Removing the duplicate alias or reformatting JSON still changes whole-file SHA hashes: inventory consumers that pin `constants.py`/`constants.json`, distinguish historical run records from freshness assertions, and do not call a metadata-only cleanup numerically consequential or rewrite an old run’s hash to match it. Prefer the smallest constants edit during integration; handle any compatibility repair explicitly.

3. **The capped status is internally stale in places.** `research/status.json` still contains “ring_fleet_17_to_22_million_tiles” and old unqualified maximum/runaway statements; it also says the column “could take” the traced shape, while newer entries say it now does. The top atmosphere README still lists explicit radiative cooling as outside the limiting model, although `thermal_column.py` now supports the added cooling and loss_response uses it. Several table labels still say “S7, proposed” after adoption. Reconcile these as an editorial integration task with explicit historical labels, not by deleting failed findings. The consolidated cap and actual products take precedence over older list fragments. Preserve distinctions between ~1,859 exact rings and 1,860 rounded prose.

4. **Known failing magnetic freshness test is real.** The current tip's `test_magnetic_architecture.py::test_parent_and_producer_content_is_pinned` fails on `research/studies/protection_architecture/results/design_point.json`. Its product expects SHA-256 `d144b2df95bbf8fa8eeb1ae4c2d37e590863ca657c78db33e56ba02a7bc27540`; current shield bytes hash to `6ee2c785ee27c056dd43f1b41eacb5c122c4fc49cd9efc6aa05a66079dbd2608`. The expected bytes are verifiably present in current main `2f2e0a1` and historical `479816d`/`c47c960`. It is scientifically misleading to overwrite the expected hash without recomputing. A clean final validation requires either genuine regeneration from restored inputs or explicit reclassification of this preserved product as historical with the exact historical input fixture and a test that verifies that fixture, while keeping the modern rerun visibly pending. The latter preserves the author's cap without pretending the old magnetic findings used the new atmosphere. This is a proposed integration resolution, not a change made here.
