# Aerial biosphere, ultraviolet and population follow-up

On 9 October 2026 the author asked for the cross-domain discussion to be evaluated, recorded on the appropriate
branches and summarized on main. The resulting work remains on `domain/ecology`, `domain/provisioning` and
`domain/resources`; this record connects their results and adopted directions. It does not merge their studies or
reopen the capped shield and infrastructure branches, whose earlier work is already integrated.

## Directions recorded across the domains

* Retain the existing short-wave shield and cold-exobase requirements. Adapt the biosphere to the available
  spectrum and use local UV for defined functions. Keep a narrow attenuated UV-B window open for evaluation;
  no extra transmission is selected.
* Retain the 20–30 billion Solar System working scale and evaluate location capacities before selecting a
  population distribution. Include lunar surface and aerial settlements explicitly. Earlier independent
  Earth-heavy or orbit-heavy allocations did not establish those capacities.
* Treat resident aeroplankton and large photosynthetic aerial organisms as complementary research directions,
  with consumers, decomposers, retained liquid water and nutrient return. A functioning phosphorus cycle must
  include harvest, surface exchange, aquatic transport and sediment recovery.
* Allow much floating habitation to roam, with collision and storm avoidance. Its operating account includes
  navigation, rendezvous, reserves and occasional repositioning. Continuous position-holding is a comparison
  case for settlements needing a fixed location.

All branches use the same [population scenario file](../../shared/scenarios/population.json), including the roaming
direction. The five cases are accounting comparisons; none is an adopted allocation or certified capacity. The
central comparison contains 10 billion on Earth, 5 billion on the lunar surface, 5 billion in lunar aerial
settlements, 3 billion in orbit and 2 billion elsewhere. The last category has no modeled capacity credited to it.

## What the evaluations establish

### A restricted ultraviolet window can matter at the surface

The ecology branch reweights the stored direct-plus-diffuse surface spectra for the same nearly ozone-free column.
At overhead Sun, the reference UV index is 0.109. Transmission of 1%, 5% or 10% at 295–315 nm gives 0.371, 1.418
or 2.727. All filtering outside each window is retained by construction. Strong scattering therefore does not
eliminate a candidate window's surface dose.

These are optical comparisons. The added downward UV at a ground surface is not global heating or exobase
heating. The old 310-nm cutoff calculation had 0.1% short-wave leakage and about 138 DU of ozone; its temperature
cannot be assigned to the new cases. New biological action spectra, vertical actinic exposure, live-atmosphere
chemistry, the actual ring/film response and solar-cycle non-LTE exobase feedback are required before adoption.
The current film suppresses most short UV-A too: 315–350 nm photon flux is about 0.194% of the Earth control.

### The aerial food web has explicit requirements

Growth in droplets and biological capture of airborne food have terrestrial evidence. A complete resident aerial
life cycle and buoyant macro-organisms remain to be established. With a 3.6-day doubling time, 30-day removal and
0.01/day additional mortality, microbial growth requires favourable conditions for 22.5% of the cycle. A long
particle settling time alone does not supply that wet, lit and nourished time.

Filter-feeding now pays assimilation, drag and metabolic costs. For a 1-m² collector at 10 m/s and 1 mg dry
food/m³, 50% capture yields gross food power of 90 W. At 60% assimilation and 25% propulsion efficiency, the net is only +5 W for an
optimistic effective drag coefficient of 0.02, and −187 W at 0.1. An attached collector can use a reaction against
wind; free drift in a uniform wind supplies no relative airflow. Large mobile filter feeders remain a conditional
hypothesis rather than a consequence of the gross-intake table.

The central floater-production comparison occupies projected area equal to 1% of lunar **surface** area and
assumes 1,000 g C/m²/year NPP. It gives 843 Mt/year of dry production and 0.379–3.79 Gt of standing dry biomass at
1–10 kg/m². At 5% harvest its energy could equal 184.6 million diets only if everything harvested were edible and
recovered; 50% edibility and 80% recovery give 73.9 million. Those are caloric scenarios, with no proven safe
harvest, complete nutrition or guaranteed supply credited to the population ledger.

Wet mass is included in floater buoyancy. Particle concentration is also tested against optical depth; a 1-km
layer with 0.1 mg/m³ in 1-µm compact particles has a geometric-extinction optical depth of 0.3. A productive sky
changes light available below, so aerial and surface production cannot independently spend the same sunlight.
No species count follows from habitat volume or primary-production arithmetic.

### The phosphorus cycle includes donors and permanent sinks

At Redfield C:P the central production case cycles 9.23 Mt P/year, holds 4.15–41.5 Mt in living stock and exports
0.461 Mt/year in the assumed harvest. The Earth-derived fallout analogue, with 10% captured, transfers only
2,655–106,211 tonnes/year to the sky. Its sources are existing lunar reservoirs, not new imports. That capture
fraction itself remains unestablished.

Without food return, perfect natural retention cannot sustain the example's 5% harvest. With 99.9% recycling of
unharvested P and 99% harvest return, the low upward-transfer case needs a further 10,726 tonnes/year returned.
The six-reservoir model conserves P across sky, land, water, sediment, human handling and unrecoverable material.
It shows why indefinitely feeding the sky from a finite donor pool fails and why even small persistent losses
matter over the billion-year horizon. Its rates and reservoir sizes are exposed scenarios, not a measured lunar
geochemical system.

Concentrated delivery can have modest gravitational work. Returning all harvest P to 10 km as 10%-P material
takes 3.37 MW at 70% lift efficiency, versus 33.7 GW in water containing about 10 mg P/L. Treatment, extraction,
concentration, vehicles, pipes and maintenance remain outside those lifting components.

### Aerial habitation is sized by mass, services and shared sky

At 10 km a reference sphere of radius 500 m provides about 575,000 tonnes of gross lift under the sky-ship
product's gas convention. Allocating half to a 50-tonne-per-person district supports about 5,750 residents.
Five billion aerial residents would require about 870,000 envelope equivalents, with summed projected area
1.80% of lunar surface area. Imposed three-diameter spacing in one square-grid layer would occupy 20.6%; this
is a geometric diagnostic, not an adopted separation or ecological occupancy limit.

The study distinguishes manufactured district and carrier mass, unused load reserve and lifting gas, including
neutral-buoyancy trim. Economy-wide material-stock proxies in resources overlap these inventories and must not be
added to them wholesale. Orbital shielding coefficients apply to orbital residents only.
In the central five-billion aerial case, district and carrier allowances total 425 Gt; the separate 75 Gt load
reserve is unallocated capacity. Full-capacity lifting-gas mixture is 48.27 Gt, including 37.30 Gt hydrogen; nominal
neutral trim uses 41.03 Gt mixture, including 31.71 Gt hydrogen. These quantities remain architectural requirements.

Continuous holding of the unstreamlined spherical case at 5 m/s relative air speed costs 34.5 TW for those five
billion residents, rising to 276 TW at 10 m/s. Those values support the author's roaming direction and delimit a
fixed-location comparison; they are not assigned to freely drifting communities. Avoidance and repositioning
still require control authority, reserve energy, reliable weather forecasts and collision-safe operating rules.
At an assumed 5 m/s maneuver speed and 1–10% control duty, the corresponding mean fleet input is 0.345–3.451 TW.
A six-hour maneuver requires about 238 MWh per reference envelope. Duty and event duration are sensitivity
parameters pending route and weather modeling; they are not a predicted operating schedule.

Domestic water recovery also matters. For five billion people aloft, 50–150 L/person/day and 90–99% reuse give
0.67–20.0 GW of makeup-water lifting to 10 km at 70% pump efficiency. Agricultural water remains a separate,
potentially larger requirement. Runoff per person is a stress indicator, not a hard population ceiling.

For the central ten-billion lunar population, the inherited crop screen requires 1.31–3.41 million km² and the
selected inherited energy case gives 27.78 TW of baseline local heat, corresponding to 0.241–0.386 percentage
points of extra dimming in its response screen. These calculations include both surface and aerial residents.
New manoeuvring loads, film infrared, local urban heat, crop water and atmospheric chemistry still need integration.
