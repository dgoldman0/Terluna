# What the literature says for provisioning the Open Moon

A cross-field reading of 9 October 2026, by topic, with what each finding means here. Full citations, DOIs and how far
each source was read are in [sources.json](sources.json). The search's complete register (104 sources) is kept with the
domain's run records on the research drive (`research/runs/provisioning/literature_register.md`). Numbers the search
derived from cited values are marked *derived*.

## Needs and their energy

- **Decent living** takes about 15.3 GJ of final energy per person a year, with a range of 13–18.4 (Millward-Hopkins
  et al. 2020). Nutrition and mobility are the largest parts at about 3 GJ each, and mobility is 51–60% of
  decent-living energy in India, Brazil and South Africa (Rao et al. 2019).
- **What lowers the energy.** Needs are met at lower energy where provisioning favours equality, public services,
  democratic quality and electricity access (Vogel et al. 2021). Satisfaction stops rising with energy near
  60 GJ per person.
- **Earlier frameworks.** Decent living standards are defined materially by Rao & Min (2018), and the gaps and energy
  needs worldwide are mapped by Kikstra et al. (2021). No country yet meets basic needs within planetary boundaries
  (O'Neill et al. 2018; Fanning et al. 2022).
- **Working time.** A decent-living basket takes an 18-hour paid working week per person, a "good life" 46 hours, and
  the UK's current footprint 65 (McElroy & O'Neill 2025).
- **Material stocks.** Decent living needs about 42 t of materials in use per person, against 335 t in developed
  countries (Vélez-Henao & Pauliuk 2023).

## Cities' metabolism

- **Megacities.** New York's energy throughput is about 94 GW, so the 200 GW metropolis is about twice it. At
  megacity intensities 200 GW would serve 50–110 million people (*derived* from Kennedy et al. 2015).
- **Material stocks.** In-use stocks grew 23-fold over the twentieth century, and half of each year's extraction builds
  or renews them (Krausmann et al. 2017). Sociometabolic research frames the flows (Haberl et al. 2019).

## Energy through a 354-hour night

- **Storage classes.** Grid storage studies stop at 160 hours or more (US DOE 2023). Multi-day storage returns about
  45% today and hydrogen about 40% (US DOE 2023; Headley & Schoenung 2020). Storage must reach about $20/kWh of
  energy capacity to matter, with $3/kWh as the target at 100 hours (Sepulveda et al. 2021; Albertus et al. 2020).
- **Pumped hydro.** Closed-loop pumped hydro sites abound on Earth (Stocks et al. 2021). On the Moon a cubic metre
  through a kilometre stores a sixth of Earth's energy, so a TWh at 1 km of head needs 2.2 km³ (*derived*).
- **The airless Moon.** Its night power has been reactant mass in regenerative fuel cells (Guzik et al. 2017) or
  fission at about 40 kWe for 10 t (Barth et al. 2022). The Open Moon's air cools and supplies oxygen to both.
- **Transmission.** High-voltage direct current loses 2–3.5% per 1,000 km plus about 1% per converter (US EIA 2018).
  Changji–Guquan carries 12 GW over more than 3,000 km (Hitachi Energy).
- **Space solar power** costs $0.61–1.59 per kWh, 12–80 times terrestrial renewables, with launch 71–77% of the cost
  (Rodgers et al. 2024, NASA OTPS). ESA's study put a 1.44 GW system at €106–206 per MWh (Frazer-Nash 2022). On the
  Moon its competitor is two weeks of storage.
- **Beaming limits.** The 1978 reference system kept beams to 230 W/m² at the centre and 10 W/m² at the edge (US DOE &
  NASA 1978). The public exposure limit is 10 W/m² (ICNIRP 2020). Receivers for 200 GW would cover 4,000–7,000 km²
  (*derived*).

## Waste heat

- **Earth's human heat** averages 0.028 W/m² (Flanner 2009), close to the Open Moon's 0.026 W/m² per TW. Energy growth
  heats a world in the long run (Chaisson 2008).
- **Concentrated release.** Heat released in cities shifts regional circulation by up to about 1 K (Zhang et al. 2013).
  The hottest city cells reach 100–577 W/m² (Allen et al. 2011).

## Food

- **Chamber yields.** NASA's crop chambers gave edible yields of 18.4 g per m² a day for potato and 11.3 for wheat
  (Wheeler et al. 2008; Wheeler 2017). Record wheat reached 60 g of grain per m² a day under 16–20-hour photoperiods
  (Bugbee & Salisbury 1988).
- **Light and dark.** Continuous light injures tomato unless a tolerance locus is present, and that locus adds up to
  20% yield (Vélez-Ramírez et al. 2014). Darkness starts carbon starvation in leaves within hours and senescence within
  days (Kacprzak & Van Aken 2022).
- **Diet and land.** Animal products use 83% of farmland for 18% of calories, and a plant diet cuts food's land by 76%
  (Poore & Nemecek 2018). Crops feed about 6 people per hectare after feed, biofuels and other uses (Cassidy et al.
  2013).
- **Closed systems.** Biosphere 2 grew 81% of eight people's diet on 0.2 ha, with crews working 66-hour weeks (Allen &
  Nelson 1999). Lunar Palace 1 regenerated 55% of its food (Fu et al. 2016).
- **Microbial protein** grown on solar electricity yields over ten times the protein per area of any crop (Leger et
  al. 2021). Plant factories take 247 kWh of electricity per kg of lettuce dry matter (Graamans et al. 2018).

## Making things

- **Embodied energy.** Steel takes 24.4 MJ/kg, cement 4.6 and concrete 0.95; a dwelling averages 5,340 MJ/m² (Hammond
  & Jones 2008). Five materials make 55% of industrial CO2 (Allwood & Cullen 2012).
- **Self-replicating industry.** A lunar industry could grow from 12–41 t landed to 156–40,000 t of assets in about 20
  years (Metzger et al. 2013). Self-replicating factories were studied in NASA CP-2255 (Freitas & Gilbreath 1982).
- **Automation.** Factories average 162 robots per 10,000 workers (IFR 2024).

## Computing

- **Data centres** used 415 TWh in 2024, about 47 GW, with 945 TWh projected for 2030 (IEA 2025). Their efficiency has
  risen faster than demand (Masanet et al. 2020).
- **Orbital data centres.** ASCEND (Thales Alenia Space 2024) and Project Suncatcher (Google 2025) study them.
  Starcloud's radiators reject 838 W/m² at 20 °C (Feilden et al. 2024), consistent with the array study's 830 km² of
  radiator per TW near 330 K.
- **The floor.** Landauer's limit sets the energy floor of irreversible computing (Landauer 1961).

## Transport

- **Freight energy per tonne-km:** trucks 2.19 MJ, rail 0.24, ships 0.14, freighter aircraft 16.4 (Krammer & Schäfer
  2025). Today's airships emit 80–90% less than jets (Prentice & Knotts 2016).
- **Scaling to the Moon.** Flight power scales with gravity and air density (Lorenz 2001). At a sixth of gravity wings
  and rotors become about six times cheaper per tonne-km, while an airship's lift per cubic metre in kilograms is
  Earth's.
- **Launch.** The 1970s lunar export plans assume vacuum at the surface (NASA SP-413; Heppenheimer 1977; O'Neill
  1974). Launching through a thick atmosphere has been studied only as balloon-borne ascent at Venus (Shibata et al.
  2017).
- **Elevators.** A lunar space elevator of today's materials lifts about 584 t a year (Pearson et al. 2005).
- **Launch exhaust.** Rocket soot warms about 500 times as much per kilogram as surface sources (Ryan et al. 2022), and
  ten gigagrams a year warms the stratosphere by up to 1.5 K (Maloney et al. 2022).

## Maintenance and circularity

- **Upkeep.** Routine maintenance runs 2–4% of replacement value a year (NRC 1990). Maintenance occupations are 3.9%
  of US employment (US BLS 2025).
- **Remanufacturing** clearly saved energy in 8 of 25 cases (Gutowski et al. 2011). Lifetime extension is the core of
  the circular economy (Stahel 2016).

## Life support and water

- **Life support.** Each person uses 0.82 kg of oxygen, 1.5 kg of food (12.6 MJ) and 2.5 kg of drinking water a day
  (Anderson et al. 2018, NASA BVAD). The ISS recovers 98% of its water (NASA 2023).
- **Minimum population.** The smallest self-sufficient settlement is about 110 people (Salotti 2020).
- **Water and energy.** Water supply is 4% of the world's electricity, and wastewater holds 5–10 times the energy its
  treatment needs (IEA 2016). Basic water needs are 50 L per person a day (Gleick 1996).
- **Nutrient recovery.** Nitrogen from urine takes 1.3–13 kWh per kg (Larsen et al. 2021), against about 9.4 for the
  best ammonia plants (IEA 2021). Urine and faeces could supply 22% of the world's phosphorus demand (Mihelcic et al.
  2011).

## Gaps the search found

The search found none of these:
- an energy-intensity dataset for modern freight airships;
- experiments on crops under 354-hour light and dark;
- maintenance's share of national energy;
- launch through a massive atmosphere at low gravity;
- a power-beaming limit worked out for the Moon.
