# Ecology's open questions

In order of what each would settle for the whole Open Moon. Each names what decides it and how it could be answered
on this laptop. The findings behind them are in the [README](README.md).

1. **What the living air holds.** Under the shield the bare column has no OH. A biosphere and cities emit:
   - methane, N2O, NO and ammonia;
   - ethylene, isoprene and dimethyl sulfide;
   - carbon monoxide and hydrogen.

   Their levels set:
   - whether cereals work, through ethylene;
   - how much methane warms the Moon;
   - whether the air smells of the sea;
   - whether biogenic NOx brings some ozone and OH back.

   **How:** the middle-atmosphere photochemistry (plan item 3) with deposition velocities for soils and waters, NO
   and VOC chemistry, emissions by biome and city, and the lunar night. That is about an hour of runs once the
   chemistry is added; the joint synthesis's air-chemistry runner restarts and runs cases in parallel.
2. **What lives aloft.** The author asked for the aerial biomes to come almost first. The sky holds 9.2×10⁸ km³ of
   air above freezing, wet and lightly ionized, where lift allows flyers six times Earth's in every length, while its
   production can come from resident aeroplankton, photosynthetic floaters and exchanges with the surface.
   The [new requirements screen](../../research/studies/aerial_ecology/README.md) makes each budget explicit.
   - **What decides it:** water from cloud and humid air; phosphorus kept within floating and flying organisms;
     floaters' gas; life cycles timed to the lunar day; how many aerial animals the land's insects, pollen and fruit
     can feed; the night's light aloft; and how people's traffic and light share the volume.
   - **How:** a food-web budget of the sky by height band from the climate runs, the land's export of pollen, spores,
     insects and migrants, and the illumination solver's twilit sky aloft.
3. **How much the reduced gases warm the Moon.** The screen puts methane near 20 ppm at central values, and N2O builds
   0.3–0.4 ppb a year. Their effect in a column 7.6 times Earth's has not been computed.

   **How:** the line-by-line radiative–convective column with CH4 and N2O added, a sweep of a few hours.
4. **The ecology's case in the ultraviolet decision (O3).** Keep the present short-wave shield, with adaptation to
   its spectrum and local UV provision as the working direction. A narrow attenuated UV-B window remains a
   candidate. The [fixed-column evaluation](../../research/studies/uv_biology/README.md) gives overhead UVI
   0.371–2.727 for 1–10% transmission at 295–315 nm; these are conditional optical results.

   **How:** biological action spectra and vertical actinic exposure, live-atmosphere ozone/OH/trace-gas chemistry,
   current ring leakage and film heating, and non-LTE exobase feedback through the solar cycle. The historical
   310-nm case used different leakage and ozone. No relaxation of EUV/far-UV rejection or selected window follows.
5. **The night's requirement, zone by zone.** Nocturnal thresholds of 0.01–0.03 lux meet nearside nights of
   2.3–2.9 lux, far-side midnights of 0.001 lux, poles never darker than twilight, and the metropolis's own skyglow on
   the far side. The value is owed to the fleet decision.

   **How:** a literature-based table of thresholds by function (melatonin, foraging, navigation, spawning), the light
   calendar by zone, and the city's skyglow in this scattering air from the illumination solver.
6. **Water in the plants.** The canopy and plant models have no stomata or water limit. At an Earth-like water-use
   efficiency, rain would cap productivity at about 30–40% of the model at 30–45° and 6–8% at 45–60° (digest
   arithmetic).

   **How:** add stomatal conductance and a soil water bucket to the plant model, forced by the GCM's rain and the
   CM1–GCM range. Hours.
7. **Nitrogen and its metals.**
   - How much fixation the biomes need and can afford.
   - Which micronutrients must be imported. Molybdenum and vanadium are the first; boron, cobalt, selenium and
     iodine need checking element by element against lunar rock.
   - How denitrification is made to end in N2.

   **How:** a nutrient budget per biome, with the resources domain's element inventory.
8. **How soils are built.** Organic loading, fines, pioneer crusts and the order of introduction, with dry land
   losing litter carbon to light.

   **How:** a soil-development box model (carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, base cations, clay), calibrated on basalt
   chronosequences and enhanced-weathering trials, coupled to the CO2 study's planned box model.
9. **A biome map.** Communities by rain, light, lakes, tides and soils on the 28% atlas, and a land cover for the
   climate models' placeholders. The author asked for a mix of climates and biomes. The models give a warm, even land,
   so the map will show how much of the mix water and light provide. It will also show whether temperature needs a
   lever, such as window films that dim some latitudes more than others.

   **How:** zoning on committed products; the lever is a question for the shield and climate work.
10. **The waters as living systems.**
   - The seas' chemistry through time and the delivered water's salts, with the resources domain.
   - Stratification and oxygen in deep crater lakes.
   - Monthly plankton cycles, intertidal monthly wetlands, and methane from lakes by depth.

   **How:** a one-dimensional lake model through the lunar month, with the seas' composition as scenarios.
11. **What may live wild.** Pollen and spores spread Moon-wide, and no containment is demonstrated for wild
    engineered organisms. This is an ethical and governance decision for the author and the human paper. The
    ecology supplies dispersal kernels at lunar gravity and the risks.
12. **Fire.** No one has measured natural fuels at 17.5% oxygen, 1.2 atm and 0.16 g. Whether the grassy biomes need
    managed fire or grazing follows from it. **How:** a literature and modelling screen of partial-gravity
    flammability, and the open question of measurement.
13. **Bodies at 0.16 g.**
    - Plant root regulation.
    - Tree height limits, which can be computed from hydraulics and buckling.
    - Animal calcium and vitamin D, vision from 0.001 to 3 lux, and development.
14. **Refreshing what has gone stale.**
    - Rerun the ecology register on the corrected products.
    - Give the living waters developed fines.
    - Bring the forest studies' leaf area to the canopy model's range.
    - Take plant twilight from the solved sky.

    These are changes to main's studies, for the author to order.
