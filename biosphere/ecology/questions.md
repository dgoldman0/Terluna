# Ecology's open questions

In order of what each would settle for the whole Open Moon. Each names what decides it and how it could be answered
on this laptop. The findings behind them are in the [README](README.md).

1. **What the living air holds.** Under the shield the bare column has no OH. A biosphere and cities emit:
   - methane, N2O, NO and ammonia;
   - ethylene, isoprene and dimethyl sulfide;
   - carbon monoxide and hydrogen.

   Their levels set:
   - whether cereals work, through ethylene;
   - how much methane warms the Moon;
   - whether the air smells of the sea;
   - whether biogenic NOx brings some ozone and OH back.

   **How:** the middle-atmosphere photochemistry (plan item 3) with deposition velocities for soils and waters, NO
   and VOC chemistry, emissions by biome and city, and the lunar night. That is about an hour of runs once the
   chemistry is added; the joint synthesis's air-chemistry runner restarts and runs cases in parallel.
2. **What lives aloft.** The author asked for the aerial biomes to come almost first. The sky holds 9.2×10⁸ km³ of
   air above freezing, wet and lightly ionized, where lift allows flyers six times Earth's in every length, while its
   production can come from resident aeroplankton, photosynthetic aerophytes and exchanges with the surface.
   The [new requirements screen](../../research/studies/aerial_ecology/README.md) makes each budget explicit.
   - **What decides it:** water from cloud and humid air; phosphorus kept within floating and flying organisms;
     aerophytes' gas; life cycles timed to the lunar day; how many aerial animals the land's insects, pollen and fruit
     can feed; the night's light aloft; and how people's traffic and light share the volume.
   - **How:** a food-web budget of the sky by height band from the climate runs, the land's export of pollen, spores,
     insects and migrants, and the illumination solver's twilit sky aloft.
3. **How much the reduced gases warm the Moon.** Answered on 12 October 2026, the next work the author set that day:
   the line-by-line column with HITRAN's CH4 and N2O lines added, about 23 minutes on six workers
   ([reduced gases](../../atmosphere/radiative_convective/README.md)). The planned climate carries neither gas, so
   whatever the Moon keeps adds to it. Earth's present levels force 4.7 W/m² all-sky on the Moon, 2.9–4.7 K; the
   screen's central steady state (methane 21.6–29 ppm, N2O 0.55 ppm) 7.9–8.3 W/m², 4.9–8.3 K; the highest (230 and
   4.2 ppm) 14 W/m². N2O forces more per ppm and keeps building.
   The author chose on 12 October to hold both gases in check by biological means, coupled with advanced technologies
   that make up the difference, with no ultraviolet window in the shield ([machines.md](machines.md)). Their levels
   balance the dimming that answers the warming against what holding them takes. On 13 October the author chose the
   middle of three pairs weighed, 1.9 ppm of methane (Earth's present) and 0.1 ppm of N2O (a third of Earth's): 3.58
   W/m² all-sky, answered by 1.69% added dimming over the sunlight the Moon absorbs. With biology at the sources
   cutting 90% of the methane, contactors in open air pass 0.03–0.09% of the air a year once methane reaches 1.9 ppm;
   N2O's catalytic decomposition takes 0.29–4.4 TW once it reaches 0.1 ppm, 365–5,610 years from none
   ([domain synthesis](../../research/studies/domain_synthesis/README.md#where-they-are-held)).
4. **The ecology's case in the ultraviolet decision (O3).** Keep the present short-wave shield, with adaptation to
   its spectrum and local UV provision as the working direction. A narrow attenuated UV-B window remains a
   candidate. The [fixed-column evaluation](../../research/studies/uv_biology/README.md) gives overhead UVI
   0.371–2.727 for 1–10% transmission at 295–315 nm; these are conditional optical results.

   **How:** biological action spectra and vertical actinic exposure, live-atmosphere ozone/OH/trace-gas chemistry, the
   current ring's UV transmission and film heating, and non-LTE exobase feedback through the solar cycle. The historical
   310-nm case used a different transmission and ozone. No relaxation of EUV/far-UV rejection or selected window
   follows.
5. **The night's requirement, zone by zone.** Nocturnal thresholds of 0.01–0.03 lux meet nearside nights of
   2.3–2.9 lux, far-side midnights of 0.001 lux, poles never darker than twilight, and the metropolis's own skyglow on
   the far side. The value is owed to the fleet decision.

   **How:** a literature-based table of thresholds by function (melatonin, foraging, navigation, spawning), the light
   calendar by zone, and the city's skyglow in this scattering air from the illumination solver.
6. **Water in the plants.** The canopy and plant models have no stomata or water limit. At an Earth-like water-use
   efficiency, rain would cap productivity at about 30–40% of the model at 30–45° and 6–8% at 45–60° (digest
   arithmetic).

   **How:** add stomatal conductance and a soil water bucket to the plant model, forced by the GCM's rain and the
   CM1–GCM range. Hours. By the fresh seas the water table is a second source: at lunar gravity soil lifts water to
   roots from about four times Earth's height above it, so shore belts draw on the sea from 15–25 m up (15 October,
   [Fresh shores and soda lakes](README.md#fresh-shores-and-soda-lakes-15-october-2026)).
7. **Nitrogen and its metals.**
   - How much fixation the biomes need and can afford.
   - Which micronutrients must be imported. Molybdenum and vanadium are the first; boron, cobalt, selenium and
     iodine need checking element by element against lunar rock.
   - How denitrification is made to end in N2.

   **How:** a nutrient budget per biome, with the resources domain's element inventory.
8. **How soils are built.** Organic loading, fines, pioneer crusts and the order of introduction, with dry land
   losing litter carbon to light.

   **How:** a soil-development box model (carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, base cations, clay), calibrated on basalt
   chronosequences and enhanced-weathering trials, coupled to the CO2 study's planned box model.
9. **A biome map.** Communities by rain, light, lakes, tides and soils on the 28% atlas, and a land cover for the
   climate models' placeholders. The author asked for a mix of climates and biomes. The models give a warm, even land,
   so the map will show how much of the mix water and light provide. It will also show whether temperature needs a
   lever, such as window films that dim some latitudes more than others.

   **How:** zoning on committed products; the lever is a question for the shield and climate work. The map also
   carries the shore belts fed by the fresh seas (resources' question 16, the shores' water table) and the soda lakes
   of the dry belts' closed basins: how many there are and where.
10. **The waters as living systems.**
   - The seas' chemistry through time and the delivered water's salts, with the resources domain. Done there on 13
     October ([sea chemistry](../../research/studies/sea_chemistry/README.md)): every lasting state is a held one, and
     the author holds the seas fresh, hard fresh water near 0.14 g/kg and pH 8.3, on the condition that they are rich in
     life (resources' register, "The seas through time"). Their communities are ecology's to design for high
     biodiversity, as Earth's old, clear fresh lakes hold hundreds of endemic species through variety of habitat: rocky
     shores, shallows, deltas, reefs and deeps.
   - The small waters' builders, from resources' terrain study of 13 October ([terrain through
     time](../../research/studies/terrain_through_time/README.md)). Left alone the small lakes and crater pools fill
     within centuries to tens of thousands of years. Dam builders keep ponds on the small streams below the 1.9 km grid,
     as beaver once held 13% of a boreal landscape in ponds. Travertine builders (mosses, microbes and stoneworts) build
     barriers that rise faster than lakes fill, on most rivers while the young soils weather fast. Once the soils age
     the rivers carry about half the load natural tufa needs and sit at 0.76 times calcite saturation, so lasting
     barriers need builders that calcify from undersaturated water, as freshwater shellfish do, or springs carrying
     twice the rivers' load.
   - Stratification and oxygen in deep crater lakes, answered on 17 October and rerun on 18 October on the corrected
     model with the deep mixing driven by the wind, as the author chose that day ([the waters'
     oxygen](../../research/studies/lake_oxygen/README.md)). The held fresh seas hold 7.06, 7.78 and 8.43 mg/L at
     saturation in the hot world, the resort and the lock. With the share of the wind's energy that Earth's lakes pass
     below the mixed layer (0.3%, 0.2–0.4%) and Osborn's rule, 352 m crater lakes turn over in 55–74 of 120 lunar
     cycles in the lock and the resort and 29–30 in the hot world; their oligotrophic and mesotrophic deep water stays
     oxic in the cooler targets, eutrophic water keeps 1.9–4.2 mg/L there and runs anoxic half the time in the hot
     world, and new reservoirs run low. By Shih and Bouffard's energetic regime, the other end of the mixing's form,
     the 0–15° lake turns over 8–12 times in ten years, oligotrophic deep water keeps 3.4–4.8 mg/L in the cooler
     targets and the lock's mesotrophic water 2.9 mg/L, and richer water runs low or anoxic. The very deep lakes, half
     the lake area, turn over about once a year with their richer depths anoxic (Osborn), or once in decades and anoxic
     throughout (energetic regime). Productive 3 m shallows stay above 2 mg/L on almost every night under the wind's
     share; at its bracket's fixed dissipation they fall to 1.7–4.0 mg/L, below 2 mg/L on up to 70% of nights in the
     hot world. A basin-scale model of one crater lake would settle the share and the form.
   - What the anoxic depths do, answered on 17 October and rerun on 18 October ([lake
     anoxia](../../research/studies/lake_anoxia/README.md)). By Osborn's rule the lakes would send 0.29–0.37 Tg of
     methane a year oligotrophic, 2.1–3.9 mesotrophic, 4.2–23 eutrophic and 7.9–52 from new reservoirs, mostly the
     floors' own bubbles where the overturns keep the depths oxic, against the 39 Tg the domain synthesis's ledger
     carries. By the energetic regime the hot world's eutrophic lakes send two to four times as much (20–37 Tg with
     the 0–15° lake standing for all), while oligotrophic and mesotrophic lakes in the cooler targets change little.
     Oligotrophic lakes hold the air at 0.91–1.7 ppm, under the held 1.87. No lake in the targets nears a limnic
     eruption; an isolated hypolimnion reaches its bubble point 3.5–6.7 times sooner than on Earth, so meromictic and
     spring-fed lakes and deep new reservoirs are the hazard. Anoxic floors can at most cancel the phosphorus
     retention, 10–27 mg/m³ at steady state. Oxygenating the depths takes 3.5–15 kW per km² at the demand, up to 59
     with the devices' stirring. The giants' own methane is the methane tracer's to settle.
   - The lakes by kind at the resort's 301 K, answered on 18 October ([the lakes by
     kind](../../research/studies/lake_kinds/README.md)). By Osborn's rule the nights reach the floors of lakes to
     168–239 m on half of them, a depth interpolated between the ladder's 100 m and 352 m rungs. Four fifths of the
     27,865 lakes, a tenth of the area (226,500–629,700 km² across that bracket), are self-ventilated. The deep lakes
     to 1,000 m, a fifth of the area, turn over in a third to two thirds of the lunar cycles with oxic floors in
     nutrient-poor water. The giants, seven tenths of the area, turn over about once a year with their mesotrophic and
     richer depths anoxic, and the deepest go anoxic even in nutrient-poor water. Chemoclines of 0.1–1 kg/m³ at 20 m
     do not hold under the wind's mixing. Needs: 6,199 profiling moorings for the deep lakes and giants, nutrient
     limits first, and oxygenation where deep-water life is chosen; degassing does not pay. By the energetic regime
     the 352 m lake at 301 K turns over a ninth to a quarter as often and its mesotrophic and richer depths go anoxic,
     so the census leans towards more mixing.
   - The lakes' and seas' methane at 301 K, answered on 18 October by a tracer on the corrected model's columns ([lake
     methane](../../research/studies/lake_methane/README.md)). At the methane share's centre, with Earth's
     methanotroph community and Osborn's form, nutrient-poor lakes and seas send 0.57 Tg a year (the seas 0.42 with
     their shelves, the lakes 0.15) and hold the air at 1.01–1.75 ppm, under the held 1.87 with 0.35 Tg a year of room
     at the ledger's high-cost end, which the 3,500 m giant settling anoxic could take most of. At the share's 0.8
     they need 0.5–2.6 Tg a year of removal; by Shih and Bouffard's form, 0.77 at the high-cost end with Earth's
     community, while a designed community fits. Mesotrophic waters send 7.8 Tg a year (3.5–4.2 ppm). In mesotrophic
     and eutrophic lakes the giants make 79–87% of the methane and their oxyclines oxidise 88–99% of what they make.
     Anaerobic oxidation, mostly by iron, cuts the nutrient-poor methane 37–50% at the high-erosion end of its
     acceptors' supply and about 5% at the conservation rate.
   - Monthly plankton cycles and intertidal monthly wetlands.
   - The seas' deep basins in the lake model, answered on 18 October at 301 K ([the seas'
     oxygen](../../research/studies/sea_oxygen/README.md)). The seas cover 10.62 million km², 28% of the surface, at
     a mean depth of 1,009 m, the large ones deep basins joined only above sills of 76–181 m. By Osborn's rule a
     basin's deep water turns over 20–330 times a century, and the seas' deepest tenth averages 4.1 mg/L
     oligotrophic, 2.1 mesotrophic (half the area under 0.5) and 1.5 eutrophic. By Shih and Bouffard's form, run at
     the two largest basins, Procellarum goes 90 years without overturning and keeps at most 1.5 mg/L in
     nutrient-poor water, and Ingenii goes anoxic in every state. Mesotrophic water leaves half the seas' area under
     1 mg/L in every column. The large basins are many internal Rossby radii across (6–170 km), so the wind drives
     upwelling along their shores and waves round them, which a column cannot hold. Shelves and bays keep their
     median night at 3.7 mg/L or more under the wind's mixing. A three-dimensional basin model would place the deep
     water between the bounds.
   - Soda lakes, which the author takes as their own local ecosystems (15 October): their communities for the Moon's
     nearly chloride-free soda at a pH near 10, from Earth's soda-lake producers, grazers, birds and the few fish that
     live in them, and how they change as a lake concentrates over its thousands of years.
   - The fresh shores: the belts on the sea's water table, the monthly freshwater tidal marsh, small islands wooded
     throughout, and the hardpan where the draw ends.
   - Saltwater life, for which the author leans to aquariums over salt seas: which marine life is kept, and in what
     volumes.

   **How:** the one-dimensional lake model through the lunar month is built ([lake.py](lake.py)); the monthly plankton,
   methane, CO2 and phosphorus as tracers of their own (the anoxia study derives them from its oxygen and debt), and the
   soda lakes' concentration can ride in it, with the seas' composition as scenarios.
11. **What may live wild.** Pollen and spores spread Moon-wide, and no containment is demonstrated for wild engineered

    and a test for each constraint.
12. **Fire.** No one has measured natural fuels at 17.5% oxygen, 1.2 atm and 0.16 g. Whether the grassy biomes need

## Aerophyte follow-up: giants, water and storms

The [coupled study](../../research/studies/aerophytes/README.md) now gives giant aerophytes' size limits, ages,
water sources, storm design and sailing. What decides it next:
- one assembled wet membrane: hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen permeation, sustained-load strength and creep at the
  renewal interval, seams, tear arrest and healing, with and without guanine-like plates;
- paths through the latitude and solar-hour winds (the design climate's wind product,
  `climate/gcm/zonal_winds.md`), with water, carbon, gas and trim closed hour by hour, and whether the low evening
  westward branch that lets aerophytes linger at sunset survives in three dimensions;
- a population model: colony budding and fragmentation, juvenile survival in canopy nurseries, lightning and storm
  losses, and the phosphorus that roosting flyers and litter carry;
- storm return levels over centuries, beyond one lunar day of one ring.

common names are recorded as the lunar peoples give them.

## The living sky: what it asks next

The [sky-ecology study](../../research/studies/sky_ecology/README.md) gives a sky of aerophytes its food web, tenants
and phosphorus as screen values. What decides it next:
- measured tissues: phosphorus, resorption and turnover of designed green tissue and envelope, which set every stock
  and flow;
- a population model on the routes: aerophytes, tenants and roosting flyers along the latitude and solar-hour winds,
  with recruitment, the division between growing and mature bodies, and the adult hazard against the giants' growth
  times (at the high hazard fermented 500-m and 1-km round giants and 150-m modules cannot replace their dead);
- the seas' food web: how much fish the 28% seas grow on the land's phosphorus, which sets how many sea-feeding flyers
  can bring it back;
- great flyers' energetics from tens of kilograms to tonnes;
- a landing and trim model: tie compliance in rafts, valve and drain areas and the envelope's heat exchange, which set
  how fast a body takes a flock;
- the guards: which threat each partner answers and at what cost, measured as host performance with and without each
  guild, with their food and phosphorus through the longest dark interval ([guards and
  symbioses](../../research/studies/sky_ecology/symbioses.md));
- one living sail: a shaped sail with its suspension, tail, finite actuator and local delay in changing airflow, judged
  by its angle error, loads, oscillation and behaviour after a loss of power, then several sails on compliant cables
  ([living sails](../../research/studies/aerophytes/sail_biology.md));
- the resources box model with aerophyte stoichiometry, both states and the falls.

## Living light: what it asks next

The [living-light study](../../research/studies/living_light/README.md) gives glowing organisms, outdoor needs and
skyglow as screen values. What decides it next:
- a plant that glows with a beetle-class luciferin cycle, and the luciferin supply and light lost in the leaf that set
  how brightly it can glow;
- the dark-night requirement's value, now that the dark belt covers 13% of the dry land and twilit or Earthlit skies
  cover most of the rest;
- lamps designed for the dark belt, where living light is not grown, and for faces, reading and work everywhere;
- skyglow in the lunar air's own haze, between the clear and hazy brackets;
- living light's effects on night-active animals, beyond the insect and glow-worm thresholds measured on Earth.
