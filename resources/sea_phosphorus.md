# Phosphorus from the waters

On 11 October 2026 the author asked for phosphorus to be drawn from the seas for human use, as a distribution system,
beside a decent natural cycle ([register](../research/decisions.md), "Phosphorus from the seas"). This note follows the
land's phosphorus into the lakes and seas, sets people's needs beside it, sizes each route from the waters to farms and
towns, and puts numbers on the natural cycle. Numbers marked *screen* come from [sea_phosphorus.py](sea_phosphorus.py)
and its product [results/sea_phosphorus.json](results/sea_phosphorus.json) (schema
`terluna.resources.sea-phosphorus/1`), or from the routing rerun in [sea_phosphorus_waters.py](sea_phosphorus_waters.py)
([results/sea_phosphorus_waters.json](results/sea_phosphorus_waters.json)); *derived* marks arithmetic on cited values.
Sources and how far each was read are in [sea_phosphorus_sources.json](sea_phosphorus_sources.json). Concentrations are
mg of P per m³ of water, the same as µg/L.

## Findings

1. **The rain-fed lakes catch 96% of the phosphorus the land loses, and the common-level waters receive 4%.** Of the
   land's runoff that reaches the common water level, 96.5% has crossed at least one rain-fed lake and 84% a lake
   holding ten years of its outflow (*screen*). With each lake keeping phosphorus as Earth's lakes keep it (Vollenweider
   1976) and trapping particles on Brune's (1953) curves, 6.5% of the dissolved load and 3.6–4.2% of the particles pass
   on. The lakes keep 1.41–4.00 Tg a year; the common-level waters receive 55–180 kt, 4.6–12 kt of it in solution.
2. **On what the rivers bring, the great seas receive almost none of it, and the fresh seas stay poor in phosphorus for
   thousands of years.** Crater waters at the common level take 54% of the dissolved phosphorus that arrives and
   Orientale 15%; the Near-side Sea gets 0.27–0.71 kt a year and the South Pole–Aitken Sea almost nothing (*screen*).
   All the common-level water mixed together holds 0.27–1.11 mg/m³ after 1,000 years, 0.4–1.6% of Earth's ocean mean of
   70 mg/m³; the Near-side Sea on its own inflow holds 0.05–0.20. Dust falling on the waters can outweigh the rivers: at
   the inherited envelope's top, 0.028 g/m² a year, the mixed waters would hold 18–28 mg/m³ after 1,000 years, 26–40% of
   Earth's mean. Earth's oceans receive 0.0009–0.010 g/m² a year from the air (Paytan & McLaughlin 2007).
3. **People's new phosphorus depends on the food system's efficiency.** On the lunar design's plant-rich diet, each
   billion people needs 124–1,283 kt a year with field crops, 90–612 kt with hydroponics and 93–468 kt with aquaponics
   (*screen*), 2–87% of the land's natural loss. The ecology design target, 44–180 kt, needs farms that lose almost
   nothing and towns that return at least 92.5% of sewage and food losses. For 4–8 billion it is 0.18–1.44 Tg a year.
4. **The easy route is dredging the lake deltas, where the land's phosphorus settles, and it brings people's losses back
   too.** People's losses run into the same rivers and lakes, so dredging returns the design target's 0.18–1.44 Tg a
   year from 4–51% of what the lakes receive (*screen*). Each kilogram of P comes in 450–2,080 kg of sediment, for 5–206
   MJ of dredging fuel (*derived*).
5. **The waters' life can spare 21–56 kt a year to seaweed, fish and sorption, against people's need of 0.18–1.44 Tg.**
   That is 30% of each water's dissolved input, a design guess for the largest harvest that leaves the food webs whole:
   1.4–3.7 kt from the common-level waters and 20–52 kt from the lakes (*derived*). If particles release phosphorus as
   in Earth's estuaries, the total rises to 44–377 kt.
6. **Pulling phosphorus straight from the lunar seas costs 1.4–106 GJ a kilogram.** At their concentration after 1,000
   years a teragram is the dissolved phosphorus of 0.9–3.7 million km³, 124–511 years of the Moon's river flow and 8–35%
   of all the common-level water (*derived*). At Earth's ocean mean it is 14,300 km³, twice the yearly river flow.
7. **Sewage recovery returns 264–540 kt for each billion people; the waters' life can spare 21–56 kt from dissolved
   phosphorus.** Struvite carries a kilogram of P in 7.9 kg and takes 0.78 kg of magnesium (*derived*).
8. **Without people, carriers return phosphorus only from the waters' bioavailable part: the land's dissolved loss,
   70–187 kt a year, 4.5–4.8% of what it loses.** If lake beds release particulate phosphorus as Earth's estuaries do,
   that part grows to 146–1,255 kt. The rest lies in lake and sea beds. The carriers feed in the lakes, which keep
   66–175 kt in solution; the common-level waters receive 4.6–12 kt (*screen*).
9. **A decent natural cycle, in numbers: carriers return the wild land's dissolved loss, 21–94 kt a year, harvests take
   at most 30% of each water's bioavailable input, and people take their phosphorus from the deposits.** With 30–50% of
   the land kept wild that is 0.6–5.3 million albatross-scale roosting flyers, or lake insects at 11–104% of Lake
   Mývatn's highest emergence. It leaves 17–38% of the lakes' dissolved input to their food webs and burial, and 45–65%
   if particles release phosphorus (*derived*).
10. **Extraction at the lake deltas is a distribution system within reach of most farms.** Half the land lies within 50
    km of standing water and 72% within 100 km (the ways-of-living product). Sediment spread on fields within that reach
    carries the farms' phosphorus; struvite and other concentrates serve the far land and the sky.

## The seas' phosphorus

### Inputs from the land

**The land loses 1.47–4.16 Tg of phosphorus a year, 95% of it on particles.** This is the ecology people product's
natural loss, 0.063–0.178 g per m² of the 23.4 million km² of dry land. Its dissolved part is 70–187 kt, 4.5–4.8% (the
sky-ecology study), and the particulate part 1.40–3.97 Tg (*derived*). Against the 0.72–0.85 kg of phosphorus in each m²
of the top metre of highland and mare soil (the lunar-cycle register), the loss would take that metre's phosphorus in
4,000–13,500 years, the loss's cycle time; erosion keeps exposing fresh rock beneath (register H9). At those soils'
0.048–0.057% phosphorus the particles imply erosion of 2.5–8.3 Gt of soil a year, overlapping the conservation study's
0.71–7.1 Gt (*derived*).

### Through the lakes

**Nearly every river crosses a lake before it reaches the common water level.** The waters run repeats the geography
drainage routing with its committed climatology and reproduces the drainage product exactly: 27,865 lakes, 229,410 m³/s
to the common level. Of that, 127,371 m³/s is the land's own runoff and 102,039 m³/s is rain on the lakes less their
evaporation (*screen*). Phosphorus rides the land's runoff. The routing chains the depressions inside a merged lake
through their spill points, so the screen counts each lake once, where water leaves it. Joining the 12 lakes cut by the
0/360° seam, in 14 joins as the routing makes them, leaves 27,851 lakes. Their spilling parts make the 25,610 bodies
that pass phosphorus on; the lakes of closed depressions, 2,660 bodies over 54,000 km², keep all they receive. 232 lakes
hold both kinds, and the screen clears their closed depressions' 23,000 km² from the spilling bodies.

A lake passes on 1/(1 + √τ) of the total phosphorus it receives, τ its residence in years, Vollenweider's (1976) simple
form; Khorasani and Zhu review its fitted versions. A lake traps exp(−β I/C) of the particles it receives, Siyam's
(2000) form of Brune's (1953) curves with β 0.0055–0.015 (Eizel-Din et al. 2010); lunar gravity slows settling sixfold,
which lets 0.3–0.5 percentage points more through. The lakes keep these Earth relations at a steady annual flow, with
lake beds that do not fill.

**The great seas receive the least.** The routing's outlets include every cell below the common level. Of the dissolved
phosphorus that reaches that level, crater waters take 54%, grid-scale pits 25%, Orientale 15% and the Near-side Sea 6%
(*screen*). The four largest rivers carry 52% of the routed water and end in Orientale, a 229 km² crater water, a 648
km² crater water and a pit. On its direct inflow the Near-side Sea receives 0.27–0.71 kt a year in solution and 3.5–10
kt on particles, Orientale 0.68–1.81 kt and 2.3–6.5 kt, and the South Pole–Aitken Sea almost none. If the lakes kept no
dissolved phosphorus, the common-level waters would receive the whole 70–187 kt; this note carries that case beside the
routed one.

### Inventory and concentration through time

**Each sea box is the resources box model's sea and sediment boxes, with its first-order burial, fed by what the lakes
pass on.** The phosphorus residence runs from the box model's own 1,000 years to 20,000, the low end of Earth's ocean's
20–100 kyr (Paytan & McLaughlin 2007). The common-level waters hold 10.7 million km³ of fresh water (the resources first
screen), 1,480 years of the routed flow, and lose water to evaporation. Four inputs bound the bioavailable supply:
- the dissolved phosphorus the lakes pass on, 4.6–12 kt a year;
- the same with dust falling on the waters at the inherited envelope's low end, 0.0007 g/m² a year, which adds 7.4 kt,
  or at its top, 0.028 g/m², which adds 297 kt; Earth's oceans receive 0.0009–0.010 g/m² a year from the air, 9.6–108 kt
  over these waters, its inorganic part bound to iron oxides and calcium and weakly soluble (Paytan & McLaughlin 2007);
- Earth's reactive share of all river phosphorus, 10–30% (Compton et al. 2000, in Paytan & McLaughlin), 5.5–54 kt, which
  salinity or beds without oxygen release from particles;
- all the land's dissolved loss, 70–187 kt, if the lakes kept none.

### Sinks

**Burial takes everything the seas and lakes receive, apart from what people and carriers take out.** In the settled
common-level waters burial matches the input, 55–180 kt a year, over the phosphorus residence above. The lakes bury
1.41–4.00 Tg a year; their particles, 1.34–3.83 Tg at the soils' grade and a dry density of 0.5–1.5 t/m³ (a design
guess), fill 1.6–16 km³ a year, so their 3.1 million km³ would take 0.19–2.0 million years to fill (*derived*). Fresh
waters lack the salinity that releases phosphorus from clays in Earth's estuaries, 2–5 times the rivers' dissolved load
there (Paytan & McLaughlin), so particles keep what they carry, apart from what diagenesis releases in the sediment.

### Where it gathers

- **Lake deltas.** The rivers drop 1.34–3.83 Tg of particulate phosphorus a year where they enter the lakes, and the
  lakes keep 66–175 kt more from solution: 0.36–1.02 g per m² of the lakes' 3.9 million km² on average and far more at
  the inlets (*screen*).
- **The common-level waters' river mouths.** 51–168 kt a year settles at the mouths; the twelve largest rivers carry 71%
  of the routed water (the drainage product). The ecology's design waters already give river mouths 1–20 mg/m³ of
  chlorophyll against the open sea's 0.03–0.3.
- **Shelves.** 14% of the common-level water lies within 200 m of the surface, 1.5 million km², and 3.7% within 50 m.
  The Near-side Sea has 16% within 200 m; the South Pole–Aitken Sea 3.3% (*screen*).
- **The deeps.** 37% of the water is deeper than 1 km, 80% of the South Pole–Aitken Sea. Dissolved phosphorus taken up
  by plankton sinks there and is buried over the residence time.

## People's phosphorus

**People eat 0.44–0.60 kg of phosphorus a year** (Cordell et al. 2009, through the ecology product), and adults excrete
what they eat. Between field and mouth a quarter to a third of food is lost (FAO 2011, as the ecology note takes it).
The lunar design seals its waste, so the works receive all excreta, and towns return 60–90% of their phosphorus by
sludge-ash routes (Egle et al. 2016) and 50–90% of the food losses' phosphorus (a design guess). Each system's
efficiency is the share of applied phosphorus that the harvest takes up:
- **field crops** 50–90%, by the balance method (Syers et al. 2008); while the soils build their stores, part of what
  the crops leave stays in the soil for later crops, and the rest reaches the waters;
- **hydroponics** 80–95%, a design guess for closed recirculation with bleed and residues returned; reusing the fifth of
  the solution that drained applied 3–18% less phosphorus than an open system (Sánchez del Castillo et al. 2021);
- **aquaponics** 92–95% of the feed's phosphorus: 17% is excreted in solution and 55% goes to the sludge (Goddek et al.
  2019), which mineralisation returns to the plants at 85–90%; fish growth and other fates take the remaining 28%
  (*derived*). A coupled system that discards its sludge keeps 45%.

## The natural cycle beside it

**Without people, the natural return reaches at most the dissolved loss, 70–187 kt a year.** The carriers:
- **Roosting flyers.** Earth's seabirds carry 99 kt a year from sea to land (Otero et al. 2018), 2.4–6.7% of the Moon's
  loss. Returning the whole dissolved loss takes 2.0–10.7 million albatross-scale flyers eating 156–833 kt of phosphorus
  a year in fish (the sky-ecology study), 2.4–4.8 times what the lakes keep in solution; the lakes' food webs would have
  to cycle that phosphorus several times a year. The common-level waters receive 4.6–12 kt.
- **Lake insects.** At Lake Mývatn's emergence, 0.15–3.7 g of dry midges per m² of lake a year (Dreyer et al. 2015),
  with 0.62–1.3% phosphorus (Woods et al. 2004; Gratton et al. 2008), the lakes would send up 3.6–189 kt a year. At
  Mývatn's highest rate everywhere that is 0.5–2.9 times what the lakes keep in solution. Most midges land within
  100–200 m of the shore, 0.02–2.4 g per m² on the 79,000–158,000 km² beside the lakes' 791,000 km of shore. The
  sea–forest migrants (G13) carry most where they grow in lakes.
- **Run fish (G42).** A tenth of the dissolved loss takes 1.2–3.1 Mt of fish dying inland each year at 0.6% phosphorus.
- **Aerophyte falls and litter.** At 1% cover the falls bring 1.0–20 kt a year to the ground and forest-floor pools drop
  3.3–12 kt of litter (the sky-ecology study); the share flyers brought from the waters is a return.
- **Dust** moves land phosphorus over the land.

**A decent natural cycle keeps three numbers.** The wild land, 30–50% of the land as the author keeps it, loses 21–94 kt
a year in solution, and carriers return that: 0.6–5.3 million albatross-scale flyers roosting over it, or lake emergence
at 11–104% of Mývatn's highest. Harvests of fish, seaweed or water take at most 30% of any water's bioavailable input.
People take their phosphorus from the deposits, 0.18–1.44 Tg a year at the design target, which no carrier returns.

## The two statements checked

**River-mouth sediments are the easy route, and the mouths that matter are the lakes'.** The lakes catch 96% of the
land's loss at their inlets; the common-level waters' mouths receive 51–168 kt a year. Seaweed and fish farms
concentrate phosphorus, and their supply is the waters' dissolved input: at the 30% ceiling 1.4–3.7 kt from the
common-level waters and 20–52 kt from the lakes.

**Open water is the hard route, and harder in the lunar seas.** At Earth's ocean mean of 70 mg/m³ a teragram is all the
dissolved phosphorus in 14,300 km³, 1.98 years of the Moon's 7,240 km³ of river flow: the statement holds. After 1,000
years the common-level waters hold 0.27–1.11 mg/m³, and a teragram sits in 0.9–3.7 million km³. The rivers bring 4.6–12
kt a year in solution, so a teragram a year from open water would take 82–218 times their supply (*derived*).

## Statements elsewhere that need correcting

These belong to other studies, for their owners to correct.
1. **The ecology land loss's water.** The ecology product's 1.47–4.16 Tg puts Earth's river concentrations on 7,546 km³
   a year, the lunar-cycle register's earlier 0.3226 m a year over the dry land, from 279,000 m³/s. The register now
   runs on the drainage product's 229,410 m³/s, 0.2651 m a year, 6,201 km³ over the dry land. The land's own runoff is
   4,020 km³ a year (the waters product); the rest of the routed flow is rain on lakes. On the land's runoff the loss
   is 0.78–2.22 Tg, 0.53 times (*derived*); the screen scales it from the register's present runoff, 0.95–2.70 Tg,
   0.65 times. This note keeps the bound 1.47–4.16 Tg.
2. **The drainage outlets.** The drainage product treats every cell below the common level as an outlet, so 71% of its
   sea-bound water ends in crater waters and grid-scale pits at that level; its second river, 31,255 m³/s, ends in 229
   km² of crater water. Such pits fill and spill onward through more lakes, which raises the retention found here. The
   Near-side Sea's direct inflow, 3,435 m³/s, renews it in 31,600 years.
3. **The sky-ecology study's sea-feeding flyers.** It sets flyers' food against the land's whole loss as the seas'
   input. The common-level waters receive 4% of that loss, 4.6–54 kt a year bioavailable, so flyers at Earth's seabird
   scale fish the lakes.
4. **The register's dredging (H10).** Eroded phosphorus settles mostly on lake beds; dredging serves the lake deltas
   first and the sea floor after.

## Reproduction and checks

Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m resources.sea_phosphorus_waters` (about a minute, 4.4 GB; it needs the ignored
LOLA, GRAIL, climatology and atlas grids) when the routing changes, then `python -m resources.sea_phosphorus` and
`python -m pytest resources/tests/test_sea_phosphorus.py`. The products bind their producer files, committed inputs and
the ignored grids by SHA-256, with the shared constants they read. The ecology and provisioning inputs are bound at
commits 5aafe2a and b82e0dc and read through Git. The waters run stops unless its routing reproduces the drainage
product and its tracer discharge matches the routing's own. The tests check that:
- the dissolved and particulate survival hold above the share of runoff that crosses no lake, and the receiving waters
  summed by atlas label add up to the tracer totals at the outlets;
- joining across the seam merges exactly the split lakes, every retention body spills, and the spilling and closed lakes
  cover the drainage product's lake area;
- the lakes and the common-level waters share the land's loss at each edge, recomputed from the survival fractions;
- the sea box conserves phosphorus and matches its closed form, and the concentration tables follow the closed form;
- each body's release case keeps at least its dissolved input, a single sea taking everything holds the most, and the
  atlas bodies' share of the volume matches the atlas;
- each food system's new phosphorus matches its closed form, and aquaponics returns 85–90% of the sludge's share;
- every ledger corner closes, its dredged share recomputed from the land loss, the population and the carriers;
- no biological route takes more than 30% of its water's dissolved input, and every lake budget leaves a remainder;
- sorption energy scales with dilution, and the dredging masses and fuel follow the soils' grades and hopper dredging;
- bound values match their sources: the land loss, the 0.003–0.008 g/m² dissolved loss, the 1–12% per billion, Earth's
  seabird shares, the fish phosphorus behind the sky-ecology study's food figures, the box model's sea burial rate, the
