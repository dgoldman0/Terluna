# What the literature says for the Open Moon's resources

A cross-field reading of 9 October 2026, by topic, with what each finding means here. Full citations, DOIs and how far
each source was read are in [sources.json](sources.json). The search's complete register (83 sources) is kept with the
domain's run records on the research drive (`research/runs/resources/literature_register.md`). Numbers the search

## Lunar rock as feedstock

Average soils from the Lunar Sourcebook (Heiken et al. 1991, Table 7.15), in wt%:

| Soil | SiO2 | TiO2 | Al2O3 | FeO | MgO | CaO | K2O | P2O5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| High-Ti mare, Apollo 11 | 42.2 | 7.8 | 13.6 | 15.3 | 7.8 | 11.9 | 0.16 | 0.05 |
| Feldspathic highland, Apollo 16 | 45.0 | 0.54 | 27.3 | 5.1 | 5.7 | 15.7 | 0.17 | 0.11 |
| KREEP-rich, Apollo 14 | 48.1 | 1.7 | 17.4 | 10.4 | 9.4 | 10.7 | 0.55 | 0.51 |

- **Redox state.** The rocks are reduced: iron is ferrous or metallic, never ferric, and soils hold 0.12–0.5 wt%
  metallic iron, much of it nanophase.
- **Thickness.** Regolith is about 4–5 m thick on the maria and 10–15 m in the highlands.
- **Ilmenite** makes up 10–18% of the coarse fragments in high-titanium mare soils.
- **The Procellarum KREEP Terrane** is about 10% of the crust's volume and holds about 40% of its thorium (Jolliff et
  al. 2000).
- **Depletion.** The Moon is depleted about 75% in potassium, rubidium and caesium against Earth, and 99% in
  thallium and cadmium (Taylor & Wieczorek 2014).
- **Volatiles.** Soils hold 1–100 µg/g each of H, C and N. Over the whole regolith the nitrogen is about 10⁻⁵ of the
  Open Moon's need (*derived*).

## Weathering

- **Rate laws.** Basaltic glass dissolves by a rate law valid from 6 to 300 °C and pH 1 to 11 (Gislason & Oelkers
  2003). In the neutral mechanism, anorthite dissolves about 2,800 times faster than albite and 33 times faster than
  forsterite (Palandri & Kharaka 2004).
- **Rates fall with time.** Field rates fall as R = 3.1×10⁻¹³ t^−0.61 mol m⁻² s⁻¹ (White & Brantley 2003).
- **Basalt landscapes** consume CO2 at φ = runoff × 323 × e^(0.0642 T) mol km⁻² yr⁻¹ (Dessert et al. 2003). Applied to
  lunar land this gives 0.18–1.9 Gt CO2 a year (*derived*).
- **Field trials** of crushed basalt:
  - US Corn Belt: 10.5 t CO2/ha over 4 years, with higher yields (Beerling et al. 2024);
  - Scotland: 0.33–0.53 t/ha in 1.5 years (McBride et al. 2025);
  - Swiss vineyards: 0.1 t/ha/yr (Dupla et al. 2025).

  The CO2 study's lunar sink, 0.13–1.3 t/ha/yr, sits inside this range. CO2 injected into basalt mineralized over 95%
  within two years (Matter et al. 2016).
- **Which cations count.** Calcium and magnesium carry CO2, with sodium and potassium on the bicarbonate route.
  Iron carries none; ferrous iron that oxidizes away from its rock makes acid water (Renforth 2019; Hurowitz et al.
  2010). Full carbonation of lunar soils binds 179–194 kg CO2 per tonne (*derived*).
- **Oxygen uptake.** Dissolved oxygen meeting ferrous minerals and microbes in rock fractures is used up within days
  (Puigdomènech et al. 2001). Earth's crust holds about 50 times its air's oxygen as excess ferric iron (Daines et al.
  2017).
- **Lunar soils in water.** A highland simulant raised water from pH 5.5 to 7 within three days, releasing calcium,
  silicon, aluminium, magnesium and potassium (Freer et al. 2024). Apollo soils hold nutrients in low-solubility
  forms (Ming et al. 1990), and bacteria release phosphorus from simulant (Xia et al. 2023).

## Closed systems

- **Biosphere 2's oxygen fell** from 21% to 14% in 16 months: microbes respired the organic matter built into its
  soils, and the CO2 reacted with the structure's concrete (Severinghaus et al. 1994). The Open Moon builds both at
  planetary scale, in imported soil carbon and fresh regolith.
- **Lunar Palace.** Lunar Palace 1 recovered 20.5% of urine nitrogen and Lunar Palace 365 99.7% (Fu et al. 2016,
  2021). Membrane bioreactors recover 80–99% (Verbeelen et al. 2021). Nitrogen and solid organic waste have been the
  hard closures in every closed system.

## Volatiles across the Solar System

- **Comets** carry little N2: N2/CO is about 0.006 on 67P (Rubin et al. 2015), whose nitrogen sits in ammonium salts
  (Altwegg et al. 2020).
- **Carbon-rich asteroids.** Ryugu holds 4.6 wt% carbon and 6.8 wt% water (Yokoyama et al. 2023); Bennu holds
  4.5–4.7 wt% carbon and 0.23–0.25 wt% nitrogen (Glavin et al. 2025).
- **Ceres** has ammoniated clays (De Sanctis et al. 2015) and up to 20 wt% carbon at its surface (Marchi et al. 2019).
- **The large N2 reservoirs.** Titan's air, at 1,467 hPa (Fulchignoni et al. 2005), holds about 3.5 times the Open
  Moon's N2 (*derived*). Pluto's Sputnik Planitia holds 0.4–3×10²⁰ mol of N2 (Glein & Waite 2018), 0.45–3.4 times
  the need, or 0.33–0.67 times under a 1–2 km sheet limit (Johnson et al. 2021).
- **The main belt** is 2.39×10²¹ kg (Pitjeva & Pitjev 2018).
- **Terraforming.** Mars's terraforming depends on accessible nitrogen (McKay, Toon & Kasting 1991). Jakosky & Edwards
  (2018) treat its CO2.
- **An artificial lunar atmosphere** lives hundreds of years or more once its density passes a threshold (Vondrak
  1974).
- **Redirecting bodies.** Gravity assists bring the redirection of an ammonia body to about 0.3 km/s (Zubrin & McKay
  1993).

## Space resource processing

- **Electro-deoxidation** extracted 96% of a simulant's oxygen, a third of it as gas (Lomax et al. 2020).
- **Ilmenite** reduced with hydrogen gave 3.4–4.4 wt% oxygen and leaves a titania-rich residue (Sargeant et al. 2020).
- **Molten regolith electrolysis** takes about 30 kWh per kg of oxygen, liquefaction included (Runyon et al.,
  *derived*).
- **Glass.** Transparent glass needs the iron removed (Landis 2005). Evaporating simulant in vacuum deposits
  transparent silica films (Freundlich et al. 2005).
- **Titania.** A modern route takes 92.6 MJ/kg (Middlemas et al. 2015). World pigment capacity was 9.8 Mt in 2024
  (USGS 2025).
- **Mass drivers.** On an airless Moon a mass driver throws payloads at 2.4 km/s for 3.8–6.1 MJ/kg (O'Neill 1974; NASA
  SP-413). Direct electromagnetic launch through a dense column is marginal even on Earth (McNab 2003).
- **Elevators.** A lunar space elevator is buildable from commercial polymers (Eubanks & Radley 2016).

## Closing the carbon cycle without volcanoes

- **Calcination** takes 3.2 GJ per tonne of lime, 4.08 GJ per tonne of CO2, at the minimum. Magnesite takes 2.57 GJ
  per tonne of CO2 at 550–800 °C. Good kilns use 3.2–4.2 GJ per tonne of lime (Schorcht et al. 2013).
- **Burial.** Lakes and reservoirs bury 19–48 g C m⁻² a year (Mendonça et al. 2017). Fjords bury at a hundred times
  the ocean's average (Smith et al. 2015).
- **Earth's thermostat.** Earth's long-term CO2 is held by the silicate-weathering thermostat and by outgassing
  (Walker et al. 1981; Berner 2001). Subaerial volcanoes emit about 51 Tg CO2 a year (Fischer et al. 2019). The Open
  Moon's weathering sink is 7–68 times that, with no natural return (*derived*).

## Soil sinks for trace gases

- **Soils control** H2, CO, CH4, N2O and NO through upland oxidizers and wetland producers (Conrad 1996).
- **Soil shares of Earth's sinks:**
  - about 76% of H2 (Ehhalt & Rohrer 2009);
  - about 5% of CH4, 32 Tg a year (Saunois et al. 2020);
  - 5–8% of CO (Liu et al. 2018; Zheng et al. 2019);
  - at most 2% of N2O's sources (Schlesinger 2013; Tian et al. 2020; Prather et al. 2015).
- **Under the shield** methane, CO and N2O build up until microbial sinks balance their emissions.

## Nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium

- **Earth's nitrogen cycle.** Total fixation is 413 Tg N a year (Fowler et al. 2013). Lightning fixes 5 ± 3 Tg N
  (Schumann & Huntrieser 2007). Denitrification runs 44% on shelves and 22% in soils (Seitzinger et al. 2006).
- **Planetary boundaries** set nitrogen and phosphorus flows (Steffen et al. 2015; Richardson et al. 2023).
- **Phosphate.** Earth's reserves are 74 Gt with no imminent shortage (USGS 2025). Their declining quality is the
  consensus concern (Cordell & White 2011).
- **Potassium.** Soils lose potassium twice as fast as it is replaced (Manning 2018).

## Material budgets

- **Material flow analysis** links stocks and flows to life-cycle and input-output methods (Graedel 2019).
- **Earth's stocks.** In-use stocks grew 23-fold over the twentieth century to 792 Pg; about half of each year's
  extraction builds or renews them (Krausmann et al. 2017). In 2005, 4 of 62 Gt processed came from recycling (Haas et
  al. 2015).
- **On the Open Moon** the shield's upkeep is 4–7% of Earth's processed-material flow (*derived*).

## Thin films for space

- **Sunshades.** A thin refractive sunshade blocking 1.8% of Earth's sunlight weighs about 20 Mt (Angel 2006). Lunar
  dust would need over 10¹⁰ kg (Bromley et al. 2023).
- **Proton damage.** Silica layers survive 10¹⁷ protons cm⁻² at 16 keV. Titania crystallizes above 10¹⁵ unless capped
  by silica (Corso et al. 2024).
- **Meteoroids.** NASA's meteoroid model is MEM 3 (Moorhead 2020).
