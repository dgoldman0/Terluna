# SCALE and the complete prescribed lunar snow column

This experiment asks whether the large aggregates and unresolved sampling dependence in the custom column also occur with SCALE's existing microphysics under the same deep, continuously supplied cloud. The comparison holds the prescribed environment and source quantities fixed where the two frameworks permit. It treats differences in particle laws and atmospheric feedback as explicit sensitivities. A stable numerical answer would remain a result of those laws, without empirical validation of lunar snow.

The [runner](scale_lunar_comparison.py) declares the native cases and the four additional custom controls. Its [adapter](scale_lunar.py) builds the pinned general SCALE release already identified in the [source audit](solver_reuse_sources.json). The new [Fortran hook](scale_lunar_hook.f90) supplies the scenario and measures arrivals; the original collision, deposition, geometry and drag routines remain in use. A separate build changes the ice collection efficiency for one labelled experiment. The [results](scale_lunar_results.md) interpret the full-column comparison.

## Quantities held in common

The input is the original 301 K reference atmosphere read by `flakes.moon_column`, with its source-product hash retained. The freezing level is 29.245 km above the surface; the cloud spans another 16.280521 km to −15 °C. The base pressure is 65.9253 kPa. Thirty equal vertical layers reproduce the original cell-centre temperature, pressure and liquid-saturation vapour pressure. A sixty-layer case refines the same profile and depth. Gravity is the shared lunar value, 1.62421887656 m/s².

Every complete case lasts 477321.407800899 s (132.589280 h). The second half supplies arrival statistics. The injected number flux is 10232.4266274 crystals/m²/s, corresponding to the original 100 crystals/L and reference entry speed. Diameters are sampled uniformly in their logarithm between 0.1 and 0.4 mm. Each injected particle has the same mass and maximum dimension under the inherited `0.0121 D^1.9` relation. SCALE starts that mass as a sphere of the required apparent density. The original projected-area convention cannot simultaneously be matched by that sphere; this shape choice is recorded with the comparison.

The ice column starts empty. Eight horizontal cells evolve independently with zero air velocity and no atmospheric dynamics. Their default area is 1 m² each. SCALE represents many physical particles by integer multiplicity, whereas the custom solver represents individuals in much smaller statistical volumes. Native particle refinement therefore changes the number of computational samples at fixed physical volume. It is a different limit from enlarging the custom solver's individual-particle volume.

## Water and temperature experiments

**Prescribed:** the original temperature and water-saturated vapour pressure are maintained. In each layer, the native deposition routine first calculates unconstrained particle growth. When positive growth demand exceeds that layer's share of 3 kg/m²/h, the adapter gives each particle the same fraction of its requested mass increment. It interpolates the native changes in the two axes and apparent density, then applies a common axis factor to preserve the admitted mass exactly. Native latent heating and vapour depletion from deposition are disabled in this experiment. Thus the externally maintained atmosphere supplies admitted deposition, and unused instantaneous capacity is discarded, as in the original column. The integration of the maximum allowed supply is 397.767840 kg/m², plus incoming seed ice. This interpolation is an additional numerical approximation assessed by timestep refinement.

**Reservoir:** the initial gas holds 48.340568 kg/m² of vapour in the active cloud. Before each step, an external source restores vapour toward liquid saturation, limited to the same layerwise 3 mm/h column allowance. Native deposition removes that vapour and releases heat. Temperature is restored to the prescribed profile at the next step; the adapter records the associated sensible cooling diagnostic. Unused source allowance is discarded, but existing vapour persists between steps. The initial reservoir and a source ceiling change the original prescribed-deposition equations; their water account is kept separately.

**Thermal:** the same reservoir and bounded source evolve with native latent heating and melting, with no imposed cooling. Vapour enters at the current local temperature. The initial hydrostatic profile is not maintained by dynamics in this control. Over five days, an atmosphere without radiative or dynamical cooling need not retain its original cold zone. Its temperature response diagnoses that omitted feedback; it is not a model of a sustained lunar storm or a complete energy-conservation validation.

In the two reservoir cases, the adapter also limits positive growth if a finite step would consume more vapour than exists. A common fraction is applied only when needed, retaining a relative `1e-12` vapour remainder. Sublimation returns water through the native update. Prescribed runs use the supply ceiling instead. Water summaries include initial vapour, added vapour or prescribed deposition, injected ice, suspended hydrometeors and precipitation. All arrived particles are retained in the output, including rain if melting occurs.

## Separating particle assumptions

SCALE uses its evolving porous spheroids, deposition-density and habit rules, projected-area law, Böhm drag and native gravitational collision kernel. Its default ice collection efficiency is 0.1. The labelled sticking sensitivity replaces only that efficiency with the original `exp[0.09(T−273.15)]` law, retaining the other native routines and the same prescribed environment. This tests whether that one difference accounts for the size contrast; it does not make the solvers physically identical.

The custom controls disable chipping and use ballistic settling, matching two native choices while retaining the original particle laws. They use two seeds at each of the two larger pre-existing statistical volumes and run the complete duration. The original, independently preserved upwind/chipping results remain in their own product. SCALE supplies no branch or contact-network fracture law. Its aggregate morphology includes an assumed minimum density; successful execution cannot establish the survival or optical properties of a very large structure.

## Numerical controls and records

Particle injection is refined from two to four to eight superparticles per minute per horizontal cell. The timestep control compares eight releases per 60 s with four per 30 s, preserving the number of samples per physical minute while refining injection timing along with the microphysical step. Other controls change the random seed and vertical spacing. The practical screen is the earlier 5% threshold for flux and mass-weighted 50th, 90th and 99th percentiles, plus 0.05 maximum mass-CDF distance. The eight-column means and standard errors accompany pooled distributions; a screen is not a statistical confidence bound or empirical validation.

The first upstream setup rejected a uniform grid because its computed spacing ratio was `1.0000000000000007`. The scenario build permits `1e-12` roundoff in that existing uniform-grid test; it does not enable stretched coordinates. All upstream source changes and executable hashes are retained. The collision observer remains passive and counts attempted and omitted collisions. Exhausting the preallocated particle capacity aborts the calculation rather than dropping incoming ice. The final partial step uses the exact remaining scenario duration, and the hook skips any further native end-label update.

Native arrival records are big-endian streams of nine float64 values: elapsed time, horizontal cell, multiplicity, mass, maximum dimension, apparent density, aspect ratio, constituent count and phase. The adapter writes a separate water/temperature ledger at regular steps and the exact end. Native atmospheric history provides an independent precipitation-accumulation check; its labels follow SCALE's existing convention of a microphysics update at time zero. Raw files, build logs and unsuccessful attempts stay in ignored `research/runs/snow_scale/`. The versioned product records source, binary and input identities and includes every declared completed or missing case.

With the dependencies and original cache restored, `python -m atmosphere.snow.scale_lunar build` builds the native adapter. The Python `build(collection='inhouse')` entry point builds the separate sticking variant. `python -m atmosphere.snow.scale_lunar_comparison run --workers 1` runs missing cases and assembles the product; `assemble` re-reads completed output without rerunning the physics. Use at most two numerical/build workers across concurrent work on this machine.
