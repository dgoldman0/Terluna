# Collision reconstruction and distribution controls

The collision repair tests the distribution as well as its water budget, mean and variance. This report records the beta closure’s failed crossing test and the entropy closure’s improved distribution with costly explicit time stepping. The subsequent [finite-time capture method](collision_jump_results.md) now completes six analytic and six snow-kernel bath controls, passing their declared distribution, moment and accounting criteria. These results establish a prescribed-bath component. A subsequent [full-column event study](event_column_results.md) separately extends the direct collision reference, passes its closed-box comparisons and completes the coupled runs, while exposing unresolved lunar volume dependence. No predictive lunar population has been adopted.

## The first reconstruction gets moments right and shape wrong

The [independent collision reference](collision_reference.md) established that fixed-pivot mass allocation spreads repeated small captures much more than the actual collision process. At 96 pivots, the final collector variance is about 95,125 times the variance of 8,192 independent exact-event histories. That experiment imposes a large collector at the start; it does not calculate how such a collector forms or survives.

The [three-moment candidate](collision_moments.py) retains particle number, mass and second mass moment inside each fixed mass interval. A bounded beta distribution supplies the unresolved shape, and collision products enter at their actual summed masses. The [method note](collision_moments_methods.md) and [source ledger](collision_moments_sources.json) distinguish the published moment-method lineage from this local adaptation. The kernel, aggregate geometry and capture environment remain unchanged in the physical-bath comparison.

The [saved bath controls](results/collision_moment_bath.json) appear promising at their two late endpoints. At the final time, the candidate variance divided by the exact-event sample variance is 0.98405 at 96 cells and 0.99518 at 191 cells. Their maximum number-CDF differences are 0.00695 and 0.00770. Both lie within the reference's 0.01501 DKW sampling band, which has 95% uniform-in-mass coverage at each saved time separately. It is neither a joint guarantee across times and controls nor a numerical convergence bound.

A separate [analytic control](results/collision_moment_boundary.json) exposes a failure those endpoints miss. It prescribes a constant capture rate of 100 per second and the same donor-to-collector mass ratio, making the exact distribution Poisson. The comparison evaluates both sides of every discrete jump within twelve count standard deviations. The mean crosses the first upper face of the 191-cell mass grid after 207.396 seconds, at an expected 20,739.566 captures. Mass is normalized by the initial collector in this mathematical test; its constant kernel is not the snow collision law.

The late recovery shows why inspecting only the final distribution can conceal the crossing error. This candidate is retained as a failed distribution closure rather than installed in the full column.

## Alternative reconstruction

The [entropy candidate](collision_entropy.py) uses an exponential quadratic within each mass interval. It retains the frozen collision event accounting and the same second-order SSPRK time-stepping method while changing the unresolved shape. The new integrator evaluates its final average in an algebraically equivalent form that preserves identical subnormal floating-point states. This family can represent a Gaussian cut by a cell face exactly; the beta family generally cannot. Positive quadratic curvature is also allowed, so the family includes distributions concentrated near both endpoints. The [method note](collision_entropy_methods.md) and [three-source ledger](collision_entropy_sources.json) document the bounded entropy construction, its conditioning limits and its status as a local adaptation.

The [benchmark runner](collision_entropy_benchmarks.py) requires separate checks of the number and water ledgers, CDF validity, mean, variance and distribution shape. It prescribes separate variations of maximum timestep, mass-grid spacing and quadrature. Fixed comparison times sample the 191-cell crossing across all grids; they do not establish accuracy at every grid's own worst crossing phase. The analytic CDF tolerance is 0.005, with omitted Poisson-tail probability reported separately. This is a numerical adequacy threshold for the stated control, not a physical uncertainty interval.

## The entropy candidate passes one crossing control

The [saved entropy boundary result](results/collision_entropy_boundary.json) completes the 191-cell, order-two control with a maximum step of 0.5 seconds. Its number-CDF differences before, at and after the face and at the later endpoint range from 0.001508 to 0.001860. At the face the difference is 0.001849, or 0.185 percentage points, against the beta candidate's 18.4 points. All four comparisons meet the preset 0.005 shape threshold. Mean mass agrees within 9 × 10⁻¹⁶ of initial mass, relative variance errors remain below 1.71 × 10⁻¹⁰, and number and mass ledger residuals remain below 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁵ in their normalized units.

This completed run took 459 seconds, with 14,163 accepted and 8,990 rejected steps. Halving the maximum allowed step to 0.25 seconds did not complete the refinement: that run reached its 600-second wall limit at model time 290.172 seconds, before the final observation at 311.093 seconds. The higher-quadrature and alternate-grid controls were not reached. The product therefore records overall acceptance as false. It establishes a much better crossing distribution in the completed case, while leaving timestep, quadrature and grid convergence unresolved. The [comparison renderer](../../visualization/snow/README.md#within-cell-collision-candidates) shows both candidates, the independent reference and the incomplete-control counts.

## Physical-bath result and incomplete refinement

The [saved entropy bath comparison](results/collision_entropy_bath.json) completes the 96-cell, order-two control with a maximum step of one second. At the two nonzero observation times, its number-CDF differences from the exact-event sample are 0.01201 and 0.00742, inside each time’s 0.01501 DKW band. The variance ratios are 0.96837 and 0.98405; mean errors remain within the preset five-standard-error criterion. Number and water residuals are zero at the saved observations.

Every saved coarse-grid state occupies a single mass cell, whose bounds are about 0.88458–1.15005 times the initial collector mass. This endpoint compatibility therefore supplies no completed physical-kernel test of transfer across a cell face. The 191-cell refinement reached its 600-second wall limit at model time 204.466 seconds, after 20,317 accepted steps, before the final observation at 272.627 seconds. The finer-grid, higher-quadrature and smaller-step bath controls were not reached. The product consequently records overall acceptance as false; the finer run's accuracy remains unestablished. The runtime is measured on this execution and machine, not a general performance bound.

## Why time integration needed a separate repair

Representing the distribution inside a mass cell does not remove the explicit method's event-rate restriction. For a point population capturing fixed donor masses at rate λ, a forward-Euler stage gives capture-count variance μ(1−μ), where μ = λΔt. A nonnegative distribution therefore requires μ ≤ 1, however little particle number that population carries. Both Euler stages must remain realizable; sufficiently concentrated cell populations can impose this event-rate restriction. Very small tail populations can consequently force short steps even when their contribution to the water budget is negligible.

The [finite-time integration method](collision_time_integration.md) now advances the Poisson capture process directly for constant rates and a uniformized birth process for size-dependent rates, with first-exit accounting and separate probability and mass-moment truncation bounds. Its [completed controls](collision_jump_results.md) pass both six-case matrices. The matched analytic control takes eight macrosteps and about 1.3 seconds in its recorded execution. The [source ledger](collision_time_integration_sources.json) distinguishes the published methods from this local adaptation. Closed aggregation additionally needs paired donor depletion and evolving collision rates, leaving that nonlinear problem to be tested independently.

The beta and entropy candidates described here remain component studies. The subsequent [event solver](event_column_methods.md) takes a different route through donor-depleting closed boxes and the complete prescribed column; its completed runs still fail lunar volume convergence. Aggregate geometry, whole-aggregate fracture and resolved moisture histories remain separate physical uncertainties; these imposed-collector controls do not validate the historical 15–60 cm tail or predictive snowstorm optics.

The [reconstruction-stage verification record](results/collision_repair_checks.json) records 290 passing snow tests and unchanged identities for all 86 snow files bound to the preceding diagnostic checkpoint. Its timing and source hashes belong to that earlier stage. The [finite-time verification record](results/collision_jump_checks.json) records the later implementation, completed controls and updated synthesis separately.
