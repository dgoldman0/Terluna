# Quadratic aggregate geometry above 1 cm

This single physical sensitivity asks whether the coupled lunar column changes when aggregate mass grows as diameter squared above a chosen 1 cm join. The completed calculation reduces upper-domain mass loss from **60.37% to 14.22%** on the matched 96-pivot grid. It still fails the finite mass-domain check and supplies no usable snow-size forecast. Its [driver](geometry_continuation.py) saved [results/geometry_continuation.json](results/geometry_continuation.json) after completing the integration and checking source and input identities.

The [targeted geometry review](geometry_literature.md) supplies a reason to investigate an exponent near two: evolving aggregate geometry can change the large–small collection asymptote. The exact 1 cm transition used here is a diagnostic choice. The calculation neither models that evolution nor calibrates its transition, and a response to this sensitivity would not establish a realistic giant-flake population.

Below the join, the corrected Brown–Francis relation and the fixed representative dendrite area ratio are unchanged. Above it, the continuous relation is `m(D) = m(0.01 m) × (D / 0.01 m)²`. Diameter changes at a fixed particle mass, so drag, encounter area and diffusional growth respond together through the existing physical laws. Sticking and the inherited smaller-parent chipping rule remain in place. A prescribed asymptotic geometry is the only new physical assumption.

The 96 mass pivots are exactly those of `moon_limited_m96`. This holds the collision allocation and mass-transport grid fixed. It places the largest resolved diameter at approximately **7.079 m**, compared with 10 m for the baseline geometry; the absorbing upper ghost is also reported in the product. The comparison therefore uses an identical mass domain, with geometry-dependent diameter limits. Numerical exit through that domain is recorded separately from snowfall.

The source retains a −15°C cloud top, 100 seed crystals per litre, 3 mm/h vapour supply, 30 height layers, the limited deposition transport, the baseline cloud-top injection velocity and the full 132.589-hour interval. Seed masses and their number distribution are identical because the source particles lie below the join. The output retains native layer populations, time means, integrated freezing-level arrivals, fluxes and the complete water ledger. A failed mass-domain check makes the surviving quantiles unsuitable for snow or optical forecasts; even a passed check requires independent convergence and physical tests. No optical calculation accompanies this sensitivity.

Cheap tests check exact mass-grid and seed parity, unchanged source velocity and duration, continuity of the geometry relation, a whole-column parity case below the join, and rejection after a producer identity change. The product check reconstructs the remaining ice and arriving flux from native arrays, checks the water ledger and verifies source identities; its completed-run result is recorded in the repair checks. The production command, run with one numerical thread when a worker is available, is:

```sh
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m atmosphere.snow.geometry_continuation
```

The supplied ice totals differ because the same maximum vapour supply does not force every layer to consume all of it. The continued geometry deposits 253.41 kg/m² over the full run, versus 306.33 kg/m² for the baseline; injected ice remains 7.2866 kg/m². Upper-domain exit falls from 189.34 to 37.07 kg/m², while freezing-level exit rises from 114.21 to 214.31 kg/m². Its relative water-ledger residual is 7.74 × 10⁻¹². These are full-run budgets; the reported snowfall flux uses the separate gathering interval.

This response follows several coupled consequences of the mass–size change. At fixed mass and area ratio, the inherited drag calculation keeps Reynolds number unchanged while a smaller diameter raises fall speed. Diffusional capacitance and encounter area decrease, and collision speeds and chipping energies change. The control therefore isolates the chosen geometry assumption, with all those consequences retained. It does not isolate aggregation alone. The surviving distribution’s 99th percentile lies on the upper resolved pivot, so its apparently large quantiles cannot be interpreted as evidence that this geometry produces larger real flakes. Independent convergence and physical calibration remain outstanding.
