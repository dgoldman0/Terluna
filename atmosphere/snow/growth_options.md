# Snow growth: reusable models and environmental evidence

The [Earth growth assessment](earth_growth_assessment.md) isolates the late dendritic runaway and narrows the remaining transfer problem. A 240-case ventilation matrix reduces the revised spherical-start crystal from 195.39 to 4.68 mm against a fitted 4.17 mm, but fitted restarts and other habits retain errors. Another 288 humidity and seed-history executions show strong sensitivity to unreported early shape. Prescribing measured dimensions and fall speeds brings conditional mass uptake much closer to the Earth fits; this tests transport while leaving autonomous shape, branching and density evolution unresolved. The reference lunar snow zone overlaps terrestrial reduced-pressure conditions. No absolute lunar size distribution or storm optical forecast is adopted.

The subsequent [native growth comparison](mcsnow_growth.md) has now executed both McSnow source versions and growth packages. It finds regime-specific improvements and substantial remaining growth failures; the review below records the earlier source and environmental assessment.

This 22 October 2026 review supports the [multi-temperature and ventilation controls](growth_benchmarks.md). It identifies reusable alternatives and additional measurements; no alternative model was newly executed or adopted. The [model source register](growth_model_sources.json) and [environmental source register](growth_environment_sources.json) preserve retrieval extent, exact versions, checksums and unresolved access failures.

## Reusable model improvements

The immediate candidate is McSnow's habit-dependent primary-crystal growth. Welss et al. compare revised intrinsic growth functions, deposition density, branching and ventilation against the Takahashi experiments. Their IGF2+ variant changes the small-particle rule and delays branching to a 200 μm equatorial radius; the intrinsic growth function near 258 K remains unchanged. Some choices were motivated by these same measurements, so this comparison is not an independent validation set. A matched isolated-crystal comparison could test whether these existing choices improve the present failure before changing the whole lunar column. The earlier discrepancy between our McSnow reconstruction and its archived author output remains unresolved. [Welss et al. (2024)](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023MS003805).

A substantive newer option is McSnow's stochastic aggregate geometry, published in June 2026. Its geometric simulations cover 2–2048 monomers with mean sizes of 50–500 μm and several habits. Span, area and aspect ratio vary jointly, providing an alternative to our uniform contact offset. The scheme redraws aggregate geometry without retaining parent structure, while deposition keeps aggregate shape/area ratios fixed; fracture and detailed field validation remain open. Its microwave-scattering database does not supply visible snow optics directly. [Seifert et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.16993/tellus.4127).

The exact [versioned archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17432967) was downloaded and its published MD5 verified. Public McSnow master still points to the older inspected revision, so updating that checkout would miss this implementation. Selected source inspection found Earth gravity in more than one routine, prescribed standalone atmospheric fields and a hydrodynamic-breakup gate excluding ice. The source needs an explicit lunar adaptation. The small source archive is retained in the ignored cache; the multi-gigabyte synthetic geometry database was not downloaded. Source files use BSD-3-Clause headers; dataset metadata separately specifies CC-BY-4.0.

CARMA offers a useful planetary cloud comparison at a different level of detail. Its terrestrial-exoplanet study varies pressure over 0.25–4 bar and gravity over 0.71–2 Earth gravities, using reduced-density spherical ice. Lower gravity increases residence time and effective ice radius within that tested range; lunar gravity remains outside it. A 2026 CAM6–CARMA application reaches 36.5-day rotation but addresses bulk cloud/climate response with prescribed sea-surface conditions. Neither study tests lunar crystal habits or aggregate strength. Exact configuration/access limitations from our earlier CARMA audit remain unresolved. [Yang et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.03570), [Yang et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.03767).

ExoLyn was screened as a steady vertical cloud model with representative particle size and multicomponent condensation/coagulation. That formulation does not provide the resolved ice axes and aggregate distribution needed here; full-paper retrieval failed, so this remains a scope screen. The previously examined newer SCALE chamber fork deliberately fixes shape and disables collisions. Newer dates alone therefore do not resolve the present growth problem. [ExoLyn primary description](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/abs/2024/11/aa51112-24/aa51112-24.html), [SCALE chamber source](https://github.com/Shima-Lab/SCALE-SDM_ICMW2024-Pi-Mixed-Phase_Wang2025).

## Newly recovered growth measurements

The most immediately useful acquisition is Takahashi's 169 individual measurements, distributed in Fuchs et al.'s public archive with Takahashi's permission. The [parser](takahashi_measurements.py) checks the workbook's published checksum, retains Excel row identities and converts mass explicitly. The author plotting code establishes minutes and full-axis millimetres; all numeric masses agree with the workbook's scientific-notation strings and the original paper's grams convention. Seventy-nine rows have no polar-axis measurement, and those entries remain missing. The [derived product](results/takahashi_measurements.json) carries original attribution, CC-BY-4.0 credit, source identity and the transformations made here. [Dataset](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15804055).

Within our diagnostic −14.4 ± 0.5 °C selection, two 30-minute observations have dimensions 2.41 and 3.77 mm and masses 0.0403 and 0.0662 mg. Their actual temperatures are −14.0 and −14.6 °C. One different entry, row 72, stores a 0.8923 mm polar dimension that conflicts with the corresponding original figure and neighbouring values. It is retained and flagged, with its polar dimension and dependent size/shape quantities excluded from calibration pending source verification. These are observed values at nearby temperatures, not confidence bounds for the −14.4 °C fitted curve. The large native-model discrepancy survives that distinction. A measured three-minute crystal at −8.7 °C also supplies an admissible spheroid initialization, unlike the mean −8.6 °C fit. Other measured triplets still exceed bulk density when mapped to a spheroid, demonstrating that representation limits are not confined to fitted curves.

## Closest environmental controls found

| Evidence | Relevance to aerial lunar winter | Remaining mismatch or access limit |
|---|---|---|
| Bailey–Hallett static-filament experiments | Reduced pressure and weak airflow; retrieved 2002 conference work spans 500–150 hPa and −20 to −70 °C. The later 2004 article's indexed text identifies a −20 °C, 550 hPa case. | Earth gravity and small crystals; 2004 original/Table 1 remains inaccessible, and no plotted rates were digitized. The 2002 paper is a separate source. |
| Gonda 1976 free-fall experiments | Pressure, gas and size change initial habits, challenging a temperature-only habit rule. | Helium/argon rather than N₂/O₂; roughly 20–50 μm crystals during the first two minutes. |
| Matsuo–Fukuta 1987 and Takahashi 2018 | Water-subsaturated growth and branching provide humidity/ventilation controls. | Earth gravity; original papers retrieved, but additional tables/figures have not been converted into numerical benchmark inputs. |
| Fuchs 2025 seeded natural stratus | Observed major-axis growth 0.17–0.81 μm/s at −5.1 to −8.3 °C; high ice concentrations reduce growth through competition for water. A sample with 1058 particle-time records and cloud variables was retrieved. | Seeding, atmospheric motion and variable exposure require a matched experiment; the sample was inspected, not replayed. |

Sources: [Bailey–Hallett 2002](https://ams.confex.com/ams/pdfpapers/42237.pdf), [Bailey–Hallett 2004](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/atsc/61/5/1520-0469_2004_061_0514_grahoi_2.0.co_2.xml), [Gonda 1976](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmsj1965/54/4/54_4_233/_article), [Matsuo–Fukuta 1987](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/mripapers/38/4/38_4_247/_article/-char/en), [Takahashi 2018](https://ams.confex.com/ams/15CLOUD15ATRAD/webprogram/Manuscript/Paper347181/105TAKAHASHI.pdf), [Fuchs 2025](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/25/12177/2025/).

Connolly et al.'s chamber aggregation study encountered a related limitation: observed aspect ratios disagreed with Chen–Lamb growth, and adjusting the intrinsic growth ratio changed inferred sticking efficiency substantially. Near −15 °C, changing the ratio from 0.45 to 0.55 altered inferred efficiency by about a factor of three. Their efficiencies therefore cannot independently calibrate sticking while our crystal geometry is unresolved. A later natural-cloud radar study inferred growth from about 0.75 to 5 mm over less than 500 m and ten minutes near −15 °C; it provides a useful aggregate-population target with retrieval/scattering assumptions attached. [Connolly et al. (2012)](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/12/2055/2012/), [Barrett et al. (2019)](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/19/5753/2019/).

No directly matched sustained lunar-gravity, N₂/O₂ vapour-growth dataset was acquired. The earlier low-gravity airflow paper remains abstract-only, while the retrieved ISS experiments grow ice from liquid and were excluded from vapour calibration. The next useful comparison is the existing McSnow growth treatment against the same mass and axis observations, with low-pressure and changing-humidity measurements reserved for transfer tests. Aggregate geometry can follow once the primary-crystal comparison is credible.
