# Earth crystal growth and transfer to the Open Moon

The [2024 source growth trace](benchmark_growth_trace.md) now identifies how mean ventilation amplifies the numerical-archive discrepancy: it speeds mass accumulation along essentially the same IGF2+ shape-versus-mass path. The native crystal reaches the archived ten-minute mass at 535.677 s, with width only 1.21% high and thickness 2.09% low, compared with a 25.91% width excess at ten minutes. An archived three-minute restart retains 43.21% excess final mass. Removing only the extra oblate mean term reduces that seed-trajectory mass error from 49.10% to 9.21%; the physical directional-radius control worsens it. The same uptake control reaches 5.64 mm at thirty minutes under exact native water saturation, with mass 26.0% high. These 60 executions preserve the original benchmark and show why a rate intervention alone does not establish the missing joint mass-and-shape agreement.

The [archived-crystal drag trace](benchmark_drag_trace.md) separates the saved-speed discrepancy from the growth comparison. Native geometry and drag agree with the authors' Python helper for the supplied states. Figures 10 and 13 do not use the saved speed field, and excluding it still leaves substantial mass-and-axis errors. The solid-envelope sensitivity is not adopted; the generating configuration remains unidentified.

The [23 October benchmark reconstruction](benchmark_reproduction.md) adds 709 source, saturation, timestep and duration controls. The author archive still differs from the identified public-source candidates, including selected fixed-state fall speeds. With exact native water saturation the revised thirty-minute plate reaches 113.52 mm against a fitted 4.17 mm; deleting both mean-ventilation operations yields 3.84 mm but only 56.6% of fitted mass. These are documented candidate extensions, not an exact paper reproduction. The earlier Murphy–Koop-saturation interventions below remain separately identified.

The large late-growth error now has a demonstrated cause: McSnow's extra mean ventilation term for thin plates. Removing it reduces the revised spherical-seed crystal at −14.4 °C from 195.39 to 5.88 mm at thirty minutes. Removing the additional directional correction gives 4.68 mm, against the laboratory fit of 4.17 mm. The closer result does **not** establish a universal repair. Other starting shapes and temperatures retain substantial errors, and the independent humidity experiment is highly sensitive to unreported seed geometry.

The conditional transport and facet calculations make the remaining problem more specific. With observed dimensions and fall speeds supplied, standard vapor and heat transport gives much closer masses, while the warm column still grows too little along its long axis relative to its width. Explicit face-transport tests now show that uniform solid faces with common-corner step sources fail during warm-column growth, while inherited equal-face parameters fail two resolved cold-chamber windows under a common-corner hypothesis. Prescribed thin rims change the rates substantially but do not jointly repair mass and both axes. Predicting dimensions, branching and deposition density remains a major difficulty; transport coefficients and published-reference comparisons also need reconciliation. A spatial diffusion component now resolves finite cavity widths, reproduces independent sphere and cylinder controls, and exposes a substantial error in the earlier thin-ring resistance approximation. Newer facet-specific kinetics remove the inherited cold symmetry, but full-history and local-supply checks reject an apparent joint match. Conservative surface-transfer tests now show that assumed micrometre-scale migration can redirect water to the tips with little change in total uptake, while nanometre-scale migration barely changes the result. The later sheath geometry, the unmeasured prism migration distance and the full growth history remain unresolved; no autonomous Earth law or physical lunar snow distribution is established.

## What the ventilation controls establish

The [240-case product](results/ventilation_benchmarks.json) compares six combinations of mean and directional ventilation, both native growth packages, five temperatures, two initial histories and two timesteps for each history. A [separate patched build](mcsnow_ventilation_controls.py) records actual pre-step source terms. Unchanged settings reproduce every available matching frozen baseline: 33 executions and 128 physical output files, including four matched failures. The frozen native sources and [85-execution comparison](mcsnow_growth.md) remain intact.

The refined revised-package comparison at −14.4 °C is:

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Laboratory fit | 4.175 | 1 | 1 |
| Spherical seed, native | 195.389 | 1776.95 | 0.979 |

| Spherical seed, plate term removed and unit directional ratio | 4.676 | 1.168 | 1.113 |

The native final vapor enhancement is about 1097; removing the extra plate term reduces it to 1.883. This is a directly measured change in the source routine. Directional suppression by itself leaves a 13.05 mm spherical-start crystal and a severely oversized, timestep-sensitive fitted restart. All controls removing the extra plate term complete. Across these controls, the largest endpoint timestep changes are 1.27% in mass and 1.16% in maximum dimension, much smaller than the principal empirical discrepancies. These are refinement measurements, not a proof of asymptotic convergence.

There is a physical reason for the failure. At fixed basal span and relative airflow, the extra term is proportional to thickness/span raised to −3/4. It therefore diverges as a plate becomes thinner, whereas transport to a finite-span thin plate has a finite limit. This deduction concerns the extrapolation of the [published mean-ventilation expression](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023MS003805), not a claim that the authors validated decimetre crystals. Removing the term exposes a further deficiency: the remaining equivalent-volume-sphere enhancement tends to one in that same limit and loses real plate ventilation. A bounded habit-specific transport treatment is preferable to either an arbitrary size cap or adopting the deletion without further testing.

The directional correction has its own uncertainty. [Sulia and Harrington (2011), section 2.5](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011JD016298), note that ventilation may already influence some observations used for the inherent growth ratios. Unit directional ventilation is therefore a defensible sensitivity. It does not establish that all such ratios already contain the correction. Changing the native volume^(1/3) denominator to a physical equivalent-volume radius also increases the runaway in the initial diagnostic; that source/paper discrepancy is not its cure.

The five-temperature results prevent selecting a switch on the dendrite alone. At −5.3 °C, removing both shape additions and the directional correction from the revised spherical case reduces mass but makes the column only 1.040 mm long against 1.861 mm fitted. A fitted restart reaches 0.573 mm. Growth package and prior shape still determine how mass is distributed between axes. At ten minutes, differentiating the separate −14.4 °C fitted axis histories implies a logarithmic polar-to-equatorial growth ratio of 0.510. The revised intrinsic growth ratio is 0.279 before directional ventilation further reduces it. This is a mismatch with the fitted shape evolution, not an independently measured facet coefficient; it explains why fixing mean uptake alone leaves a habit problem.

## Humidity evidence and unknown initial shape

The [2018 observation transcription](growth_humidity_observations.json) adds thirteen ten-minute crystal diameters with printed vapor-density excesses. These measurements were not used to choose the interventions. The [240 executions](results/growth_humidity_validation.json) compare the four plate/directional combinations with 5, 10 and 20 µm initial full spans, selected timestep refinements and temperature perturbations. The experiments report seed sizes but not their initial mass or aspect ratio. The initial spheres are declared assumptions, not reconstructed experimental states. [Takahashi's 2018 conference paper](https://ams.confex.com/ams/15CLOUD15ATRAD/webprogram/Manuscript/Paper347181/105TAKAHASHI.pdf).

For the nominal −14.3 °C, 0.17 g/m³ vapor-density-excess datum, the observed diameter is 1.46 mm. The native revised package from a 10 µm sphere gives 0.337 mm; removing both ventilation contributions gives about 0.326 mm. The timestep refinement changes dimensions by much less than this discrepancy. Selected ±0.3 °C probes also do not explain it.

An additional 48 [seed-shape and package executions](results/growth_humidity_history.json) show why that mismatch cannot yet be attributed uniquely to the growth law. At the same initial 10 µm basal span and bulk density, the combined intervention gives about 1.604 mm from an initial thickness/span of 0.3, but 4.688 mm from 0.1. Those seeds have different initial masses, explicitly derived from their geometry. The moderately flattened seed crosses the revised package's 400 µm full-span branching threshold after approximately 267 s; the spherical seed never crosses it in ten minutes. Two thin-seed native cases fail at both timesteps, giving four explicit failed executions; their partial trajectories are retained.

Neither the sphere's failure nor the flattened seed's agreement selects the physical initialization. The result diagnoses strong history dependence involving shape, the empirical growth package and the branching threshold. A humidity-dependent law needs either measured early states or demonstrably credible early shape evolution before this experiment can provide an exact trajectory validation. Its diameter alone also cannot validate mass or thickness.

## Conditional transport closes much of the mass discrepancy

The [transport replay](crystal_transport_replay.py) follows the separation used by [Westbrook and Heymsfield (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1175/JAS-D-11-017.1): prescribe the observed dimension and fall-speed histories, integrate mass uptake from the fitted three-minute mass, and compare the later mass. This avoids assuming an unobserved nucleation shape. The calculation uses the project's air transport and Murphy–Koop saturation functions; it is a new implementation of the method, not an exact reproduction of that paper's curves.

The [product](results/crystal_transport_replay.json) evaluates 61 geometry/ventilation combinations at ten temperatures, with 52 complete and nine explicitly outside a selected source correlation's range. The six compact or thick-plate cases give endpoint mass/fit ratios of 0.931–1.170 in the diffusion-only comparison. The −5.3 °C column with the published Ji–Wang column enhancement gives 1.146. The −14.4 °C dendrite scenarios give 1.115–1.459 using branched capacitance and the broad-branch ventilation correlation. Their arm widths are unmeasured geometric scenarios, not confidence bounds or fitted parameters.

The calculation carries full basal span and polar length consistently. Hexagonal-prism capacitance uses the source's sampled aspect-ratio range. Branched capacitance retains its fixed thickness/span assumption and a declared arm-width range. Ji–Wang Reynolds number uses basal width even for columns, and correlations are rejected outside their published Reynolds intervals; the source idealizations still limit applicability inside those intervals. [Westbrook et al. (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1175/2007JAS2315.1), [Ji and Wang (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0469(1999)056%3C0829:VCFFIC%3E2.0.CO;2), [Wang and Ji (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0469(1997)054%3C2261:NSOTDU%3E2.0.CO;2).

The transport comparison changes more than geometry. The in-house mixture diffusivity is about 15% below McSnow's native value at the five shared temperatures, while conductivity differs by roughly 1–1.5%. The Ji–Wang calculations used Schmidt number 0.63; applying their polynomial to the calculated mixture values near 0.74 is a similarity assumption. Repeating the ventilation evaluation at 0.63 reduces the column ratio to 1.122 and the two dendrite ratios to 1.043–1.366. Replacing only the in-house vapor diffusivity and conductivity with their native McSnow values raises endpoint mass by 6.7–13.7% in the tested valid cases. The dendrite range becomes 1.189–1.557 and the column ratio becomes 1.248. These are transport sensitivities, not new fits.

The comparison with the published reference also remains to be reconciled. Westbrook and Heymsfield report appreciably larger excess growth at −3.7 and −10.6 °C than the present fitted-mass ratios of 1.170 and 1.016. Different fitted inputs, starting observations and transport conventions may contribute. The present closer ratios therefore do not establish improved accuracy over their calculation.

The comparison supports continuing with the ordinary transport framework under the tested conditions. It leaves shape prediction untested because shape is supplied as input. It also does not establish a universally correct capacitance, drag law or ventilation correlation for evolving branches and aggregates.

Four of the five rejected three-minute fitted states are admissible under the original laboratory geometry. Takahashi's enclosing hexagonal-prism volume is 1.24049 times the spheroid volume at the same full axes. The previously rejected −8.6, −12.2, −18.2 and −22.0 °C states then have densities of approximately 822, 884, 847 and 870 kg/m³. The −16.5 °C fit remains inconsistent at about 1021 kg/m³. The [geometry audit](results/crystal_transport_replay.json) preserves both conventions. No measurements were clipped or silently corrected. A native spheroid cannot simply use the hexagonal volume while keeping its old axis reconstruction. Future comparisons need an explicit observation-to-model mapping or a geometry-aware state. Thin planar thickness was inferred from mass and basal area, so these quantities are not three independent measurements.

## What can transfer to the lunar winter

The reference Open Moon column has local pressures of approximately 659, 589, 525, 467 and 414 hPa at 0, −5, −10, −15 and −20 °C. Its great geometric altitude does not itself require different microscopic growth physics. Temperature, pressure, composition and relative flow control transport; gravity changes settling, ventilation, residence and collisions. These pressures overlap terrestrial lower-pressure experiments.

The [additional evidence register](growth_transfer_observations.json) preserves eight −20 °C, 550 hPa effective-uptake measurements, twenty-four older size/mass/speed endpoints and a reanalysis of a natural-cloud sample. The low-pressure effective capacitances include attachment and geometry effects; they cannot be substituted for geometric capacitance. Some older humidity inputs were inferred from growth. The natural-cloud rates divide observed sizes by estimated residence time and lack a matched forcing history. Each restriction is retained beside the data. [Bailey and Hallett (2006)](https://ams.confex.com/ams/pdfpapers/113510.pdf), [Matsuo and Fukuta (1987)](https://doi.org/10.2467/mripapers.38.247), [Fuchs et al. (2025)](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/25/12177/2025/).

A transferable autonomous model still needs humidity-dependent facet attachment coupled consistently to pressure-dependent transport, mass/axis evolution, and a branching-density treatment checked against early and late Earth states. The retrieved [Harrington et al. coefficient component](https://doi.org/10.26208/s7de-et44) provides a concrete reusable part. Its coefficients must be paired with the facet relation dc/da = alpha_c/alpha_a, rather than inserted into the old aspect-ratio growth relation unchanged. Source identities and numerical-domain issues are retained in the [physics source register](growth_physics_sources.json).

## A tested facet-growth component, with explicit boundaries

The [local attachment interface](facet_attachment.py) compiles the original published Fortran coefficient routines, retaining the original bytes in the ignored cache. It removes two unused assignments that read uninitialized variables and adds guarded inputs. Both single- and double-precision wrappers are compared with their respective source demonstrations. This establishes interface behavior and numerical identity, not empirical growth accuracy. The source demonstration covers spherical crystals. Additional tests find cancellation near spheres in its capacitance evaluation, including a valid double-precision integration state. A separate build uses algebraically equivalent, stable spheroid formulas; checks establish radius bounds, scale dependence, boundary monotonicity and agreement with the native formula away from the singular numerical limit. The [component audit](results/facet_attachment_checks.json) preserves source/demo comparisons. Original-source redistribution terms remain separate from the project's own wrapper.

The [compact facet prototype](facet_growth.py) couples these coefficients to axis-specific vapor and thermal resistance and the facet growth relation. It uses constant dense ice and zero ventilation, so it avoids inventing an unsupported branching-density function. It also retains the equilibrium vapor density required by the dimensionally consistent mass equation; a printed omission in the ICTG equation image is documented in the product. The axis update conserves deposited volume directly. The coefficient approximation uses bulk thermal resistance internally, so coupling it to the small-particle thermal-jump correction remains an approximation rather than exact implicit DiSKICE parity.

The [first prototype comparison](results/facet_growth.json) runs the four 2018 observations with vapor-density excess at or below 0.05 g/m³. Twenty-four shape/mechanism combinations are each integrated at two tolerances. All complete. Refining integration tolerances changes diameter by less than 3×10⁻⁷ relative and closes the independent mass/volume ledger to 8.3×10⁻⁹, but the physical sensitivities remain substantial. For the 0.26 mm observation at nominal −14.3 °C, the ledge-nucleation control gives 0.071, 0.147 and 0.291 mm from seed thickness/span values 1, 0.3 and 0.1. The dislocation-growth control gives 0.058–0.083 mm. Selecting the favorable seed or mechanism would be calibration.

These observations involved suspended falling crystals; stagnant flow, dense growth and unknown initial geometry therefore remain explicit approximations. The component supplies a usable alternative to temperature-only habit ratios, but this restricted experiment does not select a complete replacement. Branching and hollowing remain unresolved. The subsequent implicit component below supplies a consistent numerical coupling for surface humidity and ventilation under declared conductance factors.

Even successful local deposition checks would not validate many-hour moist trajectories or aggregate size distributions. Finite water supply, collision/contact geometry, fracture and observed cloud exposure remain required for lunar populations and optics. The current evidence supports repairing the transport and shape treatments in separate steps; it does not support promoting the historical 15–60 cm tail to a prediction.

## Implicit attachment, heat and ventilation coupling

The [implicit component](facet_coupling.py) solves the surface-humidity equations from Zhang and Harrington (2014) together with the same latent-heat balance used for mass uptake. Its two surface humidities determine the attachment coefficients, and the resulting uptake determines the thermal reduction of the vapour driving force. Bracketed scalar roots close both dependencies. The later published facet relation distributes the deposited volume between the axes under its assumption that steps originate near crystal edges. That shape approximation is separate from solving the two surface balances. The original semianalytic helper remains available as a comparison and supplies its declared air properties and characteristic supersaturations. [Source equations and retrieval record](facet_coupling_sources.json).

Ventilation changes the continuum conductance for vapour and heat while leaving the molecular attachment resistance intact. This permits independent, finite ventilation factors without multiplying a surface-limited growth rate indiscriminately. It does not choose a drag law or a correlation for an evolving habit. The [432-state audit](results/facet_coupling_audit.json) spans four temperatures, two pressures, three sizes, three shapes, two attachment mechanisms and three supersaturations; each state also has a factor-two conductance control. The largest normalized surface or heat balance residual is 7.88×10⁻¹². Twenty-two focused tests check molecular flux matching, mass and volume consistency, spherical symmetry, equilibrium, the independent spherical dislocation solution and attachment-limited ventilation. A root-bracket rounding error exposed by the grid was corrected algebraically and retained as a regression test.

For the 342 states where the native minimum attachment coefficient is inactive, implicit/native mass-rate ratios range from 0.791 to 1.132; the central 90% of this deliberately sampled grid lies between 0.963 and 1.011. The facet-rate-ratio comparison is less stable, ranging from 0.139 to 1.253. These are deterministic numerical sensitivities, not confidence intervals. Removing the source's minimum coefficient of 10⁻⁶ produces much smaller growth in some cold, weakly supersaturated states. Thus consistent coupling principally clarifies shape evolution and very weak growth; it supplies no evidence that the bulk Earth size discrepancy has disappeared.

The retrieved original 2020 numerical archive also requires a version qualification. Its surface-humidity files correspond to the later manuscript's Figure 2, whose caption specifies a mechanism exponent of 10. Applying the attachment equation to those archived curves yields nearly constant inferred resistance with exponent 15 instead. The archive, caption and generating implementation therefore cannot yet be treated as an exactly matched reference. Original bytes are preserved; neither the exponent nor the curve has been silently relabeled. The archive is numerical evidence, separate from the Earth measurements.

## Explicit face transport rejects two tempting shortcuts

The new [face-growth diagnostic](uniform_face_growth.py) implements the coupled cylindrical-face equations from Nelson and Baker (1996), using the already reproduced geometry functions and the inherited attachment inputs. It computes the vapour consumed by both faces at each candidate step source and includes local latent heating. Sources may be at the common edge or the face centres. This is an explicit continuum approximation; internal crystal conduction, substrate heat transfer and molecular jump corrections remain outside it. The complete 2026 model is still unretrieved. [Original theory, equations 23–26 and Appendix C](https://www.redmondphysicalsciences.com/nelson-baker1996TheoFram.pdf), [subsequent mechanism analysis](https://www.redmondphysicalsciences.com/nelson2001GrowMech.pdf), [source record](uniform_face_sources.json).

For each face, the solver uses the local supersaturation at its step source. In dimensionless form its two balances are

$$s_i+\sum_j L_{ij}\alpha_j(s_j)s_j=S_\infty.$$

Here S∞ is the prescribed ambient ice supersaturation, and the index i identifies a step-source position. The matrix includes the effects of prism and basal vapour uptake and their local latent heating. At a common corner, the two local supersaturations must coincide. A necessary condition for a uniformly advancing face is that its prescribed flux can also be supplied at its centre and edge with an attachment efficiency no greater than one. Negative surface supersaturation under a positive deposition flux, or a required efficiency above one, rejects that uniform-face state. Passing the sampled checks does not establish feasibility everywhere on the face.

The [assessment](results/uniform_face_assessment.json) requests 738 measured-shape comparisons across source locations, mechanism sensitivities and supplied ventilation. Of these, 534 calculate successfully at two solver tolerances; 180 requests lack a supported ventilation habit and 24 exceed the geometry fit's aspect-ratio range. The maximum refined balance residual is 1.31×10⁻¹³, and rate changes under refinement are below 5.34×10⁻⁹. Among 516 completed solid-face cases, 387 fail a necessary uniform-growth condition. This is a designed hypothesis screen, not an estimate of the frequency of hollow crystals. The largest mean-free-path/semiaxis ratio is 0.0131; the continuum approximation is recorded separately from numerical convergence. The published analytic geometry fits differ from eleven original numerical-table points by up to 5.46%, much more than their machine reproduction error. The older printed prism fit labels its logarithm as base ten, whereas the pinned 2026 implementation uses the natural logarithm and gives the stated table agreement. The implementation is retained unchanged; the source discrepancy is recorded without assuming an author-confirmed correction.

For the −5.3 °C column, simply replacing the earlier separate-axis approximation by common-corner solid-face growth reverses the directional error. Under supplied Ji–Wang ventilation, the calculated polar/equatorial rate ratio is 7.82–22.06 times the fitted ratio across the three tested ages. The basal centre becomes undersaturated while the rim grows. The equations therefore reject their own uniformly advancing basal-face assumption. The preceding model's low directional ratio cannot be repaired by adopting this solid-face solution as a physical trajectory.

## The cold control exposes an inherited symmetry assumption

The 2021 source routine explicitly sets the prism and basal characteristic supersaturations equal below −30 °C. At −50 °C both are 0.1731575. If the same mechanism operates at their common corner, their attachment efficiencies and normal growth rates are identical: **da/dt = dc/dt** in the cylindrical convention used here. Changing crystal size, humidity, a common ventilation factor or the prescribed basal rim does not remove that equality. This statement concerns the inherited parameters in the common-corner hypothesis; the earlier spheroidal approximation uses separate local humidities and need not have that invariant. [Source publication](https://doi.org/10.1175/JAS-D-20-0307.1).

|---|---:|---:|

## Spatial diffusion resolves finite rims and local growth

The [spatial component](spatial_diffusion.py) solves quasistatic vapour diffusion around a prescribed cylinder with an optional open cavity. Each exposed surface patch has its own supersaturation and attachment rate. The calculation therefore permits a growing rim and a nearly inactive interior without imposing a uniform end-face flux. It also calculates latent heating for an isothermal crystal, using the exterior heat conductance of the same geometry. This assumption represents rapid internal heat conduction; a supporting substrate, ventilation and molecular jump corrections remain absent. The cavity is supplied, and no profile or rim evolution is yet predicted.

Libbrecht's quantitative models provide a relevant alternative to bulk habit closures: they combine spatial diffusion, facet-dependent attachment and, in the later version, surface diffusion and moving terraces. The present component implements the stationary diffusion/attachment problem with an independent conservative finite-volume method. It does not reproduce those cellular automata or their surface-diffusion rules. Published equations and Earth experiments were located, but an original executable implementation was not found in the papers, author publication list or targeted code search. ## Surface transport changes where the incoming water becomes ice

The [surface-transfer assessment](results/surface_transfer_assessment.json) tests a mechanism that a local attachment coefficient cannot represent: molecules arriving on a prism face can travel along that face and join an adjoining thin end rim. The gas loses water at the donor surface, while the rim receives exactly that captured mass. The calculated diffusion field and latent heating respond to the resulting uptake. This separates a possible increase in length from an increase in total mass. Nelson and Swanson's observations motivate the mechanism, but their quantitative facet-spreading fit uses an unmeasured front-height profile; it does not supply a calibrated prism-face transport law for these columns. [Nelson and Swanson (2019), section 4.3 and Appendix B6](https://doi.org/10.5194/acp-19-15285-2019).

The present [diagnostic](surface_transfer.py) prescribes a half-depth hollow cylinder, an absorbing rough end rim and an exponential probability of reaching that rim from either adjoining prism surface. The migration distance and edge transmission are supplied sensitivities. Cell-averaged probabilities preserve the same physical collection distance when the grid changes. This differs from the 2015 intrafacet terrace rule, whose grid representation scales a molecular migration length by the ratio of pixel size to molecular step height. Neither rule measures the unreported prism migration distance. The 14 nm case represents an exploratory nanometre scale, while 1–10 µm cases examine the much larger distances suggested by some basal-face studies; those basal estimates are mutually disputed and cannot be adopted as prism measurements. [Source identities, parameter definitions and reading scope](surface_transport_sources.json).

The [parameter audit](results/crystal_parameter_audit.json) tests whether a geometric conversion can explain the remaining warm-column discrepancy. These mappings preserve different geometric quantities; none reproduces the three-dimensional diffusion field of a hexagonal sheath.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Circumscribed | 0.668 | 0.749 | 1.255 |

| Inscribed | 0.857 | 0.875 | 1.179 |

The radius convention changes the comparison, but it does not account for the whole discrepancy. A separate calculation checks whether the newer microscopy parameters can be combined with the broad-facet nucleation law. Classical circular-nucleus theory gives `sigma0 = pi*kappa^2*Omega/(3*k_B^2*T^2)` for polynuclear normal growth, where kappa is the equilibrium step energy per unit length and Omega is molecular area. The relation is printed explicitly by Libbrecht and Rickerby; their experiment measured basal growth, while this comparison supplies the prism molecular area reported by Miyamoto and colleagues. The comparison uses the broad-facet law, whose barrier can be interpreted thermodynamically, rather than a geometry-dependent narrow-facet reduction. [Libbrecht and Rickerby (2011), page 4](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~atomic/publist/1110.5828v1.pdf).

At the explicitly matched temperature −15.3 °C, the 2023 broad-prism law has fractional `sigma0 = 0.03614`, equivalent under that relation to a step energy of 2.30×10⁻¹² J/m. The microscopy paper's reported peak is 8.5×10⁻¹⁰ J/m, about 370 times larger. Substituting that value into the same relation gives `sigma0 ≈ 4955`, approximately 137,000 times the broad-law barrier. A mononuclear convention changes the barrier by a factor of three and cannot reconcile this difference. The printed units, molecular area and peak temperature were checked visually. [Miyamoto et al. (2022), equations 4–5 and Figure 7](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.cgd.2c00851), [source record and reading scope](crystal_parameter_sources.json).

This incompatibility prevents using those two inferences as one equilibrium parameter without further explanation. It does not identify an error in either publication. The microscopy analysis obtains local humidity from step speed and infers step energy through spiral spacing; the nucleation law describes a different step-production mechanism and experimental population. Substrates, pressure, surface state and those inversions require examination before a combined law is justified. The measured elementary-step velocity remains distinct from either inferred normal growth or equilibrium step energy. No rescaled parameter is adopted as a growth law.

## Chamber humidity can couple the two parameter inferences

The [humidity audit](results/step_humidity_audit.json) tests a possible explanation for the step-energy incompatibility. In the 2022 experiment, the kinetic coefficient is obtained from the slope of step speed against nominal chamber supersaturation, assuming that local and reservoir humidity coincide at low supersaturation. That coefficient then determines the local humidity used to infer step energy from spiral spacing. A bias in the first humidity assumption therefore affects both inferred parameters. Libbrecht's earlier reanalysis of basal-step measurements explicitly examined this problem: neighbouring crystals and the chamber-scale diffusion path can lower local humidity even when the step's own growth is slow. That reanalysis estimates a basal migration length near 10 nm, with roughly a factor-three uncertainty, and does not establish a correction to the later prism experiment. [Libbrecht (2015), equations 2–13](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06609), [Miyamoto et al. (2022), equations 1 and 5](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.cgd.2c00851).

For a supplied humidity ratio `f = sigma_true/sigma_inferred`, preserving the measured lateral speed requires `beta_true = beta_inferred/f`. Preserving spiral spacing also requires `kappa_true = kappa_inferred*log(1+f*sigma_inferred)/log(1+sigma_inferred)`. At low supersaturation, this becomes `kappa_true ≈ f*kappa_inferred`. Thus one unmeasured humidity ratio can change the two inferred parameters in opposite directions while leaving both measured observables unchanged. It also leaves the reconstructed normal growth at that measurement unchanged.

At −15.3 °C, reconciling the two energy inferences in that low-supersaturation limit requires `f = 0.00270`: local supersaturation would be about 0.27% of the inferred value. Using the nominal 44 kJ/mol step-speed fit, the corresponding coefficient becomes 7.23 mm/s instead of 0.0195 mm/s. With unit attachment near a step and one collecting terrace, this is equivalent to a collection length of 13.9 nm under the declared air convention. That proximity to the earlier nanometre estimate is a conditional consistency result, not an independent measurement. Exact logarithmic inversion over nominal supersaturations of 0.1–5% changes the target energy by 0.050–2.47%, while reproducing the supplied velocities and spacings to floating-point precision.

The required depletion must also be supplied by a physical chamber. A planar diffusion sensitivity with paths of 0.2, 2 and 16 mm requires mean attachment coefficients of 0.254, 0.0254 and 0.00317, respectively, all below the molecular upper limit of one. These lengths and collecting areas are supplied alternatives, not dimensions recovered for the 2022 experiment. The observed spiral face alone would require a planar diffusion path of 1.37 m at nominal supersaturation 0.1%, falling to 2.81 cm at 5%; none of those values lies within the supplied 0.2–16 mm paths. Additional growing surfaces are therefore necessary within this planar interpretation. At a 16 mm path, mixing the observed spiral surface with unit-attachment patches would require those patches to cover approximately 0.137–0.314% of the area. That collecting area is unmeasured. The missing experiment is therefore a jointly constrained chamber humidity and step-growth comparison; choosing the humidity ratio solely to match the energy would be circular. No corrected coefficient enters the Earth or lunar growth calculations.

A newer molecular model provides complementary mechanism evidence, without replacing that experiment. Shi and colleagues (2025) use LAMMPS with TIP4P/Ice and mW to follow recrystallization of an artificially enlarged quasi-liquid surface layer in vacuum. Their temperature-dependent facet comparisons distinguish the two molecular descriptions, but they do not prescribe atmospheric vapour supply or jointly reproduce this laboratory crystal's mass and axes. The paper's methods and conclusions were read; its supplementary dataset was not downloaded or run. [Shi et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0240795), [retrieved identities and reading scope](crystal_morphology_sources.json).
