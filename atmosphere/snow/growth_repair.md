# Snow growth repair and terrestrial checks

The new mass-transport scheme repairs a demonstrated source of artificial spreading. In the independent growth-only comparison, the 144-pivot calculation's mass above 5 mm falls from **14.47% to 0.001089%**, with number and water conserved. The reference grown from the original continuous seed distribution ends at about 3.964 mm. Both completed full lunar controls still lose 58–60% of supplied mass through the upper size boundary. This is therefore a measured numerical improvement under fixed physical laws; the remaining spatial error and the uncertain aggregate physics still prevent treating the earlier 15–60 cm values as a calibrated lunar forecast.

Checkpoint **`16cde54`** preserves the earlier audit, targeted literature review, conservative reference, failed lunar domain controls and conditional optical calculations. The following calculations extend that work while retaining its producers and products unchanged.

## What changed and what was tested

The [characteristic comparison](growth_transport.md) isolates deposition from collisions, settling and fresh particle injection. Continuous masses follow the existing diffusional growth and shared vapour supply without repeatedly projecting onto a mass grid. The old first-order transport broadens that distribution even when the timestep is reduced. A grid-matched characteristic control separates this repeated spreading from the initial and final projection errors.

The [limited transport](limited_growth.md) reconstructs the number density at mass-cell faces and restricts face values to the neighboring density range. Its vapour cap and deposited-water ledger use the actual discrete mass gain from those fluxes. The physical growth coefficients are unchanged. This different finite-grid quadrature converges toward the same continuous growth equation and substantially reduces its measured error: excess relative mass variance above the once-projected characteristic reference falls by 93.1% at 144 pivots, with further improvement at 216 pivots. Relative variance means `Var(m)/mean(m)²`. The limited variance is 0.05751, versus 0.03083 for the once-projected reference and 0.02550 for continuous trajectories starting from the same projected seeds. No size ceiling or population clipping produces that reduction.

The experimental column in [column_experiments.py](column_experiments.py) preserves the checkpoint's collision allocation, settling, source flux, integration interval and ledgers. Its default first-order option matches the checkpoint column in the numerical parity control. Its limited-growth option supplies the new flux through the same positive time integrator. Tests cover conservation, finite-domain exits and unchanged boundary forcing. Evaluating the height layers together preserves the exact floating-point results of the individual layer calculations; an independent whole-column test checks identical populations, ledgers and step histories.

The [comparison figure](../../visualization/snow/generated/growth_comparison.png) displays the independent references and both schemes at all three mass resolutions. [Its renderer](../../visualization/snow/README.md#condensation-growth-numerical-controls) checks source identities and reconstructs the plotted moments from the saved products; generated images remain outside Git.

## Full-column controls

The [runner](growth_repair.py) compares the two growth methods under the same prescribed Earth or lunar forcing and records producer/input identities before and after each calculation. These are internal transfer and resolution controls, distinct from a matched published terrestrial experiment. Domain exits represent unresolved numerical mass, not breakup or a physical maximum flake size. The latest completed cases are exported to [results/growth_repair.json](results/growth_repair.json). The separate [final exporter](growth_products.md) preserves reference qualifications and marks every case ineligible for predictive optics. It also reports occupancy of the nominal upper pivot explicitly: the inverse mass–size relation reconstructs 10 m as 9.999999999999998 m, so a literal threshold comparison otherwise excludes that endpoint. Original summaries and numerical producer identities remain intact.

The Earth reference uses a −15°C cloud top, 100 crystals per litre, a 1 mm/h vapour supply and 144 mass pivots. Using discrete mass-pivot quantiles, the limited method changes its mass median from 1.243 to 1.034 mm and mass 99th percentile from 5.914 to 4.923 mm. The arriving water-equivalent flux changes from 1.1914 to 1.1945 mm/h, including injected ice as well as deposited vapour. Both methods have negligible numerical-domain loss. This establishes behavior in the prescribed Earth column, not agreement with an observed storm or an external population model.

The matched heavy lunar controls retain 3 mm/h supply, the corrected aggregate geometry, 30 height layers, a 10 m resolved upper diameter and the full 132.589-hour integration. Both resolutions still fail the finite mass-domain check:

| Mass pivots | First-order upper-domain mass loss | Limited-growth upper-domain mass loss | Limited-growth relative water-ledger residual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 96 | 59.69% | 60.37% | −9.43 × 10⁻¹⁴ |
| 144 | 57.77% | 58.08% | −3.69 × 10⁻¹² |

These losses are fractions of all injected and deposited mass. Accurate conservation and much less isolated deposition spreading therefore coexist with a failed coupled-column domain check. The surviving metre-scale quantiles remain diagnostic outputs. Their change with resolution cannot establish convergence of a distribution that loses most of its mass through an absorbing numerical boundary. The two resolutions also cannot separate remaining growth error, collision-grid spreading and the inherited collection/chipping assumptions. A fully repaired or physically calibrated population has not yet been established.

The separate [collision-allocation diagnostic](collision_projection.py), with its [saved moments](results/collision_projection.json), demonstrates a second source of numerical spreading. When a collector at mass pivot `M` captures mass `s` smaller than the next-pivot spacing `Δ`, assigning the product to neighboring pivots adds `s(Δ − s)` to the physical second-moment change `2Ms`. For the tested approximately 0.2 mm / 15 cm pairs, that excess is 15.005%, 9.524% and 6.148% at 96, 144 and 216 pivots. Mass and the collision’s number change remain correct. The existing analytical coagulation controls demonstrate refinement for simpler kernels; neither they nor this isolated event calculation quantify the effect on the coupled lunar tail. The subsequent [independent collision comparison](collision_reference.md) now measures that spreading directly, while retaining unresolved late-time finite-volume limits.

The separate [geometry sensitivity](geometry_continuation.md) changes only the physical assumption that mass continues as diameter squared above a chosen 1 cm join. It retains exactly the baseline 96 mass pivots, seed forcing and duration, so its resolved upper diameter becomes 7.079 m at the same upper mass. Domain loss falls from **60.37% to 14.22%**, but the case still fails. At fixed mass, changed diameter affects settling, diffusional growth, encounter area and chipping together; this result demonstrates sensitivity to the geometry assumption without identifying a calibrated law or isolating aggregation. Its surviving quantiles remain unsuitable for forecasting snow or optics.

## Published Earth comparisons and physical limits

The [Böhm adapter](boehm.md) was checked against 122 independently compiled cases from the authors' pinned Fortran code, including Earth and lunar gravity. It also reproduces five archived Earth graupel fall-speed cases to their saved precision under the documented source-default surface state. The original archive namelist is absent, so this agreement does not establish exact full-height humidity or source-version identity. Independent collision-trajectory validation remains unperformed.

That adapter does not provide a universal reduction of collisions. Its raw hydrodynamic cross-section ratios suppress some disparate-size encounters and enhance others. For deterministic internal speeds, the source also has an equal-speed discontinuity. Code parity establishes that these effects come from the supplied implementation; it does not establish their applicability to a giant porous snow aggregate. The full-column controls therefore retain the existing collision laws, and adoption of the new hydrodynamics is withheld pending its geometry and normalization checks.

The separate [published Earth growth-and-settling comparison](welss_column.md) is now complete for the Welss et al. `ob_hp0` control. An independent continuous-mass calculation follows the authors’ declared geometry, air profile, source, fall speed and deposition laws, with aggregation and riming disabled. Integrated absolute differences against the archived raw profiles are 0.6645% for number flux, 0.8698% for mass flux, 1.7737% for number density and 0.5473% for mass density. Independent changes to mass quadrature, source-height quadrature, bin integration and ODE accuracy change these four integrated profiles by less than 0.00537%. These comparisons test a separate terrestrial reference; the lunar column has not adopted its physical closures.

Agreement is less close where crystals form. Number-density error is 7.62% across the nucleation belt, and the uppermost populated bin remains 31.2% above the archive after source-height refinement. Its baseline mass flux differs by 17.8%. The exact archived executable remains unidentified, and the deterministic calculation represents continuous injection rather than the source’s five-second stochastic updates. The archived atmosphere supports 5% supersaturation over ice, and source and archive labels support enabled base ventilation. These qualifications and the regional errors remain beside the results in the Earth report.

An isolated [compact-crystal capacitance sensitivity](results/welss_compact_capacitance.json) helps locate part of the source-region difference. The public source’s solid-volume residual can select `C = D/π` through floating-point rounding for nominal compact spheres. Selecting `C = D/2` below its existing compact-sphere threshold reduces source-belt number-density error from 7.62% to 3.14%, and top-bin error from 32.3% to 15.0%. Number flux is unchanged; integrated mass-flux error increases slightly, from 0.8698% to 0.9196%. This one intervention uses the baseline quadratures and tighter ODE tolerance. It neither resolves the residual difference nor identifies the archived producer, and its full quadrature convergence is untested. The original reference and lunar closures remain intact.

Archive structure supports comparison with raw bins: finite endpoint values would have been masked by the examined smoothing pass. A separately bound [archive postprocessor](results/welss_archive_comparison.json) therefore supersedes the exploratory one-sided-filter metrics retained in the frozen physical product. The number-flux values also fit a 31-snapshot rounded mean, including multiples of 31; that supports a five-hour interpretation without identifying exact times. All sampled deterministic trajectories reach ground within 1.69–1.92 hours, so the tested late-time windows coincide for this calculation; an unknown archive window still affects stochastic sampling.

A corrected numerical solver still needs evolving aggregate geometry, meaningful contact strength and largest-remnant breakup, and growth histories supported by the climate. The present chipping rule chiefly removes small fragments and does not calculate failure of a large collector's contact network. The current work therefore separates a demonstrated numerical repair from the physical assumptions that remain to be established. No new storm rendering or validated snow optics product is issued.

The subsequent [collision and moist-exposure analysis](collision_and_exposure.md) measures a large collision-distribution error under the unchanged snow kernel and follows selected kinematic paths through archived storm fields. It also extends the Earth aggregation comparison with independently compiled component checks and a declared-source author-solver rerun. Those results retain their own numerical, source-identity and meteorological limits; the geometry continuation remains an isolated sensitivity and the failed lunar domain controls still preclude a calibrated giant-flake population.
