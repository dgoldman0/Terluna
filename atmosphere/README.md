## New calculation and remaining closure

The thermal column solves its upper temperature without a fixed exobase floor. Its lower temperature/pressure, conductivity proxy, collision cross-section, molecular mixing ratio and heating distribution remain inputs. Since 8 October it radiates in the infrared, CO2's 15 um band, NO's 5.3 um band and atomic oxygen's fine-structure lines above the 0.3 Pa base ([loss_response/infrared.py](loss_response/infrared.py)), and the loss response places its heat where the limb tracing puts it. Ions, species chemistry above the base, eddy diffusion, acceleration and tides remain outside this limiting model; the loss response adds tides, atomic oxygen and hydrogen as separate steps. Kinetic and tidal approximation warnings accompany outputs. Numerical convergence is a numerical check; physical stability is a separate question.

The original solar table starts at 202 nm. The inherited optical implementation leaves a response interval between its X-ray treatment below about 24.8 nm and titania optical constants starting near 120.18 nm. A historical five-band solar benchmark is now transcribed separately, with all atmospheric/filter responses left unspecified. This recovers a coarse energy reference while preserving the unresolved optical and chemical requirements.

Read [findings](../research/findings.md) for equations, bounds, source limitations and numerical examples. The new [232-case output](../research/studies/environment_screens/results/molecular_columns.csv) is separate from the byte-pinned historical tables. Molecular losses do not constitute total escape or a billion-year lifetime estimate.
