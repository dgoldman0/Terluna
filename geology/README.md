# Geology

The geology lane holds the solid Moon's slow response to the Open Moon: the present interior's temperature and the viscosity it gives the mantle, the load and tidal Love numbers of a layered, self-gravitating viscoelastic Moon from a year to a billion years, and the orientation the Moon takes as the water's weight sinks in. It reads geography's water products and the crustal-loading study's grids as data products and writes its own; nothing here regenerates geography's atlas, lakes or shorelines.

| File | Holds |

| [viscoelastic.py](viscoelastic.py) | Load and tidal Love numbers of a layered Maxwell Moon by the correspondence principle at real s, the time response as a fitted relaxation spectrum, a Gaver–Stehfest check and the homogeneous sphere's closed forms |

## How today's Moon yields under the water

`python -m geology.interior_response` computes the present Moon's response from its thermal state and follows the Moon's orientation through time (4 November 2026). It runs in about 8 minutes on one thread and 290 MB.

- **The present Moon is stiff to several hundred kilometres for the water's turn.** The central temperature profile (Karato 2013, dry olivine) with Hirth & Kohlstedt's (2003) olivine flow laws, 3 mm grains and a dry mantle gives a viscosity falling from 10²⁸ Pa s at 100 km depth to 10²⁵ at 200 km, 10²¹ at 500 km and 10¹⁷ at 1,000 km, above the low-viscosity zone's published 2×10¹⁶ Pa s. A degree-2 load relaxes through a layer only where its viscous time, about (19/2) η/(ρga), is shorter than the time asked about, and because the Moon's rock is stiff against its weak gravity (μ ≈ 7ρga), that time is some seventy Maxwell times. The layer that holds the load therefore behaves as an elastic lid (E = 100 GPa) about 730 km thick over the first thousand years, 500 km at 100,000 years, 390 km at a million and 300 km at ten million, deeper than the depths at which the Maxwell time reaches those times (480 km for a thousand years, 200 km for ten million). The mare basins' 20–75 km recorded the Moon when it was hotter; today's rigid layer is several times thicker.
- **The turn eases slowly from the elastic 8°.** With the seas at 28% and the rain-fed lakes in place at once, the central case moves the pole 8.02° at once, 7.93° at one year as the low-viscosity zone and the hot deep mantle give way, 7.76° at a century, 7.62° at 1,000 years, 7.24° at 100,000 years, 6.59° at 10 million years and 5.62° at a billion, always toward 167° W. The point beneath Earth moves from 7.97° N, 6.10° E to 6.53° N, 5.25° E at 10 million years. The stiff bound keeps 7.97° at 1,000 years and 7.62° at 10 million. The soft bound falls to 6.78° within a year and 3.69° within a century, and settles at 1.89° by 1,000 years.
- **The temperature profile decides the range.** On the central assumptions otherwise, the cool profile (Kuskov & Kronrod 2009) holds 7.40° at 10 million years and the warm one (Khan et al. 2014) falls to 5.01° at 100,000 years and 1.89° by 10 million. A mantle with 100 ppm water or 1 mm grains gives 5.9° at 10 million years and 10 mm grains 7.0°; the creep stress (0.3–3 MPa) and the low-viscosity zone's viscosity (2–4.5×10¹⁶ Pa s) change the turn by under 0.04°. The measured heat flow leans toward the cooler half of the range without settling it. Conducted through a 2 km megaregolith of 0.2 W/m/K and bedrock of 1.4–3 W/m/K with no heat produced in the crust, the cool profile's 434 °C at 60 km needs 9–16 mW/m², the central profile's 541 °C at 40 km 15–25 mW/m² and the warm profile's 880 °C at 50 km 20–35 mW/m², against Apollo's 16–21 mW/m² and regional means of 13–17; heat produced in the crust raises each requirement. The soft bound, built on the warm profile, is therefore the least likely of the three cases.
- **The near side's warmer mantle keeps more of the turn.** The South Pole–Aitken sea holds half the seas' water (5.5×10¹⁸ kg of 1.15×10¹⁹ kg at 28% cover), does most of the turning and lies on the far side. Under the far side's response (75 K colder than the central profile) with the rest of the water under the near side's (75 K warmer), the pole stands at 6.95° at 10 million years against 6.59°, and at 6.86–7.03° for a near–far difference of 100–200 K (Park et al. 2025).
- **The 8° state is the one people live with, unless the shallow mantle is as warm as the warm profile.** Under the central profile the pole stays within 0.3° of the elastic state for a century, 0.4° for a thousand years and about 1° for a million years; under the stiff profile within 0.1° for a thousand years and 0.4° for ten million. Places drift by tenths of a degree over the centuries of building and settling. Under the soft bound the pole moves 1.2° in the first year and half the turn within a century.
- **The deep seas' floors sink by tens of metres over the first hundred thousand years.** Sized from the same response for degrees 3–20 and scaled to the crustal-loading study's relaxed result, the floors where the seas are deeper than 2 km sink against the mean land by about 6 m at once, 15 m in 1,000 years, 34 m in 100,000 and 78 m in 10 million under the central profile, and by 15 m in 10 million under the stiff one. The study's relaxed limit is 490 m under its central 40 km lithosphere and 240 m under 150 km; the present Moon reaches a sixth of the former in ten million years. The soft bound reaches 406 m by 1,000 years and 450 m by 10,000.
- **The pores' water, the re-forming bulge and the water that holds 28% change the turn by tenths of a degree.** The groundwater uptake's central history changes the central turn by −0.5° to +0.2° between 1,000 years and a million (7.0–7.2°), as the pores' uneven filling did in the orientation report, and lifts the spin axis's tilt to 2.39° at 100,000 years. The rotational and tidal bulge re-forms about the new axes only as the tidal Love number rises (k₂ from 0.024 to 0.31 by 10 million years, against the fluid 1.44), adding 0.08° to the central turn at 10 million years. The extra water that holds 28% as the floors sag, estimated from the study's relaxed cases in step with the sag, adds 0.04° at 1,000 years and 0.16° at 10 million (6.75°).

**How it is set up.** Three published present-day profiles span the Moon's thermal state, read from Khan et al. (2014, Figure 7b) to about 20 °C: Kuskov & Kronrod (2009, seismic velocities; 611 °C at 200 km, 1,311 °C at 1,000 km), Karato (2013, conductivity and tidal Q for dry olivine; 796 °C and about 1,540 °C) and Khan et al. (2014, electromagnetic sounding and geodesy; 1,003 °C and 1,498 °C). The central profile is Karato's: it lies between the others in the upper mantle, joins Khan's below 700 km and needs a surface heat flow within the Apollo values. The near and far sides add and subtract 75 K, half the middle of Park et al.'s (2025) 100–200 K near–far difference, with 50 and 100 K as its range. Hirth & Kohlstedt's (2003) flow laws, as reproduced by Turner et al. (2015), give diffusion and dislocation creep in parallel, η = σ/(3ε̇); the assumptions that come with them are grain size (1–10 mm, 3 mm central), water (dry, or 100 ppm H₂O, about 1,600 H/10⁶ Si, Karato's hydrous case) and the stress for dislocation creep (0.3–3 MPa, 1 MPa central). The laws are measured on Earth's olivine; their lunar use carries that calibration, and grain size and water are the largest assumptions after temperature. The soft bound takes the warm profile, water, 1 mm grains and 3 MPa; the stiff bound the cool profile, a dry mantle, 10 mm grains and 0.3 MPa. The interior is the crustal-loading study's (Briaud et al. 2023, Category 4a) with its mantle cut into 10 km Maxwell shells, the low-viscosity zone within 500 km of the centre at its published viscosity and the cold anorthosite crust elastic. The Love numbers come from the static self-gravitating equations (Takeuchi & Saito 1972) in the Laplace domain with μ(s) = μs/(s + μ/η), integrated by a fourth-order Magnus method at 127 values of s from 10⁻¹³ to 10⁸ per year; a shell joins the fluid region above the core once its transformed rigidity falls below 10⁻⁹ ρga/(2n + 1). The step response is a relaxation spectrum on 185 fixed rates (8 a decade) fitted by non-negative least squares, k(t) = k_e + Σ a_j(1 − e^{−r_j t}), a discretised normal-mode sum; Gaver–Stehfest inversion checks it. The response is incompressible, as the interior's source is: a compressible Maxwell shell of uniform density is unstably stratified once it flows. The orientation follows the orientation report's method (main's `research/studies/moon_orientation/`, 4 November 2026): today's moments from lunar laser ranging (β, γ; Murphy 2013) and C/MR² = 0.393112 (Williams et al. 2014), the seas' and lakes' load from the crustal-loading study's rigid geoid change at 28% cover, the pores' water from the uptake's central latitude bands on the atlas's columns, and the Cassini relation for the tilt. Through time the water's degree-2 inertia acts through 1 + k′₂(t) and the rotational and tidal bulge (k_f = 1.436 from the Darwin–Radau relation) re-forms about the moving axes through the tidal k₂(t), both as exact convolutions over loads linear within each step, iterated to agreement at each step. The deep seas' sag takes the atlas seas' load at degrees 3–20 through h′ₙ(t), the mean fall of floors under more than 2 km of water against the mean land, scaled by the ratio of the crustal-loading study's 490 m to the same measure under a 40 km elastic lid; it sizes the sag and does not solve the sea-level equation.

**The turned frame.** [results/moon_orientation.json](results/moon_orientation.json) holds the elastic state set for the second set of full climate simulations: the rotation matrix from today's body frame (x toward 0° N, 0° E; z north) to the turned one, the north pole at 81.95° N, 167.14° W, the south pole at 81.95° S, 12.86° E and the point beneath Earth at 8.00° N, 6.09° E, all in today's coordinates, with named places converted. `orientation.to_turned` and `orientation.to_today` convert latitude and longitude between the frames; Jura Island's centre, 45.0° N 36.1° W, goes to 39.4° N, 37.0° W.

**Checks.** The tests in [tests/](tests/) and the checks the runner records in [results/interior_response.json](results/interior_response.json) cover:
- a homogeneous incompressible Maxwell sphere: elastic and fluid Love numbers within 10⁻⁴ of the closed forms at degrees 2 and 5, and its single relaxation time recovered by the fitted spectrum and by Gaver–Stehfest within 3×10⁻³ of the relaxation; for the central interior the two inversions agree within 3×10⁻⁶ from 10 years to 10 million;
- the crustal-loading study's elastic interior: its compressible h′ₙ and k′ₙ at degrees 2–20 within 2×10⁻³ (h′₂ = −0.05994, k′₂ = −0.018545) and its incompressible tidal k₂ = 0.02427 and h₂ = 0.04078;
- step and fluid-threshold convergence within 10⁻³;
- elastic lids (E = 100 GPa, ν = 0.25) over a fluid interior against the study's relaxed thin shell: k′₂ = −0.633, −0.538 and −0.365 under 40, 75 and 150 km lids against its −0.718, −0.591 and −0.450. The thick lid is stiffer by 8–19% at degree 2 and 3–15% at degrees 3–5, most at 150 km, where a thin shell's membrane approximation is weakest; under 40 km its crust below the lid is fluid, where the thin shell carries the whole crustal column;
- the secular tidal k₂ under 25 and 50 km lids, 1.12 and 0.91, against Keane & Matsuyama's (2014) 1.08 and 0.86 for their own three-layer interior;
- the monthly tide: k₂ = 0.0253 and h₂ = 0.0425 against the measured 0.02405 and 0.0371. The interior's elastic k₂ (0.02427) already stands 1% above the measured monthly value and 6% above the 0.0229 left once the Moon's anelasticity is removed (Garcia et al. 2019); the low-viscosity zone, whose Maxwell time is nine days, relaxes partly within a month and adds 4%, with a phase lag that gives Q ≈ 12. Its source fits the monthly k₂ with a viscous core, which this reading takes as inviscid; the zone's relaxation enters the response within the first year;
- the orientation: today's moments against GRAIL's J2 and C22, the Cassini relation against today's 1.5424°, the Darwin–Radau relation against a homogeneous body's 3/2, a small cap against a point mass, the geoid and mass routes to inertia, the orientation report's elastic state (8.05° toward 167.1° W, the point beneath Earth at 8.00° N, 6.09° E) from the crustal-loading grids, the frame's round trip and Jura's new place.

**What it leaves out.**
- The thermal state is today's and spherically symmetric apart from the near–far split, which applies two spherical responses to two parts of the load. Lateral differences in the lithosphere, the maria's basalt and the Procellarum region's heat enter only through that split.
- The flow laws are steady-state creep of Earth's olivine; transient creep, which adds relaxation at short times, and melt weakening above the low-viscosity zone are absent. The upper mantle's orthopyroxene, a large part of it in Khan et al.'s inversion, has its own flow law, not included. The crust is elastic; under the warm profile its lower part reaches about 850 °C, where a feldspathic crust's own flow law, not sourced here, would decide its strength.
- Dislocation creep enters as an effective viscosity at a fixed stress, so the response stays linear.
- The response is incompressible. The compressible elastic k′₂ is 12% larger in magnitude and h′₂ twice as large: the elastic turn differs by 0.02°, and this lane's elastic sag of the deep floors is about 7 m against the study's 17 m.
- The sea-level equation is not solved through time: the seas' load is the rigid one, and the extra water that holds 28% as the floors sag is estimated from the study's relaxed cases. The lakes keep their load, and the pores' water follows the uptake's central history.
- Over hundreds of millions of years the Moon cools and its lid thickens; the billion-year values hold the present thermal state and mark a trend, and the turn's course beyond about 100 million years needs a cooling interior.
- The pole's path between states (its free wobble and the damping that follows a fast filling) is not computed.

## Reproducing it

Run from the repository's root with `nice -n 10`, one thread (`OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 MKL_NUM_THREADS=1`) and `ulimit -v 6000000`. The inputs are read by path and their sha256 recorded in the products: the crustal-loading grids (`crustal-loading/crustal_loading_nmax359.npz` on the research drive) and results (`geography/results/crustal_loading.json` on `domain/hydrosphere` at c2c8624a), `geography/products/atlas_28pct_4ppd.npz`, `geography/products/drainage_28pct_16ppd.npz` and `geography/results/groundwater_uptake.json`. The degree 2–50 tables of h′ₙ(t), k′ₙ(t), hₙ(t) and kₙ(t) for the central and bracketing profiles are in `geology/interior-response/interior_response_love.npz` on the research drive. `python -m pytest geology/tests -q` runs the tests in under a minute.
